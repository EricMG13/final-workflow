## Findings

### Credit view

TransDigm has a durable operating franchise, but high debt and shareholder distributions make cash retention—not earnings growth alone—the decisive credit issue. Installed-base replacement demand supports revenue durability, while FY2024 operating cash flow of USD 2,045 million and free cash flow of USD 1,880 million demonstrate debt-service capacity. The weakest point is capital allocation: gross debt reached USD 25,068 million, and the debt-and-cash-funded distribution substantially reduces the reporting-date liquidity cushion. Gross leverage of 6.01x on management-adjusted earnings leaves creditors dependent on sustained operating performance and financing access. The outcome turns on whether cash generation remains resilient and is retained after acquisitions and distributions; the available evidence supports a guarded fundamental view, not an assumption of automatic deleveraging. [B1–B6]

### Key findings

**Cash allocation is the principal credit swing factor.** TransDigm generated FY2024 free cash flow of USD 1,880 million, but paid USD 2,038 million in dividends and equivalents and deployed USD 2,347 million on acquisitions, net of acquired cash. Free cash flow after those uses was negative USD 2,505 million on the canonical definition. This was not an operating cash deficit: it was a discretionary-deployment funding requirement. The separate September financing and declaration reinforce the evidence that stronger operating cash flow does not necessarily accrue to debt reduction. **Category: Analyst Interpretation.** [B2, B4, B5]

**The apparent net-deleveraging signal is weaker than the gross-debt signal.** Gross debt/EBITDA As Defined increased to 6.01x from 5.87x, while net debt/EBITDA As Defined decreased to 4.51x from 4.84x. TransDigm held USD 6,261 million in reporting-date cash, but the October distribution was already declared and financed before the snapshot. Management's post-payment pro forma liquidity of USD 2,919 million is therefore more decision-relevant for that commitment than reported liquidity of USD 7,267 million. Declaration and financing belong to the snapshot; October settlement remains a subsequent event. **Category: Analyst Interpretation.** [B3, B4]

**Operating improvement supports debt service, without eliminating earnings-definition risk.** FY2024 operating EBIT/net interest coverage improved to 2.75x from 2.51x, and EBITDA As Defined/net interest coverage improved to 3.24x from 2.92x. The direction is favorable on both definitions, supporting current service capacity rather than merely a larger adjusted denominator. Nevertheless, gross debt/management EBITDA was 6.57x, compared with 6.01x on EBITDA As Defined; the choice of earnings measure materially affects apparent leverage tolerance. **Category: Analyst Interpretation.** [B2, B3, B7]

**The business has replacement-demand durability, not contracted immunity from aviation shocks.** Designed-in components generate aftermarket demand over an aircraft's life, but fleet utilization, airline economics, OEM schedules and defense procurement timing still transmit into sales and collections. Management's historical characterization of higher and more stable aftermarket gross profit supports a resilience hypothesis, not a forecast or guaranteed revenue stream. The credit benefit is demand persistence beyond initial aircraft production, with continued exposure to common aviation drivers. **Category: Analyst Interpretation; the historical stability characterization remains Management Language.** [B1, B6]

**The near-term funding calendar is broader than the note maturity calendar.** Refinancings and extensions demonstrate historical financing access and move term-loan and note maturities outward, but the receivables securitization matures on July 11, 2025. Its funding and availability depend on receivables, while amortization and lease obligations continue separately. Creditors should monitor renewal and borrowing-base availability rather than infer an absence of near-term funding needs from the later note maturities. **Category: Analyst Interpretation.** [B8]

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strength — Installed-base replacement demand. Category: Analyst Interpretation.** TransDigm's designed-in component positions extend the commercial relationship from original equipment into replacement consumption. This can sustain revenue through the aircraft life cycle and support debt service beyond new-build deliveries. The strength is the installed-base mechanism, not an independently verified market-share or pricing-power claim. [B1]

**Strength — Demonstrated free cash generation. Category: Analyst Interpretation.** FY2024 operating cash flow of USD 2,045 million supported USD 165 million of capital expenditures and USD 1,880 million of free cash flow. The cash measure is after reported cash interest and taxes, making it relevant to resources available before acquisitions and distributions. Conversion improved in the canonical comparison, providing an operating buffer against financing costs even though discretionary uses absorbed that buffer. [B2]

**Strength — Improved operating coverage and completed financing execution. Category: Analyst Interpretation.** Stronger EBIT/net interest coverage supports the ability to service debt from operating earnings, while completed repricings, refinancing and extensions evidence actual historical access to funding. Together these reduce dependence on an immediate operating turnaround; they do not establish future financing availability. [B3, B8]

**Weakness — Shareholder extraction competes directly with creditor protection. Category: Analyst Interpretation.** The September dividend recapitalization added secured debt and committed cash to shareholders instead of reducing liabilities. The earlier cash-funded distribution makes this more than an isolated reporting-date payable. Debt-service capacity and creditor cash retention must therefore be assessed separately, with subsequent distributions monitored as a potential reversal of organic deleveraging. [B4]

**Weakness — High gross debt leaves limited room for earnings disappointment. Category: Analyst Interpretation.** Gross debt of USD 25,068 million against EBITDA As Defined of USD 4,173 million produces gross leverage of 6.01x. A reduction in aviation demand or cash margins would weaken debt-service flexibility before considering discretionary deployment. The higher 6.57x ratio on management EBITDA also shows sensitivity to adjustment acceptance. [B3, B7]

**Weakness — Acquisition deployment complicates cash retention and financial comparability. Category: Analyst Interpretation.** Annual acquisition cash of USD 2,347 million exceeded free cash flow before distributions. Acquired businesses contribute only ownership-period earnings to actual annual results, so the observed funding requirement is clear but acquisition-pro-forma leverage is not established. Creditors need realized cash generation and integration spending rather than assumed synergies to judge whether deployment improves debt capacity. [B2, B5]
