### Transaction summary and new debt issues

FY2024 was a year of heavy refinancing, debt-funded acquisitions and a debt-funded special dividend. The 10-K states that "During fiscal 2024, the Company completed approximately $21,000 million in debt financing transactions, inclusive of new issuances and refinancing of existing debt" (10-K page 53). These are Documentary Facts unless the sentence says otherwise.

New debt and refinancing. On November 28, 2023 the company issued "$2,000 million in new senior debt ($1,000 million in 7.125% senior secured notes due 2031 and $1,000 million in Tranche J term loans)", used to fund the acquisition of CPI's Electron Device Business and for general corporate purposes (10-K page 53). On February 27, 2024 it extended the revolving credit facility from May 2026 to February 2029, increased commitments "from $810 million to $910 million", and issued "$4,400 million in new secured debt ($2,200 million in 6.375% senior secured notes due 2029 and $2,200 million in 6.625% senior secured notes due 2032)" to redeem the 6.25% senior secured notes due 2026 (10-K page 53). On March 22, 2024 it repriced the Tranche I term loans, replaced Tranche H with Tranche K maturing March 2030, and issued "an additional $550 million in 6.375% senior secured notes due 2029" to redeem the 7.50% senior subordinated notes due 2027 (10-K page 53). On June 4, 2024 it repriced Tranche J and extended part of Tranche I into Tranche J maturing February 28, 2031 (10-K page 53). On July 12, 2024 it increased the securitization facility capacity "from $450 million to $650 million" (10-K page 53). On September 19, 2024 it issued "$3,000 million in new senior secured debt ($1,500 million in 6.00% senior secured notes due January 15, 2033 and $1,500 million in Tranche L term loans" (10-K page 54).

Acquisitions. The company completed SEI on May 21, 2024 "for approximately $171 million in cash", CPI's Electron Device Business on June 6, 2024 "for approximately $1,385 million in cash", and Raptor Scientific on July 31, 2024 "for approximately $647 million in cash" (10-K page 54). Total FY2024 acquisition spending appears in the CP-1 cash-flow table.

Special dividends. A special dividend of 35 US dollars per share was paid on November 27, 2023, "funded by existing cash on hand" (10-K page 54). On September 19, 2024, "concurrently with the $3,000 million issuance of new senior secured debt", the board declared a special dividend of 75 US dollars per share. The 10-K says total payments of "approximately $4,348 million" were "funded by a combination of $3,000 million in new senior secured debt (as previously noted) and existing cash on hand" and "were made in October 2024" (10-K page 54).

Subsequent events, after the as-of date and not part of FY2024 facts. The dividend was paid on October 18, 2024 (FY2025 Q1 10-Q page 44). The remaining securitization capacity was drawn in October 2024 (10-K page 152). During the first quarter of fiscal 2025 the company "repurchased 252,800 shares of common stock ... for a total amount of $316 million" (FY2025 Q1 10-Q page 44). At December 28, 2024 the issuer reported cash liquidity of "$ 3,308" in millions, made up of cash and revolver availability (FY2025 Q1 10-Q page 44). The 10-Q states again that "There is no maturity on any tranche of term loans or notes until November 2027" (FY2025 Q1 10-Q page 45).

Liquidity effect, as Analyst Interpretation: the September 2024 notes and term loans raised cash that left the company within a month as a shareholder distribution. The year-end cash balance in CP-1 therefore overstates the cash available to creditors, which is why CP-1 shows the issuer's pro forma cash liquidity and a labelled pro forma leverage sensitivity.

Executed terms of these instruments are not in the source set (CP-0). The descriptions above come from the 10-K narrative, not from indentures or credit agreement amendments.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: high proprietary and aftermarket content and defense exposure (10-K pages 4 and 9). Mechanic: aftermarket and defense demand follow the installed fleet and government budgets more than new-aircraft deliveries, which supports revenue visibility. Implication: supports debt service at a high leverage level; the commercial OEM share remains exposed to Boeing and Airbus production rates (10-K page 11).
- Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: CP-1 EBITDA As Defined margin and free cash flow; issuer pricing strategy (10-K page 5). Mechanic: margins depend on the ability to price proprietary parts; a pricing challenge from customers or governments would reduce cash available for interest. Implication: coverage per CP-1 C4 has room for some margin pressure; it is not tested against a scenario here.
- Concentration risk. Evidence: top ten customers at about 42% of net sales, none above 10% (10-K page 17). Mechanic: distributors and OEMs concentrate purchasing; loss of a large account would reduce volume. Implication: moderate concentration; monitor distributor relationships.
- Shareholder alignment. Evidence: debt-funded special dividend in September 2024 and the stated priority order (10-K page 54). Mechanic: the issuer re-levers when earnings grow, so leverage stays high and the secured share of the capital structure grows. The described incremental tests are set on the issuer's EBITDA As Defined, so earnings growth and add-backs both raise secured debt capacity (CP-1 covenant section). Implication: creditors should not expect deleveraging; leverage tolerance and refinancing depend on continued market access before the first maturity in November 2027. Counter-evidence: no maturity before November 2027 and large reported liquidity at the year end. The logged dividend-equivalent conflict does not change this view.
- Key uncertainties. Executed debt terms, covenant capacity for further distributions, and ownership detail are not supplied.
