### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The central transaction is a pending take-private. On May 27, 2026, Caesars, Fertitta Gaming Holdco, LLC and its merger subsidiary signed an Agreement and Plan of Merger under which "Merger Sub will merge with and into the Company, with the Company continuing as the surviving corporation and direct wholly owned subsidiary of Fertitta Gaming" (10-Q line 273). The release says the agreement was "announced on May 28, 2026" (release line 106). Each share converts into cash of "$31.00", plus a ticking fee if closing has not occurred by "June 26, 2027" (10-Q line 277). Documentary Fact.

Conditions and protection. Closing needs approval "by the holders of at least a majority of all of the outstanding shares", expiry of the HSR Act waiting period and "certain gaming regulatory approvals" (10-Q line 294). Either party may terminate if the merger has not closed by May 27, 2027, with automatic extensions to August 27, 2027 and November 27, 2027 while only regulatory conditions remain (10-Q line 296). Caesars would owe "a termination fee of $200 million" in specified circumstances; Fertitta Gaming would owe "a reverse termination fee of $450 million" if the merger fails on antitrust or gaming-law grounds or at the end date with only regulatory conditions open (10-Q lines 297 to 298). A no-shop applies from "July 11, 2026" (10-Q line 290). Documentary Fact.

Financing and existing debt. The supplied documents do not describe how Fertitta Gaming will fund the merger or how the existing CEI credit facilities, notes and VICI and GLPI leases are treated at closing. The 10-Q lists among its risks "the risk that the financing required to fund the proposed transaction is not obtained on the terms anticipated or at all" and "risks associated with increased leverage from the proposed transaction" (10-Q line 1391). The Merger Agreement is exhibit 2.1 to the 8-K of May 28, 2026 (10-Q line 305), which is not in the source set. Insufficient Information.

Recent debt activity. On July 8, 2025, Caesars "fully redeemed all of the $546 million outstanding principal amount of the CEI Senior Notes due 2027", funded by revolver borrowings and proceeds of a note receivable from the WSOP trademark sale (10-K line 1367). No new bond issue is described for 2026 in the supplied documents; the six-month cash flows show revolver and term-loan borrowings and repayments (CP-1 cash-flow table). Earlier divestitures raised cash: the WSOP trademark for "$500 million" on October 29, 2024, and The LINQ Promenade for "$275 million" on December 12, 2024 (10-K lines 1053 to 1054). Documentary Fact.

Liquidity effect, as Analyst Interpretation: before closing, liquidity of about 2.9 billion (CP-1) covers the remaining 2026 debt service and lease payments the issuer estimates. After closing, liquidity and leverage depend on the acquisition financing, which is Not supplied.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: 52 properties in 18 states and a digital business in 34 jurisdictions (10-K lines 95 to 96). Mechanic: regional and digital growth offset the Las Vegas decline in Q2 2026 (release lines 13 to 15 and 50 to 52). Implication: diversified revenue supports debt service, but Las Vegas, the largest Adjusted EBITDA segment, is falling.
- Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: lease rent of about 1.4 billion a year against Adjusted EBITDA of about 3.6 billion (CP-1 C1). Mechanic: fixed, inflation-linked rent leaves less cash for debt service when earnings fall. Implication: leverage tolerance is lower than the debt-only ratio suggests.
- Concentration risk. Evidence: segment figures above. Mechanic: Las Vegas and Regional produce most of the Adjusted EBITDA; a downturn in consumer spending would hit both. Implication: cyclical exposure; monitor Las Vegas trends.
- Owner alignment. Evidence: the pending take-private (10-Q line 273). Mechanic: a new owner sets capital allocation and may add leverage; the 10-Q names "risks associated with increased leverage" (10-Q line 1391). Implication: the main credit uncertainty is the post-closing capital structure, which the supplied set does not describe.
- Key uncertainties: merger financing, treatment of existing debt and leases, covenant definitions, events after July 28, 2026.
