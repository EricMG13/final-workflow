## Findings

### Overall source-sufficiency read

The effective source set is one audited annual report, three unaudited quarterly reports and three management earnings decks, all from the issuer and all dated on or before 2026-10-22, inside the 2026-11-05 cutoff. The two filing families control their own facts; the decks are attributed management claims and cannot override a filing. For a credit snapshot of American Airlines, Inc. the set is sufficient to proceed, with bounded limitations. The assertion is SOURCE_SUFFICIENCY_ONLY: nothing here evaluates downstream model input contracts, normalizes quarters or reconciles figures (CP_MODEL_INPUT_CONTRACT_NOT_EVALUATED).

Evidence to source risk to consequence: the filings are combined AAG and American filings (C4, C11), so AAG-level and American-level statements both sit in each file; the risk is that a figure is read from the wrong perimeter, which would mislead CP-1 if left unmarked. The strongest contrary evidence to a clean READY is that no executed financing document, indenture or credit agreement is supplied (the run context says none, and the exhibit lists only incorporate such documents by reference, C9, C28), so covenant terms are known only through the filings' summaries (C23 to C26). Consequence: debt, maturity and liquidity work can proceed from the filings, but legal-term detail is limited to what the filings state, and the next evidence action is to obtain the executed credit agreements and indentures if covenant levels or baskets are needed.

### Entity and perimeter

- American Airlines, Inc. (American) is the requested issuer. It is the wholly-owned subsidiary of American Airlines Group Inc. (AAG), which holds all 1,000 shares of American (C2, C4), and it files its own statements in Item 8B of the 10-K and Item 1B of each 10-Q (C11). It has its own commission file number (C1, C10).
- American meets the conditions for the reduced disclosure format (C3), so Part III information is omitted for American; this is not an evidence gap for credit work.
- AAG is the listed parent (ticker AAL per the run context). Parent-level debt is visible: the unsecured PSP1 note is listed in the debt table (C18) and the 10-K contractual-obligations table shows a separate block for "AAG Parent and Other AAG Subsidiaries" (C6) alongside the American block (C5).
- The two perimeters carry different figures for the same line items, for example total available liquidity of AAG and of American (C22) and AAG's balance sheet against American's, which includes related-party receivables (C20). The decks speak only for AAG. No guarantor list is supplied: the run context lists no guarantors, and which entity borrows or guarantees each facility is stated only in prose in the debt note (for example that American and AAG entered into the credit agreement amendments, page:16 of the third-quarter 10-Q). The guarantor and restricted-group perimeter of each instrument is therefore an unresolved distinction (see Gaps).
