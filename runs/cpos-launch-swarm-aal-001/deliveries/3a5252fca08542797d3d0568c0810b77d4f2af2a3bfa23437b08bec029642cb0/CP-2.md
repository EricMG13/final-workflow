## Findings

### Credit view

American Airlines, Inc. is a large, liquid but heavily indebted network carrier whose current earnings are too thin to service its debt without continued access to secured financing. The strongest support is USD 11,763 million of available liquidity at September 30, 2026, with nothing drawn on revolving facilities and almost all debt backed by identified collateral. The weakest point is unhedged fuel: a 62.7% higher fuel price per gallon cut third-quarter operating income to USD 115 million on USD 16,335 million of revenue, and last-twelve-month EBITDA covered net interest only 1.74x. The outcome turns on fuel prices, whether yield-led revenue holds, and the USD 7,861 million of principal due in 2028. Confidence is Medium (Analyst Interpretation). [CP-1 table; CP-1A operating model]

### Key findings

First, earnings are the pressure point, not liquidity. Revenue grew 19.3% in the third quarter of 2026 to USD 16,335 million, but fuel expense rose USD 1,868 million to USD 4,635 million and operating income fell to USD 115 million from USD 151 million; the pre-tax loss widened to USD 334 million from USD 142 million (Documentary Fact; CP-1 table). Nine-month operating income was USD 521 million against USD 1,015 million a year earlier. The mechanism is that the company carries no fuel hedges and recaptures higher fuel only through fares, so a fuel move reaches operating income and cash flow within the same quarter (Documentary Fact; CP-1A operating model).

Second, leverage is high on every measure the sources allow. Management's own net debt was USD 28,079 million at September 30, 2026, up from USD 27,438 million at March 31, 2026; against last-twelve-month EBITDAR built from income statement lines of USD 4,127 million that is 6.80x, versus management's stated target of 3.0x (Documentary Fact for the debt figures, Analyst Interpretation for the ratio; CP-1 calculation C13). The ratio omits regional depreciation and rent, so management's own adjusted measure would differ, but the gap to the target is large on any definition the sources support. This also corrects a garbled bullet in the CP-1 handoff, which should have said that net debt rose between those dates.

Third, debt service rests on liquidity and refinancing access more than on earnings. Last-twelve-month operating income of USD 973 million was below net interest expense of USD 1,664 million, and last-twelve-month free cash flow on a simple definition was negative USD 91 million (Documentary Fact; CP-1 calculations C6 and C8). Nine-month operating cash flow of USD 4,541 million was helped by a rise in air traffic and loyalty liabilities, and the derived third-quarter operating cash flow was negative USD 153 million with third-quarter capex of USD 1,095 million (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 calculation C7). Customer-funded float lifts liquidity in good quarters and unwinds when bookings or mileage sales slow.

Fourth, the maturity profile is manageable near term and concentrated in 2028. American's principal due is USD 631 million for the remainder of 2026, USD 3,550 million in 2027 and USD 7,861 million in 2028, the peak year, against USD 11,797 million of AAG liquidity (Documentary Fact; CP-1 table). Nearly 87% of Note 5 debt is secured (CP-1 calculation C11), and 2026 refinancings pushed the 2014 term loans to May 2033 and revolvers to March 2031, so refinancing depends mainly on asset-backed markets for aircraft and loyalty collateral (Documentary Fact; CP-1A transaction summary). Nothing in the supplied sources shows how much headroom those collateral pools have.

Fifth, the business has real franchise support. Cash from co-branded credit card and other partners was USD 6,200 million in 2025, Citi became exclusive issuer of the co-branded portfolio in 2026, and premium and corporate revenue are growing per management (Documentary Fact for the partner cash, Management Language for the premium claims; CP-1A operating model). That cash is also the collateral base of the AAdvantage Financing, so it supports both revenue durability and secured borrowing capacity.

Sixth, structure matters for this issuer. The three PSP promissory notes of USD 3,746 million are the only unsecured debt and sit at the AAG parent, while American itself carries USD 25,876 million of debt and finance leases and a USD 10,430 million receivable from related parties (Documentary Fact; CP-1 table, CP-1A company profile). The terms of that affiliate receivable and the guarantor perimeter by instrument are not supplied, which limits any view of how value flows between American and its parent.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity: USD 11,763 million at American and USD 11,797 million at AAG, up from USD 9,233 million at December 31, 2025 at AAG, with no revolver draw, gives the issuer time to absorb a fuel shock (Documentary Fact). Financing access: the issuer raised about USD 4,100 million of aircraft-backed debt in the first nine months of 2026, repaid USD 1,000 million of 8.50% secured notes and a USD 629 million term loan, and extended its largest bank maturities, which shows market access and lowers near-term refinancing risk (Documentary Fact). Franchise and cash generation from loyalty partners, with USD 6,200 million of partner cash in 2025, supports revenue visibility and collateral value (Documentary Fact; Management Language for the premium and corporate growth claims). Capital allocation: no dividends or share repurchases in 2025 or 2024, and prepayments of expensive secured debt in 2026, are creditor-supportive actions (Documentary Fact). Scale and network: nine hubs, over 350 destinations and a mainline fleet in which 547 of 1,013 aircraft are owned give a large collateral base for asset-backed financing (Documentary Fact).

Weaknesses. Earnings and coverage: operating income is near break-even in the latest quarter and below net interest on a last-twelve-month basis (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation for the coverage ratios). Unhedged fuel exposure: the company states it does not hedge and fuel price per gallon rose 62.7% in the third quarter, with management guiding to a full-year adjusted loss per share (Documentary Fact; Management Language for the guidance). Leverage: management net debt of USD 28,079 million against a stated 3.0x target is a long way from target, and the company is still adding secured aircraft debt to fund USD 24,056 million of aircraft and engine purchase commitments (Documentary Fact). Cost inflation outside fuel: management's CASM excluding special items, fuel and profit sharing rose 3.0% while load factor fell 2.6 points, and mainline pilot, mechanic and fleet service contracts become amendable in 2027, inside the debt maturity window (Documentary Fact; Management Language for the CASM measure). Structural subordination: the unsecured PSP notes sit at the parent and American's own creditors rank against encumbered assets whose value is not disclosed (Analyst Interpretation). Transparency: covenant thresholds, executed documents and affiliate balance terms are not in the supplied sources, so covenant headroom cannot be tested (Insufficient Information).
