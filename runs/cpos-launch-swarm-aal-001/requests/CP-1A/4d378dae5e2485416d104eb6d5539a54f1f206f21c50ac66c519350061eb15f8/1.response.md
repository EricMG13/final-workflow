### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no single acquisition, buyout or recapitalisation transaction in the supplied sources; the transaction record is a sequence of refinancings and aircraft financings. No sponsor, use of proceeds beyond general fleet financing, or dividend recapitalisation is documented. Status of every item below is completed as of September 30, 2026 unless stated.

New debt issued or amended in 2026, taken from the third quarter 10-Q note on debt (Q3 page:16 to page:20): in February 2026 the 2025 AAdvantage Term Loan Facility was replaced in the same principal amount with a lower margin by the AAdvantage Issuers (American and AAdvantage Loyalty IP Ltd.); in March 2026 the 2013, 2014 and 2023 revolving facilities were reset with maturities in March 2031 and an aggregate size that management says rose from 3.0 billion to 3.1 billion, and a separate 350 revolver was extended to March 2028; in March 2026 American borrowed 870 under junior Class (B)R aircraft EETC financing at a fixed 5.61%, maturing 2028 to 2029; in May 2026 the 2014 term loans due January 2027 were refinanced to May 2033 with 703 of incremental term loans at a margin of 3.00% over SOFR; in May 2026 the 2026-1 aircraft EETCs of about 1.1 billion were issued; in June 2026 the 2026 Engine EETCs of 567 were issued, refinancing 228 of 2019-1 Engine EETCs and adding 339 of new borrowing; in August 2026 the 2026-2 aircraft EETCs of about 1.3 billion were issued, of which about 633 remained in escrow at quarter end and are not reported as American debt; and 705 of variable rate equipment loans were borrowed in the first nine months. Repayments in 2026 include the 629 senior short-term term loan and the 8.50% senior secured notes of 1.0 billion, plus 310 of EETC equipment notes prepaid.

Mechanics for a creditor: the 2026 activity extended the largest bank maturities (2014 term loans to 2033, revolvers to 2031) and replaced a 1.0 billion high-coupon secured note with lower-rate secured debt, which reduces near-term maturity pressure; at the same time it added secured aircraft debt, so more of the fleet is encumbered and unsecured creditors, the AAG-level PSP noteholders, rank behind a larger secured pool. Because nearly all debt is secured by identified collateral, loss given default for any single instrument depends on the collateral package and appraisal, which the sources do not provide; no recovery conclusion is drawn. The October 2026 option exercise for five Airbus A320 Family aircraft and the commitment to buy two Boeing 777-200ER aircraft off-lease in the fourth quarter of 2026 (Q3 page:65) are subsequent events to the September 30 balance sheet and are not included in the period facts above.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: yield-driven revenue growth with lower load factor (Q3 page:44, page:47) and large loyalty and air traffic liabilities (Q3 page:13). Mechanic: price-led revenue is exposed to demand elasticity and fare pressure, while customer prepayments and partner cash dampen near-term volatility. Implication: revenue is large and recurring but not contractual; liquidity depends on float that can unwind. Counter-evidence: record revenue in each of the last three quarters per the decks; no conflict logged.
- Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: fuel up and no hedges (Q3 page:44), non-fuel costs rising faster than capacity (Q3 page:47). Mechanic: unhedged fuel and sticky labor cost compress operating margin to near break-even, so interest and debt service must be met from float, asset financing and liquidity. Implication: coverage is thin and the credit relies on external secured financing access; this is a key uncertainty.
- Concentration. Evidence: reliance on Citi, Mastercard, joint business partners and a few hubs (K25 page:11 to page:13). Mechanic: a change in a partner contract changes cash inflow or network reach. Implication: monitor partner agreements; no covenant detail is supplied.
- Collateral and structure. Evidence: secured debt dominates and unsecured PSP notes sit at AAG (CP-1 rows). Mechanic: American's lenders hold liens on aircraft and loyalty assets while parent-level creditors have structurally junior claims on American's equity. Implication: recovery prospects differ by instrument and depend on collateral value, which is Not supplied.
- Sponsor alignment: not applicable, no sponsor.
