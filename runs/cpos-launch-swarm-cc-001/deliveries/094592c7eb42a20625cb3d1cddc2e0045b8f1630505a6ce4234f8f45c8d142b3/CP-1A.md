### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The supplied sources contain no sponsor transaction, merger or leveraged recapitalisation, and the context lists no deal terms. The transactions that matter to a creditor are a refinancing, debt paydown from asset-sale proceeds, a land sale, and legal settlements. This is a documentary summary. Figures reproduce CP-1 or cite a locator.

On March 12, 2026 the company closed a private offering of 7.875% senior notes due 2034 in an aggregate principal amount of 700, issued under the 2020 base indenture as supplemented by a Fourth Supplemental Indenture, with a guarantor named in the agreement but not named in the supplied text (Q3 10-Q page:27). Use of proceeds: together with cash on hand, the redemption of 188 of the 5.750% senior notes due 2028 and the redemption of all 495 of the 5.375% senior notes due 2027 (Q3 10-Q page:27). This addressed the nearest unsecured maturity: the 10-K had warned that "the Company would not have sufficient funds to repay this indebtedness at maturity absent refinancing or other capital-raising actions" (10-K page:170). The refinancing extended the unsecured maturity profile at a higher stated coupon than the notes it replaced. The coupon comparison is a documentary comparison of two stated rates and not a cost-of-debt analysis.

The company also repaid part of the euro term loan due August 2028 using land-sale proceeds and cash: EUR 140 million in April 2026 per the Q1 release, EUR 230 million in the second quarter per the Q2 release, and EUR 280 million in the nine months per the Q3 10-Q (Q1 ER page:4; Q2 ER page:5; Q3 10-Q page:26). The release amounts and the filing amount are labelled differently (April payment, quarter payment and nine-month total) and are logged here without reconciliation. A loss on extinguishment of debt is reported for the nine months (Q3 10-Q page:26). The debt balances after these actions are in the CP-1 handoff.

The Kuan Yin, Taiwan property: the company closed the site in 2023 as part of the Titanium Technologies Transformation Plan (Q3 10-Q page:16). In April 2026 it sold nine of ten land parcels for net proceeds of about 287 (Q1 ER page:4), and the Q3 release says the sale of the property was completed for total net consideration of about 340 including the remaining parcel for 56 of gross proceeds (Q3 ER page:1). A gain on sale of 316 for nine months 2026 is in the CP-1 handoff. About 25 of tax related to the sale was paid in Q3 (Q3 ER page:5). The proceeds are the stated source of debt reduction (Q1 ER page:1). Management Language: "positioning the Company to paydown" debt. This is a one-time source of liquidity and not recurring cash flow.

Legal settlements with cash consequences: a New Jersey judicial consent order was finalized in September 2026 with scheduled payments totalling 875 over 25 years, a first payment of 100 in September 2026, and a related PFAS Insurance MOU under which Corteva and DuPont funded 150 into an escrow for the company's New Jersey payments (Q3 10-Q page:155, page:39). A North Carolina settlement was entered in September 2026 with total payments of 455 over 15 years and a first payment of 51 in October 2026, which is a subsequent event relative to September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:116). A June 2026 consent decree with the EPA and the West Virginia Department of Environmental Protection carries a 23 civil penalty and a commitment to fund 90 of projects over 15 years (Q3 10-Q page:111). These are cash obligations that compete with debt service. Management's net leverage measure does not include them (CP-1 handoff, obligations outside management net debt).

Other 2026 events: a 2026 Restructuring Program with employee separation charges was begun in the first quarter (Q3 10-Q page:14); the exit from the SPS Capstone business was completed in 2025 (Q2 ER page:2); an operational outage at the Washington Works site reduced APM volumes in the first half and was reported resolved by the second quarter (Q1 ER page:2; Q2 ER page:3); the company launched a liquid cooling portfolio under the Faizeon brand (Q3 ER page:1).

New debt issues and amendments: 7.875% senior notes due 2034, 700, closed March 12, 2026. Credit Agreement Amendment No. 4 in October 2025 extended the U.S. dollar term loan from August 2028 to October 2032 with a wider margin (Q3 10-Q page:26). Moody's downgrade followed on July 17, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:149). Documents for the notes and the amendments are not supplied, so covenant and guarantee terms remain unverified. Draft or unexecuted status: none of the described transactions is described as draft; the North Carolina settlement is described as entered into and its first payment as made after the quarter. A settlement in principle with about 2,900 private well owners is described as not final (Q3 10-Q page:116).

### Governance and capital allocation behaviour (issuer level)

Documented actions only; no scoring is stated here. Dividends declared fell from 1.00 per share in FY2024 to 0.5125 in FY2025 and to a quarterly 0.0875 in 2026 (10-K page:162; Q3 10-Q page:156), and the company used land-sale proceeds and cash to repay debt (Q1 ER page:1; Q3 10-Q page:26). These are deleveraging actions that support creditors. Capital returned in the nine months of 2026 was 39 in dividends and none in buybacks (CP-1 handoff). Behaviour category: Supportive or Neutral on the documented actions, with the caveat that willingness is separate from legal capacity and feasibility. Legal capacity to pay dividends, make restricted payments or incur debt depends on the Credit Agreement and indenture baskets, which are not supplied (Insufficient Information). Feasibility is constrained by the leverage and liquidity facts in CP-1.

Reporting integrity: the company revised prior periods for immaterial errors that affected costs of goods sold timing and income tax expense (10-K page:166 to page:167), and an Audit Committee internal review covered the Ethics Hotline process, working capital practices and certain non-GAAP metrics (10-K page:169). The 10-K lists regulatory inquiries and litigation that may arise from that review as a risk (10-K page:4). The supplied text does not say that a material weakness was identified; this is not established either way beyond the risk-factor language, so it is Insufficient Information. Mechanism: reliability of reported adjusted measures and add-backs matters because leverage covenants and management leverage ratios depend on them.

Legacy liabilities: the company carries large environmental and litigation reserves that it describes as arising from pre-spin-off activities of its former parent, cost-shared under the MOU subject to an aggregate cap (Q3 10-Q page:35, page:153). Mechanism: cash settlements are paid over 15 to 25 years and are not classified as debt, which can reduce cash available for debt service and for refinancing without changing reported leverage. Implication for monitoring: track annual settlement payments against free cash flow.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability: evidence is the mix of regulation-driven Opteon demand, TiO2 pricing actions and growth in Performance Solutions (10-K page:12; Q3 ER page:3 to page:4). Risk mechanic: TSS aftermarket volume fell year over year after a prior-year channel fill, which lowers TSS earnings that carried the group, while TT improvement depends on price increases holding. Credit implication: earnings visibility is moderate and depends on TiO2 pricing and the TSS aftermarket cycle, with the fourth-quarter guidance pointing to seasonal softness (Q3 ER page:5). Counter-evidence logged: the guidance cuts across Q1 to Q3 releases.
2. Margin and cash flow risk: evidence is the reconciliation of Adjusted EBITDA to reported results, where large litigation, environmental and land-sale items are excluded (Q3 ER page:13). Risk mechanic: management earnings measures exclude items that consume cash over time. Credit implication: leverage measured on Adjusted EBITDA understates cash claims; CP-2 should present metrics before and after these items.
3. Liquidity and refinancing: evidence is the 2034 notes refinancing, the matured 220 revolver tranche, the springing maturity feature and the 2028 and 2029 maturities (Q3 10-Q page:25, page:27). Risk mechanic: a springing maturity ties revolver availability to refinancing of the 2028 and 2029 debt. Credit implication: refinancing risk concentrates in 2028 and 2029; the next debt actions determine revolver access.
4. Concentration: low single-customer concentration (10-K page:6) but material dependence on a few regulation- and price-driven products. Credit implication: moderate concentration risk by product line, not by customer.
5. Sponsor alignment: not applicable, no sponsor identified. Key uncertainties: legal capacity under financing documents, guarantor structure, post-October 7 revolver capacity and the size and timing of legacy settlement payments.
