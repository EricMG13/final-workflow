### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Pending merger, status: signed, stockholder-approved, awaiting regulatory approvals. On May 27, 2026 Caesars entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Fertitta Gaming Holdco, LLC, a Texas limited liability company, and Empire Merger Sub, Inc., its direct wholly owned subsidiary, under which Merger Sub will merge into Caesars, which will survive as a direct wholly owned subsidiary of Fertitta Gaming (10-Q Q3 p6, p38). Landry's Fertitta, LLC joins solely for Section 9.14 and Hospitality Headquarters, Inc. solely for Section 9.14(j). Each common share other than excluded, rollover and dissenting shares converts into cash of $31.00, plus a ticking fee of $0.007150 per day if closing has not occurred by June 26, 2027, counted from the first day of the following month (10-Q Q3 p7). Parent rollover shares convert into shares of the surviving corporation, so some existing equity is expected to roll; the amount is not disclosed. Equity awards are cashed out at the merger consideration. The company filed its proxy statement on August 26, 2026 and stockholders approved the proposals on September 22, 2026. A no-shop applies from July 11, 2026. Conditions include expiry of the HSR Act waiting period and certain gaming regulatory approvals. The initial end date is May 27, 2027, automatically extended to August 27, 2027 and then November 27, 2027 if only the antitrust or gaming conditions remain open. The company owes a $200 million termination fee in specified alternative-proposal circumstances, and Fertitta Gaming owes a $450 million reverse termination fee in specified regulatory circumstances, including the end date passing with only the regulatory conditions unsatisfied (10-Q Q3 p8-9). Merger-related transaction costs were $12 million in Q3 2026 and $22 million in the nine months. After closing Caesars intends to delist from Nasdaq and deregister, and the Q3 release states it "will become a private entity" and will not hold a conference call (EX-99.1 Q3 p4).

Creditor mechanics. Evidence: the 10-Q lists among risks "the risk that the financing required to fund the proposed transaction is not obtained on the terms anticipated or at all" and "risks associated with increased leverage from the proposed transaction" (10-Q Q3 p57); the Merger Agreement is only summarised, and the debt financing for the buyer, the treatment of Caesars' existing notes and credit facilities, and any change-of-control put or consent are not disclosed in the sources read. Risk mechanic: if the buyer finances with new debt at or above Caesars, leverage and fixed charges could rise after closing; if existing instruments carry change-of-control provisions, closing could force refinancing or repurchase; the reverse termination fee gives the target compensation but not the creditors. Credit implication: transaction outcome and post-closing capital structure are unresolved, so monitoring posture is event-driven until the financing plan and debt treatment are disclosed. This is Insufficient Information on financing and not an adverse conclusion. No sponsor is named in the sources; the counterparties are described only by entity name.

Share repurchases under the 2024 program stopped in 2026, and the company states no repurchases are expected during the pending merger. Dividends have not been paid.

New debt issues. No new debt instrument was issued in 2026 in the sources read: the September 30, 2026 debt table lists only instruments that were already outstanding at December 31, 2025, and the CEI Revolving Credit Facility moved from 160 drawn at December 31, 2025 to nil at September 30, 2026 (10-Q Q3 p15). The most recent issuances disclosed are the $1.1 billion 6.00% senior notes due 2032 issued October 17, 2024, the $1.5 billion 6.50% senior secured notes due 2032 and $2.9 billion CEI Term Loan B-1 on February 6, 2024, and the Caesars Virginia $400 million multi-draw term loan and $25 million revolver entered April 26, 2024 (10-Q Q3 p18-19; 10-K p122-123). On July 8, 2025 the company fully redeemed the $546 million of senior notes due 2027 using revolver borrowings and proceeds from the partial repayment and sale of $225 million of WSOP-related notes receivable (10-K p80). Maturities are concentrated: see CP-1 for the ladder, with the revolver and Term Loan A due January 31, 2028, 2029 notes and the CVA facilities in 2029, and the Term Loan B and 2030 secured notes in 2030.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: casino is about 58% of FY2025 net revenues (10-K p5) and Q3 2026 casino revenue rose while hotel and food and beverage fell (10-Q Q3 p42). Mechanic: revenue is driven by gaming volume and hold, which can rise while non-gaming softens. Implication: near-term revenue is resilient but the quality of growth depends on hold and digital promotions, so monitoring should track hold and Las Vegas occupancy.
2. Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: Adjusted EBITDA fell in Q3 2026 on revenue growth, with labor, gaming taxes, property taxes and insurance captive costs cited (10-Q Q3 p43, p46). Mechanic: fixed and tax-driven costs grow faster than revenue. Implication: coverage of fixed charges, which include rent outside Adjusted EBITDA (CP-1), tightens if margins keep compressing.
3. Concentration and structure. Evidence: lease financing obligations of 13,188 sit on the balance sheet beside debt of 11,659 face (CP-1 table). Mechanic: lease payments are a prior, largely fixed claim on property cash flow. Implication: recovery prospects for debt depend on property value after rent, a mechanic only and not a recovery estimate.
4. Ownership and control. Evidence: signed merger with stockholder approval and unresolved financing. Mechanic: change of control can alter leverage, covenants and refinancing. Implication: posture is event-driven pending buyer financing disclosure. The unresolved acquirer-name distinction counters certainty on counterparty identity.
5. Key uncertainties: buyer financing, debt treatment on closing, ownership percentages, covenant definitions, rollover size.
