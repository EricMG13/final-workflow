## Findings

### Fundamental credit view

Assessment (Analyst Interpretation, confidence Medium). Caesars is a large, diversified US casino operator whose operating earnings are stable to slightly lower, whose financial risk is driven by leverage and a heavy fixed rent burden that sits outside its reported Adjusted EBITDA, and whose credit profile is currently dominated by an unresolved ownership change. The company covers its cash debt service and fixed rent from operating cash flow with a modest surplus, has reduced debt over 2026 and holds ample revolver liquidity, but net losses, thin fixed-charge coverage on a rent-inclusive basis and a refinancing concentration in 2029 and 2030 leave limited tolerance for an earnings shock or a more leveraged post-merger structure.

Basis: CP-1 table and calculations (LTM Adjusted EBITDA 3,573; face debt 11,659; net debt 10,795; rent-inclusive coverage 1.69x on the CP-1 calculation) and the CP-1A business, transaction and governance rows.

Thesis (Analyst Interpretation): stable multi-market gaming cash flow and good near-term liquidity support the credit, while rent-inclusive leverage near five times, concentrated 2029 and 2030 maturities and an unresolved buyer financing plan keep the credit view contingent on the post-merger capital structure.

### Key findings

First, earnings scale and diversification are real but trending slightly down. Adjusted EBITDA for the latest twelve months to September 30, 2026 is 3,573 on the CP-1 calculation, compared with 3,624 for FY2025. In Q3 2026 revenue grew 2.5% while Adjusted EBITDA fell 2.1% to 865 and the reported margin slipped to 29.4% from 30.8% (10-Q Q3 p41). The mechanism is cost growth in labor, gaming taxes, property taxes and insurance captive costs outpacing revenue, and a softer Las Vegas segment where Adjusted EBITDA fell to 343 from 379 on fewer convention events and lower leisure visitation (10-Q Q3 p46). The implication is that coverage cushions are narrowing even as reported revenue grows. Category: Documentary Fact for the figures, Analyst Interpretation for the mechanism.

Second, leverage looks moderate on the company definition but is materially higher once rent is counted. Face debt to LTM Adjusted EBITDA is 3.26x and net debt 3.02x (CP-1 calculations). Adjusted EBITDA excludes lease expense on the VICI and GLPI leases, which are accounted for as financing obligations of 13,188 on the balance sheet and cost 1,370 in cash over the latest twelve months (CP-1 calculation). After deducting that rent, face debt is 5.29x and net debt 4.90x of the remaining 2,203. The mechanism is that rent is a largely fixed claim on property cash flow that is paid ahead of debt holders in practice; the implication is that a revenue or margin shock transmits to debt service more sharply than the headline leverage suggests. Category: Analyst Interpretation on documented figures; these rent-adjusted ratios are not company or covenant measures.

Third, coverage is adequate for cash obligations but thin on a total fixed-charge basis. Adjusted EBITDA covers cash interest paid for debt 4.82x over the latest twelve months, but covers cash interest plus cash rent only 1.69x (CP-1 calculations). Interest expense, net of 2,287 over the same period exceeds the 1,470 nine-month operating income annualised sense of profitability, and the company reports net losses in each period (FY2025 net loss attributable of 502; nine months 2026 of 283). Free cash flow after capex was 582 over the latest twelve months (491 after distributions to noncontrolling interests) on lower capex of 507 in the nine months versus 648. The implication is that debt reduction capacity depends on holding capex down and operating cash flow stable. Category: Documentary Fact for figures; Analyst Interpretation for conclusions. Note that the comparison of interest expense to operating income is an interpretation; CP-1 did not compute it.

Fourth, liquidity is good in the near term and the maturity wall is further out. Total liquidity at September 30, 2026 was 2,947 including cash of 864 and net revolver capacity, with the CEI revolver undrawn against 160 drawn at year-end 2025 (Q3 release p2; 10-Q Q3 p15, p19). Scheduled principal is only 28 for the rest of 2026 and 114 in 2027, then 646 in 2028 (revolver and Term Loan A due January 31, 2028), 1,574 in 2029 and 3,962 in 2030 (10-Q Q3 p16). The mechanism is that near-term amortisation is covered many times by liquidity, but the revolver and Term Loan A maturity in 2028 and the 2029 and 2030 maturities require market access or a refinancing under an owner whose capital structure is not yet known. Category: Documentary Fact.

Fifth, the pending merger changes who the borrower's owner is, and the credit-relevant terms are undisclosed. Stockholders approved the merger with Fertitta Gaming Holdco, LLC on September 22, 2026 at a cash price of 31.00 per share, with a reverse termination fee of 450 and a termination fee of 200 (10-Q Q3 p7-9). The buyer's financing, the treatment of existing notes and facilities, any change-of-control provision and the rollover amount are not disclosed in the sources read, and the 10-Q lists "risks associated with increased leverage from the proposed transaction" (10-Q Q3 p57). The mechanism is that new debt at or above Caesars, or a change-of-control trigger on existing instruments, could raise leverage or force refinancing; the implication is that the monitoring posture is event-driven until the financing plan is disclosed. Category: Documentary Fact for the terms, Insufficient Information for financing and debt treatment. Missing evidence is not treated as an adverse conclusion.

Sixth, covenant headroom cannot be stated. The CEI revolver and Term Loan A have a maximum net total leverage ratio of 6.50:1 and a minimum fixed charge coverage ratio of 2.0:1, tested on definitions that are not supplied, and the company states compliance at September 30, 2026 (10-Q Q3 p20-21). Insufficient Information: covenant EBITDA and fixed charge definitions are missing, so headroom is Not calculable and the company's compliance statement is the only evidence. The 2.0:1 fixed charge coverage level is not comparable to the 1.69x rent-inclusive figure above because the covenant definition is unknown.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Scale and geographic diversification across 54 properties in 20 jurisdictions with four distinct earnings segments (Las Vegas, Regional, Caesars Digital, Managed and Branded) reduce dependence on a single market (CP-1A; 10-Q Q3 p10); Documentary Fact. Resilient regional performance and a growing digital segment offset Las Vegas weakness in the quarter: Regional Adjusted EBITDA rose to 524 from 506 and Caesars Digital to 44 from 28 (Q3 release p1), though part of the Regional gain is acquired scope from Caesars Windsor; Documentary Fact. Liquidity is strong relative to near-term obligations at 2,947, with an undrawn 2,250 CEI revolver and 25 Caesars Virginia revolver (CP-1; 10-Q Q3 p19); Documentary Fact. Debt was reduced by 246 in face value and net debt by 223 over the nine months (CP-1 calculation), and share repurchases stopped in 2026 (10-Q Q3 p53); Documentary Fact. Cash interest paid for debt fell to 580 in the nine months from 652 (10-Q Q3 p5), helping cash conversion; Documentary Fact. A large share of debt is secured with a lien on substantially all assets and guarantees from material domestic subsidiaries, and the 2029 and 2032 unsecured notes rank equally with unsecured claims (10-Q Q3 p21); Documentary Fact on structure only, with no statement on recovery. Variable-rate borrowing is about half of debt and carries a weighted average rate of 6.12% (10-Q Q3 p54), which lowers fixed-rate refinancing pressure but exposes interest cost to Term SOFR, about 61 per 100 basis points on the 10-K sensitivity at December 31, 2025 (10-K p95); Documentary Fact.

Weaknesses. Heavy lease financing obligations of 13,188 and cash rent of 1,030 in the nine months of 2026 create a fixed-charge burden that Adjusted EBITDA excludes (CP-1); Documentary Fact. Persistent net losses (FY2025 502, nine months 2026 283 attributable) mean no earnings buffer for debt service beyond operating cash flow (CP-1 table); Documentary Fact. Las Vegas, the highest-margin segment, is weakening with fewer convention events, lower occupancy of 89.1% versus 91.6% in Q3 and compressed rates (10-Q Q3 p45-46); Documentary Fact. Margin pressure from labor, gaming taxes, property taxes and captive insurance costs, with Adjusted EBITDA down while revenue is up (10-Q Q3 p41-43); Documentary Fact. Maturity concentration in 2029 and 2030 and the 2028 revolver and Term Loan A maturity (10-Q Q3 p16); Documentary Fact. The ownership change brings an undisclosed capital structure and transaction costs of 12 in Q3 and 22 in the nine months (10-Q Q3 p10); Documentary Fact on costs, Insufficient Information on structure. Data Incident litigation from 2023 remains open with losses not estimable, though the company states no material impact so far (10-K p121); Documentary Fact, with the outcome Insufficient Information. The reliance on Adjusted EBITDA add-backs is another weakness: transaction costs and other, net was 64 in the nine months of 2026 and stock-based compensation 70 (Q3 release p6); Documentary Fact. No add-back bridge to covenant EBITDA is supplied; Insufficient Information.

### Key credit risks

1. Post-merger capital structure and change of control. Severity: Critical. Mechanic: buyer financing may add leverage at or above Caesars, and existing instruments may contain change-of-control provisions. Implication: refinancing, leverage tolerance and monitoring posture are unresolved until the financing is disclosed. Basis: CP-1A transaction summary and gaps; Insufficient Information on the terms.
2. Fixed rent burden and rent-adjusted leverage. Severity: Material. Mechanic: cash rent of 1,370 over the latest twelve months is paid before equity and acts like senior debt service; a decline in Adjusted EBITDA reduces the cushion over interest plus rent, which stands at 1.69x. Implication: limited tolerance for an earnings shock; debt service coverage depends on rent-inclusive cash flow. Basis: CP-1.
3. Las Vegas softness and margin compression. Severity: Material. Mechanic: lower convention and leisure demand and rising costs reduce Adjusted EBITDA while revenue grows elsewhere. Implication: leverage drifts upward even without new debt. Basis: CP-1A operating model rows and CP-1 Q3 figures.
4. Refinancing concentration in 2028 to 2030. Severity: Material. Mechanic: 646 due in 2028, 1,574 in 2029 and 3,962 in 2030 require market access under a new owner. Implication: refinancing risk is back-ended but large; liquidity of 2,947 mitigates the 2026 and 2027 period only. Basis: CP-1 maturity ladder.
5. Variable-rate exposure. Severity: Minor. Mechanic: about 5.8 billion of term loans reset with Term SOFR. Implication: interest cost rises with rates; the sensitivity is stated at 61 per 100 basis points at December 31, 2025. Basis: CP-1 and 10-K p95.
6. Regulatory and litigation. Severity: Minor. Mechanic: gaming approvals for the merger and open Data Incident claims. Implication: timing risk and cash cost; amounts not estimable. Basis: CP-1A.
7. Digital hold variance and promotion cost. Severity: Minor. Mechanic: sports betting and iGaming hold and promotional spending swing segment earnings, which fell in Q2 and rose in Q3 2026 (Q2 and Q3 releases). Implication: volatility in a segment that is a growing share of earnings. Basis: CP-1A.
