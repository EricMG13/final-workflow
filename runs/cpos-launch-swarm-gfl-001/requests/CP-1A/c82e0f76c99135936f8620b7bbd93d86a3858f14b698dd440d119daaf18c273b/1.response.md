### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The dominant transaction is the SECURE Waste Infrastructure Corp. acquisition. The definitive agreement was entered "On April 12, 2026" (Q1 statements note 18, subsequent events); SECURE shareholders approved it in May and it went through regulatory review (Q2 release); GFL acquired 100% of the common shares "On September 1, 2026" (Q3 statements note 3, page:7). Documentary Fact: total consideration of about 5,414.1 satisfied by share capital of 4,334.8 and cash of 1,079.3; SECURE operates waste processing, industrial waste disposal and recycling in Western Canada and North Dakota; initial accounting is provisional and based on historical carrying values, so fair value adjustments could change goodwill, intangibles, property and the 1,006.6 of other long-term liabilities assumed. Mechanism: issuing 77,708,769 subordinate voting shares funded most of the price with equity, which limits the incremental debt, while the cash portion, repayment of acquired debt (if any, not stated in the supplied text) and transaction costs were funded with new debt. Credit implication: the equity-heavy structure moderates leverage growth, but the assumed obligations, closure liabilities of 271.8 and crude oil commitments broaden the senior and structurally senior claims ahead of or alongside the notes.

Alongside SECURE, GFL completed 13 businesses in the nine months ended September 30, 2026, each individually immaterial except SECURE, with combined purchase consideration for the others of 1,598.5 including 156.3 in shares (Q3 statements page:6). Management states year-to-date completed acquisitions generate "approximately $2 billion in annualized revenue" (Q3 release page:1) which is Management Language, not a filed fact.

New financing during 2026 (Documentary Fact, Q3 statements page:11 to page:14): a Term Loan B Facility entered August 28, 2026 maturing August 28, 2033 at SOFR plus 2.000%, carried at 1,418.8; 5.500% senior notes due February 1, 2034 (1,418.8) and 5.625% senior notes due July 1, 2031 (1,064.1), the latter with a cross-currency swap fixing the rate at 4.480% (entered June 25, 2026); a swap on the Term Loan B fixing the rate at 5.009%; the revolving credit agreement amended and restated through September 1, 2026 maturing April 29, 2030. The issuance of debt in the nine months was 6,795.9 and repayment 3,823.9 (cash flow statement, page:4). The use of proceeds is not stated in the supplied text beyond the statement that financing and acquisition activity occurred; the sources and uses of the SECURE financing are therefore partly Insufficient Information. The 6.750% 2031 notes had their collateral released and are no longer secured (note 7), which is a Documentary Fact bearing on the secured-unsecured balance of the capital structure; the indenture terms that permitted the release are not supplied.

Pending event, after the cutoff in resolution: in the third quarter a Special Committee of independent directors was formed to review unsolicited proposals to take the Company private, with updated proposals due November 9, 2026 (Q3 release page:1). Management Language states "There can be no assurance that the Company will receive any proposals". A take-private would raise change-of-control, leveraged buyout and covenant questions; indentures and credit agreements are not supplied, so legal capacity cannot be assessed. No outcome may be assumed.

Subsequent events: after March 31, 2026 GFL completed 5 acquisitions for about 1,437.6 (Q1 statements note 18); that disclosure was dated to the Q1 period and is not folded into Q1 figures. The Q3 statements contain no subsequent-events note in the extracted text.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: collection is the largest service line and pricing is described as "5.7% from core pricing" (Q3 release page:2). Mechanism: route-based collection revenue with pricing above inflation supports revenue visibility and margin resilience, while landfill and transfer volumes depend on construction and industrial activity. Implication: stable operating cash generation supports debt service tolerance, subject to the pricing claim being management's and to customer contract data being absent.
2. Leverage and acquisition integration. Evidence: Net Leverage 3.8x against 3.4x at December 31, 2025 (CP-1 handoff), and an unadjusted net-debt-to-trailing-Adjusted-EBITDA calculation above 5x in CP-1. Mechanism: SECURE contributes one month of EBITDA but all of its debt-funded cost and assumed liabilities, so reported coverage and leverage understate or overstate depending on whether run-rate adjustments of 730.9 are realised. Implication: refinancing and covenant headroom depend on the Material Acquisition step-up and on delivery of run-rate earnings; monitoring posture should be heightened until two full quarters including SECURE are reported.
3. Control and take-private uncertainty. Evidence: Special Committee and November 9, 2026 deadline (Q3 release page:1). Mechanism: a leveraged buyout or sponsor takeover could add debt or trigger change-of-control provisions in notes and credit facilities. Implication: monitoring item; no conclusion without indentures and credit agreements.
4. Cash-flow conversion. Evidence: management Adjusted Free Cash Flow of 484.9 for nine months against unadjusted operating cash flow less capex of 37.3 (CP-1 calculation). Mechanism: large add-backs (transaction costs, CEO remuneration, incremental growth investments) bridge the two. Implication: the committee should read management free cash flow alongside the unadjusted figure.
5. Structural claims. Evidence: closure obligations, performance bonds and letters of credit (Q3 statements page:11, page:18, page:19). Mechanism: asset retirement and surety obligations rank with operating claims and rise with landfill acquisitions. Implication: recovery prospects for unsecured noteholders depend on asset coverage after secured facilities and these obligations; no expected recovery outcome is given.
