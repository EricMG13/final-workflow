## Findings

### Credit view

GFL Environmental Inc. is a resilient, price-led waste operator whose credit profile has weakened in the near term because the SECURE acquisition lifted carrying debt by CAD 4,248.8 million in nine months and management Net Leverage to 3.8x from 3.4x. The strongest support is steady collection and landfill earnings, with Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of CAD 678.7 million, a 31.5% margin, stated covenant compliance and mostly equity-funded purchase consideration. The weakest point is thin unadjusted cash generation: operating cash flow less capital spending was CAD 37.3 million over nine months, and net debt is 5.05x trailing reported Adjusted EBITDA. The outcome depends on SECURE earnings reaching the run-rate management assumes, headroom under the 5.00x acquisition covenant step-up, and the take-private review. The pack cannot confirm any of the three.

Confidence: Medium for the business view (Documentary Fact and Management Language mix); Low for forward leverage and covenant headroom (Insufficient Information on covenant values, run-rate bridge and liquidity).

### Key findings

1. GFL Environmental Inc. changed perimeter on September 1, 2026. SECURE was bought for about CAD 5,414.1 million, of which CAD 4,334.8 million was shares and CAD 1,079.3 million cash, but only one month of SECURE earnings is in the Q3 2026 statements. Reported leverage measures therefore carry the full debt and assumed liabilities against a partial earnings base. [CP-1; CP-1A transaction summary]

2. Leverage depends on management estimates. Net Leverage of 3.8x divides CAD 11,399.9 million of net debt by Run-Rate EBITDA of CAD 2,988.0 million, of which CAD 730.9 million (about a quarter) is management adjustment; on trailing reported Adjusted EBITDA of CAD 2,257.1 million the same net debt is 5.05x. The covenant ceiling is 5.00x for four fiscal quarters after a Material Acquisition and 4.50x otherwise, and covenant definitions are not supplied, so headroom cannot be measured. Management guides to Net Leverage "in the mid 3s" by the end of 2026, a forward claim. [CP-1 calculations]

3. Operating performance is solid by management's account. Revenue grew 27.2% in Q3 2026, Adjusted EBITDA rose 26.8%, and the quarterly Adjusted EBITDA sequence of CAD 478.5, 591.2 and 678.7 million shows growth through the year, with core pricing near 6% stated by management. The reported margin was 31.5% against 31.6% a year earlier, so margin expansion exists only in management's underlying measure, which excludes M&A and diesel effects. [CP-1; CP-1A operating model]

4. Cash generation is weaker than the management free cash flow figure suggests. Management Adjusted Free Cash Flow was CAD 484.9 million for nine months, built from add-backs including CAD 104.5 million of transaction costs and CAD 74.2 million of cash paid to the Founder and CEO, plus CAD 182.4 million of incremental growth investments. Unadjusted operating cash flow less purchase of property and equipment was CAD 37.3 million. Cash interest rose to CAD 172.5 million in Q3 2026 from CAD 118.1 million a year earlier. [CP-1]

5. The debt stack is long-dated but large. Maturities begin in 2028 (CAD 2,128.2 million of notes), then 2029 (CAD 1,844.4 million), the revolver in 2030 (CAD 868.0 million drawn) and 2031 onward; there is almost no current maturity (CAD 14.2 million). The Term Loan B and revolver are secured, the 6.750% 2031 notes no longer have collateral, and the notes' guarantor and ranking terms are not supplied. [CP-1 debt table]

6. Fixed commitments and structurally senior-type claims grew. Closure and post-closure obligations reached CAD 1,503.8 million, performance bonds CAD 2,324.1 million, letters of credit CAD 535.1 million and new crude oil transportation commitments CAD 561.4 million from the SECURE addition. These rank with operating claims and shape loss-given-default mechanics without giving an expected recovery outcome. [CP-1; CP-1A]

7. Control and event risk is live. A Special Committee is reviewing unsolicited take-private proposals with a November 9, 2026 deadline, after the cutoff. A leveraged transaction could change financial policy and may engage change-of-control terms in debt documents that are not supplied. No outcome is assumed. [CP-1A]

8. Earnings quality is mixed. Net loss from continuing operations was CAD 409.8 million for nine months, driven by items outside Adjusted EBITDA (foreign exchange, financing costs, equity-method losses), and Adjusted EBITDA adds back share-based payments (CAD 87.0 million) and transaction costs. Several guided figures imply an unusually small Q4 step (see Calculations), which is unverified. [CP-1; CP-1 calculations]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, the business is recurring and vertically integrated: collection is the largest revenue line and landfill and transfer add disposal control, which supports revenue visibility and margin resilience (Documentary Fact; CP-1A operating model). Second, earnings momentum is real in the filed figures: Adjusted EBITDA grew in each quarter of 2026 and management raised guidance three times, though guidance is Management Language. Third, the acquisition was paid mainly with equity, issuing 77,708,769 subordinate voting shares, which limits the debt added by SECURE and leaves the revolver largely undrawn on a nominal basis relative to its size (the availability figure is not supplied). Fourth, the maturity profile has no material maturity before 2028 and interest on most notes is fixed in USD, with cross-currency swaps on the newest instruments (Documentary Fact). Fifth, covenant compliance is stated at September 30, 2026 (Documentary Fact), and disclosure of non-IFRS bridges is detailed each quarter (Analyst Interpretation).

Weaknesses. First, leverage on reported earnings is high and rests on run-rate estimates for the stated ratio, so the margin for error against a 5.00x covenant depends on an unsupplied definition (Analyst Interpretation). Second, cash conversion after capital spending and interest is thin and management free cash flow relies on add-backs (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation). Third, integration risk: provisional purchase accounting, a large goodwill balance (CAD 12,583.1 million, versus CAD 6,894.9 million at December 31, 2025) and a net loss from continuing operations mean book equity is largely acquired intangibles (Documentary Fact). Fourth, the take-private review and a dual-class structure with unspecified voting terms leave control and future leverage policy uncertain (Insufficient Information on voting rights). Fifth, commodity and fuel exposure: diesel is cited as a margin headwind, and the new crude oil commitments add a fixed obligation whose counterparties and tenor are not supplied (Management Language; Documentary Fact). Sixth, foreign exchange: most notes are USD while much revenue is CAD and USD, a mix that moves reported leverage and caused a CAD 196.7 million foreign exchange loss in nine months (Documentary Fact).
