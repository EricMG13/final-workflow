### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The set contains no single leveraged-finance transaction; the creditor-relevant transactions are the 2025 and 2026 acquisitions, the asset monetization and the 2026 financings. Each item is a Documentary Fact from the filings unless it says otherwise.

Colombia consolidation. On January 27, 2026 Millicom was awarded 100 percent of EPM's remaining shares in UNE EPM Telecomunicaciones S.A. at a price of COP 418,741 per share, about COP 2.1 trillion (about $571 million); the deal closed January 29, 2026 and was accounted for as an equity transaction that reduced equity by $627 million (Q3 6-K chunk 8). On February 6, 2026 it closed the purchase of Telefónica's controlling 67.5 percent of Colombia Telecomunicaciones S.A. E.S.P. (Coltel) for about $214.4 million, of which $169 million was paid at acquisition (Q3 6-K chunk 9). Coltel was acquired with "Other debt and financing" of $1,424 million and lease liabilities of $515 million at fair value, and contributed revenue of $1,127 million and a net loss of $168 million from acquisition to September 30, 2026, including about $71 million of severance (Q3 6-K chunks 9-10). On April 27, 2026 it bought the remaining 32.5 percent from La Nación, a $219 million equity transaction (Q3 6-K chunk 9). The purchase accounting remains provisional. Creditor mechanic: control of the combined Colombian business is now full, which removes minority leakage and approvals but added acquired debt and integration costs; the management leverage rose to 2.76x at March 31, 2026 before falling to 2.53x at September 30, 2026 (CP-1). The Colombian joint operations (UNIRED and UT) are consolidated in full from the Coltel acquisition, and EPM remains a non-controlling partner in certain operations (Q3 6-K chunks 5, 22).

Ecuador and Uruguay. Millicom completed the purchase of Telefónica Móviles del Uruguay on October 7, 2025 for an enterprise value of $440 million and of Telefónica's Ecuador operations on October 30, 2025 for an enterprise value of $380 million (Q3 6-K chunks 13-14). Uruguay accounting is final; Ecuador's measurement period ended October 30, 2026 and was still being finalized when the statements were issued. Both carry local bank debt; a payment guaranty from MIC S.A. supports the Uruguay Santander loan (20-F chunk 256).

Chile with NJJ. On February 10, 2026 Celtel Chile, S.L. (49 percent Millicom, 51 percent NJJ Cactus SAS) bought Telefónica Móviles Chile for $50 million cash with contingent earn-outs "without recourse to Millicom"; Celtel carries a recognized liability of $110 million at September 30, 2026, funded by external financial debt, and mutual call options exist from the fifth and sixth anniversaries (Q3 6-K chunk 11). It is equity-accounted and the carrying value is nil because losses exceeded the investment, leaving $41 million of unrecognized losses at Millicom's share (Q3 6-K chunk 22). The 20-F describes the earn-out as up to $150 million (20-F chunk 297) and the 6-Ks as up to $490 million (see Conflicts). NJJ is described in the related party note as part of Xavier Niel's holdings (20-F chunk 295), so this is a joint investment with a party linked to the controlling shareholder; terms are as disclosed and the pricing basis of the options is not quantified.

Tower monetization and leaseback. On October 28, 2024 Millicom agreed to sell Lati International and other assets, more than 7,000 towers in Central America, to SBA Communications Corp. with a 15-year leaseback and a build-to-suit commitment for up to 2,500 further sites; Lati Paraguay was sold to Atis Group on June 3, 2025; the gain recognized in 2025 was $741 million (Q3 6-K chunks 14-15). Millicom retains Lati subsidiaries in Bolivia with about 2,000 towers (20-F chunk 91). Creditor mechanic (Analyst Interpretation): the sale converted owned assets into leased ones, so the leaseback added lease liabilities that rank as fixed claims; how the proceeds were applied is not stated in the sources reviewed.

Other portfolio actions. Tigo Paraguay signed on January 5, 2026 to sell its mobile finance business for a base price of $10 million with a potential $7 million earn-out (Q3 6-K chunk 12). The planned merger of Tigo Costa Rica with Liberty Latin America (agreement of August 1, 2024) was rejected by the regulator SUTEL in September and November 2025 (20-F chunk 83); the Costa Rica unit recorded a goodwill impairment of about $32 million in 2026, with goodwill of about $123 million remaining (Q3 6-K chunks 27-28). Tigo Sports local content was sold to FOX (agreement announced April 13, 2026; Q1 6-K chunk 22).

New debt issues and refinancings in 2026 (all subsequent to the 20-F balance sheet date). MIC S.A. reopened its 7.375% Senior Notes due 2032 for an additional $87.5 million in a Regulation S private placement on April 14, 2026, admitted to the Euro MTF market, placed with Banco General, S.A.; MIC S.A. issued an 8-year COP-USD linked private bond for COP 370,000 million at a fixed rate on March 27, 2026, subscribed by the Inter-American Development Bank and Proparco (Q3 6-K chunk 24). Tigo Paraguay redeemed its 5.875% notes due 2027, about $139.7 million, at par on April 29, 2026 (Q3 6-K chunk 25). Coltel entered a one-year $100 million bridge facility with Banco Santander at variable rate on April 13, 2026 to repay a $100 million HSBC facility, and later new three-year and one-year variable bank loans in Colombian pesos; Tigo Colombia added a four-year variable loan with Davivienda of COP 220,000 million; Tigo Panama refinanced a $75 million loan with a new six-year BAC loan in August 2026; Tigo Guatemala, Tigo Bolivia and others added bank loans (Q3 6-K chunks 23-25). After the quarter, Coltel took a COP 40,000 million BTG Pactual loan and prepaid $12.5 million of the Santander loan on October 15, 2026 (Q3 6-K chunk 28, subsequent event). Instrument-level terms such as ranking, security, covenants and guarantor groups are Not supplied for these issues, and use of proceeds is stated only for the Coltel bridge. Gross debt rose from $6,886 million to $8,648 million over the nine months (CP-1).

Capital returns. The AGM of May 20, 2026 approved a dividend of $3.00 per share in four quarterly installments and the Board on August 5, 2026 approved an interim dividend of $1.50 per share payable in January and April 2027 (Q3 6-K chunk 20). Share repurchases were $119 million in 2025 and none in the nine months of 2026 (20-F chunk 198; Q3 6-K chunk 5).

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: service revenue of 6,045 for nine months 2026 against 3,888, with organic service revenue growth of 5.3 percent (Q3 6-K chunk 41), most of the growth coming from consolidating Coltel, Ecuador and Uruguay. Mechanism: recurring subscription revenue across twelve countries gives scale, and prepaid-to-postpaid migration raises visibility, but the acquired revenue base has a short history under Millicom control and the underlying organic rate is mid-single-digit. Implication: revenue is large and recurring; the comparison to prior years is flattered by perimeter, so organic growth is the better guide to durability. Conflict that counters it: none logged.
2. Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: Adjusted EBITDA margin of 45.9 percent for nine months 2026 against 47.3 percent, with 149 of one-off items above Adjusted EBITDA (Q3 6-K chunks 34, 41). Mechanism: Coltel integration and restructuring are depressing near-term margin and consuming cash, while lease payments and spectrum are fixed claims. Implication: coverage and free cash flow depend on delivering the Colombian cost program; if integration slips, equity free cash flow guidance is at risk (management claim). Conflict that counters it: C3 does not change cash flow; the unreconciled operating cash flow figures in CP-1 gap G5 limit precision.
3. Concentration risk. Evidence: Guatemala and the two Colombian segments carry the largest segment revenue and the Guatemala entity issues $900 million 5.125% notes with subsidiary guarantees. Mechanism: a regulatory, currency or macro event in one of these countries affects a large share of consolidated EBITDA and the operating-level debt serviced from it. Implication: monitor country-level cash availability and the share of debt at operating entities (about 70 percent, Q3 6-K chunk 32), noting that MIC S.A. is dependent on upstream dividends (20-F chunk 111).
4. Sponsor alignment. Evidence: controlling shareholder holds about 42.2 percent, appoints four of eight non-executive directors, and has a related-party payable and a joint investment. Mechanism: aligned equity ownership can support the issuer, but larger distributions (a $1.50 interim on top of the regular dividend) and a related investment competing for cash with creditors are potential leakage channels. Implication: neutral to mixed on documented actions; the absence of documented creditor-adverse actions is not evidence that covenants prevent them because indentures are not supplied. Conflict that counters it: C2.
5. Key uncertainties. Evidence: Coltel and Ecuador purchase accounting provisional; covenant terms, debt schedule and revolving facility status not supplied; Chile earn-out size in conflict (C1); Guatemala litigation settled but other claims remain. Mechanism: each limits the ability to size liquidity and covenant headroom. Implication: committee readiness is limited until the named documents are obtained.
