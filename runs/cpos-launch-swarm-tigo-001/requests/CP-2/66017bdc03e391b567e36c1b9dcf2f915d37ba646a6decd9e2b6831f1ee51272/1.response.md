## Findings

### Credit assessment

Category: Analyst Interpretation. Confidence: Medium.

Basis: CP-1 figures for gross debt 8,648, net debt 7,983, cash 848, management leverage 2.53x, lease liabilities 3,190, nine-month Adjusted EBITDA 2,958, nine-month equity free cash flow 867 and the maturity ladder; CP-1A business, concentration, ownership and transaction rows.

Rationale (evidence, mechanism, implication): Millicom is a large, scaled, recurring-revenue operator across twelve Latin American countries whose earnings base has stepped up sharply through the 2025 and 2026 acquisitions in Colombia, Ecuador and Uruguay. The consolidated net debt increased from 5,357 at December 31, 2025 to 7,983 at September 30, 2026, and management leverage moved from 2.17x to a peak of 2.76x and then to 2.53x at September 30, 2026. The mechanism is that purchase payments, consolidated acquired debt and a heavier lease and dividend burden were absorbed by a much larger EBITDA and equity free cash flow base; deleveraging now depends on integrating Colombia and delivering the stated cash flow. The credit implication is a business with sound operating scale and improving cash generation but with high absolute leverage, material lease obligations outside the headline measure, a near-term maturity and refinancing agenda and an incomplete view of covenant headroom. The fundamental view is therefore that debt service capacity appears supported on the reported period, with the quality of that support limited by perimeter effects, definition risk and missing legal documents. The confidence is Medium rather than High because covenant terms, instrument-level debt after December 31, 2025, the revolving facility position and indentures are not in the evidence set.

### Thesis

Category: Analyst Interpretation. A scaled Latin American telecom group with recurring cash flows and improving but still elevated leverage after a step-change in Colombian consolidation: debt service looks supported by reported earnings and equity free cash flow, while integration delivery, shareholder distributions, refinancing and unsupplied covenant terms determine how much headroom actually exists.

### Key findings

1. The credit story is a consolidation story. Nine-month 2026 revenue of 6,440 and Adjusted EBITDA of 2,958 compare with 4,150 and 1,964 a year earlier, but the release attributes most of the growth to perimeter and currency, with organic revenue growth of 4.4 percent and organic Adjusted EBITDA growth of 8.8 percent. Basis: CP-1 figures and the CP-1A volume-drivers and Colombia transaction rows. Category: Documentary Fact for the figures; the reading that organic growth is the better durability guide is Analyst Interpretation. Mechanism: acquired revenue lifts coverage but has a short record under Millicom control. Implication: durability should be judged on organic trends and on the Colombian integration outcome.
2. Leverage has improved for two consecutive quarters but is measured on a management basis that cannot be reproduced. Management leverage fell from 2.76x at March 31, 2026 to 2.73x and then 2.53x at September 30, 2026, against a year-end target of below 2.5x. Simple net debt over reported last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA is 2.14x and 2.99x when lease liabilities are added (CP-1 Calculation 3). Category: Documentary Fact for the reported ratio and Analyst Interpretation for the simple ratios. Mechanism: lease costs are subtracted in the management denominator and acquisitions are pro forma, neither bridge is supplied. Implication: use the lease-inclusive view alongside the headline ratio, and treat the 2.5x target as a management claim.
3. Debt service capacity is supported by cash generation but nearly all of it is claimed by fixed uses. Nine-month Adjusted EBITDA of 2,958 covers interest on bonds and bank financing of 472 about 6.3 times and covers that plus lease interest about 4.2 times (CP-1 Calculation 6); equity free cash flow of 867 was followed by dividends paid of 587, leaving about 280 against acquisition payments of 924. Category: Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation. Mechanism: lease payments, spectrum and taxes are the largest fixed claims ahead of equity free cash flow. Implication: cushion for unplanned shocks is thinner than the headline EBITDA suggests.
4. Liquidity relies on refinancing access. Cash of 848 equals debt due within one year of 872; the 2027 SEK note and the 600 revolving facility, which was undrawn at December 31, 2025 and due October 2027, mature within about fourteen months, and the facility's current availability is Not supplied. Category: Documentary Fact for balances and maturities, Insufficient Information for facility availability. Mechanism: local bank facilities have been rolled routinely in 2026 and bond market access was shown by the 2032 notes reopening, but a dislocation would hit the near-term ladder first. Implication: monitor facility status and the 2027 maturities.
5. Shareholder distributions are rising at the same time as leverage is being reduced. The Board approved an interim dividend of $1.50 per share in August 2026 on top of the regular $3.00 per share cycle; the controlling shareholder group holds about 42.2 percent of voting shares. Scheduled cash dividends of about 629 follow September 30, 2026 (CP-1 Calculation 7). Category: Documentary Fact for the declared amounts; Analyst Interpretation for the pressure on deleveraging. Mechanism: distributions compete with debt reduction for equity free cash flow. Implication: neutral to mixed behaviour on the evidence, because capacity limits in the debt documents are not supplied.
6. Control and related-party links are concentrated. One shareholder group holds de facto control and four of eight non-executive board seats; a payable to the Iliad Holding Group of 195 at December 31, 2025 has no stated nature, and the Chile investment is held with NJJ, a party linked to the controlling shareholder. Category: Documentary Fact; absence of documented creditor-adverse actions is not evidence of contractual protection. Implication: governance risk is moderated by alignment but cannot be sized without the indentures.
7. Data quality and definition risk limit precision. The December 31, 2025 balance sheet was restated, 2025 comparatives were re-presented, Coltel and Ecuador purchase accounting is provisional, Q1 and Q3 statements are unreviewed, the Chile earn-out is stated as up to $150 million in the 20-F and up to $490 million in the 6-Ks (conflict C1), and 9M operating cash flow differs by 9 between the cash flow statement and the release reconciliation. Category: Documentary Fact. Implication: carry both values and label bases; do not treat any single reconciled figure as settled.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths.
- Scale and diversification. Category: Documentary Fact. Revenue streams are recurring and spread across twelve countries, with Mobile and Fixed services, and the largest segments are Guatemala and the two Colombian segments. Mechanism: geographic spread reduces the effect of any single market. Basis: CP-1A company and concentration rows and CP-1 revenue figures.
- Strong and growing cash generation. Category: Documentary Fact. Nine-month Adjusted EBITDA of 2,958, operating cash flow of 2,289 and equity free cash flow of 867, with management guidance of around 1.1 billion for 2026 (Management Language, qualified). Mechanism: cash generation funds interest, leases and capex and supports deleveraging.
- Improving leverage trajectory. Category: Documentary Fact. Management leverage 2.53x at September 30, 2026 compared with 2.73x and 2.76x in the prior two quarters; net debt was stable quarter over quarter at 7,983. Mechanism: higher EBITDA and EFCF are offsetting acquisition debt.
- Funding diversification. Category: Documentary Fact. Bonds, bank loans, development finance institution loans and local-currency issuance at both corporate and operating levels; about 70 percent of debt is at operating entities and 30 percent at the corporate level. Mechanism: reduces dependence on a single market; but structural subordination of corporate creditors to operating-level debt is also implied (Analyst Interpretation).
- Local-currency matching and hedging. Category: Documentary Fact. 61 percent of gross debt is in local currency or swapped, and the 2027 SEK note is swapped to dollars. Mechanism: aligns debt with revenue currencies.
- Transparent, frequent reporting and a no-breach statement. Category: Documentary Fact. Quarterly releases with reconciliations; the 20-F states no covenant breach at December 31, 2025 and none anticipated for twelve months. Limitation: that statement is not updated in the 2026 reports and thresholds are Not supplied (Insufficient Information).

Weaknesses.
- High absolute leverage and lease burden. Category: Documentary Fact. Net debt of 7,983 and lease liabilities of 3,190; lease-inclusive net financing obligations of 11,173 against reported last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA of 3,734 (Analyst Interpretation on the ratio). Mechanism: leases rank as fixed charges and the tower leaseback increased them.
- Emerging-market, correlated exposure and currency volatility. Category: Documentary Fact. The release cites rapid appreciation of the Colombian peso, Paraguayan guarani and Boliviano and a regime change in Bolivia. Mechanism: reported dollar results and local-currency debt values swing with exchange rates; reversal would reduce dollar EBITDA.
- Integration and execution risk in Colombia. Category: Documentary Fact for the restructuring charges and the Coltel net loss; Analyst Interpretation for the risk. Mechanism: severance and integration costs reduce EBITDA and cash flow and delay synergies.
- Floating-rate exposure and cost of debt. Category: Documentary Fact. Fixed-rate debt fell to 56 percent of debt and the average interest rate was 8.3 percent. Mechanism: higher rates pass through to interest expense; rate sensitivity is Not supplied (Insufficient Information).
- Near-term maturities and facility dependence. Category: Documentary Fact. Cash of 848 against debt due within one year of 872. Mechanism: refinancing needs are recurring.
- Controlling shareholder influence and distributions. Category: Documentary Fact. See key finding 5 and 6.
- Provisional accounting and limited legal visibility. Category: Insufficient Information. Covenant definitions, indentures, guarantor groups and the revolving facility position are not in the evidence set, so legal capacity for distributions, asset transfers and priming cannot be assessed.
