### Credit view

Advance Auto Parts has strong near-term liquidity but thin earnings, so its credit profile is cautious and improving rather than comfortable. The strongest support is USD 3,085 million of cash plus USD 894 million of undrawn revolver availability, USD 3,979 million in total, against USD 3,388 million of debt, with only USD 350 million due before 2028. The weakest point is earnings: last-twelve-month reported EBITDA of USD 508 million gives gross leverage of 6.67x and cash interest cover of 2.54x, while sales are flat. The outcome depends on whether restructuring margin gains hold as comparable sales soften, whether USD 2.6 billion of supplier finance stays in place, and how the cash is used, since USD 2.3 billion of it carries revolver conditions.

### Key findings

First, liquidity and maturities are the anchor of the credit. The financial base shows cash of USD 3,085 million and availability of USD 894 million under an undrawn revolver at October 10, 2026, USD 3,979 million in total, against total debt of USD 3,388 million, of which USD 349 million is current because the 1.75% notes mature on October 1, 2027. After liquidity less current debt, USD 3,630 million remains. Only USD 350 million falls due in 2027 and USD 271 million in 2028 before USD 1,475 million in 2030 and USD 1,325 million in 2032 and 2033. Because all notes are fixed rate and the revolver is undrawn, there is no interest-rate or refinancing pressure inside the next two years, so default risk over that horizon is low. This is a Documentary Fact for the figures and an Analyst Interpretation for the reading. The qualification is that USD 2.3 billion of the cash is qualified cash under the revolver and becomes restricted on a default or covenant breach, which limits how freely it can be used in a downside.

Second, earnings are the weakest part of the credit. Operating income was a loss in FY2025 and the last twelve months to October 10, 2026 show reported EBITDA of USD 508 million after restructuring charges, giving gross leverage of 6.67x, net leverage of 0.60x and cash interest cover of 2.54x on interest paid of USD 200 million. Net leverage is low only because cash exceeds debt, so leverage on earnings rises quickly if cash is used. The simple EBITDA includes restructuring and impairment charges, so it may understate underlying earnings, while management's adjusted, lease-adjusted measure of 2.2x is a different definition and is not adopted. This is an Analyst Interpretation resting on Documentary Fact figures.

Third, the August 2025 financing changed both ranking and carry. The company issued USD 1,950 million of 7.000% and 7.375% notes, redeemed USD 300 million of 2026 notes and held most of the rest as cash, which lifted liquidity but raised interest expense to USD 160 million over forty weeks from USD 86 million. At the same time the unsecured revolver was replaced by a first-lien asset-based revolver of up to USD 1 billion over receivables, inventory and cash. The mechanic is that the unsecured notes now rank behind secured claims on the assets that hold most of the company's liquid value, although the revolver is undrawn and carries USD 106 million of letters of credit. The implication is that ranking matters only if cash is spent or the revolver is drawn, but legal capacity to add further priming debt cannot be tested because the indentures are not supplied. This is an Analyst Interpretation.

Fourth, supplier finance is the largest claim outside reported debt. Confirmed obligations to banks were USD 2.6 billion at October 10, 2026 and sit inside accounts payable of USD 3,065 million, which fund most of the USD 3,789 million of inventories. The 10-K ties bank participation to credit ratings. If banks pull back, payable terms shorten and working capital absorbs cash, an amount comparable to the whole debt stack and large relative to liquidity. No rating is in the pack to measure this risk. This is a Documentary Fact for the amounts and an Analyst Interpretation for the sensitivity. Operating leases of USD 2,189 million at October 10, 2026 on management's count are a second claim outside debt.

Fifth, revenue visibility is limited and the margin recovery is recent. Professional customers are about half of sales and there is no customer at 10%, which supports stability, but comparable sales rose 3.5% in the first quarter, fell 0.5% in the second and fell 1.2% in the third, and management moved sales guidance toward the lower end of its earlier range while raising free cash flow guidance to about USD 150 million. Free cash flow was USD 109 million over forty weeks against an outflow a year earlier, helped by lower restructuring cash charges. The credit implication is that debt service depends on cost savings and mix holding rather than on volume growth. Management's late-quarter improvement is Management Language and is not adopted.

Sixth, ownership and governance add no identified extraction risk but little support. No sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed, dividends were cut after 2023, no shares were bought under the programme in FY2025 and FY2026 to date, and the company repurchased small amounts of two note series. No creditor-adverse action is documented. This is a statement about the pack, not a clean record, because the proxy statement, indentures and related-party information are absent.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity is the principal strength: USD 3,979 million of cash plus undrawn availability exceeds total debt of USD 3,388 million, and cash alone exceeds it (Documentary Fact, financial base). The maturity profile is back-ended, with USD 350 million due in 2027 and USD 271 million in 2028 and all notes at fixed rates (Documentary Fact, financial base). The revenue base is broad, with about half of sales to professional customers, no customer at 10% and a replacement-parts mix that is weighted to failure-driven categories (Documentary Fact, business context). Operating cash flow of USD 311 million over forty weeks against an outflow of USD 118 million a year earlier shows the restructuring benefit reaching cash (Documentary Fact; the durability is an Analyst Interpretation). The company reports compliance with its revolver covenants, and no sponsor, dividend recapitalisation or creditor-adverse transaction is documented (Documentary Fact, business context).

Weaknesses. Earnings coverage is thin, with gross leverage of 6.67x and cash interest cover of 2.54x on simple reported EBITDA (Analyst Interpretation on Documentary Fact figures, financial base). The cash pile does not pay for the debt, as guided interest expense of about USD 210 million compares with guided interest income of about USD 105 million (Management Language for the guidance, financial base and business context). The first-lien revolver ranks ahead of the unsecured notes on current assets and cash (Analyst Interpretation, business context). Supplier finance of USD 2.6 billion is a rating-sensitive source of funding (Analyst Interpretation, business context). Comparable sales are negative in two of three FY2026 quarters and the DIY channel is described as soft (Documentary Fact for the figures; management attribution is Management Language). Restructuring charges continue, with USD 5 million to USD 10 million still expected in FY2026 (Documentary Fact, business context). Legal terms, ratings and covenant headroom are not available (Insufficient Information: the indentures, revolver agreement, rating reports and compliance certificate would show lien capacity, restricted payments and distance to the springing test).
