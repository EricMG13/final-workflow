### Business and transaction view

The business view is of a scaled, single-segment North American auto-parts retailer and distributor that is mid-turnaround. It sold its Worldpac import-parts business in November 2024, closed several hundred stores and four distribution centres in 2024 and early 2025, and is now rebuilding margin through merchandising and supply chain changes. The professional channel is about half of sales and, per management, led performance, while the DIY channel is described as weaker in the second and third quarters of FY2026. The transaction view is that the August 2025 financing, not the Worldpac sale, is the dominant credit event today: it replaced an unsecured revolver with a first-lien asset-based facility, raised debt and the carry cost of holding cash, and pushed the debt stack to a mix of low-coupon older notes and high-coupon 2030 and 2033 notes. No sponsor, parent or controlling shareholder is disclosed, so there is no sponsor-support analysis to make and no ownership-based extraction risk to assess. Documented capital-allocation behaviour is a dividend cut after 2023, no repurchases under the programme in FY2025 and FY2026 to date, and a small repurchase of notes in Q2 FY2026. Evidence quality is high for the facts drawn from the 10-K and 10-Qs and low for management's forward statements in the releases. The main limits are the missing legal documents, ratings and the 10-K Part III content that the filing incorporates from a proxy statement not in the pack.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The Worldpac sale is the main disposal in the pack. The company signed a definitive agreement on August 22, 2024 to sell Worldpac for 1.5 billion with customary working-capital adjustments, the sale closed on November 1, 2024, and net proceeds after expenses, the final working-capital true-up and before tax were about 1.44 billion (10-K FY2025 page:105). The buyer is not named in the pack, so the counterparty and any continuing commercial ties beyond the transition services agreement are Not supplied. A final working-capital payment of 55 was made in the first half of FY2026 and shown in discontinued investing cash flow (10-Q Q3 page:24). In connection with the sale the company provided letters of credit of up to 200 under its unsecured revolving credit facility as credit support for Worldpac's supply chain financing programme, and none were outstanding at January 3, 2026 (10-K FY2025 page:105).

The new debt issue that matters for creditors is the August 4, 2025 private placement of 975 of 7.000% notes due 2030 and 975 of 7.375% notes due 2033, exempt from Securities Act registration, issued under an indenture of that date with Computershare Trust Company as trustee (10-K FY2025 page:87 and page:88). The notes are guaranteed jointly and severally by subsidiary guarantors on an unsubordinated, unsecured basis, and the indenture's cross-acceleration threshold for these notes is 125, against 25 for the older notes (10-K FY2025 page:89). The company used a portion of the proceeds to redeem all 300 of its 5.90% notes due 2026, recorded a loss of 3 on that redemption, and kept the rest for general corporate purposes; cash rose from 1,869 at December 28, 2024 to 3,123 at January 3, 2026 (10-K FY2025 page:87 and page:68). Debt issuance costs of 47 were paid in FY2025. The effect on liquidity was large and positive, but the effect on carry was negative: the new coupons are higher than the older notes, interest expense for forty weeks rose to 160 from 86, and FY2026 guidance includes interest expense of about 210 against interest income of about 105 (10-Q Q3 page:23; Release Q3 page:3). The pack does not supply the issue price, call schedule, covenant package or guarantor list for these notes, so legal capacity is limited to the 10-K summary, which describes lien and sale-leaseback limits and a change-of-control put at 101%.

On August 12, 2025 the 1 billion unsecured revolving credit agreement was terminated and replaced by an asset-based revolving facility of up to 1 billion, with an uncommitted accordion of up to 500 (10-K FY2025 page:90). The new facility is a senior secured first lien on substantially all accounts receivable, inventory, cash and related assets of the borrower and guarantors, with a springing fixed charge coverage test at a minimum of 1:1, and qualified-cash conditions (10-K FY2025 page:91 and page:92). For existing unsecured noteholders this changes ranking on current assets and cash, which is where most of the company's liquid value sits; the facility is undrawn, with letters of credit of 106 outstanding at October 10, 2026 (10-Q Q3 page:12). That reading is an analyst interpretation of the documents and not a legal conclusion.

In FY2026 there has been no new issuance. In the second quarter the company bought back 29 of its 5.95% notes due 2028 and 0.1 of its 1.75% notes due 2027 under a 10b5-1 plan, which ended on completion, and in the third quarter it reclassified 349 of the 2027 notes to current (10-Q Q3 page:11). Management describes this repurchase as about 30 of principal retired (Release Q2 page:2); the 10-Q figures are 29 and 0.1, and the difference is rounding and not a conflict. After the quarter, on October 26, 2026, the board declared a regular dividend of 0.25 per share (Release Q3 page:2). No acquisition, sponsor transaction, dividend recapitalisation or amendment to the notes is disclosed. The main restructuring-related transaction effects are the closure of stores and distribution centres and continuing impairments on closed locations, including a non-cash right-of-use impairment of 33 in Q3 FY2026 (10-Q Q3 page:18).
