### Business and transaction view

AdaptHealth is a national provider of home medical equipment, supplies and related services, operating three reportable segments after the pending sale of its Diabetes Health business. It is a holding company that depends on its operating subsidiaries for cash. The most important business change for a creditor is the move from fee-for-product and fixed monthly rental revenue toward at-risk capitated revenue, in which the company is paid a fixed monthly fee per member and bears the cost of the members' care. That change is driving revenue growth, equipment spending and a margin decline, and the same contract set is the main source of near-term operating uncertainty. The transactions that matter are the pending 235.0 million sale of Diabetes Health to RGH Enterprises, LLC under an agreement dated 2026-07-19, the 2026-07-29 contribution of the eCommerce subsidiary to a joint venture, the April 2026 refinancing and the August 2026 redemption of the 6.125% notes. The documents behind the sale, the joint venture and the refinancing are not in the sources, so what they permit cannot be assessed; only the filing descriptions are available. No sponsor is named in the context, and the filings show two minority holders rather than a controlling owner. Documented issuer behaviour since 2024 includes voluntary term loan repayments and no dividends or buybacks, but 2026 debt rose to fund growth and acquisitions. The reading is Neutral or Mixed on the evidence, with legal capacity for distributions and asset transfers not assessable without the credit documents.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The one transaction that changes the credit is the pending sale of the Diabetes Health business under an asset purchase agreement dated 2026-07-19 between the company and RGH Enterprises, LLC for 235.0 million in cash, subject to customary purchase price adjustments (Q3 10-Q page:27, page:59, page:96). It is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to certain regulatory approvals and customary conditions; the Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period has expired. The status is signed and pending, not closed. The business is already reported as discontinued operations, and its goodwill and intangibles are carried as assets held for sale. The filings do not say how the proceeds will be used. The credit agreement requires mandatory prepayments in connection with certain dispositions of assets to the extent proceeds are not reinvested (Q3 10-Q page:41-42), and the notes may require an offer to purchase upon the sale of certain assets (page:42), but whether either applies to this sale is not stated, and the agreement and indentures are not in the sources. Use of proceeds, any price adjustment and any transition services remain Insufficient Information. A second portfolio move, the eCommerce contribution to a joint venture on 2026-07-29, is closed; the company holds a minority interest carried at 42.5 million and the filing says the transaction is not a strategic shift (page:16-17).

On debt, two financings occurred in the year. On 2026-04-10 the company entered the 2026 Credit Agreement, with a 325.0 million term loan, a 325.0 million delayed-draw term loan and 450.0 million of revolving commitments, maturing April 2031 with a springing maturity tied to the 4.625% and 5.125% notes (Q3 10-Q page:40). On 2026-07-27 the delayed-draw term loan was borrowed in full and, with cash on hand, redeemed the 6.125% notes at par on 2026-08-03 (page:40, page:89). No new bond issue is recorded in the sources for 2026. The sale proceeds, if applied to debt, would be a prepayment rather than a new issue. The financing terms, security, guarantors and covenants are described in the financial base handoff and rest on filing summaries because the executed credit agreement and indentures are not supplied. Ratings and market quotes are not supplied.
