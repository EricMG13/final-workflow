### Credit view

AdaptHealth's fundamental credit view is cautious: a large, cash-generative home-care provider whose earnings and free cash flow are weakening while its debt is rising. The strongest support is scale and recurring revenue, with USD 395,613 thousand of operating cash flow for nine months 2026, USD 349,387 thousand of liquidity at 2026-09-30, a refinancing that leaves no notes maturing before 2029, and no dividends or buybacks. The weakest point is cash conversion: patient equipment spending of USD 392,981 thousand absorbed nearly all operating cash flow, Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 17.5% from 20.0%, and the new capitated contracts put cost-of-care risk on the company. Three things decide the outcome: whether capitated contract margins keep recovering, how the USD 235.0 million Diabetes Health sale proceeds are used, and whether the 2029 and 2030 notes are refinanced before the secured facilities spring forward.

### Key findings

AdaptHealth earned less per dollar of revenue in 2026 even as revenue grew. Revenue for nine months 2026 was USD 2,165,828 thousand, up 9.72%, but Adjusted EBITDA was USD 378,462 thousand, down 4.23%, and the margin fell by 2.5 points to 17.5%. The decline sits in Sleep Health and Wellness at Home, whose margins fell, while Respiratory Health improved. The mechanism is a rapid ramp of capitated contracts, which carried start-up labour cost and a supplier price increase before volumes settled; margin recovered in each quarter of 2026 but remains below Q3 2025 [Financial base, Business context].

Cash generation is the weakest link. Free cash flow was USD 2,632 thousand for nine months 2026, against USD 140,095 thousand a year earlier, because equipment purchases rose to USD 392,981 thousand to serve capitated members. On a continuing basis Adjusted EBITDA less equipment purchases was negative USD 6,796 thousand, against positive USD 123,716 thousand a year earlier. Acquisitions of USD 127,422 thousand and tax receivable agreement payments of USD 26,846 thousand were funded by borrowing, which is how principal debt rose to USD 1,894,969 thousand from USD 1,750,000 thousand [Financial base].

Leverage has risen in direction but cannot be measured on one perimeter. Trailing Adjusted EBITDA on the current business equals recast FY2025 less USD 16,717 thousand, while debt is higher, so the ratio is higher than at year-end 2025. No recast FY2025 figure exists in the sources. On management guidance, principal debt is 3.66x the midpoint of USD 517.5 million of guided Adjusted EBITDA, an indicative figure that treats a forecast as given [Financial base].

Liquidity and maturities are sound in the near term. Cash was USD 80,687 thousand and undrawn revolver availability USD 268.7 million, for USD 349,387 thousand. Scheduled repayments are USD 4.0 million in the rest of 2026 and USD 16.0 million in 2027. The 6.125% notes were redeemed in August 2026. The 4.625% notes of USD 500.0 million fall due on 2029-08-01 and the 5.125% notes of USD 600.0 million on 2030-03-01, and the USD 794,969 thousand of term loans and revolver spring to about 2029-05-02 if more than USD 150.0 million of the notes remains outstanding then [Financial base].

Interest cover is adequate but lower. Adjusted EBITDA was 3.92x cash interest paid for nine months 2026 against 4.12x for nine months 2025. Covenant tests exist on total leverage and interest coverage, the company reports compliance at 2026-09-30, and the levels are not supplied, so headroom is not measurable [Source readiness, Financial base].

The business is shifting toward at-risk capitated revenue. It was 13.3% of nine-month 2026 revenue against 4.8%, and under these contracts the company bears members' care cost. The counterparty, term and price of the main contract are not disclosed, so concentration cannot be sized. Management claims a sequential improvement in contract profitability in Q3 2026 and offers no contract profit to test it [Business context].

Portfolio change and claims outside debt add outcomes that are not yet resolved. The sale of Diabetes Health for USD 235.0 million in cash is signed but not closed, and use of proceeds is not stated. The eCommerce subsidiary moved into a joint venture for a minority stake. Claims outside the debt figure include the tax receivable agreement liability of USD 238.9 million, operating leases of USD 126,716 thousand, finance leases of USD 39,357 thousand, two federal civil investigative demands, an unquantified cybersecurity incident and a consumer class settlement with USD 14.1 million remaining liability [Financial base, Business context].

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Scale and diversification of referral sources: the company serves about 4.8 million patients a year through about 660 locations in 48 states, with fixed monthly equipment reimbursements and product resupply forming most revenue [Documentary Fact; Business context]. Operating cash flow is large: USD 395,613 thousand for nine months 2026, against USD 418,587 thousand a year earlier, which still funds a substantial equipment programme [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Liquidity and structure: USD 349,387 thousand of liquidity, a revolver running to April 2031 subject to the springing maturity, and no notes due before 2029 [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Shareholder behaviour: no dividends or buybacks, voluntary term loan repayments of USD 218.8 million in 2025, and no controlling sponsor shown among holders near 10% and 8% [Documentary Fact; Business context]. Recovery of margin through 2026: the Adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 15.4% in Q1 2026 (derived) to 17.8% in Q2 and 19.0% in Q3 [Documentary Fact; Financial base].

Weaknesses. Margin and earnings quality: nine-month margin down to 17.5% from 20.0%, with "other non-recurring" add-backs recurring in both years at USD 15,085 thousand and USD 20,439 thousand [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Thin free cash flow after equipment spending, with growth funded by debt [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Rising debt: principal debt up USD 144,969 thousand and net principal debt up USD 170,418 thousand since year-end 2025 [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Cost-of-care risk and concentration in a small set of capitated contracts whose terms are undisclosed [Documentary Fact for the share, Insufficient Information for the terms; Business context]. Goodwill impairments of USD 328,987 thousand in nine months 2026 and a Sleep Health reporting unit within 20% of carrying value, which signal weaker projected cash flows in two reporting units and a risk of more [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Goodwill of USD 2,158,313 thousand is 51.38% of total assets and exceeds total equity, so tangible equity is negative and balance sheet asset quality offers less comfort [Analyst Interpretation; Calculations]. Unquantified claims: investigations, the cybersecurity incident and a supplier bed recall [Documentary Fact; Business context]. Management continuity: a change of Chief Financial Officer between the Q2 and Q3 2026 reports and an unreconciled restructuring savings figure [Documentary Fact; Business context].
