### Financial base view

On the perimeter the filings now use, AdaptHealth is growing revenue faster than it is growing earnings. Nine-month 2026 revenue rose 9.72% year on year, but Adjusted EBITDA fell 4.23% and the Adjusted EBITDA margin fell by 2.5 points, to 17.5%. The pressure is concentrated in the Sleep Health and Wellness at Home segments, whose margins fell, while Respiratory Health improved. Management attributes it to the ramp of an exclusive capitated agreement with a national integrated delivery network, a manufacturer price increase and elevated labour, and says contract profitability improved sequentially in Q3 2026. The recast quarterly margin did rise each quarter of 2026, from 15.4% (derived) to 17.8% to 19.0%, but remains below the 21.7% of Q3 2025. Cash generation is the weakest part of the base: operating cash flow was 395,613 thousand for nine months, but equipment purchases of 392,981 thousand absorbed nearly all of it, leaving free cash flow of 2,632 thousand. Debt is higher, with principal of 1,894,969 thousand against 1,750,000 thousand at year-end 2025, while cash is lower at 80,687 thousand. Liquidity is adequate on the filing's numbers, at 349,387 thousand of cash plus undrawn revolver, and the maturity profile improved because the 6.125% notes were redeemed in August 2026 with a delayed-draw term loan. The base has four limits. There is no recast full-year 2025 figure, so a trailing leverage ratio on the current perimeter cannot be computed. The covenant levels and the issuer-by-issuer guarantor map are not supplied. The Diabetes Health sale for 235.0 million in cash, expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, would change the debt picture but is not closed. The goodwill impairments of 328,987 thousand are non-cash and are excluded from Adjusted EBITDA, but they signal weaker projected cash flows in two reporting units.

### Interpretation

#### Leverage

Principal debt at 2026-09-30 was 1,894,969 thousand against 1,750,000 thousand at 2025-12-31, an increase of 144,969 thousand. The term loans rose by 332,969 thousand (647,969 less 315,000) and the revolver by 147,000 thousand, while senior unsecured notes fell by 335,000 thousand (1,435,000 less 1,100,000, being the 10.0 million January 2026 repurchase and the 325.0 million August 2026 redemption of the 6.125% notes). Cash flows in the period also funded 127,422 thousand of acquisitions and an expanded equipment programme. Net of cash, principal debt rose by 170,418 thousand. Over the same nine months Adjusted EBITDA on the recast perimeter was 4.23% lower than a year earlier. The direction of trailing leverage is nonetheless clear. Trailing twelve months Adjusted EBITDA on the recast perimeter at 2026-09-30 equals recast FY2025 plus nine months 2026 less nine months 2025, which is recast FY2025 less 16,717 thousand (378,462 less 395,179), so the denominator is lower than at year-end while principal debt is higher; the ratio has risen. Its size cannot be built, because the 10-K annual Adjusted EBITDA includes Diabetes Health and the recast annual figure is not supplied. The only like-for-like ratio is on forward guidance: principal debt of 1,894,969 thousand against the 2026 guidance range of 510 million to 525 million gives 3.61 to 3.72 times gross, an indicative figure that rests on a management claim and ignores the 235.0 million of Diabetes Health sale proceeds, which are not yet received.

#### Coverage

For nine months 2026, Adjusted EBITDA of 378,462 thousand against interest expense, net of 80,403 thousand is 4.71 times. On cash interest paid of 96,639 thousand the cover is 3.92 times, against 4.12 times for nine months 2025. This is not the covenant definition, which is not supplied. The Q3 2026 interest expense of 28,600 thousand was 12.69% above Q3 2025 even though the nine-month figure was slightly lower, consistent with a larger drawn balance after April 2026. Cash interest paid for nine months 2026 (96,639) exceeded interest expense, net (80,403); accrued interest fell from 28,447 to 6,790 (page:38), which is consistent with settlement of accrued coupons, though the filing does not itself give this reason. The rate on the term loans is 5.06%, below the 6.125% coupon of the notes they replaced (which alone does not give an interest saving number, because the revolver and the first term loan also changed).

#### Cash flow quality

Operating cash flow was 5.49% lower for nine months 2026 than for nine months 2025 despite revenue growth. Purchases of equipment rose from 278,492 to 392,981 thousand; the filing links this to patient equipment for capitated contracts (page:92). Patient equipment depreciation was 308,050 thousand of the 332,483 thousand continuing-operations depreciation and amortization (page:32, page:74-76), so Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes this recurring replacement-type cost, flatters cash earnings; on a continuing basis, Adjusted EBITDA less equipment purchases was negative 6,796 thousand for nine months 2026 against positive 123,716 thousand for nine months 2025. The 2026 free cash flow of 2,632 thousand and the nine-month acquisitions of 127,422 thousand were funded by borrowing. Add-backs outside goodwill, gains and equity compensation (restructuring, litigation and other non-recurring) were 21,655 thousand for nine months 2026, or 5.72% of Adjusted EBITDA, against 20,439 thousand, or 5.17%, in nine months 2025. The company's 2026 free cash flow guidance of 50 million to 75 million implies a fourth quarter of 47,368 to 72,368 thousand against a reported nine-month figure of 2,632 thousand; that is arithmetic on guidance, not a forecast.

#### Liquidity

Cash was 80,687 thousand and the revolver had 268.7 million of availability, equal to commitments of 450.0 million less 147.0 million drawn and 34.3 million of letters of credit, for 349,387 thousand. The delayed-draw term loan is fully drawn so it adds no liquidity. Revolver availability is not within a year of expiry: the facility runs to April 2031 subject to the springing maturity below. The next-twelve-month scheduled term loan repayments of 16,250 thousand (current portion) are covered by liquidity. Tax refunds of 16.7 million are expected later in 2026 (page:89).

#### Springing maturity and the wall

The 2026 facilities mature in April 2031, but 91 days before the stated maturity of the 4.625% notes (2029-08-01) and the 5.125% notes (2030-03-01) the facilities mature earlier if more than 150.0 million of the notes remains outstanding (page:40). Counting 91 days back gives 2029-05-02 and 2029-11-30 (derived). The wording does not say whether the 150.0 million test applies to each series or to both together. With 1,100,000 thousand of notes outstanding, the effective maturity of 794,969 thousand of senior secured term loans and revolver is therefore the 2029 date unless the notes are refinanced first. The amortization wall is in Calculations.

#### Earnings quality and definitions

Adjusted EBITDA adds back equity-based compensation, goodwill impairment, restructuring, litigation settlement and "other non-recurring" items. The other non-recurring line was recurring in both periods shown. It was 15,085 thousand for nine months 2026 and 20,439 thousand for nine months 2025, mainly consulting on disposals and acquisitions. Goodwill impairments of 144,236 thousand in Q2 2026 and 184,751 thousand in Q3 2026 hit Respiratory Health (228,484 for nine months) and Wellness at Home (100,503 for nine months) and followed a goodwill test as of 2026-06-30 prompted by the Diabetes Health plan and revised projections (page:33-34). Sleep Health's fair value exceeded its carrying value by less than 20% (page:35), a source of further impairment risk that is stated in the filing. The Q3 release's headline "net loss attributable to AdaptHealth Corp." of 168.2 million is the continuing-operations loss net of noncontrolling interest, and differs from the 10-Q total of 153,987 thousand, which includes discontinued operations; they reconcile in Calculations.
