### Credit view

Alkermes plc is a moderately leveraged specialty drug company whose secured term loans look serviceable from current cash flow, but whose coverage depends on managing expected VIVITROL generic entry in 2027 and on integrating LUMRYZ. The strongest support is liquidity: USD 787.6 million of cash and investments against USD 26.5 million of principal due in 2027 and only USD 164.2 million due before 2031. The weakest point is that total debt of USD 1,497.7 million is 3.40x LTM Adjusted EBITDA but 10.25x reported EBITDA, so leverage rests on management add-backs, and covenant levels cannot be read. The outcome will be decided by whether legacy cash flow is replaced as VIVITROL erodes, and by the covenant terms not yet supplied.

### Key findings

Alkermes borrowed its full USD 1,525.0 million of term loans to buy Avadel, moving from no debt at December 31, 2025 to USD 1,497.7 million of senior secured debt at September 30, 2026. The debt is two bank term loans with no bonds. Scheduled principal is USD 6.625 million for the rest of 2026, USD 26.500 million in 2027, USD 40.563 million in 2028 and USD 45.250 million in each of 2029 and 2030, which leaves 89.1% of principal due in 2031 (new arithmetic, see Calculations) [financial base: debt and capital structure]. Liquidity of USD 787.6 million of cash and investments, with no revolver found, is what the creditor can rely on, and it comfortably covers amortization.

Leverage is the central judgement. On LTM Adjusted EBITDA of USD 440.2 million, total debt is 3.40x and net debt 1.61x, but LTM reported EBITDA is only USD 146.2 million, which gives 10.25x and 4.86x. The difference is acquisition costs, inventory step-up, share-based compensation and a contingent consideration charge, and the FY2025 part of LTM Adjusted EBITDA is derived by the analyst. Expense-basis cover is 5.08x on Adjusted EBITDA, with cash interest unreported [financial base: leverage and coverage].

Business risk is concentrated. VIVITROL was 31.7% of FY2025 revenue and generic entry is expected in 2027, while ARISTADA patents run to 2039 and LYBALVI patents to 2041, with LYBALVI facing paragraph IV challenges, and the legacy products rely on three wholesalers. LUMRYZ supplied 16.5% of nine-month 2026 revenue through four specialty pharmacies, bears a 3.85% royalty to Jazz and faces a generic challenge [business context: operating model and credit translations]. Cash from operations of USD 72.9 million for nine months 2026, against USD 350.7 million a year earlier, shows how heavily the first year of integration weighed on cash, while LTM free cash flow of USD 227.4 million shows the recovery to date [financial base: cash flow quality].

Documented behaviour is not extractive: no dividend has been paid and buybacks of USD 27.7 million occurred only in Q1 2026, but ownership, board and covenant capacity are not supplied, so legal capacity for distributions cannot be tested [business context: ownership, governance and capital allocation]. This corrects the business context handoff, which said buybacks stopped after the term loans were drawn: the sources show only that none occurred in Q2 or Q3 2026 and do not date the Q1 purchases against the February 12, 2026 closing.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. (1) Liquidity and maturity profile: USD 787.6 million of cash and investments against light amortization and a 2031-weighted maturity wall, which gives time to absorb the 2027 VIVITROL change (Documentary Fact; financial base). (2) Cash generation: LTM free cash flow of USD 227.4 million with low capital spending, equal to 15.2% of debt (Documentary Fact for the inputs; the ratio is Analyst Interpretation). (3) Portfolio breadth beyond one product: four proprietary products plus royalties, with ARISTADA patents to 2039, LYBALVI to 2041 and LUMRYZ orphan exclusivity to 2030 for adults (Documentary Fact; business context). (4) Interest rate protection on USD 750.0 million of notional through collars entered in October 2026, though covenant effects are unknown (Documentary Fact). (5) Documented financial behaviour has been conservative: debt prepaid in 2024, no dividends, no buybacks in Q2 or Q3 2026 (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on what it implies).

Weaknesses. (1) Dependence on add-backs: 3.40x on Adjusted EBITDA against 10.25x on reported EBITDA, with a derived FY2025 base and share-based compensation add-backs that do not tie to the statements (Documentary Fact; the dependence is Analyst Interpretation). (2) Product concentration and generic risk: VIVITROL at 31.7% of FY2025 revenue with generic entry expected in 2027, and product-level margins Not supplied, so the cash flow effect cannot be sized (Documentary Fact; Insufficient Information for the effect). (3) Secured structure without visible covenants: all debt is secured on substantially all assets, but levels, definitions and the margin grid are Not supplied, and Term Loan B is outside the maintenance tests (Insufficient Information). (4) Floating-rate exposure: Term SOFR plus 1.75% to 2.25% on Term Loan A and 2.25% on Term Loan B, half of principal hedged by collars (Documentary Fact; the share is arithmetic). (5) Acquisition accounting and contingent liability: a preliminary purchase price allocation, intangibles and goodwill that make up most of the consideration, and a possible payment of USD 165.7 million carried at USD 136.9 million (Documentary Fact; the impairment exposure is Analyst Interpretation). (6) Channel and counterparty concentration: three wholesalers for legacy products and four specialty pharmacies for LUMRYZ (Documentary Fact). (7) Thin interim cash conversion: nine-month 2026 cash from operations of USD 72.9 million (Documentary Fact).
