### Business and transaction view

AMC ENTERTAINMENT HOLDINGS, INC. is a listed, widely held theatre circuit with no sponsor identified in the supplied sources, and its creditor story is the interaction of an operating recovery with a history of active balance-sheet engineering. The operating base is large and geographically split: 855 theatres and 9,640 screens in 11 countries at December 31, 2025, and 840 theatres and 9,472 screens at September 30, 2026, with U.S. markets contributing 3,298.5 of 4,353.2 of nine-month 2026 revenue. Revenue is driven by attendance, which depends on a film slate controlled by a few studios, so revenue visibility is short and cash flow is seasonal. Against that, a largely fixed cost base (rent, operating expense and leases) gave strong operating leverage in 2026: third quarter revenue rose 31.6% and Adjusted EBITDA margin was 20.3% against 9.4%. The credit-relevant history is a sequence of transactions that moved debt and assets between entities: the July 2024 transfer of 175 theatres and intellectual property to unrestricted subsidiaries, the July 2025 exchange of part of the Existing 7.5% Notes into Muvico notes, repeated exchanges of debt for shares, and the October 5, 2026 refinancing that repaid the Muvico and Odeon term loans, the New 2029 Notes and most of the Existing 7.5% Notes with 2,000.0 of first lien notes, 850.0 of first lien term loans and 1,120.0 of second lien term loans. That refinancing is a subsequent event to the September 30, 2026 balance sheet and is kept separate from period facts below. Supportive actions are also documented, namely large equity issuance and note-for-share exchanges, but they were dilutive: shares outstanding rose from 512,943,561 at December 31, 2025 to 892,604,638 at September 30, 2026. Ownership concentration, sponsor behaviour beyond the issuer's own actions, and executed covenant terms cannot be read from the sources and remain open.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transaction that matters most for creditors is the October 5, 2026 refinancing, which closed after the September 30, 2026 balance sheet and is a subsequent event. S4 page:33 states that on that date "the Company completed a series of refinancing transactions", issuing 2,000.0 of 8.875% First Lien Notes due 2031 as issuer, borrowing 850.0 of first lien term loans under an amended and restated credit agreement, and borrowing 1,120.0 of second lien term loans from a lender identified as Deutsche Bank Special Situations Group (S4 page:34). The first lien term loans mature on October 5, 2031 with 0.25% quarterly amortization from March 31, 2027, and the second lien term loans mature on October 5, 2033 at a fixed 11.25% (S4 page:36). The 2031 Notes are secured on a first-priority basis, pari passu with the first lien term loans, by substantially all tangible and intangible assets of the company and the guarantors (S4 page:35). Intercreditor agreements separate three collateral groups: the AMC collateral, the Centertainment group and the Odeon group, and rank the Existing Exchangeable Notes first lien on the AMC collateral but second lien on the Centertainment collateral (S4 page:37). The proceeds repaid in full the Muvico Term Loans and the Odeon Term Loans, redeemed all 903.4 of the New 2029 Notes at 100.000% plus a make-whole premium of 144.3, accepted tenders for 355.5 of the 360.0 Existing 7.5% Notes and discharged the remainder for redemption on or about February 15, 2027 (S4 page:37-38). The company used about 32.7 of balance-sheet cash at closing and estimates a loss on extinguishment of about 182.7 in the fourth quarter of 2026 (S4 page:38). Taken from the CP-1 accepted figures, pro forma principal is 4,086.1 against 3,782.6 before, with 79.2% fixed rate, and the nearest large maturity moves from 2029 to 2031. The creditor mechanics are two: near-term refinancing risk falls, because the Muvico term loans, the Odeon term loans and the New 2029 Notes were the main 2029 and 2031 obligations; but senior secured claims are larger and a 1,120.0 second lien layer now sits behind 2,850.0 of new first lien debt, so recovery prospects for junior claims depend on collateral value that the sources do not quantify. This handoff states no expected recovery.

Earlier transactions explain why the structure is layered. On July 22, 2024 the company transferred the net assets of 175 theatres and 100% of certain intellectual property to Muvico through Centertainment, and Muvico licensed the intellectual property back to the operating subsidiary and engaged it under a management agreement to run the transferred theatres; Muvico and Centertainment (the Muvico Group) "are unrestricted subsidiaries under the indenture governing Holdings" for the purposes of the Existing 7.5% Notes (S1 page:100, S3 page:67). In the same month the company and Muvico jointly borrowed 2,024.3 of term loans and Muvico issued 414.4 of exchangeable notes to repurchase second lien notes (S1 page:156). In July 2025, under a transaction support agreement with "certain holders" of the Existing 7.5% Notes, Muvico issued 857.0 of New 2029 Notes for 590.0 of those notes plus 244.4 of new money (S1 page:95; S4 page:16), reducing the Existing 7.5% Notes from 950.0 at December 31, 2024 to 360.0 at December 31, 2025 (S1 page:142). The sources do not say whether every holder was offered the exchange. In 2026 the company paid consent fees in shares rather than cash, including 17,739,549 shares to holders of the New 2029 Notes for indenture amendments, exchanged all 155.8 of the New Exchangeable Notes for 142,102,295 shares with a loss on extinguishment of 33.0, refinanced the 400.0 Odeon Senior Secured Notes due 2027 with 425.0 of Odeon term loans that carried a Holdings guarantee, and raised equity (S4 page:14-18; S2 page:27). The 125.5 Senior Subordinated Notes due 2027 were redeemed on July 24, 2026 with proceeds of the June 2026 registered direct offering, within net equity issuance proceeds of 333.6 for nine months 2026 (S4 page:4, page:16, page:45). No dividends or shareholder distributions are printed in the nine-month cash flow statement (S4 page:4). Evidence quality for these transactions is High: they are filed, dated and issuer-specific, but executed indentures and credit agreements are not supplied, so legal capacity (what the documents permit) is inferred only from the filings' summaries.
