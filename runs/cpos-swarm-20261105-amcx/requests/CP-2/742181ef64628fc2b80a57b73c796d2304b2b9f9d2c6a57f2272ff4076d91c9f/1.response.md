### Credit view

AMC Global Media has a weakening but refinanced credit profile: it faces no debt maturity until February 2029, but its earnings are falling and its liquidity is now cash alone. The strongest support is cash generation, with USD 190.5 million of free cash flow in the twelve months to June 30, 2026 and cash of USD 464.0 million. The weakest point is earnings: adjusted operating income fell to USD 313.0 million from USD 411.9 million in FY2025, which lifted net debt to adjusted operating income to 4.06x from 3.10x while net debt was flat at USD 1,271.6 million, and the revolving credit facility no longer exists. The outcome depends on whether streaming growth and the Netflix licence cash offset linear decline before USD 420.5 million of unsecured notes fall due in February 2029.

Assessment: weakening, refinanced credit; category Analyst Interpretation; confidence Medium. The basis is the leverage, cover and liquidity figures [CP-1 C3, C4, C5, C11] and the business rows on revenue, control and transactions [CP-1A]. Confidence is below High because the business module judged this module ready only with limitations and listed unsupplied indentures as a high-severity gap.

Thesis: AMC Global Media has traded near-term maturity risk for a higher-cost, longer secured structure at the point its earnings are declining, so the credit now rests on stabilising earnings and cash generation rather than on committed back-up liquidity.

### Key findings

1. Earnings, not debt, drove the rise in leverage. Net debt moved from USD 1,275.9 million at December 31, 2025 to USD 1,271.6 million at June 30, 2026, while adjusted operating income over twelve months fell to USD 313.0 million from USD 411.9 million, taking net debt to adjusted operating income to 4.06x from 3.10x and total debt to adjusted operating income to 5.54x from 4.32x [CP-1 C3]. The fall came mainly from Domestic Operations, whose segment adjusted operating income was USD 61.0 million in Q2 2026 against USD 126.3 million in Q2 2025 as subscription, advertising and content licensing revenue all fell [CP-1 table; CP-1A operating model]. Management expects linear subscriber and advertising declines to continue, so the denominator carries more risk than the numerator.
2. Cash interest cover is thinner. Adjusted operating income covered cash interest paid 2.07x in the twelve months to June 30, 2026 against 2.63x in FY2025 [CP-1 C4]. Run-rate contractual coupon on the notes outstanding at June 30, 2026 is USD 156.0 million, which on the same earnings gives 2.01x [CP-1 C8]. On operating income plus depreciation and amortization with no add-backs, total debt is 12.66x earnings, but that basis includes a USD 97.8 million non-cash impairment and is shown only to expose the gap to the adjusted measure [CP-1 C7].
3. Liquidity is cash only. The company repaid the remaining Term Loan A and terminated the USD 175.0 million revolver on May 12, 2026, leaving cash of USD 464.0 million at June 30, 2026, of which about USD 136.5 million is held by foreign subsidiaries [CP-1 liquidity rows]. The unused USD 125.0 million receivables sale program is not a committed facility. This replaces a position where cash of USD 502.4 million was backed by USD 175.0 million undrawn at December 31, 2025.
4. The maturity profile is long but ordered against unsecured holders. After the March 2026 exchange, USD 420.5 million of 4.25% unsecured senior and convertible notes mature in February 2029 ahead of USD 1,315.1 million of 10.50% secured notes in July 2032 [CP-1 C11; CP-1A transactions]. Management states that cash and operating cash flow will not repay all debt at maturity and that it depends on capital markets access. Whether the 2032 indenture constrains refinancing the earlier notes is Insufficient Information.
5. Cash flow remains positive after capital spending, but is weaker. Free cash flow was USD 108.1 million in six months 2026 against USD 189.9 million in six months 2025, and USD 190.5 million over twelve months [CP-1 C5]. Debt principal has fallen by USD 633.8 million since December 31, 2024 [CP-1A calculations], largely through discounted repurchases, a tender and Term Loan A repayments.
6. Control and capital allocation matter to creditors. The Dolan family group owns all Class B stock and about 79% of voting power outside director elections, and the company bought back USD 30.0 million of stock in six months 2026 under an indenture amendment that allows up to USD 50.0 million of equity repurchases, with USD 87 million of board authorization remaining at June 30, 2026 [CP-1A governance]. Restricted payment capacity beyond the summarised amendment is Insufficient Information.
7. Two dated events sit after the balance sheet or before an unreported outcome. The Netflix licence of July 30, 2026 brings USD 500 million of fees, with management expecting about USD 25 million of cash in 2026 and about USD 100 million a year in 2027 to 2030; and a damages claim above USD 200 million had a trial set for October 27, 2026 with no outcome supplied [CP-1 subsequent events; CP-1A credit translations]. Neither is in June 30, 2026 balances, and both are limited by documents not supplied.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, the company has no debt maturing before February 2029 and its secured notes run to July 2032, after it exchanged USD 861.3 million of 2029 secured notes (tagged Documentary Fact; basis maturity rows and the 2026 transaction summary). Second, it generates cash: USD 190.5 million of free cash flow over twelve months to June 30, 2026 and cash of USD 464.0 million, with total debt principal USD 633.8 million lower than at December 31, 2024 (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 C5 and CP-1A calculations). Third, it owns a large share of its scripted content and franchises, and has licensed the Walking Dead Universe to Netflix for USD 500 million of fees over five years; the licence terms are Management Language and the contract is not supplied (basis CP-1A operating model and subsequent events). Fourth, its documented behaviour towards debt has been supportive: it repurchased and tendered senior notes at discounts in 2025 and repaid its Term Loan A (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1A governance table). Fifth, reporting quality is high, with an unqualified audit opinion and effective internal control over financial reporting for FY2025 (Documentary Fact).

Weaknesses. First, earnings are falling: Q2 2026 revenue was USD 547.5 million against USD 600.0 million and operating income USD 15.9 million against USD 64.5 million, while operating cost lines rose (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 table). Second, leverage and cover have weakened to 5.54x total and 4.06x net on adjusted operating income, and 2.07x cash interest cover (Analyst Interpretation of CP-1 C3 and C4; the adjusted measure adds back restructuring and share-based compensation). Third, there is no committed back-up facility, and the company states it depends on capital markets to refinance (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 liquidity rows and CP-1A refinancing translation). Fourth, the earlier maturity is the junior one: USD 420.5 million of unsecured notes fall due in February 2029 ahead of the secured notes (Analyst Interpretation). Fifth, revenue is exposed to a few distributors and to advertising and subscriber declines that management expects to continue, and a customer filed for Chapter 11 on June 30, 2026 with exposure not stated (Documentary Fact for the filing; Insufficient Information for the exposure). Sixth, controlled equity, USD 30.0 million of buybacks and USD 87 million of authorization remaining while earnings fall (Documentary Fact; the creditor effect is Analyst Interpretation). Seventh, the unresolved damages claim above USD 200 million against cash of USD 464.0 million (Documentary Fact; the outcome is Insufficient Information). Eighth, ranking and collateral for the 2032 notes cannot be settled because the filing describes the guarantees as unsecured while the notes are secured by a first-priority lien (Insufficient Information; no recovery conclusion is drawn).
