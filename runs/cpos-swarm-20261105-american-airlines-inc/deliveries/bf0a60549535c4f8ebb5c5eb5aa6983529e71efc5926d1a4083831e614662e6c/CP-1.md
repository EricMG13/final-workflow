### Financial base view

The financial base for American is a highly levered airline whose reported earnings fell in 2026 while its balance sheet was kept intact by refinancing. On the issuer's own statements, operating income for the nine months of 2026 was 535 against 1,042 a year earlier, with fuel expense up by 4,427 over the same nine months, and the third quarter alone produced a pre-tax loss of 183. Last-twelve-month EBITDA, derived here as operating income plus depreciation and amortization, is 2,916, against 3,395 in FY2025 and 4,573 in FY2024. Gross debt including finance leases is 25,876 and net debt after unrestricted cash and short-term investments is 17,623, so net leverage is 6.04x and cash interest cover is 2.06x, both weaker than FY2025. Liquidity is large at 11,763 but was built partly through 5,933 of new debt, and the filings put 7,861 of principal due in 2028 plus a very large set of purchase commitments behind it. Parent-level facts matter for this issuer: American guarantees three parent promissory notes and carries a net 10,430 receivable from related parties, mainly the parent. Confidence in the base is medium, because the metrics are calculated by this analysis from filed statements and several covenant and document inputs are not supplied.

### Earnings and cash flow quality

Evidence: American's revenue for the nine months of 2026 was 46,979 against 40,628, an increase of 6,351, while fuel expense rose from 8,017 to 12,444, an increase of 4,427, and operating income fell from 1,042 to 535. In the third quarter revenue was 16,334 against 13,688, fuel 4,635 against 2,767, and operating income 114 against 167. Mechanism: fuel absorbed about seven tenths of the nine-month revenue gain (4,427 of 6,351, calculated below), and other cost lines (salaries, wages and benefits 14,019 against 13,058) rose as well, so incremental revenue converted to very little operating profit. The filing states that the company does not hedge fuel (Q3 10-Q page:45, "Our current policy is not to enter into transactions to hedge our fuel consumption"), so the exposure is open. Implication: coverage is thin, and a further fuel increase flows straight to EBITDA, which is the denominator of both leverage and interest cover.

Operating cash flow of 4,440 for the nine months against 2,229 looks strong but has a specific source: the filing attributes the increase mainly to working capital increases in air traffic liability and loyalty program liability and to changes in net receivables from related parties (Q3 10-Q page:63). Air traffic liability rose from 7,158 to 9,414 and the total loyalty program liability from 10,564 to 11,514 over the nine months, which are seasonal and advance-sale balances that reverse, not earnings. In FY2025 operating cash flow was 1,930 after a 1,701 outflow for net receivables from related parties, so cash generated by American was in part passed to affiliates. Operating cash flow less capital expenditure and aircraft purchase deposits was negative 1,786 in FY2025, positive 1,780 in the nine months of 2026, and negative 132 on the last twelve months (calculated below). Capital expenditure is sizeable at 2,660 in the nine months and is set to continue, given 24,056 of aircraft and engine purchase commitments (Q3 10-Q page:66).

Net income for the nine months was a loss of 231, including nonoperating special items of 259 of which 78 is an impairment of the equity investment in Republic Holdings (Q3 10-Q page:28). The special-items line within operating income is small (29 for the nine months), so EBITDA excluding it is within 3 percent of EBITDA as derived, as shown below.

### Leverage and coverage

Gross leverage is calculated on balance-sheet debt and finance leases, because that is the only debt aggregate the issuer statements print as a sum of components; net leverage deducts cash and short-term investments (not restricted cash). On this basis total debt over LTM EBITDA is 8.87x at 2026-09-30, against 7.44x at 2025-12-31 and 5.63x at 2024-12-31, and net debt over EBITDA is 6.04x, against 5.73x and 4.10x. Net debt itself fell from 19,443 to 17,623 over the nine months because cash and short-term investments rose from 5,816 to 8,253 while gross debt rose from 25,259 to 25,876; the ratio nevertheless rose because LTM EBITDA fell from 3,395 to 2,916. Mechanism: the cash build was funded by 5,933 of new borrowing in the nine months, so net leverage improves in dollars but not in ratio and the cash carries interest cost. Cash interest cover (EBITDA over interest paid, net) is 2.06x LTM, against 2.19x in FY2025 and 2.53x in FY2024. Interest paid is the issuer's net figure and the filing does not say whether it nets capitalised interest or interest income, which limits the measure.

The ratios exclude operating lease liabilities (6,592 at 2026-09-30), pension and postretirement obligations (1,098), fuel financing (933) and the parent guarantees described below. Management's own AAG net debt of 28,079 and its target of 3.0x net debt to EBITDAR are parent-level, lease and pension inclusive, and are not comparable to the issuer ratios here (Q3 deck page:6 and page:10). No attempt is made to restate American on the management definition, because the deck's pension figure (92) differs from the issuer balance sheet line (1,098) and the definitions cannot be reconciled from the supplied text.

### Debt structure and maturity profile

American's debt note (Q3 10-Q page:30 to page:31) lists, at 2026-09-30, secured debt of 25,575 in principal: the 2013 term loan (960, due February 2028), the 2014 term loan (1,850, now due May 2033), the 2023 term loan (1,078, due June 2029), 7.25% senior secured notes (750, due February 2028), 5.75% senior secured notes (2,750, amortising to April 2029), the 2021 and 2025 AAdvantage term loans (2,247 due April 2028 and 988 due May 2032), enhanced equipment trust certificates (EETCs, 9,332, 2027 to 2039, fixed rates 2.88% to 7.15%), equipment loans and other notes (4,926, 2027 to 2038) and special facility revenue bonds (694, 2031 to 2036). The 8.50% senior secured notes (1,000 at 2025-12-31), the 5.50% AAdvantage notes (583) and the unsecured short-term term loan (629) were repaid in the nine months. Count check: the table lists 12 secured lines (two of them, the 8.50% and 5.50% notes, now nil) and one unsecured line (the short-term term loan, now nil). Note total 25,575 less unamortised discount and costs of 344 gives 25,231, less current maturities of 3,580 gives long-term debt of 21,651 (calculated); the balance-sheet lines add finance leases of 109 current and 536 noncurrent (calculated), so debt including finance leases is 25,876.

Security and guarantors as printed in the 10-K: AAG guarantees the credit facilities (10-K page:222), the 7.25% and 8.50% secured notes (page:223 to page:224) and the AAdvantage financing, which is also guaranteed by special purpose subsidiaries and secured on AAdvantage program assets (page:226 to page:227). The EETC equipment notes are direct obligations of American and in certain instances guaranteed by AAG (10-K page:189). Guarantor detail for the 2026 EETCs and equipment loans is not stated in the Q3 10-Q. The co-issuer of the AAdvantage loans is AAdvantage Loyalty IP Ltd. (Q3 10-Q page:33).

Maturity profile (principal, from the contractual obligations table for American, calculated shares in the next section): remainder of 2026, 631; 2027, 3,550; 2028, 7,861; 2029, 3,884; 2030, 972; 2031 and thereafter, 8,677; total 25,575. The 2028 bucket is the largest of the named years. Instruments due in 2028 carry face balances of 960 (2013 term loan, February), 750 (7.25% notes, February) and 2,247 (2021 AAdvantage term loan, April), together 3,957 (calculated); the remainder of the 2028 amount is scheduled amortisation of other instruments. The filing prints only the aggregate by year, so the full split is not available. The May 2026 amendment moved the 2014 term loans from January 2027 to May 2033 and removed that 2027 maturity (Q3 10-Q page:32). Contractual finance lease obligations total 834 undiscounted, shown separately in the filing and not in the principal profile.

### Liquidity and covenants

Liquidity at 2026-09-30 is 11,763 (cash 1,094, short-term investments 7,159, undrawn facilities 3,510), against 9,213 at 2025-12-31, 10,787 at 2026-03-31 and 11,266 at 2026-06-30. By facility, availability is 363 (2013 revolver, matures March 2031), 1,296 (2014 revolver, March 2031), 1,451 (2023 revolver, March 2031) and 400 of other facilities, comprising a revolving credit facility of up to 350 extended to March 2028 and 50 under a cargo receivables facility that expires in December 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:31 and page:32 to page:33). The filing states there are no amounts drawn under the other facilities; it does not print letters of credit outstanding at 2026-09-30, and the 10-K shows none outstanding under the 2013 and 2014 revolvers at 2025-12-31 (page:220 to page:222). Availability is taken as printed, with the date gap noted. Restricted cash and short-term investments of 555 are excluded (Q3 10-Q page:26). Escrowed proceeds of about 633 held for holders of the 2026-2 EETCs are not American's assets and are not in cash or debt (Q3 10-Q page:35).

Covenants as printed (substantially the same wording in the 10-K and the Q3 10-Q): a significant portion of debt agreements require at least 2.0 billion of unrestricted cash and cash equivalents plus amounts available under revolving credit facilities; secured debt carries loan-to-value or collateral coverage tests, with consequences of additional collateral, cash withholding, prepayment or higher interest; the AAdvantage financing has a peak debt service coverage test that can trigger early repayment; the 7.25% notes pay 2.00% extra interest if the collateral coverage ratio is below 1.6 to 1.0 (10-K page:224). Management states compliance at the latest measurement dates. Headroom on the liquidity covenant cannot be measured exactly, because the covenant's definition of qualifying cash is not supplied. A conservative proxy using cash of 1,094 plus the three revolvers (3,110) gives 4,204, which is 2,204 above 2,000 (calculated below); it excludes short-term investments and the 400 of other facilities. The reported measured values of the collateral coverage and debt service coverage tests are Not supplied. The definition of these tests differs from the cash-based leverage and cover metrics computed here, which are not covenant measures.

Operating lease expense is not added back, so this is not EBITDAR.

### Debt and net debt
