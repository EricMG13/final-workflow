### Business and transaction view

American is the main operating airline of AAG, a listed holding company, and it is the issuer whose creditors this review serves. The business is a hub-based network carrier whose revenue is overwhelmingly passenger, with a loyalty program and co-branded card relationships that provide cash and collateral, and whose cost base is dominated by fuel, labor and regional capacity purchased from carriers under the parent. There is no financial sponsor, no pending acquisition and no dividend recapitalisation in the sources; the transaction record for the period is a sequence of refinancings, aircraft financings and facility amendments, plus a small set of aircraft purchase decisions in October 2026 after quarter end. The governance picture is Mixed rather than Supportive or Adverse: the parent guarantees a large share of American's debt and American guarantees parent notes and funds the parent through intercompany balances, both documented actions, while American has also repaid its own secured debt early. The main creditor consequences are structural (support running in both directions, secured debt ranking ahead of unsecured claims and collateral that includes the loyalty business) and operational (open fuel exposure and heavy fleet commitments). Confidence is medium because executed documents, ratings and any information after the Q3 10-Q are not supplied.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No acquisition, sponsor transaction, dividend recapitalisation or change of control involving American is in the sources, and the context lists no deal terms, so that transaction fact is Insufficient Information for any such event and nothing is inferred. The financing record is executed and closed as reported in the Q3 2026 Form 10-Q (unaudited), covering the first nine months of 2026, and is American's own activity, with the parent a co-party to the credit agreements and a guarantor.

New debt in the period: American issued enhanced equipment trust certificates in several series. In March 2026 it borrowed 870 million of junior financing secured on aircraft already delivered (the 2026-1 Class (B)R financing, fixed rate 5.61 percent, maturing 2028 to 2029). In May 2026 it created the 2026-1 pass-through trusts (about 1.1 billion face, 32 aircraft). In June 2026 it issued 567 million of engine EETCs to refinance 228 million at maturity and raise 339 million of new borrowing. In August 2026 it created the 2026-2 trusts (about 1.3 billion face, 37 aircraft), of which about 692 million had been used to buy equipment notes at 2026-09-30 and about 633 million was held in escrow for holders, not reported as American's debt. It also borrowed 705 million of variable-rate equipment loans on aircraft. The use of proceeds is aircraft financing, and the filing states that proceeds of the nine months totalled about 4.1 billion for EETCs, equipment loans and other notes (page:46). 

Refinancing and liability management: in May 2026 American refinanced the 2014 term loans of about 1.1 billion due January 2027 and added 703 million of incremental term loans, maturing May 2033 at SOFR plus 3.00 percent; in the first quarter it re-established its three revolving facilities to March 2031 at about 3.1 billion in aggregate; the 2025 AAdvantage term loan was repriced to SOFR plus 2.75 percent. American prepaid the 8.50 percent senior secured notes of 1.0 billion, repaid a 629 million short-term term loan and prepaid 310 million of EETC equipment notes. Liquidity effect: total available liquidity rose to 11,763 at 2026-09-30 from 9,213 at 2025-12-31 (US$ millions, from the financial base), partly funded by the new borrowing. Credit effect: the maturity wall in 2027 eased (the 2014 term loans moved from 2027 to 2033), but the added EETC and equipment debt is secured on aircraft and adds to claims ahead of unsecured creditors; these EETC notes are direct obligations of American and in certain instances guaranteed by AAG (10-K page:189).

Subsequent events after the quarter end are limited to what the Q3 10-Q reports: purchase options on five Airbus A320 Family aircraft exercised in October 2026, a commitment to purchase two Boeing 777-200ER aircraft off-lease in the fourth quarter of 2026, and an October 2026 opposition filed in the Northeast Alliance antitrust case against AAG and JetBlue. Nothing dated after 2026-10-22 is supplied. The terms of any debt issued after the Q3 10-Q, and of the 2026-2 equipment notes still to be issued against the escrow, are Not supplied.
