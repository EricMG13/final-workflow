### Credit view

American is a highly leveraged airline whose credit rests on liquidity and asset-backed funding rather than on current earnings. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 11,763 million at 2026-09-30, with maturities extended during 2026 and recurring loyalty and card cash. The weakest point is earnings: operating income for the nine months of 2026 was USD 535 million against USD 1,042 million a year earlier, because unhedged fuel rose faster than revenue, leaving net debt at 6.04x last-twelve-month EBITDA and cash interest cover at 2.06x. The outcome will be decided by the fuel price and demand path, by access to financing for USD 24,056 million of aircraft and engine commitments, and by how the parent's support and intercompany claims behave. Confidence is medium.

### Key findings

Earnings have not kept up with fuel. For the nine months of 2026, American's revenue was USD 46,979 million against USD 40,628 million, while aircraft fuel and related taxes were USD 12,444 million against USD 8,017 million, and no fuel hedges were outstanding at 2026-09-30 [financial base: Earnings and cash flow quality; business context: Fuel hedging row, Documentary Fact]. Because fuel absorbed about seven tenths of the revenue gain, operating income fell to USD 535 million from USD 1,042 million. Fuel is the most volatile cost line and the exposure is open, so a further price rise lands directly on EBITDA, the denominator of every credit metric. This is a Documentary Fact for the figures and an Analyst Interpretation for the sensitivity.

Leverage is high and moved the wrong way on the ratio even as net debt fell. Net debt was USD 17,623 million at 2026-09-30, down from USD 19,443 million at 2025-12-31, yet net debt over EBITDA rose to 6.04x from 5.73x because last-twelve-month EBITDA fell to USD 2,916 million from USD 3,395 million; total debt of USD 25,876 million is 8.87x EBITDA [financial base: Leverage and coverage]. Cash interest cover is 2.06x, against 2.19x for FY2025 and 2.53x for FY2024. With cover near twice, small declines in EBITDA or increases in floating-rate interest have an outsized effect on debt service capacity. These ratios exclude operating leases (USD 6,592 million), pensions (USD 1,098 million) and fuel financing (USD 933 million), so the true burden is higher; EBITDA is derived by the analysis and not reported (Analyst Interpretation).

Liquidity is the main support but is partly borrowed, and the maturity profile is lumpy. Liquidity was USD 11,763 million, made up of cash and short-term investments of USD 8,253 million and undrawn facilities of USD 3,510 million, up from USD 9,213 million at 2025-12-31, while USD 5,933 million of debt was issued in the nine months [financial base: Liquidity and covenants]. Principal due in 2028 is USD 7,861 million, the largest named year, with USD 631 million in the remainder of 2026 and USD 3,550 million in 2027. Liquidity comfortably covers the USD 4,181 million of principal due in the remainder of 2026 and 2027, but the 2028 concentration requires market access or cash accumulation; the 2014 term loan extension to 2033 shows that access exists at present. A cargo receivables facility of USD 50 million expires in December 2026, which is minor.

Capital commitments and cash conversion limit free cash. Operating cash flow less capital expenditure was negative USD 132 million for the last twelve months, even though nine-month operating cash flow was USD 4,440 million, because the nine-month figure was lifted by seasonal growth in air traffic and loyalty liabilities [financial base: Earnings and cash flow quality]. Aircraft and engine purchase commitments total USD 24,056 million, with regional capacity commitments of USD 4,461 million and other purchase obligations of USD 19,612 million [financial base: Claims outside the debt figure]. The filing intends to fund deliveries with cash and long-term financing, so these commitments are a standing call on the market access that the liquidity position supports.

The parent relationship is a two-way credit link. AAG guarantees about USD 14.1 billion of American's debt, American guarantees AAG's USD 3,746 million of promissory notes, and American carries a net USD 10,430 million related-party receivable, mainly owed by AAG, which is not counted as liquidity [business context: Ownership, governance and behaviour, Documentary Fact rows]. American's 2025 borrowing was in part used to repay a parent convertible note. The mechanism is that value and support move between issuer and parent, so American's creditors are exposed to AAG's liquidity and to the performance of its intercompany balance, while the guarantee gives secured creditors a second obligor with no AAG collateral behind it. Governance behaviour is Mixed (Analyst Interpretation).

Revenue durability is supported by the loyalty business. Co-branded card and other partner cash was USD 6.2 billion in 2025 at group level, with Citi the exclusive U.S. issuer from 2026 [business context: Loyalty and cards row, Documentary Fact]. This recurring cash supports debt service and also secures the AAdvantage financing, so it is both a strength for revenue visibility and a pledged asset that limits what is free for other creditors. The sources give no customer concentration beyond the card partners, which is an Insufficient Information item.

The capital structure is heavily secured. The debt is mostly secured on aircraft, engines, route and gate rights and the loyalty program, and secured instruments carry loan-to-value or collateral coverage tests that can force collateral, cash retention or prepayment [financial base: Debt structure and Liquidity and covenants]. Management states compliance, but covenant values and executed documents are not supplied, so headroom is not calculable beyond a conservative liquidity proxy of USD 4,204 million against a USD 2,000 million test. The mechanic is that unsecured and junior claims sit behind a large asset-backed claim base; the sources do not allow an assessment of collateral values or outcomes.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity is large: USD 11,763 million at 2026-09-30, including USD 8,253 million of cash and short-term investments and USD 3,510 million of undrawn facilities, with revolver maturities extended to March 2031 (Documentary Fact; basis: financial base figures). Maturities were pushed out in 2026: the 2014 term loans moved from 2027 to 2033 and the 8.50 percent notes of USD 1.0 billion were prepaid, which reduced near-term refinancing pressure (Documentary Fact). Loyalty and co-branded card cash is recurring and contractual, which supports revenue visibility (Documentary Fact, with the management claim about premium revenue carried only as Management Language and not adopted). Net debt fell by USD 1,820 million to USD 17,623 million over the nine months to 2026-09-30 (Analyst Interpretation, calculated from the financial base net debt figures; the fall reflects cash built from borrowing). A network of nine hubs and a large alliance structure support demand (Documentary Fact).

Weaknesses. Earnings are thin: operating income for the nine months of 2026 was USD 535 million, and last-twelve-month EBITDA of USD 2,916 million supports leverage of 6.04x net and 8.87x gross (Documentary Fact for figures; Analyst Interpretation for the derived ratios). Interest cover is 2.06x on cash interest, leaving limited cushion (Analyst Interpretation). Fuel is fully unhedged and has risen sharply (Documentary Fact). The 2028 maturity of USD 7,861 million and the USD 24,056 million of aircraft commitments create concentrated future financing needs (Documentary Fact). The parent link adds guarantee exposure of USD 3,746 million and an intercompany receivable of USD 10,430 million outside liquidity (Documentary Fact). Operating cash flow less capital expenditure was negative USD 132 million over the last twelve months (Analyst Interpretation). The secured capital structure with collateral tests places unsecured claims behind an asset-backed claim base, and the executed documents that would define priority are not supplied (Insufficient Information).

Thesis (Analyst Interpretation): American's credit rests on a large, partly borrowed liquidity position and asset-backed access to financing that offset thin, fuel-sensitive earnings and high leverage, so the credit depends on fuel prices, demand and continued market access.
