### Business and transaction view

Amentum is a global advanced engineering and technology solutions provider to U.S. and allied governments, organised in two segments (Digital Solutions and Global Engineering Solutions). It exists in its present form since September 27, 2024, when the Jacobs Critical Mission Solutions business and parts of the Jacobs Divergent Solutions business were spun off and merged with the legacy Amentum business in a Reverse Morris Trust. Creditors therefore lend to a company that has existed as a combined entity for about two years, that carries purchase-accounting intangibles and goodwill (7,378 of 11,199 of total assets at July 3, 2026), and whose pre-merger history does not include the larger business.

The operating model is low-capital and contract-based. Roughly four-fifths of revenue is from the U.S. Government, mostly as prime contractor, with about 56% of 9M 2026 revenue on cost-plus-fee contracts that pass through inflation and a rising fixed-price share. Visibility is large in headline terms (backlog 48.2 billion) but only 6.2 billion is funded, so revenue durability depends on appropriations and option exercise rather than signed funding. The principal operating risks the filings describe are budget timing (shutdowns from October 2, 2025 to November 12, 2025 and a partial shutdown from January 31, 2026 to February 3, 2026), contract compliance and audit exposure, joint venture structure changes that reduce consolidated revenue while moving profit into equity earnings, and estimate catch-ups on fixed-price work.

Financial policy so far is creditor-friendly: no dividends, no share repurchases in FY2025, debt prepayments from divestiture proceeds and cash, and a stated priority of reducing debt. The weaknesses for a creditor are in control and documentation rather than behaviour: the sponsor-linked stockholder keeps board nomination rights tied to its ownership, the tax-free merger structure restricts some strategic actions, and the credit agreement, its April 2026 amendment, the indenture and the tax matters agreement are not supplied, so capacity to pay out cash, incur debt or release collateral cannot be tested. Evidence on creditor-adverse behaviour (priming, uptiering, drop-downs) is neither found nor testable.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The defining transaction closed on September 27, 2024. Legacy Amentum (Amentum Parent Holdings LLC, owned by Amentum Joint Venture LP) merged with the spin-off of the Jacobs Critical Mission Solutions business and portions of the Jacobs Divergent Solutions business in a tax-efficient Reverse Morris Trust. Amentum Parent Holdings LLC was the accounting acquirer and is the predecessor, so statements before September 27, 2024 exclude the acquired business and are not comparable (10-K page:117, page:128). Before the spin-off the acquired business reorganised under a new company, distributed a cash payment of about 911 million to Jacobs, and Jacobs distributed about 80.95% of its shares to Jacobs shareholders (10-K page:6 to page:7, page:128). Immediately after closing there were about 243 million shares outstanding, held 58.5% by Jacobs and its shareholders and 37.0% by Amentum Joint Venture LP, with 4.5% placed in escrow; in March 2025 the escrow was released, 3.5% to the former Jacobs holders and 1.0% to the sponsor stockholder (10-K page:128). Consideration transferred was 3,948 and total consideration 4,032 including a previously held joint venture interest, and goodwill was recognised at 3,004 on final allocation (10-K page:129 to page:131). Status: completed, with purchase accounting finalised in FY2025.

New debt at the Transaction: a seven-year term facility of 3,750 and a five-year revolver of 850 under a credit agreement dated September 27, 2024 with JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. as administrative agent, plus 1,000 of 7.250% senior notes due August 1, 2032 issued August 13, 2024 into escrow and released at closing. Proceeds of the credit facility were used together with other cash sources to repay in full the prior first lien and second lien credit agreements and to pay related fees and expenses (10-K page:145, page:149). The filing does not state a sources-and-uses split, so how much of the borrowing funded the 911 payment to Jacobs is Not supplied. An equity contribution of 235 from the legacy equityholder immediately before the Transaction is disclosed (10-K page:153).

Later debt events. In FY2025 the company made voluntary term loan payments of about 191 (June 27, 2025), 250 (July 31, 2025) and 281 (September 30, 2025), together 722, after selling the Rapid Solutions hardware and product business, part of Digital Solutions, to Lockheed Martin Corporation on June 26, 2025 for 360 in cash (10-K page:98, page:105, page:132, page:145). On April 24, 2026 a first amendment to the credit facility (executed; the amendment itself is not in the pack) created a 1,400 term loan A due April 24, 2031, amended the existing term loan into a 1,591 term loan B due September 27, 2031 on lower margins, and raised the revolver by 150 to 1,000 with maturity April 24, 2031 (Q2 10-Q page:17; Q3 10-Q page:11 to page:12). The Q2 2026 release headline describes the term loan A proceeds as used to repay and reprice term loan B (management language, Q2 release page:1). A further voluntary 125 payment on term loan B was made on June 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:12). Subsequent to the quarter end, only the August 10, 2026 earnings release (updated FY2026 guidance, with revenue lowered and Adjusted EBITDA low end raised) and the August 11, 2026 filing are in the pack; no later debt transaction is evidenced up to the cutoff. Sponsor: the filing refers to the equityholder and its affiliates as private equity firms (10-K page:70) but the context names no sponsor and the consulting agreement partners American Securities LLC and Lindsay Goldberg LLC are named only as former advisers (10-K page:153); this handoff does not infer more.

### Capital allocation, acquisitions and disclosure

Capital allocation has prioritised debt: cash from the Rapid Solutions sale and operations funded 722 of voluntary term loan payments in FY2025, and a further 125 was paid in FY2026, with no dividend or buyback (see above). The leverage effect is in the financial handoff (net leverage 3.23x at October 3, 2025 and 3.03x at July 3, 2026). The business strategy states reliance in part on acquisitions and strategic investments to sustain growth, as well as targeted divestitures (10-K page:23 risk factor list); one acquisition-related cash outflow (70, the working capital settlement for the Transaction) and one divestiture occurred in FY2025 (10-K page:116, page:130), and 9M 2026 shows no acquisition spend (Q3 10-Q page:4). No acquisition appetite is inferred from these. Contributions to equity method investments were 53 in 9M 2026 against 36 in 9M 2025 (Q3 10-Q page:4), a use of cash that supports the joint venture strategy. Disclosure quality is high for the Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation, net leverage and segment data, but monitoring lacks: the current debt schedule (no maturity table at July 3, 2026), the credit agreement covenant certificate and definitions, and an add-back bridge to the covenant definition.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: 81% of revenue from direct U.S. Government contracts, backlog of 48.2 billion with 6.2 billion funded (10-K page:8; Q3 10-Q page:27). Risk mechanic: customer credit risk is minimal, but appropriations delays, continuing resolutions and shutdowns defer awards, billing and cash collection; unfunded backlog converts only as funding arrives. Credit implication: debt service risk comes from timing and liquidity, not demand collapse; a liquidity cushion of 1,376 at July 3, 2026 (financial handoff) is the relevant mitigant. Counter: the rating-statement conflict does not affect this.
2. Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: fixed-price share rose to 29% of 9M 2026 revenue from 24% in FY2025, and net favorable estimate adjustments of 83 against 40 (Q3 10-Q page:8, page:27). Risk mechanic: fixed-price work carries cost overrun risk and catch-up adjustments move reported profit between periods. Credit implication: Adjusted EBITDA of 1,128 on a trailing basis may include non-recurring catch-up benefit; leverage of 3.44x gross and 3.03x net is more sensitive to margin slippage than the headline suggests. Counter: cost-plus work remains 56% of revenue.
3. Concentration. Evidence: Department of War and Intelligence Community 5,334 of 10,205 in 9M 2026, and the U.S. Government at 80% in total (Q3 10-Q page:7). Risk mechanic: shifts in defense or intelligence budgets, contract recompetes, or protests concentrate effects. Credit implication: single-sovereign dependence limits diversification; debarment or a large program loss is a tail risk with the greatest effect on enterprise value and on a collateral package secured by substantially all assets.
4. Sponsor alignment and creditor control. Evidence: no dividends and repurchases, voluntary prepayments, board nomination rights linked to ownership, tax-free merger restrictions (10-K page:24, page:71, page:93). Risk mechanic: a holder with board influence could favour distributions or recapitalisations when leverage falls; the restricted-payment capacity in the credit agreement is not visible. Credit implication: current behaviour supports deleveraging, but the sources do not allow a view on whether the documents would stop a distribution; monitoring should track holder changes and the amended credit agreement.
5. Key uncertainties. Evidence: no credit agreement, amendment, indenture or tax matters agreement in the pack; no ratings report; current holdings unknown. Risk mechanic: covenant, priming and payout analysis cannot be performed. Credit implication: conclusions on creditor control are provisional.
