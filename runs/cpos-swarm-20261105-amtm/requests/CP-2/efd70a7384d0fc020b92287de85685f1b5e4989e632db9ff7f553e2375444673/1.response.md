### Credit view

Amentum Holdings, Inc. has an adequate and improving credit profile with sound liquidity, but its leverage, thin cash conversion and untested loan documents leave limited room for error. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 1,376 million, net leverage of 3.03x (3.23x at FY2025) and cash interest cover of 3.93x, backed by revenue that is 81% U.S. Government and a backlog of USD 48.2 billion. The weakest point is cash generation: nine-month free cash flow of USD 213 million falls to USD 93 million without a USD 120 million receivable-sales inflow, and guidance implies fourth-quarter free cash flow of USD 312 million to USD 362 million. The outcome turns on whether that cash arrives, on the expiry of USD 1.3 billion of interest rate swaps by January 31, 2027, and on credit agreement terms the sources do not supply.

### Assessment and thesis

Fundamental credit view (Analyst Interpretation, confidence Medium). Basis: financial handoff (leverage, cover, liquidity, cash flow tables) and business handoff (customer, backlog, contract mix, financial policy rows). Business risk is moderate: the customer is largely the U.S. Government, which removes customer credit loss but exposes revenue timing to appropriations, and the contract mix is moving toward fixed price. Financial risk is moderate: net leverage of 3.03x and gross leverage of 3.44x on trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,128 million, a floating-rate structure, and cash conversion that depends on receivable sales and on catch-up adjustments. Liquidity is a strength. Confidence is Medium rather than High because the credit agreement, the first amendment, the indenture, ratings and current ownership are not supplied, and because the business handoff judged this synthesis as ready with limitations.

Thesis (Analyst Interpretation): Amentum Holdings, Inc. is a de-levering, liquid government contractor whose debt service rests on timely federal funding and on cash conversion that has yet to match its reported earnings, with creditor protections that the available evidence does not allow us to test.

### Key findings

First, liquidity and refinancing risk are low in the near term. The group holds cash of USD 459 million and an undrawn revolver of USD 917 million, a total of USD 1,376 million (Documentary Fact; financial handoff liquidity table). The April 24, 2026 amendment moved the revolver and the new term loan A out to April 24, 2031 and repriced term loan B (Documentary Fact; business handoff, new debt events). Scheduled principal over the next twelve months is about USD 50.9 million on the indicative schedule, small against liquidity. The indicative profile is back-ended: about 92% of scheduled principal falls in the years ending September 2031 and 2032, which defers refinancing risk but concentrates it.

Second, leverage is moderate and falling, but its quality depends on a management measure. Net leverage was 3.03x and gross leverage 3.44x at July 3, 2026, against 3.23x and 3.63x at FY2025 (Documentary Fact; financial handoff leverage table). On EBITDA before management adjustments, net leverage is 3.37x (financial handoff). Add-backs of USD 76 million in the nine months and equity earnings equal to 7.8% of trailing Adjusted EBITDA sit inside the denominator, and the trailing period spans 53 weeks (financial handoff gaps). The improvement from 3.23x is therefore real but flatters slightly.

Third, cash conversion lags reported earnings. Nine-month operating cash flow was USD 235 million against USD 273 million a year earlier, and free cash flow of USD 213 million includes a net USD 120 million inflow from selling receivables (Documentary Fact; financial handoff). The guidance ranges imply fourth-quarter free cash flow of USD 312 million to USD 362 million against USD 261 million in the prior-year fourth quarter, which had 14 weeks (financial handoff calculation 14; Management Language for the guidance itself). The fourth quarter is therefore the test of whether conversion improves.

Fourth, business risk is anchored by the customer but not by funding. About 81% of FY2025 revenue came from direct U.S. Government contracts and backlog is USD 48.2 billion, but funded backlog is USD 6.2 billion (Documentary Fact; business handoff, customers and visibility rows). Shutdowns in the first half of FY2026 delayed awards and reduced revenue. Revenue fell 2.5% in the nine months and management lowered its FY2026 revenue range while raising the low end of its Adjusted EBITDA range, so margin gains, partly from favorable contract estimate adjustments of USD 83 million against USD 40 million, are carrying earnings (Analyst Interpretation; business handoff, estimate catch-ups row).

Fifth, creditor-control conclusions cannot be drawn. The credit agreement, its amendment, the indenture and the tax matters agreement are not supplied, the amended covenant level is unknown (the only printed level is the pre-amendment 5.00x first lien net leverage maximum, against an indicative 2.13x), and current holdings of the sponsor-linked stockholder, who holds board nomination rights, are not disclosed (Insufficient Information).

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity (Documentary Fact; financial handoff): USD 1,376 million of liquidity with no drawings on the revolver and the first committed maturity in April 2031. Deleveraging record (Documentary Fact; financial and business handoffs): total debt fell from USD 4,008 million to USD 3,875 million, net leverage from 3.23x to 3.03x, with voluntary term loan payments of USD 722 million in FY2025 and USD 125 million in June 2026 and no dividends or buybacks. Customer quality and visibility (Documentary Fact; business handoff): 81% of FY2025 revenue from U.S. Government contracts, 91% prime contractor revenue in the nine months, backlog of USD 48.2 billion and remaining performance obligations of USD 8.9 billion, of which about 79% fall within twelve months. Asset-light, cost-plus-weighted model (Documentary Fact; both handoffs): 56% of nine-month revenue on cost-plus contracts, which limits inflation risk, and capital spending of USD 22 million in the nine months, about 0.2% of revenue. Lower interest burden (Documentary Fact; financial handoff): cash interest cover of 3.93x against 3.61x, helped by the April 2026 repricing of term loan B to a 1.75% margin.

Weaknesses. Cash conversion (Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation; financial handoff): free cash flow of USD 93 million in the nine months excluding receivable sales, against a reported USD 213 million; the programme has a maximum of USD 400 million and USD 300 million was sold and outstanding. Earnings quality (Analyst Interpretation; both handoffs): Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,128 million against USD 1,013 million before adjustments; equity earnings inside the measure; a 53-week trailing period; and a rise of USD 43 million in net favorable estimate adjustments that exceeds the USD 24 million rise in nine-month Adjusted EBITDA, which suggests underlying earnings growth was smaller, although the filing does not give the EBITDA effect. Interest-rate exposure (Documentary Fact; financial handoff): about 74% of debt is floating rate, hedged by swaps of USD 1.3 billion that mature through January 31, 2027, and the company puts the nine-month sensitivity at about USD 23 million per one percentage point. Balance sheet composition (Documentary Fact; financial handoff): goodwill and intangibles of USD 7,378 million are 66% of total assets, so tangible equity is negative USD 2,589 million, and creditor value rests on contract cash flows. Revenue and funding conditionality (Documentary Fact; business handoff): funded backlog is 12.9% of total backlog, revenue declined 2.5% in the nine months, and fixed-price work rose to 29% of revenue from 24%. Documentation and control (Insufficient Information; business handoff): no loan documents, ratings or current ownership, so payout capacity, collateral release and covenant headroom cannot be tested; the tax-free merger structure also limits some strategic actions.
