### Business and transaction view

APi Group Corporation is a US-listed holding company whose subsidiaries deliver fire and life safety, security, elevator and escalator, and specialty contracting, fabrication and infrastructure services, reported as two segments, Safety Services and Specialty Services. Safety Services, which is mostly contracted inspection, service and monitoring plus installation, carried FY2025 revenue of 5,456, with Specialty Services at 2,460, and about two thirds of revenue is United States based. The creditor-relevant strengths are documented diversification (no customer above 5% of 2025 net revenues), statutory and insurance-driven inspection demand described by management, a cost base the company describes as largely variable, and capital spending that management puts below 1.5% of net revenues. The weaknesses are equally documented: Specialty Services and part of Safety Services are project-driven, fixed-price contract cost estimation is a critical audit matter, gross margin fell in Q3 2026 on mix and input costs, margins and cash flow are seasonal (net revenues are typically lower in the first and second quarters), and the share of revenue that is truly recurring is not quantified in any supplied source. The decisive change for a creditor is the 2026 acquisition programme: CertaSite (February), Onyx-Fire (June) and WTech (July) closed for a combined 1,197, funded by a 500 million 5.750% senior note issue in May, a larger and extended revolver, revolver draws and a fall in cash, so that last-twelve-months total debt over adjusted EBITDA is 3.02x and net 2.71x (CP-1, derived), against 2.67x and 1.79x at FY2025. Management pairs the acquisitions with continued share repurchases (252 in nine months and about 143 after quarter-end) and a Series A Preferred Stock that converts into 6,000,000 common shares on the last day of 2026. No priming, uptier, drop-down, collateral release or debt-funded dividend recapitalisation is documented in the supplied sources; the 2024 conversion and repurchase of the Series B Preferred Stock was part debt-funded and is the closest precedent for shareholder-return financing. These statements are limited by the absence of the executed financing documents, which would show what the issuer is permitted to do.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability (Analyst Interpretation). Evidence: contracted inspection and service demand described as statutory and insurance-driven ((10-K_2025-12-31_apg-20251231.htm.jsonl page:10: "Inspections are often required by legislation or insurance mandates, providing a strong recurring revenue stream")), no customer above 5%, remaining performance obligations of 4,863. Risk mechanic: demand for inspection and service work is less discretionary, supporting steady cash flow, but project and fixed-price work in both segments exposes margin to cost overruns and mix, and contract terms are mostly under six months. Credit implication: supports debt service capacity in a downturn better than a pure contractor would, but durability cannot be sized without the recurring share, which is not disclosed. Counter-evidence: Q3 2026 gross margin fell year on year (CP-1) and the audit-matter on fixed-price estimates.
2. Acquisition-driven leverage (Analyst Interpretation). Evidence: 1,356 of acquisition spend in nine months 2026 (CP-1) and last-twelve-months total debt over adjusted EBITDA of 3.02x and net 2.71x (CP-1, derived). Risk mechanic: purchase prices are fully in debt and cash while acquired EBITDA enters the trailing figure only from closing dates, so reported leverage overstates run-rate leverage but also depends on unproven integration. Credit implication: deleveraging depends on organic growth and integration rather than debt paydown, because buybacks continue; refinancing risk is limited by 2029 and later maturities. Counter-evidence: the 2025 acquisition consideration totals differ across filings (conflict 1), showing purchase accounting can move.
