### Business and transaction view

ATS is a long-established, publicly listed Canadian builder of automated manufacturing and assembly systems, with products and services spread over four end markets and a global footprint. For a creditor the central business facts are visibility and cyclicality. Order Backlog of 2,009 at September 27, 2026 and a recurring revenue share that management expects at 25 to 35 percent of trailing twelve month revenue give some protection, but customer capital decisions, the timing of large life sciences programs, tariffs and a fall in revenue have moved earnings down, while management has said it will not meet its prior FY2027 growth and margin goals. The company is in the middle of a restructuring: a transportation reorganization that is repositioning a former end market, a services reorganization, and a multi-phase Fixed Cost Transformation Program with facility closures and a workforce reduction of 300 to 400. These actions are meant to lift margins toward a 15 percent long-term target but cost cash first. There is no leveraged buyout, sponsor or acquisition financing in the sources: the debt arose from earlier note issues and a revolving credit facility amended in December 2025, and the only transaction events in the period are facility sales, share repurchases and restructuring. Ownership is dispersed public equity as far as the pack shows, though the annual information form that would confirm holders is missing. Governance signals are mixed: a new CEO and an interim CFO, a reported material weakness in internal controls, and share buybacks while leverage is above target, set against deleveraging during FY2026. Customer concentration is named as a risk factor but never quantified.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no leveraged buyout, merger, sponsor transaction or acquisition financing in the pack, and the context carries no deal terms. The sources describe corporate actions rather than a single transaction. On debt, the company amended its senior secured credit facility on December 4, 2025, extending maturity to December 4, 2029, with a revolving line of 900.0 and a fully drawn term facility of 150.0, and it incurred transaction costs of 2.6; the facility was utilized at 190.0 at September 27, 2026. The Canadian dollar senior notes of 600.0 at 6.50 percent due August 21, 2032 were placed in two tranches in August and December 2024, and the U.S. dollar senior notes of US$350.0 at 4.125 percent are due December 15, 2028. Neither note series was issued in the period covered by the current or prior fiscal year, and no new debt issue is disclosed in FY2026 or the first half of FY2027 other than draws of 85.0 in FY2026 and 40.0 in Q2 FY2027 reported as proceeds from long-term debt (financial base module). On equity, the company announced a normal course issuer bid in December 2025 allowing purchase of up to 8,225,621 shares, and bought 2,244,461 shares for 61.3 in Q2 FY2027. On assets, two U.S. facilities and one German facility are held for sale as part of the transportation reorganization; after quarter end the company completed the sale of the two U.S. facilities for 71.2 with an estimated gain of about 12.2, with one leased back, to be recognized in Q3 FY2027, and the proceeds are expected to fund restructuring. On operations, the Fixed Cost Transformation Program was announced with Q1 FY2027 results on August 6, 2026. The earlier business acquisitions in FY2025, the companies Heidolph and Paxiom, are named as contributors to growth, but their dates, prices and financing are not in the pack. The legal terms of the notes and credit facility, such as covenant levels and baskets that would govern capacity for dividends, debt incurrence or asset disposals, are described only in general terms because the executed documents are absent.
