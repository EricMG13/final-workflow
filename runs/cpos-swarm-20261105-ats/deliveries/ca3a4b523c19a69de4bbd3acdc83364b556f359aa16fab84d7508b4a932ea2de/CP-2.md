### Credit view

ATS Corporation is a moderately leveraged, well-funded industrial automation supplier whose earnings are weakening and whose leverage has moved above its own target. The strongest support is liquidity: C$ 231.0 million of cash and C$ 966.7 million of unutilized credit, a revolver running to December 2029, and a first large principal maturity only in December 2028, alongside four end markets and a C$ 2,009 million backlog. The weakest point is the trend: trailing pro forma adjusted EBITDA of C$ 381.3 million is down from C$ 413.0 million, net debt to EBITDA is 3.1x against a 2.0x to 3.0x target, and cash generation is lumpy. The outcome turns on whether the Fixed Cost Transformation Program savings arrive and backlog converts before further buybacks or working capital absorb cash, and on covenant terms nobody outside the company can see in this pack.

### Credit assessment and thesis

Category: Analyst Interpretation. Confidence: Medium, held below High because the business context was ready only with limitations and the covenant, ownership and guarantor evidence is missing. Basis: financial base leverage, liquidity and cash flow rows; business context profile, operating model, governance and credit translation rows.

Thesis: ATS Corporation can service and refinance its debt from a large liquidity cushion and a diversified backlog, but its credit quality depends on stabilizing earnings and returning leverage to range, which the current evidence does not yet show.

### Key findings

1. Leverage is above target and rising (Documentary Fact). Net debt to pro forma adjusted EBITDA was 3.1x at September 27, 2026 against 2.8x at March 31, 2026 and 3.0x at December 28, 2025, with the company's own range at 2.0x to 3.0x [financial base, leverage chart and finding on leverage]. Net debt rose by C$ 41.0 million to C$ 1,192.2 million while trailing EBITDA fell by C$ 31.7 million, partly because ATS spent C$ 61.3 million on share repurchases; excluding them management puts leverage at 3.0x. Management expects to stay moderately above the range for the rest of FY2027 [business context, leverage expectation conflict]. On the same figures, net debt of C$ 48.3 million less, or EBITDA of C$ 16.1 million more, would return leverage to 3.0x (calculated here, see Calculations). The pending C$ 71.2 million sale of two U.S. facilities is earmarked by management for restructuring costs, so it should not be assumed to reduce net debt.

2. Liquidity is strong and the maturity wall is distant (Documentary Fact, with Analyst Interpretation on the risk). ATS holds C$ 231.0 million of cash and C$ 966.7 million of unutilized credit, giving C$ 1,197.7 million. The secured revolver of C$ 900.0 million matures December 4, 2029, and letters of credit of about C$ 274.4 million sit on separate facilities and do not reduce it [financial base, liquidity build]. The first large principal maturity on disclosed instruments is the US$ 350.0 million 4.125% senior notes in December 2028, hedged only in part and only to December 2027, followed by the C$ 600.0 million 6.50% notes in August 2032. Refinancing risk is therefore concentrated in the FY2029 notes and the FY2030 credit facility expiry rather than in the next twelve months.

3. Earnings are falling on volume and mix (Documentary Fact). Six month revenues fell 7.2% and Q2 FY2027 adjusted EBITDA margin was 12.1% against 14.2%, while Order Bookings rose 12.7% to C$ 827 million [financial base; business context operating model]. The backlog of C$ 2,009 million is 2.9% lower than a year earlier, with industrial and consumer backlog at C$ 312 million against C$ 431 million. Management no longer expects its prior FY2027 revenue growth and margin goals and guides Q3 FY2027 revenue to C$ 640 million to C$ 680 million. The risk mechanic is operating leverage: a fixed cost base on lower volumes compresses margins faster than revenue, which lowers the EBITDA that covenants and leverage ratios are measured on.

4. Adjusted EBITDA flatters earning power (Analyst Interpretation on documentary add-back data). The net add-back from EBITDA to adjusted EBITDA was C$ 20.2 million in Q2 FY2027, 25.0% of adjusted EBITDA, and restructuring charges have been added back in FY2025, FY2026 and the first half of FY2027 [financial base, add-back review]. The credit agreement definition of EBITDA is not available, so covenant EBITDA may be higher or lower. Creditors should treat the C$ 381.3 million as an upper bound for recurring earnings until restructuring stops recurring.

5. Cash flow is dominated by working capital timing (Documentary Fact). FY2026 operating cash flow of C$ 448.4 million included a C$ 246.6 million working capital release, 55.0% of the total; six month FY2027 operating cash flow was C$ 70.3 million and free cash flow C$ 36.5 million against C$ 149.6 million a year earlier, when an EV customer settlement was collected [financial base, cash flow quality]. Debt reduction capacity therefore moves with project billing, and restructuring provisions point to cash costs still to come.

6. Cover is adequate today (Documentary Fact with calculation). Trailing EBITDA to cash interest paid is 4.18x against 4.24x for FY2026, helped by lower debt and net finance costs of C$ 42.9 million for six months against C$ 50.0 million. A forward swap fixes the term loan at 3.264% from November 4, 2026; floating exposure on the revolver and the end of the cross-currency hedge in December 2027 are sensitivities [financial base, instruments].

7. Business risk is moderate and visibility is reasonable but not strong (Documentary Fact and Management Language). Four end markets, three regions and a backlog of C$ 2,009 million support revenue visibility, and management targets recurring revenue of 25% to 35% of trailing revenue, a figure not yet reported and so treated as Management Language [business context, operating model]. Offsetting this, project timing is lumpy, tariffs under Section 338 delayed non-life-sciences orders from late August 2026 (partly eased by late October modifications), and cross-border equipment sales into the U.S. were just over 20% of FY2026 adjusted revenues.

8. Governance signals are mixed (Analyst Interpretation). ATS reduced net debt between March 2025 and March 2026, a supportive record, but repurchased shares at above-target leverage, changed CEO and has had an interim CFO since February 2026 [business context, governance table]. Disclosure controls were not effective at September 27, 2026 because of a material weakness, so every reported figure carries added reliability risk. No sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed and no creditor-adverse action is described in the sources reviewed, which is a statement about the pack rather than proof.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity depth (Documentary Fact): C$ 231.0 million cash plus C$ 966.7 million unutilized credit against a modest near-term maturity profile, with the revolver expiring in December 2029. Diversification (Documentary Fact): four end markets and three regions, with life sciences holding the largest backlog at C$ 1,238 million. Backlog and recurring revenue ambition (Documentary Fact and Management Language): C$ 2,009 million of backlog supports near-term revenue, and services growth is a stabilizer. Deleveraging record (Documentary Fact): net debt to EBITDA fell from 3.9x at March 31, 2025 to 2.8x at March 31, 2026 before the recent reversal. Interest cover (Documentary Fact with calculation): 4.18x on trailing cash interest. Restructuring programme (Management Language, qualified): management targets C$ 65 million to C$ 75 million of annualized savings, which is a claim and not yet a result.

Weaknesses. Leverage above target (Documentary Fact): 3.1x against 2.0x to 3.0x, with management expecting to remain above range through FY2027. Falling earnings (Documentary Fact): trailing pro forma adjusted EBITDA C$ 381.3 million down from C$ 413.0 million, and revenue and margin goals withdrawn. Earnings quality (Analyst Interpretation): recurring restructuring add-backs of C$ 20.2 million in the latest quarter. Cash flow volatility (Documentary Fact): FY2026 relied on a C$ 246.6 million working capital release. Capital allocation (Analyst Interpretation): C$ 61.3 million of buybacks while leverage was above range. Control environment (Documentary Fact): material weakness and ineffective disclosure controls, with a new CEO and an interim CFO. Missing structure (Insufficient Information): no covenant levels, no credit agreement or indentures, no guarantor names, no customer concentration figures and no ownership detail, so covenant headroom, priority of claims and single-customer risk cannot be assessed.

## Calculations

No source figure was recalculated; amounts are quoted from the handoffs. One new calculation, using financial base inputs (net debt C$ 1,192.2 million; trailing pro forma adjusted EBITDA C$ 381.3 million; target ceiling 3.0x):

- Net debt consistent with 3.0x at current EBITDA: 3.0 × 381.3 = 1,143.9; reduction needed 1,192.2 − 1,143.9 = 48.3.
- EBITDA consistent with 3.0x at current net debt: 1,192.2 / 3.0 = 397.4; increase needed 397.4 − 381.3 = 16.1.
- Both use the company's own net debt and EBITDA definitions, not the credit agreement's, and ignore changes in lease liabilities and exchange rates.
