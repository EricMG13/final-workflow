### Business and transaction view

Bally’s Corporation is a Providence, Rhode Island based operator that the 10-K describes as a global gaming, hospitality, entertainment and technology company. Since February 2025 it has been rebuilt by three transactions: the February 7, 2025 merger with entities affiliated with Standard General and The Queen Casino & Entertainment, Inc.; the October 8, 2025 combination of its International Interactive business with Intralot, which left Bally’s with 57.9% of Bally’s Intralot; and, in 2026, a refinancing and sale-leaseback programme. The business has four reportable segments: Casinos & Resorts, Bally’s Intralot B2B (lottery technology), Bally’s Intralot B2C (online gaming, mainly the UK) and North America Interactive. For a creditor, the structure matters more than the segment mix: ownership is concentrated in one sponsor, the largest growth businesses sit in a subsidiary with its own non-guaranteeing debt group, most casino real estate is leased from GLPI, and two large development projects need capital: Chicago, partly funded by GLPI advances of up to 940.0 million, and the Bronx, whose funding beyond the license fee is not committed. The documented sponsor behaviour is mixed and incomplete: there is no documented priming, uptiering or extraction event in the pages read, and there is also no documented equity injection. The 2025 and 2026 transactions added debt service, fixed rent and project commitments faster than they added reported earnings, which is the mechanism behind the going concern note in the latest filing. This view is a fact pack, not a recovery or relative value judgement.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The February 7, 2025 merger. Bally’s completed merger transactions with SG Parent LLC and The Queen Casino & Entertainment, Inc., an affiliate of Parent (10-K page:7). Public holders received $18.25 per share in cash, Parent and its affiliates then beneficially owned 73.8% of the common stock, and Queen became a wholly owned subsidiary (Q2-26 10-Q page:8). Share repurchases of 22,804,384 shares for 416,180 thousand in the Successor period to June 30, 2025 equal the number of shares multiplied by $18.25 (Q2-26 10-Q page:4, page:5; calculation below), which is consistent with the cash consideration; I read it as Analyst Interpretation because the filing does not label the repurchase that way in the pages read. Non-cash consideration issued was 955,647 thousand for the company merger and 555,751 thousand for the Queen merger (Q2-26 10-Q page:6). At closing the company issued $500 million of 11% first lien senior secured notes due October 2, 2028 under a note purchase agreement (10-K page:85); the use of proceeds is not stated in the pages read, so whether any distribution was debt-funded is Insufficient Information. The notes were repaid in October 2025: Intralot transaction proceeds first paid down 500.0 million of secured debt pro rata across the 2028 Notes and the Term Loan Facility, then an additional payment of 395.0 million settled the remaining 2028 Notes, with a make-whole payment (10-K page:87).

The Intralot transaction. On October 8, 2025 Bally’s completed the transaction under the July 18, 2025 agreement: Intralot paid $1.8 billion in cash and issued about 873.7 million shares for all of Bally’s Holdings Limited, which held the International Interactive business, and Bally’s ownership rose to a controlling 57.9% (Q2-26 10-Q page:8). It was accounted for as an equity transaction with a 42.1% non-controlling interest and no gain or loss (Q2-26 10-Q page:8). Bally’s assumed Intralot debt of about $1.97 billion principal (10-K page:166). Intralot Capital Luxembourg S.A. is the main borrower: a 400.0 million pound term loan, a 200.0 million euro Greek term loan, a super senior revolver with 160.0 million euros of commitments, 600.0 million euros of 6.75% fixed notes and 300.0 million euros of floating notes (Q2-26 10-Q page:31 to page:37). Parent group debt is not guaranteed by Bally’s Intralot, and Bally’s Intralot debt carries its own covenants (a total net leverage ratio not exceeding 4.75:1.00) (Q2-26 10-Q page:11, page:38).

The 2026 refinancing and sale-leaseback. On February 11, 2026 the company entered into $1.1 billion of senior secured 2026 Term Loans: Term SOFR (3.0% floor) plus 7.5%, with up to 3.5% payable in kind, secured pari passu with the revolver, due February 11, 2031, or March 1, 2029 if the 2029 Notes remain outstanding on that date (Q2-26 10-Q page:34, page:32). It repaid the previous Term Loan Facility in full, paying $1.48 billion and recognising a $63.4 million loss on extinguishment (Q2-26 10-Q page:33). The same day it sold the Bally’s Twin River land and real estate to GLPI for total consideration of $700.0 million, leased it back under Master Lease No. 2, and agreed 56.0 million of added annual rent; it recorded a gain of $105.8 million (Q2-26 10-Q page:39). Revolver commitments fell by 22.5% to about $519.3 million, with the revolver maturity split into October 2026 and October 2028 tranches and total availability falling to about $319 million from October 2026 (Q2-26 10-Q page:33, page:66). A revolver drawing of $448.0 million made in January 2026 for the New York license fee was repaid in February 2026 (Q2-26 10-Q page:66).

Covenant relief and amendments. In May 2026 the required revolving lenders conditionally waived compliance with the consolidated net leverage ratio covenant from the quarter ended March 31, 2026 until the earlier of the company’s certification election or the day before delivery of the compliance certificate for the quarter ending March 31, 2027; the waiver is conditioned on minimum liquidity, limits on additional secured debt and an agreement to amend the revolver (Q2-26 10-Q page:38). A Fourth Amendment dated May 5, 2026 raised revolver margins (Q2-26 10-Q page:64, page:74); a Fifth Amendment dated July 29, 2026 is listed as incorporated from a Form 8-K of August 5, 2026 whose terms are Not supplied (Q2-26 10-Q page:74). The Q2-26 10-Q states the company does not project that it would satisfy the liquidity maintenance requirement or the reinstated covenant over the next twelve months absent the financings under discussion (page:11).

Development and licence commitments. The company was awarded a New York downstate gaming license in December 2025 and paid the $500.0 million license fee in the first quarter of 2026, plus a $115 million golf course concession contingent payment (Q2-26 10-Q page:44; Q2-26 release page:2). A conveyance arrangement recorded a liability of $183.6 million for specified capital improvements (Q2-26 10-Q page:25), with improvements of approximately $161.0 million required (page:44). The Bronx project is described as a $4.0 billion integrated casino project expected to open by 2030 (Q2-26 release page:2, management language), and a bond or cash deposit equal to 5% of total project investment is required (Q2-26 10-Q page:44). Chicago: the permanent casino targets opening in early 2027 on management’s account (Q2-26 release page:2); GLPI has committed to advance up to $940.0 million of hard costs in exchange for added rent of 8.5% of advances, on top of 20.0 million initial annual rent (Q2-26 10-Q page:70). In the Las Vegas site, the Athletics stadium is slated for completion before the 2028 season (Q2-26 release page:2).

Investments and acquisitions. In April 2025 the company committed A$200 million in convertible notes and subordinated debt for an approximately 38% economic interest in The Star Entertainment Group Limited (10-K page:6); it now holds a 37.7% equity interest (Q2-26 10-Q page:15). Bally’s Intralot agreed in June 2026 to acquire evoke plc, with a cooperation agreement dated June 5, 2026 (Q2-26 release page:1; Q2-26 10-Q page:74). The consideration, financing and approvals timetable are Not supplied; since the buyer is Bally’s Intralot, which does not guarantee parent debt, any financing sits with the Intralot group unless otherwise documented, which is an Analyst Interpretation. Bally’s Intralot also signed a total return equity swap on June 15, 2026 over its own shares for up to 50.0 million euros (Q2-26 10-Q page:28).

Subsequent events and new debt after the quarter. Bally’s Intralot, through Intralot Capital, signed a sterling term facilities agreement for 261.8 million pounds on July 27, 2026, drawn in two three-year tranches (Q2-26 10-Q page:37). In July 2026 the company signed a non-binding term sheet for a loan to fund the Bally’s Bronx project and general corporate purposes (Q2-26 10-Q page:11), and in August 2026 a letter of intent with a potential equity investor (Q2-26 release page:2, management language). No later event is supplied.

### Credit translations

- Evidence: Standard General holds 67.1% and the Executive Chairman is its principal (10-K page:66). Risk mechanic: a controlling holder decides whether to inject equity, sell assets or hold, and the company says an equity sale is among its plans (Q2-26 10-Q page:11). Credit implication: the going concern note makes the sponsor’s response the main uncertainty, and there is no documented commitment either way. Counter-evidence: no conflict logged.
- Evidence: Bally’s Intralot does not guarantee parent debt while its cash flows are consolidated (Q2-26 10-Q page:11). Risk mechanic: reported earnings and cash include a subsidiary whose creditors rank first against its assets, and 42.1% of its equity belongs to others. Credit implication: parent-group leverage is higher than the consolidated 9.57x implies on the share of earnings that cannot be moved up. This reading is Analyst Interpretation; the intercompany restrictions are Not supplied.
- Evidence: the Twin River sale-leaseback added 56.0 million of annual rent against 700.0 million of consideration (Q2-26 10-Q page:39). Risk mechanic: property value was converted into a fixed rent claim ahead of the debt. Credit implication: fixed charge cover is lower than the CP-1 interest cover suggests, because rent sits in Adjusted EBITDA as a deduction and is not shown in interest.
- Evidence: UK gaming tax and cost ratios (Q2-26 release page:3; Q2-26 10-Q page:57). Risk mechanic: a tax increase on the largest interactive market reduces cash conversion. Credit implication: revenue growth is not a reliable indicator of debt service capacity. Counter-evidence: the 5.50 to 1.00 trigger in the Rhode Island agreement has no logged conflict but is untested.
- Key uncertainties: waiver conditions, minimum liquidity level, outcome of the financings under discussion, evoke terms, funding of Bronx and Chicago, and regulatory leverage definitions.
