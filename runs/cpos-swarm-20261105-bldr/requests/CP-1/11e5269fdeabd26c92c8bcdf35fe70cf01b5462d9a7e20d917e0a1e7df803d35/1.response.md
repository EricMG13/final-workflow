### Financial base view

The financial base is sufficient to state the debt, earnings, cash flow and liquidity position of Builders FirstSource, Inc. at September 30, 2026, with the limitations listed under Gaps. Over the twelve months to September 30, 2026, reported earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization were well below FY2025 because gross margin fell and selling, general and administrative expense was broadly flat, while debt principal rose by 173.9 million, driven by 197.0 million of revolver borrowing. Total debt to EBITDA therefore moved from 3.26x to 5.15x and EBITDA to cash interest from 5.29x to 3.18x on the reported basis. Operating cash flow for the nine months to September 30, 2026 was 302.0 million against 1,021.1 million a year earlier, and the company repurchased 303.5 million of stock in the same nine months, almost all of it in the first quarter. Liquidity was 1,514.1 million at September 30, 2026 and stayed in a range of about 1.5 billion to 1.7 billion at the four balance dates read. The only financial covenant is a fixed charge coverage test of 1.00 to 1.00 that applies only if excess availability falls below a minimum; availability was far above it, and the ratio itself is not reported. The senior unsecured notes and the revolver are fixed in tenor, with the first maturity in 2030. The figures that most need a second look before use are the EBITDA definition (built here, not printed), the management Adjusted EBITDA that carries a large technology implementation add-back, and the guarantor perimeter, which no pinned source names.

### Covenants and definitions

- Maintenance test: the revolver has one financial covenant, a minimum fixed charge coverage ratio of 1.00 to 1.00 that applies only when excess availability falls below the specified minimum. [K10 page:93] "of 1.00 to 1.00 if our excess availability falls below the greater of $165.0 million or 10% of the maximum borrowing amount, which was $160.7 million as of December 31, 2025."
- Level at September 30, 2026: [Q3 page:27] "Excess availability must equal or exceed a minimum specified amount, currently $173.2 million, or we are required to meet a fixed charge coverage ratio of 1.00 to 1.00." Reported availability is 1,459.1 million, so headroom to the trigger is 1,285.9 million (C15). The same calculation gives 1,257.9 million at March 31, 2026, 1,379.7 million at June 30, 2026 and 1,362.3 million at December 31, 2025 against the 165.0 million then in force. The threshold moves with the borrowing base (10 percent of 1,731.7 million is 173.17 million).
- Reported figure: the fixed charge coverage ratio itself is Not supplied, because the test is not currently applicable and no pinned source prints a computed ratio. The definition of fixed charges and of the numerator is in the credit agreement, which is not supplied; the EBITDA used in this module is not that covenant definition.
- Compliance: [Q3 page:13] "We were not in violation of any covenants or restrictions imposed by any of our debt agreements at September 30, 2026."
- Negative covenants in the revolver and the indentures limit debt, liens, restricted payments, investments, asset sales and affiliate transactions (K10 page:93, K10 page:95); the baskets are Not supplied.
- Definition differences: reported EBITDA here (income from operations plus D&A) differs from management Adjusted EBITDA, which adds technology implementation expense, stock compensation, acquisition expense and other management-identified adjustments (R3 page:13), and from any covenant definition.

### Interpretation

- Leverage. Evidence: the debt table shows principal of 4,485,046 thousand at December 31, 2025 and 4,658,904 thousand at September 30, 2026, with the revolver drawn 197,000 thousand (Q3 page:13), while nine-month income from operations fell from 724,496 thousand to 244,113 thousand (Q3 page:2). Mechanism: the debt is largely fixed-rate notes with no scheduled principal payment before 2030, so leverage moves with earnings, and management attributes the gross profit decline mainly to a lower housing starts environment (R3 page:1). Implication: total debt to EBITDA is above the FY2025 level by about 1.9 turns on the reported basis, and further EBITDA decline raises it without any change in debt.
- Coverage. Evidence: net interest expense for nine months rose from 206,139 thousand to 222,740 thousand (Q3 page:2), and cash interest for the twelve months is 284,752 thousand (C2). Mechanism: the 6.75% 2035 notes were issued on May 8, 2025 and so were outstanding for only part of the nine-month 2025 comparator, and the revolver was drawn in 2026 (K10 page:92, Q3 page:13). Implication: fixed charges are stable to rising while EBITDA is falling, which is why cover moved from 5.29x to 3.18x.
- Cash flow. Evidence: nine-month operating cash flow of 302,031 thousand against 1,021,092 thousand (Q3 page:3), with working capital absorbing cash (Q3 page:28). Mechanism: receivables and inventories absorbed cash of 229,466 thousand and 122,131 thousand, partly offset by payables of 178,060 thousand (Q3 page:3). Implication: operating cash flow is already after cash interest, and free cash flow after capex of 150,875 thousand (C11) was positive but smaller than the repurchase of 303,519 thousand in the same period.
- Liquidity. Evidence: availability of 1,459.1 million against a trigger of 173.2 million (Q3 page:27). Mechanism: availability is borrowing-base driven, so it follows receivables and inventory and can contract as sales fall; the borrowing base grew from 1,606.9 million to 1,731.7 million as receivables and inventory rose, which offset part of the 197.0 million draw, so availability fell only from 1,527.3 million to 1,459.1 million. Implication: liquidity is currently ample, but part of it rests on the same working capital build that reduced operating cash flow.
- Maturities. Evidence: the maturity table above. Mechanism: no note or revolver is due before 2030 and the revolver and the 5.00% notes are both due in 2030, so refinancing risk concentrates in that year. Implication: no near-term refinancing pressure, with the 2030 date as the first test of market access.
