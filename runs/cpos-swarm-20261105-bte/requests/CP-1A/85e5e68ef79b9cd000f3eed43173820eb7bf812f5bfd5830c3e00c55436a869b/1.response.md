### Business and transaction view

BAYTEX ENERGY CORP. is an Alberta-incorporated, listed oil and gas producer whose retained business is entirely in the Western Canadian Sedimentary Basin: heavy oil at Peace River, Peavine and Lloydminster, and light oil in the Viking and the Pembina and Gilby Duvernay (B1, B7, B8, B17). The defining transaction is the sale of the U.S. Eagle Ford assets, completed on December 19, 2025 for net proceeds of US$2.2 billion (C$3.0 billion) (B2, B3). Management states that it then repaid the majority of the credit facilities, redeemed the 8.5% Senior Notes and partially redeemed the 7.375% Senior Notes (B5), leaving one note series of US$64.1 million, an undrawn secured revolver and net cash of C$566,286 thousand at June 30, 2026 (from the financial handoff). For a creditor the mechanism is clear: the sale converted a leveraged, two-country borrower into a debt-light single-basin producer, which removes refinancing and debt-service risk in the near term and moves the credit question to commodity exposure, capital returns and non-debt claims. Three things carry that question. First, revenue is price-taking: realised heavy oil prices track Western Canada Select with a differential and diluent cost (B9, B10, B25), and the legacy oil hedges that cost C$84,146 thousand of realized losses in Q2 2026 have expired, leaving no WTI hedges after Q2 2026 (B11), so cash flow now moves fully with oil prices. Second, management's declared priority is to return cash through buybacks while maintaining the dividend (B4, B20), and buybacks plus dividends exceeded free cash flow in the first half of 2026 (financial handoff; B33), so the cash buffer is discretionary. Third, the pack contains no ownership, control, rating, sponsor or sale-agreement document, so ownership concentration, control rights and the sale's indemnities are Insufficient Information rather than adverse findings. Management's claims (an "industry-leading balance sheet", a net cash position maintained through 2028, a long-term sustaining break-even target) are tagged Management Language and are not adopted.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The one transaction in the pack is a disposal, not a debt financing. Type: sale of the operated and non-operated Eagle Ford assets, the whole U.S. operating segment (B2). Parties: Baytex as seller; the buyer is not named in any pinned source (Not supplied). Status: completed on December 19, 2025 (B3). Consideration: net proceeds of US$2.2 billion, C$3.0 billion in Canadian dollars after closing adjustments (B3), shown in the results release as an acquisitions and (divestitures) line of (3,006,514) thousand in Q4 2025; post-closing adjustments of C$6.3 million in Q2 2026 and C$18.6 million for the six months are recorded within discontinued operations (S6 page:7). Sponsor: none; Baytex is listed, and the run context names no sponsor. Use of proceeds: management states that Baytex repaid the majority of its credit facilities, redeemed the 8.5% Senior Notes and partially redeemed the 7.375% Senior Notes in Q4 2025 (B5), then resumed share buybacks on December 24, 2025 (S3 page:6). Liquidity effect: cash of C$953,113 thousand at December 31, 2025, against C$1,400 thousand of credit facility drawings and C$95,947 thousand of 7.375% notes (financial handoff). Loss and accounting effects: the 2025 net loss reflected a loss on the Eagle Ford disposition, a deferred tax adjustment related to the transaction structure and a Viking impairment (B40, B41), with management stating no cash-flow impact. Terms not in the pack: purchase agreement, indemnities, retained liabilities and escrow; the Q2 2026 discussion states that Baytex retains liability for matters related to its prior ownership of assets located in the U.S., without amount (S7 page:50). There are no new debt issues in the evidence period: the only debt event after the sale is the repurchase and cancellation of US$5.8 million of 7.375% notes in Q1 2026 at 103.613% of par (S6 page:10), and the revolving facilities were reset between September 30, 2025 and March 31, 2026 to C$750 million maturing June 27, 2030 with no amending document in the pack (financial handoff, gap H5). A second structural transaction is the Gibson infrastructure agreement: Gibson funded Baytex's construction of oil and gas infrastructure under a 15-year take-or-pay agreement, ownership transferred to Gibson in February 2026 at cost, and the agreement was determined to contain a lease, adding lease obligations (B21a, B22a, B23a). Subsequent events after June 30, 2026 in the pack: a dividend declared July 30, 2026, board changes, and buybacks through July 29, 2026 (B28, B38, B42).
