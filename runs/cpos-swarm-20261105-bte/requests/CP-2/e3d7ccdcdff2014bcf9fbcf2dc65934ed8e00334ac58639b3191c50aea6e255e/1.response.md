### Credit view

BAYTEX ENERGY CORP. is a low-leverage credit with strong near-term liquidity, but its business is fully exposed to oil prices and its cash buffer is one that management can spend. The strongest support is liquidity: C$720,337 thousand of cash and C$745,600 thousand of undrawn committed facilities against C$91,107 thousand (US$64.1 million) of 7.375% notes due March 15, 2032, with Total Debt at 0.13x Bank EBITDA. The weakest point is that free cash flow follows oil prices, the oil hedges have run off, and buybacks plus dividends exceeded free cash flow in the first half of 2026. The outcome depends on whether management keeps net cash as it has said it will, and on what the missing debt documents allow it to distribute.

### Key findings

First, the issuer's debt burden is small enough that debt service is not a near-term concern. After the Eagle Ford sale it had no drawings on its C$750 million secured revolving credit facilities, which mature June 27, 2030, and C$91,107 thousand of notes, against cash of C$720,337 thousand and company-defined net cash of C$566,286 thousand at June 30, 2026. Liquidity (cash plus undrawn facilities net of letters of credit) was C$1,465,937 thousand. Interest cover on the credit-agreement measure was 9.31x against a 3.5x minimum, and Total Debt was 0.13x Bank EBITDA against a 4.0x maximum. [Financial handoff; Analyst Interpretation on the reading, Documentary Fact on the figures.]

Second, the credit question has moved from the debt stack to the cash buffer. Management states that buybacks are the priority and targets 15% annual total shareholder return, and in the six months to June 30, 2026 shareholder returns of C$342.9 million exceeded free cash flow of C$129.8 million. Company-defined net cash fell from C$765,785 thousand to C$566,286 thousand over the same period. Because the stated three-year plan is to maintain net cash, but the restricted-payment baskets in the debt documents are not in the pack, creditors cannot tell how much of the buffer is contractually protected. [Financial and business handoffs; Documentary Fact for the amounts, Management Language for the plan, Analyst Interpretation for the reading.]

Third, cash generation is price-driven and no longer hedged. Free cash flow was C$1,705 thousand in Q1 2026 when WTI averaged US$71.93 per barrel and C$128,045 thousand in Q2 2026 when it averaged US$92.79, and the issuer states that it has no WTI hedges after Q2 2026. The legacy hedges cost C$84,146 thousand of realized derivative losses in Q2 2026. A price fall would therefore pass straight through to adjusted funds flow, which was C$254,433 thousand in Q2 2026. [Analyst Interpretation on the sensitivity, Documentary Fact on the figures.]

Fourth, claims outside the debt metrics are large. Asset retirement obligations were C$546,500 thousand and lease obligations C$60,256 thousand at June 30, 2026, both excluded from the covenant debt definition, and the Canada Revenue Agency asserts C$244.8 million of tax, C$244.2 million of late-payment interest and a C$4.1 million penalty on a reassessment the issuer contests and for which it purchased C$272.5 million of insurance coverage. These claims need cash that low debt ratios do not show. [Financial and business handoffs; Documentary Fact.]

Fifth, the evidence base limits how far the favourable picture can be pushed. The issuer reports no EBITDA, Bank EBITDA is a company-defined measure pro forma for the sale, the FY2025 ratios are Not supplied, and comparatives before 2026 mix reporting bases. Ownership, the sale agreement and the debt documents are absent, which is Insufficient Information, not an adverse finding. [Source readiness, financial and business handoffs.]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The issuer has almost no debt after the Eagle Ford sale: C$91,107 thousand of notes principal, undrawn facilities and net cash of C$566,286 thousand, so leverage on the company's own measures is 0.13x and net cash is 0.75x of Bank EBITDA (Documentary Fact; calculated in the financial handoff). Liquidity of C$1,465,937 thousand is about 16.1x the note principal, and no drawn debt principal falls due before 2032 (the notes), while the undrawn revolver commitment matures in 2030 (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on the multiple). Covenant headroom is wide: interest cover of 9.31x against 3.5x means Bank EBITDA could fall by about C$473.65 million before breach if the denominator stayed at C$81.5 million (Analyst Interpretation; the denominator is a credit-agreement measure, not cash interest). Operations are strengthening: production of 71,243 boe/d, adjusted funds flow of C$254,433 thousand and free cash flow of C$128,045 thousand in Q2 2026, with 2026 production guidance raised to about 71,000 boe/d (Documentary Fact; the outlook is Management Language). Cash interest is now immaterial: net cash interest was income of C$702 thousand in Q2 2026 (Documentary Fact). Reporting is transparent, with covenants and perimeter disclosed each quarter (Analyst Interpretation).

Weaknesses. The issuer is a price-taker: heavy oil is 65% of production, realised heavy prices follow Western Canada Select less a differential that widened to US$14.62 per barrel from US$10.20, and no WTI hedges remain after Q2 2026 (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on the margin mechanism). It has one basin and one country after the sale, so egress, royalty and regulatory exposure are concentrated (Analyst Interpretation). Cash returns are discretionary and exceeded free cash flow in the first half of 2026, so net cash is a policy choice that can be reversed (Documentary Fact on the amounts, Analyst Interpretation on the reading). Non-debt claims of C$606,756 thousand for asset retirement and leases, plus the contested tax reassessment, are not reflected in debt ratios (Documentary Fact; sum calculated here). The covenant and leverage measures are company-defined, the issuer reports no EBITDA, and the debt documents that would show guarantors, security, baskets and call terms are not in the pack (Insufficient Information on the terms: the missing datapoint is the credit agreement and note indenture, and it matters because restricted-payment limits decide how much of the buffer is protected). Ownership and control are not documented, and the sale agreement and its indemnities are not in the pack (Insufficient Information; absence is not an adverse conclusion).

## Calculations

All inputs are accepted figures of the financial and business handoffs.

- **C1 Non-debt claims.** Asset retirement obligations C$546,500 thousand + lease obligations C$60,256 thousand = C$606,756 thousand (June 30, 2026). Against cash of C$720,337 thousand this is 0.8423 times (84.2%); against notes principal of C$91,107 thousand it is 6.66 times.
- **C2 CRA asserted amounts.** C$244.8 million tax + C$244.2 million late-payment interest + C$4.1 million penalty = C$493.1 million, as asserted in the CRA statement of account; whether any amount is recognised is not stated.
- **C3 Liquidity cover of the notes.** Liquidity C$1,465,937 thousand / notes principal C$91,107 thousand = 16.09x; cash alone C$720,337 thousand / C$91,107 thousand = 7.91x.
- **C4 Distributions versus free cash flow, six months to June 30, 2026.** Share repurchase consideration C$310.1 million + dividends declared C$32.750 million = C$342.85 million, which agrees with the printed C$342.9 million; free cash flow C$129.75 million (printed C$129.8 million); excess = C$213.10 million. This is the same calculation as in the business handoff, repeated for the credit view.
- **C5 Covenant headroom (from the financial handoff).** Interest-cover floor 3.5 x C$81.5 million = C$285.25 million; headroom C$758.9 million - C$285.25 million = C$473.65 million (62.4%). The denominator is held fixed; this is not a forecast.
