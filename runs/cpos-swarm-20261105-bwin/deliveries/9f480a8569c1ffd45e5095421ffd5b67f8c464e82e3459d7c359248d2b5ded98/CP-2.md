### Credit view

Baldwin Insurance Group, Inc. has an elevated-leverage but adequately liquid credit profile, and this synthesis holds that view with Medium confidence. Its strongest support is a diversified, commission-based client base, first-lien debt with no maturity before the revolver on 2029-05-24 beyond scheduled amortization, and USD 184.5 million of cash plus USD 259.4 million of undrawn revolver capacity at 2026-06-30. The weakest point is leverage: total debt to adjusted EBITDA rose to 6.30x for the last twelve months to 2026-06-30 from 5.01x at FY2025, on earnings that rely on large addbacks and hold only six months of the January 2026 acquisitions. Whether acquired earnings reach run rate, whether earnout and deferred payments are met without new borrowing, and covenant headroom, which is not supplied, decide the outcome.

### Key findings

First, leverage rose in each period. Baldwin's total debt, including the revolver, was USD 1,435.8 million at FY2024, USD 1,710.6 million at FY2025 and USD 2,495.0 million at 2026-06-30, giving total debt to adjusted EBITDA of 4.59x, 5.01x and 6.30x, and net debt to adjusted EBITDA of 4.31x, 4.65x and 5.83x [financial base, leverage chart]. The mechanism is the USD 600.0 million incremental term loan of 2026-01-02 and revolver drawings that rose by USD 195.0 million in six months, set against adjusted EBITDA of USD 396.2 million for the last twelve months that includes about six months of the January 2026 acquisitions. The implication is that the 6.30x probably overstates leverage relative to a full-year pro forma basis by an amount the sources do not size, while the use of company-defined earnings could push the other way, so the ratio is an indicator and not a covenant test.

Second, earnings quality limits every ratio. Adjustments other than interest, amortization and depreciation were 42.5% of FY2025 adjusted EBITDA and 48.6% of last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA, and EBITDA before addbacks, calculated from the statements, was USD 58.1 million for the last twelve months against USD 396.2 million adjusted [financial base]. The gap includes USD 128.8 million of Tax Receivable Agreement expense recognized in six months 2026, which the company adds back although the liability is a future cash obligation. The implication is that creditors who underwrite on adjusted EBITDA are relying on management's definition, and the covenant EBITDA may differ.

Third, interest cover held while leverage rose. Adjusted EBITDA to cash interest was 2.81x at FY2024, 2.96x at FY2025 and 2.89x for the last twelve months, but the six-month figure fell from 3.66x to 3.33x as cash interest rose faster than adjusted earnings [financial base]. About USD 500.0 million of the floating-rate term loans is swapped to a fixed 3.244% to 2028-09-14, and a 100 bp rise in SOFR would add USD 14.0 million a year to interest. The implication is that cover is moderate relative to leverage but protected for part of the rate exposure until 2028.

Fourth, liquidity is adequate and narrowing. Cash and equivalents were USD 184.5 million and company-stated undrawn revolver capacity USD 259.4 million at 2026-06-30, against USD 123.7 million and USD 477.0 million at FY2025, with USD 302.0 million drawn on a USD 600.0 million revolver [financial base, liquidity chart]. Fiduciary cash of USD 426.2 million is excluded because it is fiduciary and not free cash. The mechanism is revolver borrowing used alongside USD 126.8 million of share repurchases and acquisition-related payments in six months 2026. The implication is that near-term liquidity covers scheduled amortization of about USD 16.0 million a year, derived, but it is more exposed to cash calls from earnouts and the Tax Receivable Agreement.

Fifth, claims outside debt are large and time-bound. Contingent earnout liabilities were USD 346.8 million at fair value, with undiscounted expected payments of USD 410.5 million of which USD 131.1 million fall within one year, deferred payments were USD 192.5 million undiscounted, and Tax Receivable Agreement liabilities were USD 144.6 million at 2026-06-30 [financial base]. The January 2026 acquisitions cost USD 1,559.6 million in total, of which equity was USD 510.6 million and the maximum potential earnout is USD 544.9 million [business and transaction]. The implication is that the true fixed-claim burden exceeds the debt total, and a strong year for the acquired businesses raises earnout payments in cash.

Sixth, control and governance have changed. The Delaware Supreme Court reversed the Court of Chancery on 2026-06-09, the 2024 Stockholders Agreement is in effect, and holders have stated approval rights over debt above 10% of total assets, dividends, large asset transactions and a bankruptcy filing, while the independent committee created in 2024 has been disbanded [business and transaction]. There is no sponsor, and no creditor-adverse transaction is documented. The implication is that leverage and distribution decisions are insider-influenced, and how approvals were applied to the 2025 and 2026 debt additions is not disclosed, so legal capacity cannot be assessed.

Seventh, the business is diversified by client but not by carrier or cycle. The largest client is under 1% of core commissions and fees, while two insurance partners provided about 17%; commissions follow premium levels, organic revenue growth was 2% for six months 2026 against reported growth of 29%, and the Captive adds underwriting exposure of USD 29.5 million of assumed premium earned in six months 2026 [business and transaction]. The implication is that revenue is durable against any single client but exposed to a soft premium market and carrier appetite, and the growth in the period was bought rather than organic.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Baldwin's client base is highly diversified, with more than three million clients and no client above 1% of core commissions and fees, so single-client loss is not a credit driver [business and transaction; Documentary Fact]. Its funded debt is long-dated, with the revolver due 2029-05-24 and the term loans and the 7.125% senior secured notes due in 2031, so refinancing risk is back-ended beyond scheduled amortization [financial base; Analyst Interpretation, because the maturity rows are derived]. Liquidity of USD 184.5 million of cash and USD 259.4 million of undrawn revolver capacity at 2026-06-30 covers near-term scheduled amortization many times over [financial base; Documentary Fact for the inputs]. Cash interest cover on adjusted EBITDA has stayed near 2.9x across three periods, and part of the floating-rate exposure is fixed by a USD 500.0 million swap at 3.244% to 2028-09-14 [financial base; Documentary Fact]. Capital intensity is low, with capital expenditures of USD 39.5 million in FY2025, so cash flow is driven by earnouts, interest and acquisitions rather than reinvestment [financial base; Analyst Interpretation]. Management reports faster integration of CAC Group than planned, but this is Management Language and carries no supporting bridge [business and transaction; Management Language].

Weaknesses. Total debt to adjusted EBITDA is 6.30x for the last twelve months and net debt to adjusted EBITDA 5.83x, up from 5.01x and 4.65x at FY2025, with the denominator holding only about six months of acquired earnings [financial base; Documentary Fact for inputs, Analyst Interpretation for the reading]. Adjusted EBITDA relies on addbacks of 48.6% over interest, amortization and depreciation, and the reported result is a net loss of USD 131.8 million for the last twelve months [financial base; Documentary Fact]. All funded debt ranks as first lien and is secured on substantially all assets, so the structure has no subordinated layer beneath the term loans, revolver and notes [business and transaction; Documentary Fact]. Claims outside debt are material: earnouts of USD 346.8 million, deferred payments of USD 192.5 million and Tax Receivable Agreement liabilities of USD 144.6 million [financial base; Documentary Fact]. Operating cash flow after capital expenditures was USD 43.7 million for the last twelve months and negative USD 68.9 million in FY2025, because earnout payments in excess of the accrual run through operations [financial base; Documentary Fact]. Revenue growth is mostly acquired, with organic growth of 2% for six months 2026 [business and transaction; Documentary Fact]. Control rests with insiders through approval rights over debt, dividends and a bankruptcy filing, with the independent committee disbanded, and Tax Receivable Agreement payments are owed to LLC members [business and transaction; Documentary Fact]. Two insurance partners provided about 17% of core commissions and fees, and the book was historically concentrated in the Southeast [business and transaction; Documentary Fact]. The financial maintenance covenant of 7.00x total first lien net leverage is described only in the FY2025 annual report, with no later reported ratio, so headroom is unmeasured [financial base; Insufficient Information].
