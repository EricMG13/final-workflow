### Business and transaction view

Baldwin Insurance Group, Inc. is a publicly listed holding company whose sole material asset is its interest in The Baldwin Insurance Group Holdings, LLC, the operating company, borrower and note issuer. It distributes commercial property and casualty, employee benefits, personal lines, wealth and retirement, and Medicare products through three operating groups, and manufactures proprietary insurance products through its managing general agent platform. The credit story of the period is a step change in scale and leverage. Three partnerships, led by CAC Group, closed on 2026-01-01 and 2026-01-02 for total consideration of 1,559,612 thousand, financed through a mix of 600,000 thousand of incremental term loans under Amendment No. 4, Class A shares issued to sellers, contingent earnouts and deferred payments, while cash and revolver borrowing also rose over the period. No private-equity sponsor owns the issuer. Control rights instead rest with the pre-IPO members of the operating company, who hold Class B shares and, under a stockholders agreement that the Delaware Supreme Court reinstated on 2026-06-09, approval rights over mergers, large asset deals, borrowing above stated thresholds, equity issuance and dividends. Revenue is recurring in form, since it is commissions, fees and carrier bonuses on renewing policies, but it follows premium levels and carrier relationships, and the reported growth for the first half of 2026 is mostly acquired. The facts that matter most to a creditor are the acquisition-related cash claims outside debt, the insider approval rights and the absence of a supplied credit agreement, so permitted debt, restricted payments and guarantor perimeter cannot be tested here. The view is Medium confidence: the business and transaction facts are well documented, while ownership percentages, governance practice and legal capacity are not.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transactions in the pack are a set of completed acquisitions with debt financing, and there is no pending transaction at the cutoff other than a small disposal. Their status is completed unless stated. Source for the terms below is the Q2 2026 10-Q unless a different file is named.

CAC Group (Cobbs Allen Capital Holdings, LLC) was acquired effective 2026-01-01 under a Transaction Agreement dated 2025-12-02 (exhibit 10.32 in the 10-K exhibit list, page:200), amended 2025-12-30. The 10-K describes closing consideration of 438.0 million of cash, 23,200,000 Class A shares and a deferred payment of 70.0 million payable on the fourth anniversary, with contingent consideration payable in cash on post-closing revenue targets (10-K page:188). Obie (Creisoft, Inc.) and Capstone (Foley Insurance Agency, Inc.) were acquired effective 2026-01-02. Obie's consideration was stated as 90.0 million of cash and 396,573 shares plus 122.5 million of deferred payments (10-K page:188). In aggregate the three businesses were recorded at total consideration of 1,559,612 thousand, comprising cash of 567,889, contingent earnouts at fair value of 315,998, equity of 510,561 and deferred payments of 165,164, with goodwill of 1,134,895 and intangible assets of 550,983, and a maximum potential contingent earnout of 544,855 (page:16-19). The purchase accounting is provisional. These businesses contributed total revenues of 202.1 million and a net loss of 42.3 million in six months 2026 (page:17). Management says CAC Group integration is "meaningfully ahead of plan, with approximately 80% of targeted three-year expense synergies already actioned" (Q1 release page:1); that is Management Language and the pack gives no synergy bridge.

Financing and new debt. On 2026-01-02 Amendment No. 4 to the JPM Credit Agreement added 600,000 thousand of incremental term loans on the same terms as the existing Term Loans, with about 12.0 million of debt issuance costs (page:25). No new notes were issued in 2026; the 7.125% Senior Secured Notes due 2031-05-15 remained at 600 million. The company says it intends to fund earnout obligations near term with cash, unused proceeds of the incremental term loans, operating cash flow and revolver borrowings (page:74). The revolver rose from 107,000 thousand to 302,000 thousand over six months 2026, share repurchases were 126,833 thousand, and a 25.0 million deferred payment to a partner was settled on 2026-01-02 (10-K page:189). Use of proceeds therefore combined acquisition funding, earnout and deferred payment settlement and shareholder returns; the pack does not give a sources and uses statement for the add-on, and none is inferred.

Earlier financings, for the timeline: on 2024-05-24 the company issued 600 million of 7.125% Senior Secured Notes and put in place an 840 million first lien term loan and a 600 million revolver (10-K page:161); incremental term loans followed on 2025-01-10 (100.0 million) and 2025-09-18 (75.0 million) (10-K page:164-165).

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: commissions follow premium levels and carrier profit-sharing varies, with organic growth of 2% for six months 2026 (10-K page:8; 10-Q Q2 2026 page:60). Mechanic: a soft market lowers premium-based commissions while the people cost base is largely fixed. Implication: margin and debt service capacity are exposed to the premium cycle, and acquired growth does not show underlying durability. The two carriers providing approximately 17% of core commissions and fees counter the diversification across clients.
- Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: the 10-Q shows Tax Receivable Agreement expense of 128.8 million, contingent consideration fair value changes of 14,272 and large addbacks in adjusted EBITDA (financial base). Mechanic: acquisition-related items run through earnings and cash, and earnout payments sit in operating cash flow. Implication: free cash flow after earnouts is lower than the company's adjusted free cash flow, and conversion depends on payment timing.
- Concentration risk. Evidence: two carriers at approximately 17% of core commissions and fees, a Southeastern history and several regional concentrations (10-K page:14, page:24).
