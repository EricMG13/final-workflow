### Business and transaction view

CACI is a Delaware holding company whose operating subsidiaries provide technology and expertise to national security customers, with essentially all revenue from the U.S. federal government. The view for a creditor is that business risk is dominated by funding and contract-structure mechanics rather than by market competition: revenue visibility comes from a large backlog but depends on annual appropriations and on contracts that the government may terminate for convenience; margin resilience depends on the contract mix, which moved toward fixed-price work in the latest quarter; and cash conversion depends on receivable collection and on a receivable sale program that is nearly at its cap and has a stated term ending in December 2026. The one transformative transaction in the record is the March 2026 acquisition of a space-domain technology business, financed with an 800.0 million term loan tranche, a 500.0 million add-on of senior unsecured notes and revolver borrowings, after which the revolver balance has fallen. Ownership is public and dispersed on the evidence supplied, but control rights and holder concentration are Not supplied because the proxy statement is incorporated by reference and is not in the pack. No sponsor behaviour can be assessed because none is named. The record shows no dividend ever paid and sharply reduced share repurchases in FY2026. The fiscal-year change disclosed on October 20, 2026 and the October 2026 court developments are subsequent events and are kept out of FY2026 facts.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The principal transaction is the acquisition of ARKA Group L.P., which closed on March 9, 2026 under a purchase agreement and plan of merger dated December 19, 2025, among a CACI operating subsidiary, the issuer, a merger subsidiary, the target and two other limited partnerships (10-K exhibit index, page:104). The 10-K states purchase consideration of 2,642.7 million net of cash acquired, subject to post-closing adjustments, that the acquisition is intended to enhance the ability to deliver advanced technology for national security customers in the space domain, and that it was funded from increased borrowings and cash on hand (10-K page:79). The allocation recorded 1,145.0 million of intangible assets and 1,462.9 million of goodwill (10-K page:80). The target's sellers, any sponsor or minority interest and any earnout are not described in the supplied text, which is an Insufficient Information item. A smaller acquisition of a data and cloud migration business by the U.K. subsidiary closed on February 17, 2026 for 10.7 million net of cash acquired (10-K page:81). Management reports post-acquisition revenue of 166.8 million and net income of 17.4 million for the two businesses together in FY2026, after 26.0 million of intangible amortisation (10-K page:81); this is a partial-period contribution and not a run-rate figure. In the latest quarter the consideration was reduced to 2,637.7 million after a 5.0 million purchase price refund from the final working capital settlement, and the allocation is still preliminary (Q1 10-Q page:5). The two figures are logged as a conflict of versions below and are not reconciled here.

New debt linked to the transaction. On March 9, 2026 the company amended its Term Loan B agreement to add a 800.0 million seven-year senior secured Term Loan B-2 (10-K page:88, page:89). On March 12, 2026 it issued an additional 500.0 million of its 6.375% senior unsecured notes due June 2033, forming part of the same series as the 1,000.0 million notes issued on June 2, 2025, at a premium, with net proceeds of 516.5 million used to repay revolver borrowings (10-K page:89). A senior secured bridge loan commitment of up to 1,300.0 million dated December 19, 2025 was never drawn and was terminated on March 9, 2026 (10-K page:81). Earlier and related financing events: the Term Loan B of 750.0 million on October 30, 2024; the 1,000.0 million notes of June 2, 2025, whose net proceeds repaid revolver borrowings; and the November 25, 2025 amendment of the senior secured credit facility, made primarily to extend its maturity (10-K page:88, page:89). Revolver borrowings also funded part of the price: the revolver was drawn 928.0 million at March 31, 2026 and 605.0 million at September 30, 2026 (CP-1 base). Liquidity effect: cash and undrawn revolver stood at 1,674.389 million at September 30, 2026 per the CP-1 base.

Status and subsequent events. The acquisition is closed; the allocation is preliminary; the purchase agreement text, the credit agreement amendments and the indenture supplements are named in the exhibit index but are not supplied, so conditions, guarantors and covenants for the new debt are Not supplied. ### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: 95.6% federal revenue, termination for convenience, backlog of 34.0 billion of which 6.2 billion is funded (10-K page:12, page:8; Q1 10-Q page:20). Mechanic: appropriations, continuing resolutions and option exercise determine conversion of backlog to cash; a lapse in funding delays awards and collections for all agencies at once. Implication: revenue is durable in normal budget conditions but its stress case is a funding disruption, not competition; the nearest dated exposure is the continuing resolution ending December 11, 2026. Countering item: a book-to-bill of 2.0x in the latest quarter, although contract awards were below the prior-year quarter (Q1 FY2027 release pages:1, 9).
2. Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: fixed-price share up from 26.7% to 33.2% and cost-plus-fee down from 60.5% to 52.8% between Q1 FY2026 and Q1 FY2027 (Q1 FY2027 release page:8). Mechanic: the contractor bears cost overruns on fixed-price work and loses automatic inflation pass-through. Implication: earnings and cash flow become more variable with execution; this is a year-over-year change in one quarter, and a counter-signal is the higher EBITDA margin reported for Q1 FY2027 in the CP-1 base.
3. Acquisition and leverage mechanics. Evidence: 2,642.7 million consideration funded by borrowings, 800.0 million term loan, 500.0 million notes (10-K pages:79, 88, 89). Mechanic: debt arrived before a full period of acquired earnings, so reported leverage in the CP-1 base is not a run-rate figure. Implication: deleveraging depends on cash flow after interest and on future acquisition appetite; conflicts logged above affect the price but not the financing amounts.
4. Concentration. Evidence: Department of War 53.6% of FY2026 revenue and ten contracts at 22.5% (10-K page:12, page:8). Mechanic: agency-specific budget decisions and re-competes. Implication: moderate contract concentration, high payer concentration.
5. Key uncertainties. Covenant levels, guarantors, ownership and control rights, acquired earnings, the effect of the fiscal-year change, and the renewal of the receivable sale program.
