### Credit view

CACI's fundamental credit profile is sound but more leveraged after its March 2026 acquisition, resting on a federally funded business that converts earnings into cash. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 1,674.389 million at September 30, 2026 against scheduled debt maturities of USD 46.750 million in FY2027, with EBITDA covering cash interest 5.60x over the twelve months to that date. The weakest point is leverage of 3.94x total debt to EBITDA, in a business that relies on one payer and on government funding and termination conditions. The outcome depends on deleveraging from cash flow while federal funding stays orderly; covenant terms and guarantors are not disclosed in the supplied sources and remain unverified.

### Key findings

1. CACI's leverage stepped up with the March 2026 acquisition and has only partly eased. Total debt to EBITDA was 3.04x at FY2025, 4.20x at FY2026 and 3.94x on the twelve months to September 30, 2026, and net debt to EBITDA was 2.93x, 4.04x and 3.72x [CP-1 financial base]. The acquired business entered earnings for under seven months of that latest period while its financing is fully in debt, so the reported ratio may overstate run-rate leverage; the size of the effect is Not supplied because no pro forma earnings are given [CP-1 financial base; CP-1A transaction summary]. Credit implication: leverage tolerance is the main variable, and it improves only if cash flow after interest is applied to debt.

2. CACI's liquidity is strong and its near-term maturity profile is light. Cash was USD 279.389 million and the undrawn revolver USD 1,395.0 million at September 30, 2026; scheduled maturities are USD 46.750 million in FY2027 and no more than USD 78.000 million a year until FY2031, when USD 1,706.750 million falls due, with USD 2,959.250 million after that [CP-1 financial base]. Credit implication: refinancing risk sits in FY2031 and beyond, and the revolver matures in November 2030. The receivable sale program is close to its USD 350.0 million cap and its stated term ends on December 18, 2026, so it should not be counted as spare liquidity [CP-1 financial base; CP-1A transaction summary].

3. CACI's cash generation covers its interest comfortably on a historical basis. EBITDA covered cash interest 6.67x in FY2025, 5.49x in FY2026 and 5.60x on the latest twelve months, and operating cash flow less capital expenditures was USD 775.961 million on the latest twelve months [CP-1 financial base]. The quarterly cash interest figure omits semi-annual note coupons, and the income statement interest charge for the latest quarter exceeds cash paid, so the historical cover will not hold if the interest run-rate stays at the higher level [CP-1 financial base]. Credit implication: debt service capacity is adequate but the cushion has narrowed since FY2025.

4. CACI depends on a single payer, which concentrates funding and contract risk. Federal customers were 95.6% of FY2026 revenue, the Department of War 53.6%, and the ten largest contracts 22.5%; the government may terminate contracts for convenience, and a continuing resolution funds the government fiscal year only through December 11, 2026 [CP-1A company and operating model; CP-1A timeline]. Credit implication: revenue visibility from the USD 34.0 billion backlog is conditional on funding and option exercise, and only USD 6.2 billion is funded [CP-1A company and operating model].

5. CACI's contract mix is moving toward more fixed-price work. Fixed-price revenue was 33.2% in the first quarter of FY2027 against 26.7% a year earlier, and cost-plus-fee fell from 60.5% to 52.8% [CP-1A company and operating model]. Mechanism: the contractor bears cost overruns on fixed-price work and loses automatic inflation pass-through. Countering evidence: management EBITDA margin rose from 11.7% to 13.3% between the two first quarters [CP-1 financial base]. Credit implication: earnings variability rises with execution, offset so far by margin gains.

6. CACI's creditor protections cannot be verified. The senior secured instruments (revolver, term loans) total USD 3,364.438 million of floating-rate principal with swaps covering USD 1,400.0 million notional, and USD 1,500.0 million of senior unsecured notes sit beside them [CP-1 financial base]. Covenant levels, definitions, guarantors and baskets are Not supplied; management states compliance with all financial covenants, which is an attributed claim [CP-0; CP-1A governance table]. Credit implication: headroom and default distance cannot be sized, and ranking by entity is not assessable.

7. Subsequent events are mixed. The Board approved a calendar fiscal year on October 20, 2026, so the next report covers a six-month transition period; the litigation judgment was vacated on October 21, 2026, though plaintiffs may seek further review; and fiscal 2027 guidance was raised on October 21, 2026 [CP-1A timeline; CP-1A conflicts]. Credit implication: period comparisons will change and a USD 42 million contingent claim has eased but is not closed.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, revenue is almost entirely federal and backlog-supported, which gives revenue visibility in normal budget conditions [CP-1A company and operating model] (Documentary Fact). Second, liquidity is ample: cash plus undrawn revolver of USD 1,674.389 million against light scheduled amortisation [CP-1 financial base] (Documentary Fact). Third, cash generation is solid, with operating cash flow less capital expenditures of USD 775.961 million and cash interest cover of 5.60x on the latest twelve months [CP-1 financial base] (Documentary Fact). Fourth, financial policy has been creditor-supportive on the record: no dividend has been paid, share repurchases were cut and the revolver balance fell from USD 660.0 million to USD 605.0 million in the latest quarter [CP-1A governance table] (Documentary Fact). Fifth, the margin trend is positive, with management EBITDA margin of 13.3% in the first quarter of FY2027 against 11.7% a year earlier [CP-1 financial base] (Documentary Fact). Management describes its execution as a differentiator, which is Management Language and is not adopted here [CP-1A company and operating model] (Management Language).

Weaknesses. First, leverage is higher after the acquisition, at 3.94x total debt to EBITDA on the latest twelve months against 3.04x at FY2025, and the effect of acquired earnings is unquantified [CP-1 financial base] (Documentary Fact; the overstatement of run-rate leverage is Analyst Interpretation). Second, payer concentration and funding risk: 95.6% federal revenue, termination for convenience and a continuing resolution ending December 11, 2026 [CP-1A company and operating model] (Documentary Fact). Third, fixed-price work is growing as a share of revenue, which transfers cost-overrun risk to the company [CP-1A company and operating model] (Analyst Interpretation). Fourth, floating-rate exposure: USD 1,964.438 million of floating-rate principal is not covered by swap notional, so higher rates would raise cash interest and lower cover [CP-1 financial base] (Analyst Interpretation). Fifth, the company is an active acquirer, with eight acquisitions in three fiscal years and a preliminary purchase price allocation for the latest, so leverage depends on future deal appetite [CP-1A governance table] (Analyst Interpretation). Sixth, the receivable sale program is at its cap with a stated term ending December 18, 2026 [CP-1 financial base] (Documentary Fact). Seventh, covenant terms, guarantors and ownership are not supplied, which limits the assessment and is not an adverse finding in itself [CP-0; CP-1A company profile] (Insufficient Information).
