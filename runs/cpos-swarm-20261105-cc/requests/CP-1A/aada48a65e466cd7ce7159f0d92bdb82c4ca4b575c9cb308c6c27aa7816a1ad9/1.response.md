### Business and transaction view

The company is a listed operating business, not a sponsor-owned credit, and there is no acquisition or leveraged buyout in the sources. The transactions that matter are financing, disposal and litigation settlements: a 700 senior notes issue in March 2026 that refinanced shorter notes, repayment of euro term loan principal funded partly by the sale of the Kuan Yin, Taiwan land, a revolver tranche that matured in October 2026, and two large state PFAS settlements. Business risk rests on three segments with different cycles: refrigerants (growth driven by regulation, now reversing from a one-off channel fill), titanium dioxide (a commodity-like pigment where pricing and a concentrated customer base drive earnings) and high-performance materials (smaller, with technology exposure to semiconductors and data centers). Ownership is public with no controlling holder disclosed in the supplied sources. The creditor-relevant change over the period is a lower dividend, lower gross debt after asset sales, higher unrecovered legal obligations, a Moody's downgrade to B1 with a stable outlook, and a continuing audit committee review overhang. Governance evidence is documentary and mixed rather than adverse; legal capacity cannot be assessed without the debt documents.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No acquisition, sponsor buyout, dividend recapitalisation or other change of control appears in the supplied sources; transaction type is therefore Insufficient Information as an ownership event and the relevant events are financing, disposal and settlement transactions. The one new debt issue in the period is the 7.875 percent senior notes due 2034, closed on March 12, 2026 in a private offering of 700 aggregate principal amount under the 2020 base indenture and a Fourth Supplemental Indenture, with Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas as trustee and "the guarantor named therein" (unnamed in the filings); proceeds were 690 net of 10 of fees (10-Q Q3 page:27, page:151; status: completed). The net proceeds, with cash on hand, redeemed 188 of the 5.750 percent senior notes due 2028 for about 190 and all 495 of the 5.375 percent senior notes due 2027 for 499 (page:27), which removed the 2027 maturity and left the 2028 notes at 595. The effect on leverage is neutral because 700 replaced 683 of principal; the effect on the maturity profile is to push 700 to 2034, and management expects annual interest expense to fall only about 3 (page:151, Management Language). Separately, the senior secured Euro term loan due August 2028 was reduced from EUR 415 million to EUR 135 million through repayments of EUR 280 million in the nine months, including EUR 140 million in April 2026 funded partly by Kuan Yin land sale proceeds (release Q1 page:4; 10-Q Q3 page:26). The U.S. dollar term loan had its maturity extended from August 2028 to October 2032 and its margin raised to adjusted Term SOFR plus 3.50 percent under the Fourth Amendment of October 15, 2025 (10-Q Q3 page:25-26, status: executed and in effect), and the Third Amendment of May 2, 2025 raised the revolver to 1,000 commitments with a temporary higher senior secured net leverage test through the quarter ended September 30, 2026 (page:25). Of those commitments, 220 matured on October 7, 2026, a subsequent event before the cutoff that the filings state as fact without saying whether it was extended or replaced (page:149). Liquidity use of proceeds: the Kuan Yin disposal, ten parcels with a purchase price of approximately 360 under January 2026 agreements (10-K page:16), closed with nine parcels in April 2026 for gross proceeds of 300 and net cash of 287, a gain of 266 in Q2 2026, and the tenth in September 2026 for total consideration of 56 and a gain of 50 (10-Q Q3 page:22); management states total net consideration of about 340 (release Q3 page:1). These three measures (agreement price, gross consideration and net consideration) are on different bases and are not a conflict. Litigation settlements are the other transactions: the New Jersey judicial consent order, entered in August 2026 and final in September 2026, sets payments totaling 875 over 25 years shared equally between Chemours and DuPont and Corteva, with 100 paid in September 2026 and the company's remaining share recorded at 176 on a net present value basis (10-Q Q3 page:91); the North Carolina settlement of September 2026 sets overall payments of 455 over 15 years with legal reserves of 205 recorded, and a first annual payment of 51 was made in October 2026, a subsequent event (page:116). The filings do not state whether the 455 is the company's share or the total for all parties. A 2026 Consent Decree with the EPA and the West Virginia environmental authority carries a 23 civil penalty over three years (page:111) and expanded off-site drinking water obligations. All these terms are the company's own descriptions: the executed settlement documents, the indentures and the Credit Agreement are not supplied.

### Credit translations

- **Customer concentration in titanium dioxide.** Evidence: ten largest customers over 44 percent and one over 20 percent of segment sales (F3). Mechanic: loss or repricing of one buyer moves segment volume and margin quickly because the product is a commodity-like pigment with a demand base tied to construction and packaging (F7). Implication: segment earnings, which recovered in Q3 2026 on pricing (F6), carry volatility that leverage should be tested against; the portfolio-wide customer breadth (F2) does not remove it.
- **Refrigerant earnings normalizing.** Evidence: the large rise in Opteon sales in FY2025 (F4) and the Q3 2026 decline attributed to lower aftermarket sales against elevated prior-year demand (F5, F6). Mechanic: a regulatory transition filled distribution channels once, so growth is front-loaded and the following years show a destocking air pocket. Implication: the strongest segment's earnings are falling while the weakest recovers, so the group earnings base is less stable than the segment margins suggest. Counter-evidence: management cites data center and semiconductor demand growth in the materials segment (release Q3 page:1).
- **Legacy obligations compete with debt service.** Evidence: the New Jersey and North Carolina settlements and the 2026 Consent Decree (F18, F19, F22), with 100 paid in September 2026 and 51 in October 2026. Mechanic: scheduled annual payments over 25 and 15 years are fixed cash calls recorded as accrued litigation at net present value, partly shielded by the cost-sharing MOU with a remaining cap (F13). Implication: free cash flow after settlements, not Adjusted EBITDA, is the relevant coverage test, and the recorded reserves do not cover matters judged reasonably possible but not estimable (F21).
- **Control and disclosure history.** Evidence: F14, F15, F16. Mechanic: restatement-type revisions and regulatory inquiries raise the risk of further adjustments and costs. Implication: a monitoring flag, not an adverse conclusion, because the revisions were assessed as immaterial and the weaknesses were remediated.
- **Near-term liquidity.** Evidence: availability of 953 included the 220 tranche that matured on October 7, 2026 (F27). Mechanic: the headline liquidity is reduced if the tranche lapsed, leaving 780 of commitments due in 2030 with a springing maturity linked to the 2028 and 2029 debt (F27). Implication: the 2028 refinancing must be addressed within the revolver's springing window; management's stated liquidity horizon is November 2027 (F28).
