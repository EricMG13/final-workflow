### Credit view

CCC Intelligent Solutions Holdings Inc. has a sound standalone credit profile today, but the pending take-private makes its post-closing credit unassessable. The strongest support is recurring subscription revenue at 96% of the total, leverage of 2.64x gross and 2.22x net on LTM Adjusted EBITDA of USD 484.9 million, free cash flow of USD 311.4 million and liquidity of USD 451.9 million against a single term loan due in 2032. The weakest point is that headline EBITDA adds back USD 126.7 million of share-based pay, so gross leverage is 3.58x once it is deducted, and that debt has funded shareholder returns. What decides the outcome is whether the existing term loan stays in place or is replaced by new buyer financing, which the pack does not disclose.

### Thesis

CCC is a cash-generative subscription software borrower whose moderate, mostly term-debt leverage and ample liquidity support its existing 2032 term loan, but whose headline earnings flatter leverage and whose creditor position will be reset by the take-private on terms the pack does not show. (Analyst Interpretation; basis: CP-1 leverage and liquidity figures, CP-1A transaction and governance rows.)

### Key findings

1. The existing capital structure is simple and moderately levered. CCC has one senior secured term loan with USD 1,281.2 million of principal at 2026-09-30 and a USD 250.0 million revolver that is undrawn, with USD 249.0 million available after USD 1.0 million of letters of credit [CP-1 debt register]. Gross leverage is 2.64x and net leverage 2.22x on LTM Adjusted EBITDA of USD 484.9 million, down from 2.96x and 2.71x at FY2025; the fall in net debt of USD 101.5 million over nine months is USD 91.7 million of cash build and USD 9.8 million of scheduled repayment [CP-1 leverage chart data; calculation K5]. (Documentary Fact for the inputs; the reading is Analyst Interpretation.)

2. Headline leverage rests on an earnings measure that adds back share-based pay. LTM Adjusted EBITDA adds back USD 126.7 million of share-based compensation and related payroll tax; after deducting it, gross leverage is 3.58x and net 3.01x, and at FY2025 the same sensitivity gave 5.07x gross [CP-1 financials and credit metrics]. The covenant definition that lenders use is not in the pack, so which of the two bases the lenders see is unknown. (Analyst Interpretation; covenant definition is Insufficient Information.)

3. Cash conversion and debt service are strong. LTM free cash flow is USD 311.4 million, 64.2% of Adjusted EBITDA, against USD 13.0 million of annual scheduled amortisation, and LTM Adjusted EBITDA covers cash paid for interest 6.69x (4.95x after the share-based pay deduction) [CP-1; calculations K3 and K1]. Capital spending is light at 5.9% of LTM revenue. (Documentary Fact for inputs; ratios are Analyst Interpretation.)

4. Revenue is recurring and contracted, with named limits. Software subscriptions are 96% of revenue; insurer contracts run about three to five years; remaining performance obligations were USD 1,808 million at 2026-09-30, of which USD 806 million falls within twelve months, against USD 1,891 million at year end; net dollar retention is 108% and gross dollar retention 98% [CP-1A company and operating model]. Total backlog is lower than at year end and renewals are excluded from it, so visibility is twelve-month rather than long-dated. (Documentary Fact.)

5. Liquidity and maturities leave no near-term wall. Cash was USD 202.9 million at 2026-09-30, liquidity including the revolver is USD 451.9 million, and the term loan has USD 3.3 million of remaining 2026 amortisation, USD 13.0 million a year for 2027 to 2031 and a USD 1,212.8 million balloon on 2032-01-23 [CP-1 liquidity and maturity chart data]. The springing leverage test applies only if revolver borrowings reach the threshold, and the revolver was undrawn. (Documentary Fact.)

6. Debt has funded shareholder returns. Term loan principal rose from USD 776.0 million at FY2024 to USD 1,291.0 million at FY2025, with new term loans of USD 525.0 million in 2025: USD 225.0 million alongside the EvolutionIQ acquisition and USD 300.0 million for an accelerated share repurchase [CP-1A governance table; calculation K5]. Leverage rose from 1.95x to 2.96x over FY2025 and has since fallen to 2.64x. No priming, uptiering, drop-down or non-pro-rata transaction appears in the sources read, but the credit agreement that would show the permitted capacity is not in the pack. (Documentary Fact; the willingness reading is Analyst Interpretation.)

7. The take-private is the decisive uncertainty. The merger agreement of 2026-10-14 converts each share to USD 7.75 in cash, is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, has no financing condition and carries fees of USD 149.52 million payable by CCC in stated cases and about USD 313.99 million payable by the buyer if it fails to fund [CP-1A transaction summary]. The equity price on the basic share count is about USD 4,581 million, against net debt of USD 1,078.3 million, so the buyer must fund a purchase price several times the existing net debt; how much of it is new debt, how the existing term loan is treated and what covenants follow are Not supplied. (Documentary Fact for the terms; the funding reading is Analyst Interpretation; financing terms are Insufficient Information.)

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, CCC has recurring, contracted revenue: 96% of revenue is subscription, insurer contracts average about three to five years and the customer base includes 27 of the top 30 automotive insurers, with retention at 108% net and 98% gross (Documentary Fact; CP-1A company and operating model). Second, CCC generates cash against light debt service: free cash flow of USD 311.4 million LTM, cash interest cover of 6.69x and a single term loan with USD 13.0 million of annual amortisation until 2032 (Documentary Fact; CP-1). Third, CCC has ample liquidity: USD 202.9 million of cash and USD 249.0 million of undrawn revolver, with the revolver's springing leverage test untested while undrawn (Documentary Fact; CP-1). Fourth, leverage has been trending down: gross leverage has fallen from 2.96x at FY2025 to 2.64x at LTM Q3 2026 while cash built by USD 91.7 million (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1).

Weaknesses. First, earnings quality: Adjusted EBITDA adds back USD 126.7 million of share-based pay and related payroll tax over the LTM period, and the lender definition is unknown, so gross leverage is 2.64x on the headline base and 3.58x on the stricter base (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1). Second, capital allocation: debt-funded repurchases of USD 300.0 million in December 2025 and repurchases from the principal equity owner in 2023 and 2025 show a willingness to return capital using the balance sheet (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1A governance table). Third, customer power and concentration: no customer exceeded 10% of FY2025 revenue, but the filing states a relatively small number of customers account for a significant portion of revenue, large customers have bargaining power and the company has had to cut average selling prices before; customer-level data is Not supplied (Insufficient Information on size; CP-1A). Fourth, floating-rate exposure: USD 531.2 million of the term loan is unhedged and the USD 750.0 million of swaps at about 3.94% expire on 2027-07-31, so a 100 basis point rise adds about USD 5.3 million of annual interest now and about USD 12.8 million after the swaps expire (Analyst Interpretation; calculation K2). Fifth, asset base: goodwill and intangible assets are USD 2,897.6 million, 80.4% of total assets, and tangible equity is negative USD 1,055.7 million, so asset coverage for creditors depends on the franchise and not on tangible assets; this is a mechanic and not a recovery conclusion (Analyst Interpretation; calculation K4). Sixth, change of control: the take-private leaves the post-closing leverage, ranking of the existing loan, covenants and sponsor behaviour Insufficient Information, and sponsor behaviour must not be inferred from the buyer's identity (Insufficient Information; CP-1A).

## Calculations

All inputs are CP-1 or CP-1A figures; amounts in USD thousands unless stated.

### K1 Cash interest cover

LTM Adjusted EBITDA 484,946 / LTM cash paid for interest 72,448 = 6.69x. After share-based pay: 358,259 / 72,448 = 4.95x. (CP-1 C4, C5.)

### K2 Rate sensitivity

- Unhedged principal at 2026-09-30: 1,281,216 − 750,000 swap notional = 531,216. A 1.00% rise: 531,216 x 0.01 = 5,312 a year, 7.3% of LTM cash interest of 72,448.
- After the swaps expire on 2027-07-31: 1,281,216 x 0.01 = 12,812 a year, 17.7% of LTM cash interest. The effect of resetting the fixed portion depends on SOFR then and is Not calculable.

### K3 Fixed-charge cover

(LTM Adjusted EBITDA 484,946 − purchases of software, equipment and property 67,253) = 417,693. Cash interest 72,448 + scheduled amortisation 13,033 = 85,481. Ratio = 417,693 / 85,481 = 4.89x. LTM free cash flow 311,375 / 13,033 = 23.9x scheduled amortisation. Capital spending 67,253 / revenue 1,139,163 = 5.9%.

### K4 Asset composition

Goodwill 1,955,551 + intangible assets, net 942,018 = 2,897,569; / total assets 3,602,061 = 80.4%. Total stockholders' equity 1,841,885 − 2,897,569 = −1,055,684 (tangible equity). Inputs: CP-1 balance sheet.

### K5 Leverage movement

- Term loan principal change over FY2025: 1,290,990 − 776,000 = 514,990, equal to proceeds 525,000 less repayments 10,010; of the proceeds, 225,000 was incremental term loans in January 2025 and 300,000 in December 2025, so 300,000 / 525,000 = 57.1% funded the accelerated share repurchase.
- Gross leverage: FY2024 1.95x, FY2025 2.96x, LTM Q3 2026 2.64x. Net debt: 1,179,798 at FY2025 and 1,078,290 at 2026-09-30, a fall of 101,508 = cash increase 91,734 plus principal repaid 9,774.

### K6 Earnings headroom (illustration, not covenant)

Gross leverage on principal 1,281,216 reaches 4.00x if EBITDA falls to 1,281,216 / 4 = 320,304, a fall of 34.0% from 484,946 (headline), or 10.6% from 358,259 (after the add-back). On the headline base 3.5x is reached at EBITDA of 366,062, a fall of 24.5%; on the after-add-back base leverage is already 3.58x, above 3.5x. The 3.5 figure is the term loan's prepayment trigger on a lender-defined leverage ratio, so these ratios are not comparable with it.
