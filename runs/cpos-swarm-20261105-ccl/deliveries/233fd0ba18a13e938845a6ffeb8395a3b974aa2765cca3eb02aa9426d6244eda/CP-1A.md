### Business and transaction view

Carnival Corporation Ltd. is a Bermuda-incorporated, NYSE-listed cruise operator with eight brands, 94 ships and 272,380 lower-berth capacity at November 30, 2025, a tour business in Alaska and the Yukon, and seven owned or operated ports and destinations, which together form a cruise and destination business sold through travel agents and company websites (10-K page:10, page:12, page:15; Q3 10-Q page:1). Evidence: ticket revenue of 13,825 and onboard and other revenue of 7,438 for the nine months to August 31, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:2), and customer deposits of 7,639 (Q3 release page:9). Mechanism: guests pay deposits months in advance and the balance before sailing, so deposits are collected before cost is incurred and form a large current liability, which the company describes as the reason for a structural working capital deficit (Q3 10-Q page:25). The third quarter carries the largest share of operating income (2,220 of the nine-month 3,678, or 60.4 percent), so earnings are seasonal and credit-relevant events in the northern summer weigh disproportionately. Creditor read: revenue visibility is high because of advance bookings (management states 2027 booked occupancy and pricing at record levels, Q3 release page:1, management language), margin resilience depends on fuel and commissions, and cash conversion is supported by deposits, but a fleet-wide shock is uninsured for loss of earnings (10-K page:19).

The transaction history that matters is structural. The dual listed company of Carnival Corporation (Panama) and Carnival plc (England and Wales), operated as a single economic enterprise with identical boards but separate legal identities (10-K page:7), was unified in May 2026 under Carnival Corporation Ltd. (Bermuda), listed solely on the NYSE, with Carnival plc shares exchanged one-for-one (Q3 10-Q page:5). The group states there were no material changes to its business after the unification (Q3 10-Q page:5, management language). Over fiscal 2025 the company refinanced heavily: it issued six series of senior unsecured notes, took 1.0 billion of unsecured term loans and a 4.5 billion revolving facility, and prepaid 11.6 billion of debt including all secured term loans, with 409 of extinguishment and modification costs (10-K page:80, page:81, page:63). In the nine months to August 31, 2026 it issued no debt, settled the 1.1 billion convertible notes in shares and cash of 500, and redeemed the 7.00% notes due 2029 (500) with cash on hand (Q3 10-Q page:3, page:10; Q3 release page:3). The effect is a simpler, fully unsecured debt stack with lower interest cost, but one in which several subsidiary guarantees were also released (Q3 10-Q page:10). No sponsor is identified, no controlling holder is disclosed and no dividend recapitalisation is documented in the supplied sources. Distributions resumed with a dividend declared in December 2025 and a 2.5 billion buyback authorized in March 2026, funded in the nine months from operating cash flow rather than new debt (10-K page:62; Q1 release page:4; Q3 10-Q page:3, page:28). Behaviour read at issuer level: Supportive on deleveraging and reporting transparency, Neutral or Mixed on capital allocation because debt is being repaid while distributions rise, and unresolved on the release of security and guarantees because the documents that permit and govern it are not supplied.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Unification and redomiciliation (status: completed May 2026). The boards of Carnival Corporation and Carnival plc recommended unifying the dual listed company under Carnival Corporation as the single listed parent, with Carnival plc shareholders receiving shares on a one-for-one basis and a move of incorporation from Panama to Bermuda; the plan was described as expected to preserve shareholder voting and economic rights (10-K page:7, management language). It was subject to shareholder, regulatory and UK court approvals and shareholder meetings in April 2026 (10-K page:8). The Q2 10-Q states it was completed in May 2026 (Q2 10-Q page:5) and the exhibit list of the Q3 10-Q includes a termination agreement dated May 6, 2026 between the corporation and Carnival plc and the memorandum and bye-laws of Carnival Corporation Ltd. (Q3 10-Q page:28). Uses of proceeds and debt raised: none, it is a share exchange with related costs shown in equity (Q3 10-Q page:4). Liquidity effect: not stated in the sources. Creditor mechanics: debt that was previously issued or guaranteed by "substantially the same entities" remains so (Q3 10-Q page:10), the obligor perimeter for a planned shelf is Carnival Corporation Ltd. with Carnival UK Ltd. (Q3 10-Q page:26), and the treatment of the former Carnival plc debt guarantees is not described. The sources do not give debt-document terms, so legal capacity for any future priming or release is Insufficient Information.

New debt issues, fiscal 2025 (status: completed). Senior unsecured notes: 1.3 billion 5.13% due 2029, 1.0 billion 5.75% due 2030, 1.0 billion 5.88% due 2031, 1.2 billion 4.13% euro notes due 2031, 3.0 billion 5.75% due 2032 and 2.0 billion 6.13% due 2033; unsecured term loans maturing in 2027 of 0.4 billion, 0.3 billion and 0.3 billion at SOFR plus 1.13%, 1.25% and 1.38%; a 4.5 billion unsecured multi-currency revolving credit facility maturing June 2030 with a 1.0 billion accordion; 0.8 billion of export credit borrowings (10-K page:80, page:81). Use of proceeds: with cash on hand, they prepaid 7.63% notes due 2026, 5.75% notes due 2027, first-priority secured term loans due 2027 and 2028, 10.38% senior priority notes due 2028, 6.00% notes due 2029 and 10.50% notes due 2030, an aggregate 11.6 billion, with 409 of debt extinguishment and modification costs including 271 premium paid (10-K page:81). Liquidity effect: 6.4 billion of liquidity at November 30, 2025 with 4.5 billion revolver availability (10-K page:59). Maturity effect: the new notes mature in 2029 to 2033 (10-K page:79).

Fiscal 2026 to August 31, 2026 (status: completed unless stated). No new debt issued (Q3 10-Q page:3). The 1.1 billion 5.75% convertible notes were settled in December 2025: 69.1 million shares issued and 500 cash (Q3 10-Q page:10). The 7.00% notes due 2029 (500) became unsecured in June 2026 and were then redeemed with cash on hand, with 23 of extinguishment costs (Q3 10-Q page:10; Q3 release page:3). The 7.88% notes due 2027 and 4.00% notes due 2028 are no longer secured, and the company states it has no remaining secured debt (Q3 10-Q page:10; Q3 release page:3, management language). Several unsecured notes, loans and export credit facilities are no longer subject to subsidiary guarantees (Q3 10-Q page:10). Rating events: a Moody's upgrade with a positive outlook (Q2 release page:3) and an S&P upgrade giving a second investment grade rating (Q3 release page:3); levels are not supplied. Planned: a Form S-3 expected on September 29, 2026 for debt securities guaranteed by Carnival UK Ltd. (Q3 10-Q page:26, status: expected, not confirmed as filed). Total debt moved from 27,383 to 24,559 and liquidity from 6,428 to 5,720 (CP-1). Shareholder-return actions: dividend reinstated in December 2025 at 0.15 per share quarterly; initial 2.5 billion buyback announced in March 2026 without expiry; dividends of 618 and repurchases of 929 in the nine months (10-K page:62; Q1 release page:4; Q3 10-Q page:3).

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: advance bookings and customer deposits of 7,639 (Q3 release page:9) with record 2027 booked occupancy and pricing stated by management (Q3 release page:1). Mechanic: deposits and balance payments arrive before voyage cost, so cash converts early and revenue is visible several months ahead; most revenue is ticket (65.0 percent) and sourced largely from United States guests (55.8 percent of nine-month revenue, Q3 10-Q page:8). Implication: revenue is durable in ordinary conditions, but a sourcing-market or geopolitical shock lands on a concentrated base and on a peak quarter that earns 60.4 percent of nine-month operating income; the Q2 release cites disruption in the Mediterranean from the Middle East conflict (Q2 release page:2). Countering conflict: none logged.
- Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: fuel 615 against 451 in the quarter and operating income down despite revenue growth (Q3 10-Q page:2). Mechanic: fuel cost per metric ton rose to 826 from 607 and is partly offset by lower fuel consumption per thousand ALBDs (26.9 against 28.0, Q3 10-Q page:21). Implication: earnings are exposed to fuel; the sensitivity of 59 per 10 percent move in fuel price for the fourth quarter is a management figure (Q3 release page:5).
- Concentration and structure. Evidence: obligor group revenue of 9,636 against consolidated 21,263 for nine months (Q3 10-Q page:26, page:2). Mechanic: guaranteed shelf debt would be backed by a group earning under half of consolidated revenue. Implication: ranking depends on guarantor coverage that the documents must confirm.
- Sponsor alignment and control. Evidence: no sponsor or controlling holder identified; distributions funded from operations (above). Mechanic: shareholder-return policy is set by management and boards. Implication: alignment is with public shareholders; creditor protection rests on covenants and rating-linked pricing, not on an owner's support.
- Key uncertainties: legal terms (indentures, guarantee release triggers), covenant headroom, the cause of the guarantee releases and rating levels.
