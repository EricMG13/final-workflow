### Credit view

Carnival Corporation Ltd. has a strong and improving fundamental credit profile, but its improvement so far comes from repaying debt rather than from earnings growth. Its strongest support is visible, deposit-funded demand (customer deposits of USD 7,639 million) and liquidity of USD 5,720 million with debt that is now unsecured and falling, to USD 24,559 million at August 31, 2026. The weakest point is that LTM EBITDA of USD 7,311 million is almost unchanged from USD 7,273 million for fiscal 2025, while fuel costs rise, loss of earnings is uninsured, and shareholder returns and USD 18.3 billion of ship commitments compete for cash. What decides the outcome is whether EBITDA grows enough to keep net debt to EBITDA, now 3.19x, falling while distributions rise.

### Key findings

First, leverage fell because debt fell, not because earnings grew. Total debt (principal) fell from USD 27,383 million at November 30, 2025 to USD 24,559 million at August 31, 2026, while LTM EBITDA (operating income plus depreciation and amortization, unadjusted) rose only from USD 7,273 million to USD 7,311 million; total debt to EBITDA moved from 3.77x to 3.36x and net debt to EBITDA from 3.50x to 3.19x [CP-1, Chart data: leverage]. Because the repayment was funded from cash, net debt rose by USD 12 million over the third quarter, from USD 23,327 million to USD 23,339 million, while the company paid dividends of USD 204 million and repurchased shares worth USD 549 million in the quarter [CP-1, Financial base view]. For creditors this means the next step down in leverage requires either further repayment, which competes with distributions, or EBITDA growth.

Second, near-term liquidity is sound but maturities depend on refinancing or cash flow. Liquidity is USD 5,720 million (cash USD 1,220 million plus USD 4,500 million available under a revolving facility maturing in June 2030) against current debt of USD 2,036 million, and no letters of credit usage is printed [CP-1, Chart data: liquidity]. Scheduled principal is USD 301 million for the remainder of 2026, USD 2,517 million in 2027, USD 3,961 million in 2028, USD 3,631 million in 2029 and USD 2,885 million in 2030 [CP-1, Chart data: debt maturities], which total USD 13,295 million through 2030 against LTM cash from operations less capital expenditure of USD 3,176 million. Management states the company has two investment grade ratings, and the revolver pricing is linked to ratings, but rating levels are not supplied [CP-1A, Transaction summary and new debt issues]. The implication is that debt service is comfortable but principal reduction at the recent pace relies on cash flow, and a larger part relies on market access.

Third, operating earnings are flat while costs rise, which limits cushion. In the third quarter revenue was USD 8,435 million against USD 8,153 million, while operating income was USD 2,220 million against USD 2,271 million, as fuel expense rose to USD 615 million from USD 451 million [CP-1, Financial base view]. Net income benefited from lower interest (USD 285 million in the quarter against USD 317 million) and lower debt extinguishment costs. Accrual EBITDA to interest expense is 6.21x on an LTM basis, but cash interest cover exists only for fiscal 2025 at 6.06x [CP-1, Chart data: interest cover]. Management guides fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA to approximately USD 7.14 billion, a management forecast rather than a result [CP-1, Latest quarter and after]. Fuel exposure is therefore the main near-term earnings swing factor for debt service.

Fourth, the business model gives revenue visibility but concentrates risk in time and place. Revenue is 65.0 percent ticket and 35.0 percent onboard, 55.8 percent of nine-month revenue comes from United States guests, and the third quarter produces 60.4 percent of nine-month operating income; customer deposits fund operations and form part of a working capital deficit of USD 9.1 billion [CP-1A, Company and operating model]. The company states it carries no insurance for loss of earnings or revenue [CP-1A, Company and operating model]. A disruption in the peak quarter or in United States demand would reach cash flow directly, and deposits that include refundable amounts (no split is given) could be called.

Fifth, the debt structure became simpler, with less structural protection for some holders (an interpretation). After the May 2026 unification into one Bermuda parent, all debt is unsecured, the 7.00% notes were redeemed, and notes, loans and export credit facilities of about USD 12,520 million on the recast basis moved to the no-subsidiary-guarantee category [CP-1, Debt schedule; CP-1A, Transaction summary and new debt issues]. The obligor group planned for a guaranteed shelf earned 45.3 percent of consolidated nine-month revenue [CP-1, Perimeter and reporting basis]. The cause of the releases, indenture terms and guarantor lists are not supplied, so ranking by legal entity is not assessable and conclusions about structure are provisional.

Sixth, capital allocation is the main discretionary variable. Dividends of USD 618 million and repurchases of USD 929 million in nine months equal 48.9 percent of cash from operations less capital expenditure; management targets distributing more than 40 percent of cash from operations by 2029 and a net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio of 2.75x, and ship commitments have risen to USD 18.3 billion against USD 10.7 billion of undrawn export credit [CP-1, Cash flow and capital returns; CP-1A, Capital allocation and governance behaviour]. For comparison, reaching 2.75x on the unadjusted metric used here would need net debt lower by USD 3,234 million at constant EBITDA, or EBITDA of USD 8,487 million at constant net debt (see Calculations); the management target is defined on adjusted EBITDA and a minimum cash balance, so this is an illustration of scale only. Creditors depend on management choosing deleveraging over faster distributions, and the documents that could constrain that choice are not supplied.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The business has high demand visibility: customer deposits of USD 7,639 million, record 2027 booked occupancy and pricing as stated by management, and 93 percent of 2026 booked at the time of the second quarter release [CP-1A, Company and operating model], so revenue and cash arrive ahead of voyage cost. The group is diversified across eight brands and two segments, with 94 ships and 272,380 lower-berth capacity, so a problem at one brand does not stop the group [CP-1A, Company profile]. Debt is falling, now unsecured, and mostly fixed-rate (52 percent fixed plus 33 percent euro fixed), with euro debt (43 percent including floating) matched against euro-functional operations, which limits rate and currency sensitivity [CP-1, Debt schedule]. Liquidity of USD 5,720 million exceeds current debt of USD 2,036 million, and USD 10.7 billion of undrawn export credit pre-funds part of the ship commitments [CP-1, Liquidity].

Weaknesses. EBITDA growth has stalled at the operating line while fuel and other costs rise, so leverage improvement relies on repayment [CP-1, Financial base view]. Loss of earnings is uninsured and the peak quarter and United States guests concentrate exposure [CP-1A, Company and operating model]. Ship commitments of USD 18.3 billion, of which USD 11.4 billion falls after 2030, and rising shareholder returns place long-dated claims on cash flow [CP-1, Claims outside the debt figure]. Creditor protection is less certain than the ratings story suggests, because security and several guarantees were released and the governing documents, covenant ratios and rating levels are not supplied [CP-1A, Monitoring items the source set lacks]. The headline leverage measure used by the company changed definition between releases, and fiscal 2025 adjusted EBITDA is missing, so the company's own leverage cannot be rebuilt [CP-1A, Conflicts logged].
