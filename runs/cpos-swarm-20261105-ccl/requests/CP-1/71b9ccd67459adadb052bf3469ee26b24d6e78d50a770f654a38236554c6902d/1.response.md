### Financial base view

Carnival's financial base at August 31, 2026 is a large, cash-generative cruise operator whose reported leverage is improving through deliberate debt repayment, with earnings broadly flat at the operating line and a growing claim on cash from ship commitments and shareholder returns. Evidence: revenue was 8,435 in the quarter against 8,153 a year earlier and 21,263 for nine months against 20,292, while operating income was 2,220 against 2,271 in the quarter and 3,678 against 3,748 for nine months (Q3 10-Q page:2). Fuel expense rose to 615 from 451 in the quarter (Q3 10-Q page:2), and the MD&A attributes 149 of the operating expense increase to higher fuel prices (Q3 10-Q page:22). Mechanism: pricing and onboard spend are growing faster than capacity, but fuel and other costs absorb the gain at the operating line, so the credit improvement comes from the financing side. Net income rose to 2,725 for nine months from 2,348, helped by interest expense net of capitalized interest falling to 862 from 1,034 and debt extinguishment costs falling to 23 from 366 (Q3 10-Q page:2).

On the metrics built here, EBITDA (operating income plus depreciation and amortization, unadjusted) for the twelve months to August 31, 2026 is 7,311 against 7,273 for fiscal 2025, an increase of 38, while principal debt fell by 2,824 to 24,559 (Q3 10-Q page:9). Total debt to EBITDA therefore moved from 3.77 to 3.36 and net debt to EBITDA from 3.50 to 3.19. The improvement is a debt-reduction effect, not an earnings effect. Between May 31, 2026 and August 31, 2026 the net debt figure rose by 12 to 23,339, because the 1,011 reduction in principal debt was funded from cash, which fell by 1,023 to 1,220, while the company also paid dividends of 204 and repurchased shares worth 549 in the quarter (Q3 10-Q page:2, page:4, page:9). Creditor read: the next leg of deleveraging depends on EBITDA growth and on how much of operating cash flow is directed to shareholders, since management targets distribution of more than 40 percent of cash from operations (approximately $14 billion through 2029) and a 2.75x net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio (Q1 release page:3, page:4), against a company-reported 3.1x at May 31, 2026 (Q2 release page:3).

Structure and liquidity: all remaining debt is unsecured after the 7.00% notes due 2029 were redeemed and the 7.88% 2027 and 4.00% 2028 notes were released from security (Q3 10-Q page:10; Q3 release page:3), which improves flexibility but leaves the 20.6 billion net book value of ships under negative pledges (Q3 10-Q page:10). Liquidity is 5,720 (cash 1,220 plus 4,500 available under the revolving facility, which runs to June 2030), against a stated minimum liquidity covenant of 1,500 (Q3 10-Q page:10, page:25). Undrawn export credit facilities of 10.7 billion fund ship deliveries and are not general liquidity. Ship commitments rose to 18.3 billion from 11.9 billion at November 30, 2025, almost entirely in the "thereafter" bucket (11.4 billion), consistent with the three Princess LNG ship orders announced for delivery in 2035, 2038 and 2039 (Q2 release page:4); the link between the order and the increase is an inference, since the 10-Q does not itemize the order.

Limitations that restrict conclusions: fiscal 2025 adjusted EBITDA is not supplied, so every leverage and coverage figure uses unadjusted operating income plus depreciation and amortization and is not the company's measure; interim cash paid for interest is not printed; covenant ratios are not printed; issuer and guarantor by instrument are not printed. Confidence is Medium overall: reported statements are High, derived leverage and coverage are Medium.

### Perimeter and reporting basis

- Reporting entity. The 10-K and the first quarter report are joint reports of Carnival Corporation and Carnival plc with statements titled "CARNIVAL CORPORATION & PLC" (10-K page:63; Q1 10-Q page:2). The second and third quarter reports are those of Carnival Corporation Ltd. after the unification completed in May 2026 (Q2 10-Q page:5; Q3 10-Q page:5). The consolidated group is the same: the Q3 10-Q states "There have been no material changes to our business, including strategy, underlying assets and operations" (Q3 10-Q page:5). Amounts for fiscal 2025 and the nine months of 2025 are therefore used as the same group, a judgment based on that statement and on the equity statement showing the share exchange (Q3 10-Q page:4).
- Obligor perimeter. The Q3 10-Q gives summarized information for the obligor group of Carnival Corporation Ltd. and Carnival UK Ltd. for a planned guaranteed debt shelf (Q3 10-Q page:26): revenues of 9,636 for nine months to August 31, 2026 against consolidated revenues of 21,263, and 12,910 for the year to November 30, 2025 against 26,622 (10-K page:63). The obligor group therefore earned under half of consolidated revenue (45.3 percent of nine-month revenue, 48.5 percent of fiscal 2025) and 1,604 of nine-month operating income against 3,678 consolidated. At August 31, 2026 it was owed 3,093 by non-obligor subsidiaries and had noncurrent liabilities of 22,022. This suggests that creditors of the parent-level notes rely in part on intercompany balances and subsidiary guarantees (an inference); the guarantor list per instrument is not supplied.
- Reclassification. Fiscal 2025 comparatives in the Q3 10-Q separate noncontrolling interests of 14 from additional paid-in capital (17,253 against 17,267 in the 10-K); total equity is 12,284 in both (10-K page:64; Q3 10-Q page:3, page:5). Net income used here is the total before noncontrolling interests (1,923 for the quarter) unless stated.
