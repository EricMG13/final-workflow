### Business and transaction view

CCO Holdings is a holding company whose principal assets are the equity of its operating subsidiaries, which deliver Spectrum Internet, Mobile, TV and Voice to residential and business customers in 41 states under a single reportable segment. It is wholly owned by CCH I Holdings, LLC and sits inside Charter's chain; it has no sponsor, no listed equity and no public ownership of its own. Its business is a mature, subscription-based, capital-intensive network operator: about 89% of revenue is monthly subscription fees, residential revenue is 77.74% of FY2025 revenue, and revenue is drifting down slightly (Q2 2026 revenue 13,526 against 13,766) because Internet and video customers are eroding faster than mobile lines and commercial revenue grow. The creditor-relevant structure is the more important feature. Of the issuer's 93,845 of principal debt at June 30, 2026, 67,111 is secured and guaranteed by the issuer and the operating group, while its own 26,734 of unsecured notes are structurally and effectively subordinated, and the issuer's subsidiaries are the primary funding source for Charter's buybacks through distributions. The transaction that matters is the pending Cox acquisition by Charter and Charter Holdings: the issuer's filings say Cox's unsecured notes are expected to become guaranteed and secured debt and Cox entities to become Obligors, which would add claims ahead of the issuer's unsecured notes while the filings do not show where Cox's earnings will sit. The Liberty Broadband Combination is a second pending parent-level transaction. Nothing is supplied after July 24, 2026, so closing status, Cox financing and any new issuance since June are unknown.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The principal transaction is the pending Cox acquisition by Charter and Charter Holdings, not by the issuer. On May 16, 2025, Charter, Charter Holdings and Cox Enterprises, Inc. entered into a Transaction Agreement under which Charter buys 100% of certain Cox Communications subsidiaries that conduct its commercial fiber and managed IT and cloud services businesses, and Cox Enterprises contributes Cox Communications' residential cable business to Charter Holdings, with Cox Enterprises paying $1.00 for a new class of Charter Class C common stock (10-Q Jun 2026 page:8). Consideration is 3.5 billion in cash from Charter for the equity sale and, for the contribution, cash from Charter Holdings plus convertible preferred units of Charter Holdings with a 6.0 billion liquidation preference paying 6.875% and about 33.6 million Charter Holdings common units; the cash from Charter Holdings is 650 million in the 2026 10-Qs and 500 million in the 10-K and the Q3 2025 10-Q (10-Q Jun 2026 page:9; 10-K page:28; 10-Q Sep 2025 page:9). The combined entity will assume Cox Communications' approximately 12.4 billion of net debt and finance leases (10-Q Jun 2026 page:9), a figure the 10-K gave as approximately 12.6 billion on a pro forma basis for Cox refinancings between signing and closing (10-K page:28). The status is pending: the June 2026 10-Q uses the future tense throughout, lists the ability to satisfy closing conditions among its risks and gives no closing date; the 10-K lists conditions including Charter stockholder approvals, expiry of the Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period and approvals of the Federal Communications Commission and certain local franchise, state franchising and state public utility commission authorities (10-K page:61). Which approvals have been received, the end date and the termination fee amounts are not in the pack (the 10-K says only that Charter "could owe a substantial termination fee" (10-K page:62)).

For creditors the key text is the issuer's own: the 10-Q says the company is "also required to fund approximately $4.2 billion of cash purchase price at the closing of the Cox Transactions" (10-Q Jun 2026 page:27), while the 10-K says "Charter is obligated to fund $4.0 billion of cash consideration under the Transaction Agreement which we expect to fund by incurring indebtedness" (10-K page:64). After closing the issuer "presently expect[s]" that Cox's unsecured notes assumed at closing "will constitute Guaranteed and Secured Debt", that Cox Communications and substantially all of its subsidiaries "will become Obligors", and that all or part of the approximately 4.2 billion of indebtedness issued to finance the cash payment "may constitute Guaranteed and Secured Debt when issued" (10-Q Jun 2026 page:34). Charter and its subsidiaries, including CCO Holdings, are also subject to restrictions on the conduct of their business before closing, including in certain cases the ability to incur certain indebtedness (10-K page:65). The contribution is to Charter Holdings (10-Q Jun 2026 page:8), and the filings do not say whether Cox's operating results will be consolidated in CCO Holdings' statements after closing or sit elsewhere in Charter's structure. That is Insufficient Information, not an adverse conclusion; it matters because Cox debt could be guaranteed inside the Obligor Group, which the issuer guarantees, while Cox earnings might not be in the issuer's own statements. The cash-purchase financing is described only as debt to be incurred; no sources and uses, commitment papers, rating agency actions or liquidity effect are given. Cox transition costs are already in operating results (65 in Q2 2026 and 89 in 6M 2026) with 34 and 37 of transition capital expenditure (10-Q Jun 2026 page:14 to page:15, page:37).

The second pending transaction is the Liberty Broadband Combination, a merger agreement dated November 12, 2024 under which Charter acquires Liberty Broadband Corporation through a merger and upstream merger, with Liberty Broadband becoming a wholly owned subsidiary of Charter (10-Q Jun 2026 page:28). Pending its closing, Charter buys Charter Class A shares from Liberty Broadband monthly, and in May 2026 Charter advanced a term loan of approximately 359 million to Liberty Broadband (10-Q Jun 2026 page:29 to page:30). The 10-K expects additional debt "in connection with the Cox Transactions and Liberty Broadband Combination" and the assumption of indebtedness of Liberty Broadband and its subsidiaries (10-K page:52). The amount and ranking of any Liberty Broadband debt are not stated.

New debt issues in the periods covered: in September 2025 Charter Operating and Charter Communications Operating Capital Corp. jointly issued 1.25 billion of 5.850% senior secured notes due December 2035 at 99.932% and 750 million of 6.700% senior secured notes due December 2055 at 99.832%, with proceeds used for general corporate purposes including repayment of the 6.150% senior secured notes due 2026 and potential buybacks, and a loss on extinguishment of about 28 million (10-K page:155). In January 2026 CCO Holdings and CCO Holdings Capital Corp. jointly issued 1.75 billion of 7.000% senior notes due February 2033 and 1.25 billion of 7.375% senior notes due February 2036, both at par, with proceeds for general corporate purposes including the call of 750 million of the 5.500% notes due 2026 and part (2.25 billion) of the 5.125% notes due 2027 (10-K page:155; 10-Q Mar 2026 page:11). In November 2025 the equipment installment plan facility was amended to extend maturity to November 3, 2029 and raise capacity to 2.0 billion (10-K page:161). In the second quarter of 2026 the company repurchased 1.2 billion of principal of various series on the open market at a gain of about 243 million (10-Q Jun 2026 page:11). No new notes are reported for the first half of 2026 other than the January issuance, and no source reports issuance after June 30, 2026.
