### Business and transaction view

Century Communities, Inc. is a US single-family homebuilder that operates in 16 states and over 45 markets under two brands, Century Communities and Century Complete, with a captive mortgage, title, insurance and escrow business and a small multi-family rental development segment in Colorado. Revenue is almost wholly home sales, which depend on affordability, mortgage rates and the use of incentives; the company itself ties its latest price decline to incentives and its lower deliveries to slower absorption. The transaction that matters most to a creditor is the October 7, 2026 purchase of the assets of HistoryMaker Homes in Dallas for about $160.2 million of initial cash, which is a post-balance-sheet event that adds communities and deliveries but also a cash call whose funding is not stated, coming days after the September 30, 2026 revolver amendment that raised commitments to $1.2 billion. Ownership is public with no sponsor; the Executive Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, who are brothers, hold a documented 13.9% stake and have employment agreements with change-of-control severance. No dividend recapitalisation, related-party leakage or creditor-adverse transaction is documented in the sources, but the proxy material and debt agreements that would show control rights, baskets and permitted payments are not supplied, so legal capacity is not assessed. The facts that move the credit most are demand and margin resilience, funding of the acquisition and shareholder returns while operating cash flow is negative, and the structural subordination of the unsecured notes to secured construction and warehouse debt.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transaction of record after the balance sheet date is the acquisition, completed on October 7, 2026, of substantially all of the assets and certain liabilities of HMH Lifestyles, L.P., doing business as HistoryMaker Homes, a Dallas-area homebuilder, for initial cash consideration of approximately $160.2 million, subject to customary holdbacks and post-closing adjustments (Q3 10-Q page:7, page:34). It adds 29 active communities to the Texas segment (Q3 10-Q page:7). The company says it will increase its Dallas division's active communities from 12 to 41 as of September 30, 2026 and triple trailing-twelve-month deliveries from 211 to 669 homes, and its outlook includes 100 deliveries from the acquired business in the fourth quarter (Q3 release page:1, page:3); these are Management Language, not filed fact. The company also states it will keep a land relationship with HistoryMaker's sister land development company (Q3 release page:2). Because the deal closed after September 30, 2026, the acquired assets and assumed liabilities are not in the balance sheet, purchase accounting is incomplete, and the filing does not state how the cash consideration is funded. This is a subsequent event and it is kept out of every September 30, 2026 figure. Parties other than the target, any sponsor, a use-of-proceeds statement, and the effect on liquidity are Not supplied; the missing funding statement matters because revolver borrowings already stood at $456.0 million and nine-month operating cash flow was negative (financial base handoff).

New debt and financing events are as follows. On September 30, 2026 the company signed the First Amendment to its Credit Agreement dated November 1, 2024 (administrative agent U.S. Bank National Association): commitments rose from $1.0 billion to $1.2 billion, maturity for participating extending lenders moved to November 1, 2030 with $100.0 million still maturing November 1, 2028, new lenders were added, certain financial covenants were revised, and the 0.10% credit spread adjustment was eliminated (Q3 10-Q page:16). The revised covenant terms are not supplied. In September 2025 the company issued $500.0 million of 6.625% senior notes due September 2033 and extinguished $500.0 million of 6.750% senior notes due 2027 at 100% of principal, for $511.4 million including accrued interest, with a loss on extinguishment of $1.4 million (10-K page:75). Earlier, the revolver was raised from $900.0 million to $1.0 billion on April 22, 2025 (10-K page:77). No new bond issue after September 2025 appears in the sources. The mortgage repurchase facilities, construction loans and revolver are described in the financial base handoff.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability and margin. Evidence: Q3 2026 average price down from 384.2 to 345.0 thousand and gross margin 15.0% against 17.9%, with the company citing incentives and slower absorption (Q3 10-Q page:35, page:37). Mechanic: price cuts fall straight to margin on a cost base that is partly fixed (selling, general and administrative expense rose to 14.1% of home sales revenue). Implication: earnings and cash flow are exposed to further rate rises; the leverage figures in the financial base already reflect lower earnings. Countering evidence: adjusted homebuilding gross margin of 19.3% and a stated backlog of 1,106 homes (Q3 10-Q page:37).
- Cash conversion and liquidity. Evidence: operating cash outflow of 267.475 in nine months 2026 and inventory up to 3,744.023 (Q3 10-Q page:2, page:3). Mechanic: inventory is funded by the revolver, whose availability is subject to covenants that are not supplied. Implication: liquidity is adequate on reported figures but sensitive to the unpublished covenant tests and to the HistoryMaker cash call.
- Acquisition and capital allocation. Evidence: repurchases that the company says it finances with cash and the revolver, and a 160.2 million acquisition seven days after a covenant amendment (Q3 10-Q page:7, page:16, page:62). Mechanic: external growth and returns compete with deleveraging. Implication: monitoring posture should follow post-acquisition leverage and any change in repurchase pace. The company has not shown a deleveraging plan in the sources (Insufficient Information).
- Structural position. Evidence: unsecured notes guaranteed by operating subsidiaries and effectively subordinated to secured debt of the guarantors; Financial Services subsidiaries do not guarantee (Q3 10-Q page:49). Mechanic: warehouse and construction lenders have first claim on their collateral; recovery prospects for unsecured claims depend on inventory value realization, which this handoff does not conclude.
- Concentration and sponsor alignment. Evidence: no customer concentration is given; the insider stake of 13.9% is documented (10-K page:48). Implication: owner-operator alignment is present; no sponsor.
