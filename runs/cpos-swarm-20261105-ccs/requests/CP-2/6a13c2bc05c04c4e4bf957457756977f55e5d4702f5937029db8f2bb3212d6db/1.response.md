### Credit view

Century Communities, Inc. has a weakened but still liquid credit profile in which earnings are falling faster than debt is being repaid. The strongest support is USD 744.0 million of undrawn revolver and USD 86.086 million of cash at 30 September 2026, no bond maturity before August 2029, and a 16-state footprint with a captive mortgage arm [CP-1; CP-1A]. The weakest point is leverage: total debt reached 6.16x last-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA, from 2.53x in FY2024, while nine-month operating cash flow was negative USD 267.475 million and revolver borrowings funded inventory, buybacks and dividends. The outcome will be decided by covenant headroom, which the sources do not show, by how the USD 160.2 million HistoryMaker Homes purchase is funded, and by whether incentive-driven margin pressure eases.

### Key findings

1. Leverage has risen sharply on both a weaker earnings base and higher debt. Century Communities, Inc. reported total debt of USD 1,812.587 million at 30 September 2026 against last-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA of USD 294.366 million, or 6.16x, and net debt of 5.87x, up from 2.53x and 2.27x in FY2024 (total leverage has more than doubled in under two years) and 4.13x and 3.81x in FY2025 [CP-1, Documentary Fact for inputs, Analyst Interpretation for the derived ratios]. Holding adjusted EBITDA at its FY2025 level, September 2026 debt alone gives 5.18x, so debt growth and earnings decline each contribute. On unadjusted EBITDA the ratios are 7.68x total and 7.31x net, because adjusted EBITDA adds back inventory impairment, which has occurred in every period shown. Implication: debt service capacity depends on a recovery in volume and margin rather than on deleveraging.

2. Operating cash flow is negative and bank debt funds the gap. Net cash used in operating activities was USD 267.475 million in nine months 2026 against USD 57.654 million a year earlier, inventories rose by USD 382.865 million to USD 3,744.023 million, and the revolver balance rose from USD 51.5 million to USD 456.0 million (net drawings of USD 404.5 million) while still paying USD 27.519 million of dividends and USD 71.198 million of repurchases [CP-1; CP-1A, Documentary Fact]. Mechanic: homebuilding converts cash late, so growth in land and homes under construction absorbs liquidity ahead of closings. Implication: leverage on the company's own homebuilding measure rose to 34.9% net debt to net capital from 25.9%, and a slower sales pace would extend the cash outflow.

3. Liquidity is adequate on reported figures, with a long bond runway. Undrawn revolver capacity was USD 744.0 million after the 30 September 2026 amendment raised commitments to USD 1.2 billion, and company-reported liquidity was USD 863.8 million including cash held in escrow [CP-1]. The first bond matures in August 2029 (USD 500.0 million), the next in September 2033 (USD 500.0 million), and USD 1.1 billion of revolver commitments run to November 2030. The near-term maturities are secured warehouse lines of USD 191.266 million through May 2027 and construction loans of USD 129.450 million from December 2026. Implication: refinancing risk for bondholders is back-ended, but revolver availability depends on covenants that are not supplied.

4. Demand and margin are under pressure from incentives and rates. Third-quarter 2026 average selling price was USD 345.0 thousand against USD 384.2 thousand (down 10.2%), gross margin on home sales was 15.0% against 17.9%, deliveries were 2,442 against 2,486, and the company attributes the price decline to increased incentives and the volume decline to slower absorption, as management's explanation [CP-1A, Management Language]. Selling, general and administrative expense rose to 14.1% of home sales revenue from 12.6% on a partly fixed cost base. Mechanic: price cuts fall straight to earnings, and a large part of the customer base is entry-level buyers, whose demand is sensitive to affordability and mortgage rates [CP-1A, Documentary Fact]. Implication: the earnings base that supports leverage could fall further if rates rise.

5. Interest cover has weakened, with only annual cash interest available. Adjusted EBITDA covered cash interest paid 7.40x in FY2024 and 4.30x in FY2025; for the last twelve months only an accrual proxy of 3.62x exists, because cash interest for 2026 is not disclosed [CP-1, Analyst Interpretation]. Most interest is capitalized to inventory and recognized through cost of sales, so EBITDA is stated before most interest cost. Implication: coverage is declining, and the recent trend cannot be confirmed on a cash basis.

6. The HistoryMaker Homes acquisition adds a cash call outside the reported balance sheet. The company bought the business on 7 October 2026 for initial cash consideration of about USD 160.2 million, adding 29 Texas communities, and the filing does not state how it is funded [CP-1A, Documentary Fact; the Dallas community and delivery targets are Management Language]. Implication: reported September leverage and liquidity understate the position after the deal, and the revolver is the likely but unconfirmed source.

7. Governance is aligned but capital returns continued as borrowing rose. The Executive Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, who are brothers, held 13.9% of shares at 31 December 2025; no related-party leakage, dividend recapitalisation or creditor-adverse transaction is documented, and the company says repurchases use cash and the revolver [CP-1A, Documentary Fact]. Implication: the behaviour reads as Neutral or Mixed, with discretion to cut returns still unused.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. (1) Liquidity and maturity profile [Documentary Fact; CP-1]: USD 744.0 million undrawn on the revolver, USD 86.086 million of cash, no bond before August 2029, so near-term refinancing is limited to bank and warehouse lines. (2) Diversified operations [Documentary Fact; CP-1A]: five regional homebuilding segments across 16 states, none above 25.0% of nine-month revenue, with Century Complete the largest, which limits single-market exposure. (3) Flexibility of land and cash use [Documentary Fact; CP-1A]: about 45.1% of lots are controlled through options with obligations generally limited to deposits, and dividends and repurchases are discretionary. (4) Insider alignment [Documentary Fact; CP-1A]: 13.9% ownership by the two senior executives. (5) Equity cushion [Documentary Fact; CP-1]: stockholders' equity of USD 2,560.998 million against total debt of USD 1,812.587 million.

Weaknesses. (1) High and rising leverage [Analyst Interpretation; CP-1]: 6.16x adjusted EBITDA and 7.68x unadjusted. (2) Negative operating cash flow [Documentary Fact; CP-1]: bank-funded growth and returns. (3) Margin compression [Documentary Fact; CP-1A]: price cuts via incentives on a partly fixed cost base. (4) Floating-rate bank dependence [Documentary Fact; CP-1]: the revolver of USD 456.0 million, repurchase facilities and construction loans carry floating rates, and the revolver's covenants are not visible. (5) Acquisition and capital allocation [Documentary Fact; CP-1A]: a USD 160.2 million cash purchase while the company continued buybacks, with funding unstated. (6) Structural subordination [Documentary Fact; CP-1]: unsecured notes sit behind at least USD 320.716 million of identified secured debt and the non-guarantor mortgage subsidiary's lines.
