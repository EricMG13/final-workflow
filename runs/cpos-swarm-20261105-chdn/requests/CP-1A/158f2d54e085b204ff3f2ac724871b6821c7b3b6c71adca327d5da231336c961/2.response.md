### Business and transaction view

Churchill Downs Incorporated is a Kentucky-incorporated, Nasdaq-listed operator whose earnings come from three segments: Live and Historical Racing, Wagering Services and Solutions, and Gaming. Live and Historical Racing, which combines the Kentucky Derby franchise with historical racing machine venues in Kentucky, Virginia and New Hampshire, is the largest segment by revenue and Adjusted EBITDA for nine months 2026. Gaming is the second largest and carries the equity income from two unconsolidated casinos. The company is publicly owned with no controlling holder disclosed in the supplied text, so no sponsor behaviour is inferred; governance is assessed from documented issuer actions only. The most important transaction fact for a creditor is the July 29, 2026 announcement that the company is exploring a sale of nine wholly owned regional gaming properties, set against a recently refinanced bank structure, a pending note redemption funded by the revolver, an active growth capital program and a larger buyback authorization. These pull in different directions for creditors: asset-sale proceeds and lower buybacks would reduce leverage, while the growth program and buybacks would absorb cash. Confidence is Medium because the sale has no disclosed terms.

### Credit translations

1. Concentration and seasonality. Evidence: nine-month 2026 historical racing revenue of 780 is 33.3% of group revenue, Churchill Downs Racetrack revenue is 262 in nine months but 12 in Q3, and the second quarter produced 477 of the nine-month Adjusted EBITDA of 998 (47.8%). Mechanic: earnings and the revolver draw are tied to the Derby calendar and to Virginia and Kentucky machine regulation, so a stand-alone quarter misstates run-rate and one regulatory or tax change can move group cash flow. Implication: leverage tolerance and liquidity should be judged on LTM and seasonal-peak working capital, and monitoring should track Virginia tax and competition. No logged conflict counters this.
2. Portfolio review. Evidence: the Q3 10-Q p32 statement and the Gaming segment Adjusted EBITDA of 384 for nine months, of which 143 is equity income that would not be part of a sale of wholly owned properties. Mechanic: a sale of 280 to 300 of annual Adjusted EBITDA removes 22.5% to 24.1% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA of 1,245.3. At the 3.68x net leverage of CP-1, proceeds of about 1,030 to 1,104 would be needed to leave net leverage unchanged if all proceeds repaid debt and nothing else changed; this is an illustrative threshold, not a valuation or a forecast. Implication: the debt-reduction benefit depends on proceeds, tax leakage and any mandatory prepayment or collateral-release terms, none supplied; the remaining company would be more concentrated in racing and historical racing machines. No logged conflict counters this.
3. Capital allocation. Evidence: FY2025 outflows exceeded free cash flow with net borrowings of 216.4, followed by 2026 deleveraging and a 750 authorization. Mechanic: buybacks are discretionary and can be paced to leverage; project capital is guided at 160 to 180 for 2026 with Rockingham at 180 to 200 and a 280 to 300 Churchill Downs Racetrack building announced (10-K p58). Implication: no sponsor-style extraction is evident, but the board has shown it will let total debt rise while returning cash and buying assets, so monitoring posture should follow leverage targets and revolver usage. No logged conflict counters this.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The refinancing completed in the quarter has four parts. On September 25, 2026 the company closed the eighth amendment to its credit agreement, extending the maturity of the revolver and Term Loan A from July 2029 to September 2031, removing the 10 basis point credit spread adjustment and amending other provisions, with the extension subject to a springing maturity if notes or other material debt maturing before September 25, 2031 are not refinanced at least 91 days before maturity (Q3 10-Q page:14). On the same date it entered a new 500 million senior secured term loan B due September 25, 2033 under the credit agreement, issued at 99.875% of principal at SOFR plus 175 basis points with quarterly amortization of 0.25% of the original balance; net proceeds were used to repay the Term Loan B-1 due 2028, to repay revolver borrowings, to pay fees and for working capital and general corporate purposes (page:14 and page:15). Debt issuance costs of 7 were paid in the nine months (Q3 10-Q page:4). Third, on September 18, 2026 the company issued a conditional notice to redeem the 600 million 5.50% senior notes due April 1, 2027, and on October 19, 2026 it redeemed them with revolver borrowings, a subsequent event after the September 30, 2026 balance sheet (pages 16 and 28). The redemption price and accrued interest are not supplied. Fourth, it declared an annual dividend of $0.469 per share payable January 5, 2027 and approved a new 750 million repurchase program that includes the 306 million remaining under the prior program (page:28).

No new bond issuance is disclosed in the supplied sources. The net effect on structure is that secured bank debt increases while unsecured note debt falls: after the 2027 notes redemption the next note maturity is the 700 million 4.75% notes due January 15, 2028, and the springing-maturity test for those notes falls on or about October 16, 2027 on the 91-day wording (CP-1 calculation). Executed amendment text, guarantor identities and the redemption price are the main missing documents.

Other transactions with creditor relevance: the exploration of a sale of nine wholly owned gaming properties announced on July 29, 2026 (page:32), the pending 85 million purchase of the Preakness and Black-Eyed Susan intellectual property announced April 21, 2026 (Q1 10-Q page:20, closing status not supplied in later sources), the August 2026 purchase of the 49% United Tote interest (cash flow line of 31 for acquisition of noncontrolling interest, Q3 10-Q page:4) and the Rockingham Grand Casino redevelopment with a mid-2027 opening (10-K page:11). Other developments in the sources include the Louisiana historical racing ruling and the Virginia tax change described above.
