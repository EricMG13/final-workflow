### Credit view

Churchill Downs Incorporated is a sound, moderately leveraged credit whose direction depends on how it uses the next year's cash. The strongest support is deleveraging: net debt to LTM Adjusted EBITDA fell from 4.11x to 3.68x, free cash flow was USD 529.9 million and liquidity was USD 1,278 million at September 30, 2026. The weakest point is that the cushion can be spent: a USD 750 million buyback authorization, USD 545 to 585 million of announced projects and a possible sale of assets worth USD 280 to 300 million of annual Adjusted EBITDA all compete with debt reduction, while the 2027 notes now sit on the revolver. The outcome turns on sale proceeds, buyback pace and refinancing the 2028 notes before about October 2027. Confidence: Medium.

### Key findings

Churchill Downs reduced total debt from USD 5,155 million at December 31, 2025 to USD 4,748 million at September 30, 2026, and its net leverage on LTM Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,245.3 million fell from 4.11x to 3.68x (gross 4.28x to 3.81x). The reduction came from operating cash flow of USD 660 million in nine months after capital expenditures of USD 176 million, and from sharply lower buybacks of USD 44 million against USD 396 million a year earlier. LTM free cash flow before distributions was USD 529.9 million, and Adjusted EBITDA covered cash interest 4.54x, up from 4.09x at year-end 2025.

The most consequential new fact is the July 29, 2026 announcement that the company is exploring a sale of nine wholly owned gaming properties representing USD 280 to 300 million of annual Adjusted EBITDA, or 22.5% to 24.1% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA. At the current 3.68x net leverage, sale proceeds of roughly USD 1,030 to 1,104 million used to repay debt would leave leverage unchanged; any lower price would raise it, any higher price would reduce it. Terms, buyer, timetable and use of proceeds are not disclosed, and the creditor consequences of mandatory prepayment or collateral release cannot be assessed without the credit agreement.

The September 2026 refinancing extended the revolver and Term Loan A to September 2031 and added a USD 500 million term loan B due 2033 that retired the 2028 term loan, but it left a springing maturity: if notes maturing before September 25, 2031 are not refinanced 91 days ahead, the bank debt matures earlier. For the USD 700 million 2028 notes that date is about October 16, 2027. The USD 600 million 2027 notes were redeemed on October 19, 2026 with revolver borrowings, a subsequent event; illustratively that cuts revolver availability from USD 1,111 million to USD 511 million and liquidity from USD 1,278 million to USD 678 million if nothing else changed. Even so, computed secured net leverage after the redemption is about 1.67x against a December 2025 covenant limit of 4.0x, and the reported December 2025 interest coverage of 3.9x compares with a 2.5x minimum, so maintenance covenant pressure is not evident, although 2026 covenant figures are not supplied.

Earnings quality and concentration deserve weight. Live and Historical Racing is 49.1% of nine-month 2026 revenue, historical racing machines 33.3% and Virginia 17.5%, and Virginia faces competition and a new state tax from July 1, 2026. The second quarter produced 47.8% of nine-month Adjusted EBITDA because of the Derby. Adjusted EBITDA adds the issuer's share of affiliate EBITDA, so USD 28 million of affiliate interest, depreciation and amortization and USD 9 million of undistributed equity income in nine months 2026 are not cash to the issuer.

Capital allocation is the governance question. In FY2025 shareholder returns, share-settlement taxes and the gaming rights acquisition exceeded free cash flow by USD 166.0 million and total debt rose by USD 216.4 million. In 2026 debt fell, but the board approved a USD 750 million repurchase program on October 27, 2026 and raised the dividend. No sponsor or related-party extraction is evident, and no leverage target or creditor-protection policy is stated in the supplied sources.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Churchill Downs operates Churchill Downs Racetrack, home of the Kentucky Derby, within a diversified portfolio across racing, historical racing, online wagering and regional casinos, with three segments each producing Adjusted EBITDA. Cash conversion is strong: LTM operating cash flow of USD 755.8 million is 60.7% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow is 42.6%. Gross leverage on the LTM basis has fallen at every quarter-end of 2026, from 4.28x in December 2025 to 4.07x in March, 3.86x in June and 3.81x in September 2026. Liquidity of USD 1,278 million and maintenance covenants with wide computed cushions give room, and the bank maturity extension to 2031, subject to the springing maturity, removes near-term bank refinancing. At September 30, 2026 about 34.7% of debt was secured and 65.3% was unsecured notes, and after the October 2026 redemption no note matures until January 2028.

Weaknesses. Leverage of 3.68x net is still material for a seasonal, regulation-exposed business, and the company's own net bank leverage metric of 3.8x is undefined. Earnings are concentrated in the Derby quarter, historical racing machines and Virginia. Adjusted EBITDA includes affiliate EBITDA and excludes stock compensation, pre-opening and other charges, and covenant EBITDA is not supplied. The springing maturity ties USD 2,248 million of illustrative post-redemption secured debt, being the term loan A, term loan B and revolver, to note refinancing. Buybacks and projects are discretionary but large, and the portfolio review would remove about a quarter of earnings before any deleveraging is certain.
