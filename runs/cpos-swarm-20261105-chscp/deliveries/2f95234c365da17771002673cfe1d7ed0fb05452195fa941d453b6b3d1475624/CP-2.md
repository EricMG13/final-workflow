### Credit view

CHS Inc. has a solid fundamental credit profile at the 2026-08-31 balance sheet date, but one that rests on volatile, commodity- and policy-driven earnings rather than steady cash flow. The strongest support is leverage of 1.27x total debt to derived EBITDA, cash interest cover of 11.79x, long-dated unsecured private placement debt with no fiscal year above USD 450.0 million through fiscal 2031, and USD 2,800.0 million of undrawn committed revolver. The weakest point is earnings quality: Energy supplied the whole improvement, about USD 205.0 million came from a refinery exemption, and half of pretax income is equity income from ventures CHS does not control. What decides the outcome is whether refining spreads and nitrogen prices hold while about USD 1,536.1 million of identified fiscal 2027 cash demands are met, and whether covenants not yet seen leave room.

### Key findings

CHS Inc. entered fiscal 2027 with year-end total debt of USD 2,501.7 million against derived EBITDA of USD 1,969.5 million, a ratio of 1.27x, compared with 2.12x a year earlier (CP-1 C5). The improvement is real but partly seasonal, which the annual figure hides: the fall in total debt of USD 486.6 million, from USD 2,988.3 million, came entirely from notes payable, which fell by USD 703.5 million while long-term debt rose by USD 216.9 million (CP-1A D6), and on a last-twelve-months basis total debt over derived EBITDA was 2.53x, 2.92x and 2.57x at the three interim quarter ends (CP-1 C14). Leverage at the working capital peak is therefore 2.92x, not the 1.27x of the year-end balance sheet.

Earnings improved sharply and narrowly. Pretax income rose to USD 1,128.7 million from USD 614.8 million, and Energy alone moved from a loss of USD 19.0 million to earnings of USD 554.6 million, more than the group improvement of USD 513.9 million (CP-1 C16). Within Energy, the sources identify wider crack spreads and crude discounts, higher throughput, and a small refinery exemption benefit of approximately USD 205.0 million that management says only partially offsets total renewable fuel compliance costs and may not recur (CP-1A, 10-K page:64). Equity income was USD 569.2 million, 50.4% of pretax income (CP-2 E7), of which CF Nitrogen provided USD 406.3 million and USD 447.3 million of cash distributions. The fourth quarter derived from audited year less nine months carried USD 739.3 million of pretax income, and the annual report's own quarterly table disagrees with that split (CP-1A conflicts), so the phasing of the recovery is unverified while the annual totals stand.

Cash generation is strong in fiscal 2026 and not flattered by working capital. Operating cash flow was USD 1,584.0 million after a USD 184.0 million net working capital outflow (CP-2 E6), free cash flow after major maintenance was USD 1,052.5 million, and after preferred dividends and owner distributions it was USD 753.4 million (CP-1 C3, C4). This corrects CP-1A, which described operating cash flow as helped by working capital timing. In fiscal 2025 free cash flow after distributions was negative USD 1,120.9 million, so cash generation moved by USD 1,874.3 million between adjacent years (CP-2 E11).

Fixed charges are covered but the margin depends on the earnings base. EBITDA covers cash interest plus preferred dividends 5.87x in fiscal 2026 against 4.47x in fiscal 2025, and on the narrower operating EBITDA measure the cover is 3.78x against 2.35x (CP-2 E1, E2). Adding operating lease liabilities of USD 237.5 million gives 1.39x leverage, and treating the preferred stock at its redemption value of USD 2,273.3 million as a debt-like fixed claim gives 2.42x (CP-2 E3, E4), an analytical adjustment and not a statement of legal ranking.

The forward funding position is adequate but not abundant. Identified fiscal 2027 demands of USD 1,536.1 million comprise capital expenditures of USD 850.8 million, major maintenance of USD 41.8 million, preferred dividends of USD 168.7 million, owner cash returns of USD 315.0 million on the filing figures, scheduled long-term debt maturities of USD 58.6 million and interest on long-term debt of USD 101.3 million (CP-2 E5). That is close to fiscal 2026 operating cash flow of USD 1,584.0 million, so a repeat of fiscal 2026 funds the plan almost exactly, and any shortfall would fall on the USD 2,800.0 million revolver, which is USD 1.40 of capacity for every USD 1.00 of the largest quarter-end notes payable balance of USD 1,995.1 million (CP-2 E8), or on the board's discretion over owner payments. Two announced projects of USD 450 million and USD 700 million are additional and unfunded in the sources.

What cannot be concluded: covenant headroom, because the net worth and ratio tests are described but no level is printed; the effect of letters of credit on revolver availability; structural position of creditors, because guarantors and security are not stated; and ratings and market pricing, which are out of scope.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, balance sheet and liquidity: year-end leverage of 1.27x, USD 263.6 million of unrestricted cash and USD 2,800.0 million of undrawn committed revolver to 2028-04-21 give CHS Inc. a liquidity base of about USD 3,063.6 million gross of letters of credit that are not supplied (CP-1, CP-2 E9). Second, debt profile: the long-term debt is unsecured private placement notes and a USD 300.0 million term loan, with USD 899.3 million, 44.9% of the total, maturing in the five fiscal years to 2031 and the largest single year at USD 450.0 million (CP-2 E10). Third, a discretionary distribution lever: the board decides the cash portion of patronage and the level of equity redemptions, and cash patronage paid fell from USD 300.4 million to USD 30.0 million between fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2026, so a stress case can release cash without a default event (CP-1A). Fourth, diversification across Energy, Grains and Agronomy, a financing arm and two minority joint ventures. Fifth, demonstrated cash support from CF Nitrogen of USD 447.3 million of distributions in fiscal 2026.

Weaknesses. First, thin and volatile operating earnings: operating earnings were USD 591.9 million on revenues of USD 39,231.2 million, a margin of 1.5%, and USD 90.8 million in fiscal 2025, so pretax income relies on equity income and other income (CP-1). Second, policy and spread dependence in Energy, where the largest blending obligation ever issued, open-market RIN purchases and a refinery exemption that may not recur all decide margin. Third, concentration: CF Nitrogen provides 71% of equity income (CP-1A D3), a non-controlled venture whose distributions follow nitrogen prices. Fourth, a working capital model that takes quarter-end leverage to 2.92x and ties up large inventory and receivables, with derivative liabilities of USD 546.6 million at year end against USD 178.0 million a year earlier, a mechanic for margin and liquidity calls if commodity prices spike, which the sources do not quantify (CP-1). Fifth, unverifiable structure and covenants: no agreement is supplied. Sixth, growth capital: expected capital expenditure of USD 850.8 million in fiscal 2027 against USD 463.3 million in fiscal 2026 plus two projects with no stated funding. Seventh, a small related-party exposure of USD 10.3 million past due from a director's farm, under Audit Committee-approved terms (CP-1A).

### Corrections to upstream handoffs

CP-1A stated that fiscal 2026 operating cash flow of 1,584,009 was swollen by working capital timing. The cash flow statement shows the opposite: receivables, inventories, payables and other lines together used USD 184.0 million in fiscal 2026 and USD 464.4 million in fiscal 2025 (CP-2 E6; 10-K page:146), so fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was not flattered by a working capital release. CP-1A's related remark that free cash flow swings with working capital is true of the year-on-year change only in part: the swing between fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2026 also reflects higher earnings and lower capital spending. No other statement in CP-0, CP-1 or CP-1A is changed. The CP-1A conflict on the quarterly share table and the CP-0 and CP-1 point on fiscal 2027 redemptions (USD 165.0 million on the filing, against up to USD 150 million in the release) are carried; this module uses the filing.
