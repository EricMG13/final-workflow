### Business and transaction view

Columbus McKinnon is a century-and-a-half-old US industrial manufacturer of hoists, lifting and securement hardware, precision conveyors, digital power controls and actuators, sold mainly through distributors and crane builders. On February 3, 2026 it bought Kito Crosby Limited from funds associated with KKR in an all-cash deal, financed by a 900,000 senior secured notes issue, a 1,650,000 term loan, a revolver draw and 800,000 of Series A preferred shares sold to CD&R XII Keystone Holdings, L.P. (the CD&R Investor). To gain regulatory approval it also sold its U.S. power chain hoist and chain operations on March 4, 2026 and used the proceeds to prepay the term loan. The resulting credit profile is a larger, more consumables-weighted business that is loss-making on a reported basis, carries total debt of 2,412,308 at September 30, 2026 and 531,980 of liquidity (the same figures as the CP-1 handoff), and has a large investor with board and preemptive rights whose preferred shares accrue a 7% payment in cash or in kind ahead of common shareholders. Business risk is moderated by breadth of product, brand and geography, and by consumable-type replacement demand that management describes (Management Language); it is raised by cyclical exposure to industrial activity, the distributor channel, the integration of a business of about the same size and a short-tenured finance leadership. Ownership and governance evidence shows supportive documented actions (equity funding, use of divestiture proceeds for debt reduction, in-kind preferred dividends so far) and no documented creditor-adverse or extraction-oriented action, but the legal capacity for such actions depends on credit and indenture documents that are not supplied. Confidence is Medium: the filed business and transaction facts are well sourced, while the executed transaction, investor and financing documents and any customer concentration data are missing.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transaction is the acquisition of 100% of the equity of Kito Crosby Limited from funds associated with KKR, completed on February 3, 2026 after a definitive agreement announced on February 10, 2025. Status: completed; the purchase price allocation remains preliminary and the working capital adjustment is final. Three figures describe its size and they differ: the December 2025 quarterly report describes an all-cash transaction "valued at $2,700,000,000" subject to customary adjustments and regulatory approval ([10-Q Dec25 p:9]); the annual report's allocation table totals 2,811,907 ([10-K p:88]); and the September 2026 quarterly report states the all-cash transaction value as 2,821,907, which includes a 10,000 increase for the final working capital adjustment, paid after September 30, 2026 ([10-Q Sep26 p:9]). The last two reconcile by the stated adjustment (2,811,907 plus 10,000, derived); the headline-to-consideration difference is not explained in the pack and is logged as a conflict, not reconciled. Acquired cash was 184,307 ([10-Q Sep26 p:49]).

Financing, as the notes print it, was 900,000 of 7.125% senior secured notes due February 1, 2033 (issued January 30, 2026), a 1,650,000 senior secured 2026 Term Loan B maturing February 3, 2033, a new revolving credit facility of 500,000 (a 75,000 draw at closing per the December 2025 report), and 800,000 from the sale of 800,000 Series A Cumulative Convertible Participating Preferred Shares at $1,000 per share to the CD&R Investor ([10-Q Sep26 p:10, p:27-28]; [10-Q Dec25 p:10]). Proceeds also refinanced the existing 2024 Term Loan B and paid fees and expenses ([10-Q Dec25 p:10]); fees paid for borrowing on long-term debt were 91,004 in FY2026 ([10-K p:77]). Of the three principal funding pieces (notes, term loan, preferred), debt is 2,550,000 and preferred equity 800,000, so debt is about 76% and the preferred about 24% (derived); the preferred is equity-classified. The 2024 facilities were fully replaced, with no balance on the old revolver at replacement ([10-K p:110]). The new debt issues are the notes and the term loan; the term loan was repriced by 50 basis points on September 21, 2026, and the receivables facility was amended on October 29, 2026 (see CP-1 for terms and amounts). The notes carry a 101% change of control put and optional redemption from February 1, 2029 ([10-K p:110-111]); most covenants are suspended if the notes are rated investment grade by either agency ([10-K p:113]).

The related divestiture is the sale of the U.S. power chain hoist and chain manufacturing operations (Damascus, Virginia and Lexington, Tennessee) to Star Hoist Intermediate, LLC, agreed January 14, 2026 and completed March 4, 2026, which management says the company was required to make to gain regulatory approval; it produced a pre-tax gain of about 103,306 and net cash proceeds of about 183,976, and 193,010 was applied to prepay the term loan ([10-K p:12, p:89]; [10-Q Sep26 p:28]). Transition services of up to six months were provided to the buyer ([10-K p:90]). Use of proceeds therefore combined acquisition funding, refinancing and, after closing, partial deleveraging. Subsequent events inside the cutoff are the term loan repricing, the receivables facility amendment and a planned 150,000 paydown over the next twelve months (see CP-1), the 10,000 working capital payment, and the guidance increase on November 3, 2026. Liquidity effect: total liquidity moved from 561,216 at March 31, 2026 to 531,980 at September 30, 2026 (CP-1). Pro forma information in the annual report states combined net sales of about 2,035,000 for FY2026 and a net loss of about 292,000, as if the acquisition and divestiture had occurred on April 1, 2024, and is unaudited ([10-K p:90]). The commitment papers, purchase agreement, investment agreement, indenture and credit agreement are not supplied, so conditions, termination rights, representations and indemnities cannot be assessed (Insufficient Information).
