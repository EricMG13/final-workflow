### Credit view

Columbus McKinnon is a leveraged but liquid industrial manufacturer whose credit profile is acceptable only if integration of its February 2026 acquisition delivers the earnings and cash flow management has assumed. Strongest support: a diversified, consumables-linked product base, USD 800.0 million of preferred equity beneath the debt, USD 532.0 million of liquidity and lender-defined net leverage of 4.9x with back-ended term debt maturities. Weakest point: reported earnings and cash flow do not yet carry the capital structure, with first-half cash interest covered only 2.45x and free cash flow of USD 4.6 million. What decides the outcome is synergy delivery, conversion of adjusted EBITDA into cash for the planned USD 150 million paydown, and the conduct of the preferred investor.

### Key findings

First, the leverage improvement is real in the lender's measure but earned through earnings, not repayment. The company reports a Credit Agreement Net Leverage Ratio of 4.9x at September 30, 2026 against 5.1x at March 31, 2026, while total debt rose from USD 2,393.0 million to USD 2,412.3 million, an increase of USD 19.3 million, and the lender-defined trailing EBITDA rose from USD 441.0 million to USD 467.8 million [Documentary Fact; CP-1 Financials and credit metrics, Calculations]. That trailing figure includes USD 132.4 million (28.3%) of annualised acquisition, divestiture and synergy adjustments, and acquisition-related items together are 66.1% of it; on company-reported adjusted EBITDA without pro forma the same debt is 7.28x gross and 6.95x net [Documentary Fact for the figures, Analyst Interpretation for the reading; CP-1 Earnings quality and definitions]. For a creditor the mechanism is that leverage tolerance depends on synergies being delivered; a shortfall of 10% in the pro forma figure would add about 0.57x to gross leverage [Analyst Interpretation, derived in CP-1].

Second, cash coverage of the debt service is thin. In the six months to September 30, 2026 adjusted EBITDA was USD 222.6 million against cash interest paid of USD 90.9 million (2.45x), net cash from operations was USD 21.1 million and free cash flow USD 4.6 million, after USD 24.3 million of cash costs related to the acquisition [Documentary Fact; CP-1 Financials and credit metrics, Interpretation]. Management's guidance for fiscal 2027 is adjusted EBITDA of USD 425 million to USD 440 million and interest expense of USD 185 million to USD 190 million, which implies interest cover of about 2.3x at the midpoints, with capital expenditure guided at USD 45 million to USD 55 million [Management Language for guidance; derived in Calculations]. The mechanism is that interest, taxes, integration costs and capital spending absorb most operating cash flow, so the planned USD 150 million paydown within twelve months needs working capital release and synergy savings that are not yet in the cash flow; debt service itself is not at risk on the figures supplied.

Third, liquidity is adequate but concentrated and structurally uncertain. Liquidity was USD 532.0 million at September 30, 2026 (cash USD 110.9 million and USD 421.1 million of revolver availability), down from USD 561.2 million, with no capacity left on the USD 60.0 million receivables facility, which is borrowed by a special-purpose subsidiary, needed a waiver of its delinquency and default ratios at September 30, 2026 and was amended on October 29, 2026 [Documentary Fact; CP-1 Liquidity by facility, Covenants and compliance; CP-1A Corrections to earlier handoffs]. The revolver maturity is stated as 2031 in the latest report and as 2028 in two earlier statements, and the covenant trigger and step-down dates for the first lien leverage test (maximum 7.75x, stepping down to 6.25x) are not supplied, so the liquidity runway and covenant headroom cannot be confirmed [Documentary Fact for the conflict, Insufficient Information for the trigger; CP-0 C1, CP-1 Covenants and compliance]. The mechanism is that one revolver supplies almost all committed undrawn liquidity; a 2028 maturity would put USD 421.1 million of availability about fifteen months out.

Fourth, the structure is back-ended but floating-rate heavy. The notes of USD 900.0 million and the term loan of USD 1,449.2 million fall due in February 2033, the receivables facility in August 2028, and the only near-term maturities are planned paydown, small amortisation and a small secured building loan; about USD 586.5 million of floating-rate principal is unhedged against interest rate swaps of USD 965.0 million that begin to mature in March 2027 [Documentary Fact; CP-1 Debt structure, Calculations]. A 100 basis point rise in base rates would cost about USD 5.9 million a year on the unhedged amount (illustrative) [Analyst Interpretation]. Refinancing risk is therefore low before 2028, while interest-rate and hedge-roll risk is a monitoring item.

Fifth, business risk is moderate and better diversified than before, but its key supports are partly unproven. The product base spans hoists, lifting and securement consumables, conveyors, power controls and actuators sold through distributors across regions with about 45% of first-half revenue from outside the U.S.; management describes consumables as safety-critical, frequently replaced products, a claim that supports revenue durability if true [Management Language for the consumables claim; CP-1A Company and operating model]. The acquired business is now 55.7% of quarterly net sales, orders and sales grew in the quarter but book-to-bill was 1.0x, and legacy sales growth was 5.3% [Documentary Fact and Management Language; CP-1A]. The mechanism is that cash flow stability depends on replacement demand and industrial activity; customer and distributor concentration and the consumables share are not disclosed, so durability is a hypothesis to monitor, not a finding.

Sixth, ownership and governance are supportive on documented actions but control sits with an investor. The CD&R Investor holds all 800,000 preferred shares, about 43% as-converted (42% in one earlier report), up to three board seats and preemptive rights; the 7% preferred dividend is accruing in kind, USD 28.6 million through September 30, 2026, and the shares rank senior to common stock [Documentary Fact; CP-1A Ownership and control, CP-1 Claims outside the debt figure]. Debt reduction from divestiture proceeds and equity funding of the deal are supportive actions; the finance chief changed on July 1, 2026 during integration [Documentary Fact; CP-1A Governance and sponsor behaviour]. The mechanism is that the preferred claim accretes ahead of common holders, could turn into a cash drain if the company pays in cash or the rate rises to 10%, and gives the investor influence over capital allocation; creditor consent rights and legal capacity are unknown without the executed documents.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength 1, diversified, consumables-linked franchise (Management Language for the consumables characterisation, Documentary Fact for mix and geography). Hoists are 48% of FY2026 net sales and no other line exceeds 13%; the acquisition roughly doubled first-half revenue and added consumables and Asian and Japanese operations; the company states that consumables serve safety-critical functions and are frequently replaced [CP-1A Company and operating model]. This supports revenue visibility and margin resilience; it is not yet quantified.

Strength 2, equity beneath the debt (Documentary Fact). USD 800.0 million of preferred equity, equity-classified with a carrying value of USD 818.4 million, funded about 24% of the three main funding pieces and sits below all debt [CP-1A Transaction summary, CP-1 Claims outside the debt figure]. It adds loss-absorbing capital beneath the lenders, subject to the unknown liquidation preference and redemption terms.

Strength 3, adequate liquidity and back-ended debt maturities (Documentary Fact). Liquidity of USD 532.0 million, no committed facility expiring within twelve months on the dates used, term loan and notes due in 2033 and a small mandatory amortisation [CP-1 Liquidity by facility, Debt structure]. This gives time to execute integration. The revolver date conflict and the covenant trigger qualify it.

Strength 4, active deleveraging intent and a documented use of proceeds (Management Language for the aim, Documentary Fact for the action). The company prepaid USD 193.0 million of term loan from divestiture proceeds and says it is committed to paying down debt in the near term [CP-1A Governance and sponsor behaviour]. Total debt nonetheless rose in the first half, so the intent is not yet shown in the balance sheet.

Weakness 1, high leverage that depends on pro forma earnings (Documentary Fact for the ratios, Analyst Interpretation for dependence). Gross leverage on the lender basis is 5.16x and on reported adjusted EBITDA 7.28x; 28.3% of the lender-defined EBITDA is annualisation and synergy [CP-1 Chart data, Earnings quality and definitions]. Any synergy shortfall flows straight into leverage tolerance.

Weakness 2, thin interest cover and weak free cash flow (Documentary Fact). First-half adjusted EBITDA covered cash interest 2.45x; free cash flow was USD 4.6 million; FY2026 free cash flow was negative USD 164.1 million [CP-1 Financials and credit metrics]. Debt reduction from operations is not yet visible.

Weakness 3, floating-rate exposure and one-instrument liquidity (Documentary Fact). Unhedged floating principal of about USD 586.5 million, swaps beginning to expire in March 2027, a receivables facility that required a waiver, and a revolver supplying almost all undrawn liquidity [CP-1 Debt structure, Liquidity by facility].

Weakness 4, control, governance and information gaps (Documentary Fact and Insufficient Information). A large investor with board seats and a senior accreting claim; a recent finance chief change; open conflicts on revolver maturity, ownership percentage and deal value; executed documents, customer concentration and ratings not supplied [CP-1A Ownership and control, Conflicts, Gaps]. These limit confidence rather than prove adverse behaviour.

Weakness 5, cyclical and distributor-channel exposure with acquisition-driven reporting noise (Management Language and Documentary Fact). Demand follows industrial capacity utilisation and tariffs and raw material costs are stated pressures; reported results combine two perimeter changes and preliminary purchase accounting [CP-1A Company and operating model, CP-1 Request].

## Calculations

New calculations in this module; all other figures are repeated from the upstream handoffs.

1. Increase in total debt in the first half of fiscal 2027 = 2,412,308 - 2,393,007 = 19,301 (USD 19.3 million). Increase in lender-defined trailing EBITDA = 467,796 - 441,030 = 26,766.
2. Implied cover at guidance midpoints = adjusted EBITDA midpoint (425 + 440) / 2 = USD 432.5 million divided by interest expense midpoint (185 + 190) / 2 = USD 187.5 million = 2.31x. Interest expense includes non-cash amortisation of financing costs that CP-1 does not quantify, so cash cover would be somewhat higher; guidance is management language and not a forecast made here.
3. Illustrative cash after interest and capital expenditure = 432.5 - 187.5 - capital expenditure midpoint (45 + 55) / 2 = 50.0 gives USD 195.0 million, before cash taxes (USD 25.6 million paid in the first half), working capital, integration cash costs, common dividends and pension contributions. This is arithmetic on guidance, not a free cash flow forecast.
4. 10% shortfall sensitivity (from CP-1): 467,796 x 0.9 = 421,016; 2,412,308 / 421,016 = 5.73x against 5.16x, about 0.57x higher.
5. Unhedged floating principal and rate sensitivity (from CP-1): 1,551,465 - 965,000 = 586,465; 1% of 586,465 = 5,865 (USD 5.9 million a year).
6. Shares of lender-defined EBITDA (from CP-1): 132,389 / 467,796 = 28.3%; (132,389 + 99,441 + 39,889 + 37,563) = 309,282; 309,282 / 467,796 = 66.1%.
7. Kito Crosby share of Q2 FY2027 net sales (from CP-1A) = 300,204 / 539,285 = 55.7%. Debt share of the three main funding pieces = 2,550,000 / 3,350,000 = 76.1%; preferred 23.9% (from CP-1A).
