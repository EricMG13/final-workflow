### Credit view

Centene is a large, government-funded health insurer whose credit quality improved in 2026 but remains sensitive to medical costs and regulation. The strongest support is deleveraging: total debt fell to USD 15,542 million from USD 17,401 million, calculated leverage on last-twelve-month EBITDA is 3.62x against 6.86x for FY2025, and a USD 4,000 million revolver is undrawn. The weakest point is earnings stability and parent-level cash: calculated EBITDA was USD 2,568 million in Q1 2026 but USD 325 million in Q3 2026, and cash available for general corporate use was USD 175 million. What decides the outcome is whether the health benefits ratio holds through the fourth quarter and the 2027 rate cycle while Marketplace, Medicaid and Star Ratings changes take effect.

Thesis (Analyst Interpretation): Centene has repaired its balance sheet faster than its earnings are proven, so the credit is improving but depends on a stable health benefits ratio and on rate and regulatory outcomes that are outside its control. Basis: CP-1 leverage and quarterly EBITDA shape; CP-1A Credit translations.

### Key findings

First, Centene has reduced debt materially: total debt was USD 15,542 million at September 30, 2026 against USD 17,401 million at December 31, 2025, the 2027 notes, carried at USD 2,211 million at year-end, fell to USD 568 million at September 30, 2026, and the remainder was redeemed in October 2026 (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Debt register, Chart data: debt maturities; CP-1A Timeline). The mechanism is note repurchases funded by cash and by a USD 970 million receivable sale, and the implication is that no scheduled note maturity falls before 2028 on the CP-1 register.

Second, leverage looks sound on last-twelve-month figures but less so on the quarterly shape. Calculated total debt over EBITDA is 3.62x, against 2.98x for FY2024 and 6.86x for FY2025, and EBITDA over cash interest paid is 6.85x (Analyst Interpretation on an analyst-defined EBITDA; CP-1 Chart data: leverage and interest cover). Calculated EBITDA was USD 2,568 million in Q1 2026, USD 1,933 million in Q2 2026 and USD 325 million in Q3 2026, and Medicare Advantage earnings are front-loaded by the issuer's own account (Management Language; CP-1A Company and operating model, Seasonality). A creditor should therefore treat 3.62x as a ceiling on comfort until fourth quarter results confirm the recovery.

Third, parent liquidity is the binding constraint. Consolidated cash of USD 20,700 million sits mostly in regulated subsidiaries, unregulated cash is USD 376 million, and USD 175 million is available for general corporate use; management expects only about USD 20 million of net insurance-subsidiary dividends for the rest of 2026 (Documentary Fact and Management Language; CP-1 Liquidity at September 30, 2026; CP-1A Company and operating model, Subsidiary dividends). Debt service therefore rests on the undrawn USD 4,000 million revolver and on dividend capacity that state regulators control, with interest paid of USD 627 million over the last twelve months.

Fourth, reported cash flow overstates recurring generation. Nine-month operating cash flow of USD 4,287 million includes net receivable sale proceeds of USD 970 million, and Q3 2026 operating cash flow was an outflow as state pass-through payments were paid through (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Reported cash flows, Narrative review). Free cash flow of USD 3,943 million over the last twelve months should not be read as a recurring figure.

Fifth, business risk is driven by government payors and dated regulatory change. Medicaid was 57% of FY2025 external revenues, Florida and New York each exceed about 10% of Medicaid premium revenues, certain Hawaii Medicaid contracts and the Florida Children's Medical Services contract were lost, and Texas and Georgia protests are open (Documentary Fact; CP-1A Company and operating model, Concentration and Contract renewal risk). Marketplace membership fell to 3,403,300 from 5,828,100, 2027 Star Ratings cover about 42% of Medicare Advantage membership at 3.5 stars or higher against about 60%, and California and Medicaid work-requirement changes take effect in 2027 and 2028 (Documentary Fact; CP-1A Timeline, Credit translations). Revenue and margin visibility into 2027 and 2028 is therefore lower than the 2026 recovery suggests.

Sixth, the covenant has wide stated room but cannot be verified. Issuer debt to capital is 41.1% against a 0.60 to 1.00 maximum, and the issuer states compliance (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Covenants as disclosed), but the agreement's definition, guarantors and any restrictions on distributions are not supplied, so covenant headroom is Not calculable and legal capacity is not assessed (Insufficient Information).

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Scale and diversification across Medicaid, Medicare, Commercial and Other, with Medicaid in 30 states and Medicare Advantage in 32 states, spreads exposure across many payors even though two states are material (Documentary Fact; CP-1A Company profile, Company and operating model). Deleveraging is real and documented: debt of USD 15,542 million, debt to capital of 41.1% against 46.5% at December 31, 2025, and the 2027 notes cleared (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Reported balance sheet, Debt register). Liquidity at the parent is supported by an undrawn USD 4,000 million revolver with no letters of credit against it, maturing March 5, 2030 (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Liquidity at September 30, 2026). Interest cover has recovered to 6.85x from 3.92x on an analyst-defined EBITDA (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 Chart data: interest cover). Documented management behaviour is creditor-supportive, with note repurchases, no common stock repurchases in Q3 2026, no sponsor and no documented value extraction (Analyst Interpretation on documented actions; CP-1A Governance and issuer behaviour). The medical cost position improved on a nine-month basis, with a health benefits ratio of 89.9% against 91.1% a year earlier (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Financials and credit metrics; CP-1A Credit translations).

Weaknesses. Earnings are volatile and front-loaded: calculated EBITDA fell to USD 2,535 million in FY2025 from USD 6,213 million in FY2024, and was USD 325 million in Q3 2026 (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 Financials and credit metrics, Narrative review). Parent cash is thin relative to USD 15,542 million of debt, and upstream dividends are regulator-controlled (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Liquidity at September 30, 2026). The business depends on government rate setting and procurement, with dated headwinds in 2027 and 2028 (Documentary Fact; CP-1A Company and operating model, Timeline). Cash flow is distorted by pass-through timing and a receivable sale that carries repurchase obligations under the agreement, which is not supplied (Documentary Fact and Insufficient Information; CP-1A Conflicts logged). Documentation gaps limit the view: covenant definitions, indentures, guarantors and the Magellan sale agreement are Not supplied (Insufficient Information). A Securities Action alleging false statements about 2025 guidance is pending, with no loss estimate and accrued amounts described as not material (Management Language; CP-1A Governance and issuer behaviour).
