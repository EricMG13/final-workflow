### Credit view

CNX Resources Corp has a moderate credit risk profile on the evidence supplied: low leverage and no maturities before 2029 are offset by single-basin gas price exposure and shareholder returns that ran ahead of free cash flow. The strongest support is debt of USD 2,278.924 million against trailing adjusted EBITDAX of USD 1,276 million (1.79x), cash interest cover of 8.07x, USD 1,673.053 million of undrawn facilities and 461.2 Bcf hedged for 2026. The weakest point is that the earnings measure is management's non-GAAP figure, covenant headroom is unverified and nine-month buybacks of USD 434.678 million exceeded operating cash flow less capital expenditures of USD 359.500 million. Gas prices after the hedges thin in 2028 and 2029, and the pace of buybacks, decide the outcome. [Assessment: Analyst Interpretation, confidence Medium.]

### Key findings

The first finding is leverage and coverage. At September 30, 2026 CNX Resources Corp carried USD 2,278.924 million of balance-sheet debt and USD 1.977 million of cash, which is 1.79x gross and 1.78x net of trailing adjusted EBITDAX of USD 1,276 million, against 1.94x at FY2025; trailing cash interest cover is 8.07x. These are management-measure results, not covenant ratios, and a principal-based gross figure is 1.80x (Documentary Fact from the financial handoff; the ratios are calculated there). The mechanic is that adjusted EBITDAX removes unrealized derivative gains but keeps realized settlements, so it tracks cash earnings better than reported revenue, which includes a derivative gain of USD 160.563 million in Q3 2026; the implication is low leverage tolerance risk today, with a cushion that depends on gas prices.

The second finding is liquidity and refinancing. Cash is minimal, so liquidity is revolver capacity: USD 1,140.053 million is unused on the parent revolver (after USD 27.097 million of letters of credit) and USD 533.000 million on the CNXM revolver, which the parent does not guarantee. Undrawn capacity fell from USD 1,790.703 million at March 31, 2026 to USD 1,673.053 million. Both revolvers mature on May 17, 2029, together with USD 299.85 million drawn, so the refinancing risk is concentrated in one year; the notes follow in 2030 (USD 400 million, CNXM), 2031 (USD 500 million), 2032 (USD 600 million) and 2034 (USD 500 million), and nothing falls due in 2026 to 2028 (Documentary Fact).

The third finding is capital allocation. In the nine months of 2026 CNX repurchased shares for USD 432.781 million, while cash purchases of USD 434.678 million exceeded operating cash flow less capital expenditures of USD 359.500 million by USD 75.178 million, with net revolver borrowings of USD 67.100 million (Analyst Interpretation of cited figures). The convertible notes were settled in shares without cash and the 5.875% notes due 2034 were issued in the same period in which the 6.00% notes due 2029 were repurchased. The mechanic is that discretionary buybacks consume the cash flow that services the revolvers; the program is discretionary and can be reduced, which limits the credit risk if management responds in a price downturn. The use of proceeds of the 2034 notes is not stated (Insufficient Information).

The fourth finding is the business risk. Production is 92% natural gas, sold at market prices under short-term contracts in one basin, mainly through Shale; three marketers each exceeded 10% of FY2025 customer revenue. Hedges cover 461.2 Bcf for 2026, 409.4 Bcf for 2027, 233.1 Bcf for 2028 and 38.2 Bcf for 2029, so revenue visibility is about two years. Fixed commitments sit outside the debt figure: unconditional purchase obligations of USD 1,248.484 million, surety bonds, leases and letters of credit (Documentary Fact). In a weak-price year these fixed costs compress margin against variable revenue.

The fifth finding is structure and information. The parent and CNXM are separate creditor pools: the parent does not guarantee the CNXM notes or revolver and parent covenants exclude CNXM, so consolidated ratios blur which group bears which claim. Covenant compliance is stated for both groups but no ratio or headroom is supplied, and the indentures, credit agreements, ratings and proxy statement are not in the pack, so legal capacity for distributions, liens and asset transfers is untested (Insufficient Information).

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Low leverage on management's measure and strong interest cover give debt service a wide margin today [Documentary Fact]. There are no maturities before 2029 and the notes are staggered across 2030 to 2034 [Documentary Fact]. A large hedge book, with 461.2 Bcf covered for 2026 and 409.4 Bcf for 2027, supports cash flow visibility for roughly two years [Documentary Fact]. Revolver capacity of USD 1,673.053 million gives liquidity without cash on hand [Documentary Fact]. The business is large and long-lived, with 9.7 Tcfe of proved reserves, 72.2% proved developed, and owned gathering systems that reduce reliance on third parties [Documentary Fact]. Buybacks and the absence of dividends are discretionary, so management can redirect cash to creditors in stress [Analyst Interpretation].

Weaknesses. Earnings rest on a management non-GAAP measure and the covenant definition is not supplied, so covenant headroom cannot be shown [Insufficient Information]. Revenue is single-basin, market-priced and short-term, so unhedged volumes and basis follow Appalachian prices, and hedges thin after 2027 [Documentary Fact]. Buybacks exceeded free cash generation in the nine months and revolver borrowings rose [Analyst Interpretation]. Liquidity depends on two revolvers that mature together in 2029, one of them at the CNXM level that the parent does not guarantee [Documentary Fact]. Fixed commitments of USD 1,248.484 million, surety bonds and leases add claims outside the debt figure [Documentary Fact]. Customer concentration in a few marketers is a counterparty risk [Documentary Fact]. Governance and legal capacity cannot be tested without the executed documents and the proxy statement [Insufficient Information].
