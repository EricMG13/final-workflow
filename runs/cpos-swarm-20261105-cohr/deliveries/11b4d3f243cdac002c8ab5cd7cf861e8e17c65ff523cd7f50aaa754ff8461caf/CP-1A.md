### Business and transaction view

The business and transaction picture is usable for the credit view, with limits. Coherent Corp. is a vertically integrated maker of lasers, transceivers and optical and optoelectronic devices, reported in two segments. The Datacenter & Communications segment is the growth engine and the Industrial segment is shrinking through disposals. Ownership is public: no sponsor or controlling holder is named, although the sources do not list beneficial owners, and no dividend recapitalisation, priming or other creditor-adverse action is documented. The material events are the 2 billion NVIDIA share purchase, the conversion of the Series B preferred stock, the 2025 and 2026 disposals, the 2025 amendments to the Credit Agreement and the 2026 foreign credit facilities. The fact that most borrowing growth sits at foreign subsidiaries outside the guarantor group is a structural point for downstream work.

Three limits apply. First, ownership detail is incomplete: the annual report refers beneficial ownership to a proxy statement that is not in the pack, and the holder of the former Series B preferred stock is not named. Second, there is no transaction document, so use of proceeds is known only from filing summaries. Third, management statements about growth, margins and the AI opportunity are tagged Management Language and are not adopted. The governance view is a view of issuer-level behaviour from documented actions, not of individuals, and it is not a forecast of willingness under stress.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no leveraged buyout, sponsor transaction or new bond issue in the sources. The transactions that matter to a creditor are of four kinds. The first is the equity raise: on 2026-03-02 the company sold 7,788,161 shares to NVIDIA at 256.80 US dollars a share for aggregate gross proceeds of 2 billion US dollars, a private placement with a six-month lock-up and a six-month price protection provision that expired in September 2026 without being triggered. The company says the proceeds support research and development, capacity expansion and operations, and that a multi-year capacity agreement with NVIDIA may require incremental investment in equipment, labor and working capital through 2030 and may result in material future cash requirements. No amount is given for that agreement. This was equity, not debt. FY2026 cash flow shows share proceeds of 1,998,552 thousand while debt principal fell from 3,736,506 thousand to 3,257,482 thousand, but the sources do not say the proceeds were used to repay debt.

The second is the debt structure. On 2025-09-26 Amendments No. 4 and No. 5 to the Credit Agreement added 350 million of revolving commitments for a total of 700 million, added a 1,250 million Term A tranche to repay the existing Term A loans, and repriced the Term B loans; the revolver and Term A mature on 2030-09-26 unless a springing maturity applies. No new bond was issued; the 5.00% Senior Notes of 990 million date from 2021. The new borrowing is a set of unsecured facilities at foreign subsidiaries: the 2026 Credit Lines of up to 475 million, with 205 million outstanding at 2026-09-30, and the August 2026 Facility of about 950 million, with 598 million outstanding. These sit outside the guarantor group and are 802,793 thousand in total; the Company and its other subsidiaries are not parties to them. Their purpose includes funding working capital and repaying existing intercompany working capital loans, so the borrowing partly replaces internal funding with external debt at the subsidiary level.

The third is portfolio change. The aerospace and defense business was sold on 2025-09-02 for about 400 million with a 115 million gain, the Munich tools division was sold on 2026-01-30 at a stated loss of 96 million with a 9 million gain in the third quarter of FY2026, and a further entity classified as held for sale in the fourth quarter of FY2026 is expected to be sold within one year. The Q1 FY2027 note attributes a 115 million gain to the Munich sale, which conflicts with the annual report; the annual report and the quarterly statements are followed here. The fourth is the Silicon Carbide subsidiary: in December 2023 outside investors paid 1.0 billion for 25 percent of its equity, and the cash, 581 million of which remained at 2026-09-30, is restricted to that subsidiary. Use of proceeds, status and parties are Documentary Fact in each case; the executed agreements are not in the pack.
