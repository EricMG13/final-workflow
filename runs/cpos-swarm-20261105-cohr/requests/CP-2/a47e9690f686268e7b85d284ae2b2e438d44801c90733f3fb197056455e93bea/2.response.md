### Credit view

The fundamental credit view is of a company with strong and improving operating economics that is financing a very large capacity build, with the quality of that build to be proven. On the financial side, analyst EBITDA rose to 1,563,235 thousand LTM from 843,476 thousand in FY2025. Total debt to EBITDA fell from 4.43x in FY2025 to 2.29x in FY2026, and is 2.58x at 2026-09-30, with net debt to EBITDA of 1.46x. Cover is 8.80x on cash interest, and liquidity is 2,421.9 million made up of cash and short-term investments of 1,752.9 million and revolver availability of 669 million. The weight on the other side is cash flow. LTM free cash flow was an outflow of 1,833,632 thousand, Q1 FY2027 capital additions were 775,547 thousand, inventories and customer prepayments grew sharply, and the claims outside reported debt are large: purchase commitments of 3.4 billion for fiscal 2027 at 2026-06-30, contract liabilities, a capacity agreement that may need material cash, and operating leases.

On business risk, the company is a vertically integrated photonics manufacturer whose Datacenter & Communications segment was 74.1 percent of FY2026 revenue and 82.3 percent of Q1 FY2027 revenue, with two customers at 20 percent and 12 percent of FY2026 revenue and contract terms not supplied. Margins are expanding on yields, input costs and pricing, the equity base was strengthened by a 2 billion NVIDIA share purchase, and governance behaviour documented in the sources is supportive and not extractive. The structure of new debt is the point creditors should watch: most of the Q1 FY2027 increase of 775,916 thousand in principal is unsecured borrowing at foreign subsidiaries outside the guarantor group.

The overall view is therefore constructive on earnings and leverage, cautious on cash conversion, concentration and structure, and limited by missing documents. Confidence is Medium, not High: the readiness judgment for this module in the business handoff is READY_WITH_LIMITATIONS, covenant levels are not tied to a reported ratio, and no customer, ownership or legal-capacity evidence is supplied.

### Thesis

Coherent can support its current debt because earnings and liquidity are strong, but the credit depends on converting a heavily funded capacity build and a concentrated AI datacenter demand base into EBITDA before cash and commitments tighten. Category: Analyst Interpretation. Basis: T1, T2, T6; CP-1 free cash flow and liquidity.

### Key findings

The first finding is cash conversion. In Q1 FY2027 operating cash flow was an outflow of 92,681 thousand and capital additions were 775,547 thousand, so free cash flow was an outflow of 868,228 thousand, and the LTM outflow was 1,833,632 thousand. The mechanism is the capacity and inventory build for AI demand: inventories rose by 760,330 thousand in the quarter, partly offset by accounts payable and customer prepayments, with accrued capital spending of 527,734 thousand in payables at 2026-09-30. The credit implication is that liquidity, not EBITDA, is the near-term constraint: 2,421.9 million of liquidity equals about 2.8 quarters of the Q1 FY2027 burn, and EBITDA less capital additions was negative for the LTM period. The company funded the gap with the 2 billion equity raise of March 2026, cash reserves (short-term investments fell from 825,000 thousand to 200,000 thousand) and 802,793 thousand of foreign borrowing. This is a Documentary Fact for the figures and an Analyst Interpretation for the reading (basis T2; F8, F10, F22).

The second is concentration. Datacenter & Communications was 82.3 percent of Q1 FY2027 revenue, North America was 65.1 percent of FY2026 revenue, and two customers were 20 percent and 12 percent of FY2026 revenue. Revenue visibility cannot be tested because customer contracts, volumes and pricing are not supplied, even though a multi-year supply agreement with NVIDIA is disclosed. The implication is that a pause by one large customer would reduce EBITDA quickly while debt service, leases and purchase commitments stay fixed. Basis: T1; F3, F7.

The third is structure. Unsecured borrowing at foreign subsidiaries of 802,793 thousand, which is 19.9 percent of principal, ranks ahead of group creditors at those subsidiaries, is not guaranteed by the Company or its other subsidiaries, and partly refinances intercompany loans. Foreign headroom is not group liquidity. Whether the debt documents cap such borrowing is not supplied. Basis: T3; F26, F27, F33.

The fourth is the covenant and maturity picture. Maintenance covenants of 4.25 to 1.00 net leverage and 2.50 to 1.00 interest cover apply to Term A and the revolver, and the company states compliance at 2026-09-30. On the analyst measure the cushions are about 65.7 percent and 71.6 percent of LTM EBITDA, but the credit agreement definitions are not supplied, so these are indicative only. At 2026-06-30, 95.8 percent of principal falls in FY2030 and FY2031, with the Term B on 2029-07-01 and the notes on 2029-12-15; the revolver and Term A can spring to 91 days before those dates if the notes or Term B are outstanding and liquidity is below the stated test. Basis: CP-1 covenant and maturity data.

The fifth is governance and external exposure. The documented record is supportive: 502 million of voluntary term loan repayment in FY2026, preferred stock converted and its dividends waived, and no common dividends. Against that, most of the workforce is in Asia-Pacific, China was 11.4 percent of FY2026 revenue, and an export-control inquiry about past sales to Huawei has no loss estimate. Basis: T4, T5; F5, F11 to F15, F24, F25.

### Strengths and weaknesses

The strengths are as follows. First, scale and operating leverage: FY2026 revenue was 7,118,181 thousand, up 22.5 percent, Q1 FY2027 revenue was up 49.9 percent, and Q1 FY2027 gross margin rose 254 basis points on yields, input costs and pricing, to 39.1 percent, so EBITDA is growing faster than debt (basis F21; CP-1 revenue and margin). Second, moderate leverage and strong cover: total debt to EBITDA of 2.58x, net debt to EBITDA of 1.46x and EBITDA to cash interest of 8.80x LTM, though on an analyst EBITDA that includes 8,922 thousand of gain (CP-1 leverage and cover data). Third, liquidity and a distant maturity wall: 2,421.9 million of liquidity, an undrawn revolver, and only 7.916, 34.375 and 93.629 million due in FY2027 to FY2029 as of 2026-06-30. Fourth, supportive governance in documented actions and a 2 billion equity infusion with no debt service (T5; F8, F13, F15). Fifth, vertical integration and a disclosed multi-year supply agreement with a major AI customer (F1, F7); the claim of a leading supplier position is Management Language and is not adopted (F6).

The weaknesses are as follows. First, concentration in one end market and two customers (T1; F3). Second, negative free cash flow and capital intensity, with capital additions of 32.7 percent of Q1 FY2027 revenue (T2; F22). Third, structural subordination from foreign unsecured debt (T3). Fourth, claims outside reported debt that raise cash demands: purchase commitments of 3.4 billion for fiscal 2027 and 8.4 billion thereafter at 2026-06-30, contract liabilities of 333 million, operating lease liabilities of 346,382 thousand, and a capacity agreement with no stated amount (F10; CP-1 claims outside debt). Fifth, refinancing concentration in 2029 to 2031 and about 75.0 percent of principal at variable rates, where the effect of hedges is not in the upstream handoffs. Sixth, operational and trade exposure in Asia-Pacific with an unquantified export inquiry (T4). Seventh, limited visibility: no reported EBITDA, covenant ratio, customer terms or executed financing documents, and three logged conflicts in the latest quarterly report (F32, F33; C1 to C3).
