### Business and transaction view

The issuer is a publicly listed research and testing company with no sponsor, and its creditor-relevant story is a portfolio reshaping. After a 2025 Cooperation Agreement with a large shareholder, the board ran a strategic review that ended in November 2025 with a plan to focus on core scientific services, divest non-core assets and allocate capital across acquisitions, buybacks and debt repayment. In 2026 the issuer executed that plan: it bought the Cambodian NHP Supplier and the rest of PathoQuest, and sold its CDMO, Cell Solutions and certain European Discovery businesses. The operating model is anchored in regulated safety testing and research models for biopharma clients with no concentration above 4% of revenue.

For creditors the mechanism is that the sales and the cost programmes aim to lift margin, while acquisitions, deferred payments and buybacks consume cash and the revolver funded the gap, taking total debt from 2,152,221 thousand to 2,456,500 thousand. Governance evidence is mixed: the buyback authorization and the shareholder agreement point to equity-friendly allocation, but the sources show no controlling owner, no related-party leakage and no creditor-adverse action; buybacks are ordinary shareholder returns. Willingness to delever is not documented. Legal capacity under the credit agreement and indentures cannot be assessed because they are not supplied.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No new debt issue, refinancing or credit agreement amendment is described in the sources for fiscal 2026. The debt note shows the same revolver and three note series at September 26, 2026 as at December 27, 2025, with a larger revolver balance (Q3 10-Q page:25). The transactions below are acquisitions and disposals, not financings.

Cambodian NHP Supplier. On January 14, 2026 the issuer acquired certain assets of K.F. Cambodia Ltd for a preliminary purchase price of 507.3 million, of which 335.0 million was paid at closing and 172.3 million is deferred consideration payable on post-close conditions. The issuer paid 100.0 million of deferred consideration in Q3 2026 and 170.0 million in nine months 2026, with the remaining balance stated as 5 million. It was funded by cash and the Credit Facility and is reported in DSA and RMS (Q3 10-Q pages 13, 36, 48). Status: completed. The Q3 10-Q describes the acquired NHPs as vertically integrated into the DSA supply chain or sold to third-party clients through RMS.

PathoQuest SAS. On April 17, 2026 the issuer acquired an additional 79% equity interest for 67.4 million, funded by cash and the Credit Facility, and it now holds 100% (Q3 10-Q pages 13, 36). It is reported in Manufacturing. Status: completed.

Wilmington site. In March 2026 the issuer sold buildings for 60.1 million net of costs, recognized a pre-tax gain of 38.5 million, and entered a long-term operating lease for certain buildings (Q3 10-Q page:23). The creditor effect is cash in and a new lease claim outside debt.

European Discovery Services sale. Signed February 25, 2026 at 145.0 million in cash plus up to 10.0 million of contingent payments (Q1 10-Q page:14); closed May 22, 2026 to IQVIA Inc. at a preliminary 125.2 million net of costs to sell (Q2 10-Q page:15), later stated as 126.4 million net of costs (Q3 10-Q page:16). The contingent payments are valued at 2.8 million. Status: completed. The three headline figures are logged in Conflicts below.

CDMO and Cell Solutions sale. Completed May 6, 2026 to GI Partners. The issuer paid net cash of 12.4 million to the buyer, may fund up to 45.0 million of future EBITDA losses and capital expenditures of the divested businesses over four years, and may receive up to 50.0 million of contingent payments valued at 15.7 million. Pre-tax losses were 118.0 million in Q1 2026 and 63.7 million in Q2 2026, totaling 181.7 million for nine months 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:16; Q1 10-Q page:14). Status: completed; the funding obligation is a contingent claim outside reported debt.

Use of proceeds and liquidity effect: nine-month 2026 sale proceeds of 176,563 thousand and a further 58,127 thousand from surrendered life insurance policies (Q3 10-Q page:7) were outweighed by 467,254 thousand for acquisitions and 324,105 thousand for treasury stock, and the revolver rose by 326,671 thousand (CP-1 handoff). ### Credit translations

Revenue durability. Evidence: no client above 4% of FY2025 revenue (10-K page:26) and DSA backlog of 2.0 billion at September 26, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:35). Mechanism: spread clients and a backlog worked off over varying durations reduce dependence on any one contract, but termination without fees is possible (10-K page:43). Implication: debt service is supported by diversified, contracted cash flows; a sharp biotech funding downturn is the main swing factor. The counter-evidence is that Q3 2026 revenue fell year over year, driven by the May 2026 divestitures (Q3 release page:2).

Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: cost programmes of about 300 million of annualized savings by the end of 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:36) against restructuring charges of 73.8 million in nine months 2026. Mechanism: savings flow to margin only if volumes hold, and charges consume cash first. Implication: EBITDA expansion is partly management-claimed and not yet reflected in cash conversion (CP-1 shows lower LTM free cash flow than FY2025).

Capital allocation. Evidence: 324,105 thousand of buybacks and 467,254 thousand of acquisitions in nine months 2026 with the revolver up by 326,671 thousand. Mechanism: shareholder returns and M&A are funded partly from the revolver, lifting leverage to 3.00x on the CP-1 basis from 2.68x. Implication: leverage tolerance is a policy choice; the 10-K lists debt repayment as one of several uses without a target (10-K page:25). Willingness to reduce debt is Insufficient Information.

Concentration and supply. Evidence: the DOJ and SEC investigations of NHP shipments are closed (10-K page:91) and the Cambodian supplier was acquired (Q3 10-Q page:13). Mechanism: owning an NHP source can reduce third-party supply risk but concentrates a regulated animal-sourcing exposure in the DSA and RMS chain. Implication: an unfavourable sourcing event would hit DSA volumes; no current evidence of one.

Sponsor alignment. No sponsor exists. The large shareholder's identity, stake and influence are Insufficient Information, so alignment with creditors cannot be assessed.
