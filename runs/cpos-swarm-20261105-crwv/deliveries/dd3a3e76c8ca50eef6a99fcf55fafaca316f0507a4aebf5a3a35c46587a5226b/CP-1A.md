### Business and transaction view

CoreWeave sells GPU-based AI cloud capacity, mostly through multi-year take-or-pay contracts, and finances the equipment and data center build-out with asset-level debt backed by those contracts, supplemented by corporate notes, convertibles and equity. The evidence supports a creditor view built on three mechanics. First, revenue visibility is long: committed contracts have a weighted-average duration of about five years, and the filing reports a large contracted backlog, but that backlog converts to revenue only as capacity is delivered and made available, and the filings disclose power still undelivered at leased sites. Second, concentration is the main operating risk: a handful of customers account for most revenue, named customers include Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta and Jane Street, and the identity of the top customers changes between filings. Third, funding is the main structural risk: each growth step is paid for by new debt or equity, and the debt mixes recourse parent obligations, parent-guaranteed subsidiary facilities and one non-recourse facility, so the claim a creditor holds depends on which entity borrowed and who guaranteed. Ownership is public with founders holding control through a ten-vote share class; no sponsor is named, and no ownership percentages are disclosed in the sources read. Documented issuer behaviour is mostly supportive (equity issued alongside debt) and otherwise neutral; no dividend recapitalization, priming or uptier is documented in the pages read, but the executed documents that would show what is permitted are not supplied. Management claims in releases (power, backlog, partnerships) are attributed and not adopted.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no merger, buyout or sponsor transaction in the sources. The transaction content is a financing programme, set out in date order with status as of the filing of the Q2 2026 quarterly report, and the figures match the financial base handoff. In January 2026 NVIDIA bought about 23 million Class A shares for 2.0 billion gross (completed). In March 2026 a subsidiary, CoreWeave Compute Acquisition Co. VIII, LLC, signed the DDTL 4.0 facility of 8.5 billion with MUFG Bank as administrative agent, available for draws to June 30, 2027 and maturing in March 2032 (10-Q 2026-06-30, pages:28-29); it is non-recourse apart from limited carve-out guarantees, is secured on the equity and substantially all assets of that subsidiary, and had 2,837 million outstanding at June 30, 2026 (CP-1). Floating loans bear SOFR plus 2.25% and fixed loans bear 2.00% over a Treasury-based rate. In April 2026 the company issued 2.8 billion of 9.75% senior notes due October 2031 (2,750 million principal) and 4.0 billion of 1.75% convertible senior notes due October 2032 (conversion price about 119.60, no redemption before October 8, 2029, capped calls costing 492 million with a cap price of 230.00); a portion of the convertible proceeds funded the capped calls, the remainder is for general corporate purposes, and the senior note proceeds were retained for general corporate purposes (10-Q 2026-06-30, pages:32-33). The revolver was repaid in April 2026 and convertible promissory notes of 172 million principal (168 million carrying at December 31, 2025) were settled in shares at 106.61 per share (pages:31 and 34). In May 2026 CoreWeave Financing DDTL V, LLC signed the DDTL 5.0 facility of 3.1 billion with Morgan Stanley as administrative agent at SOFR plus 4.50%, drawable to September 30, 2026, repayable monthly from November 2026, guaranteed by the parent and secured on subsidiary equity and assets, with 1,101 million outstanding at June 30, 2026 (page:30). In June 2026 the company issued 1.3 billion of 9.625% senior notes due July 2032 and 2.0 billion euro of 8.50% senior notes due July 2032, hedged with a cross currency swap, with proceeds retained for general corporate purposes (pages:19 and 33-34). Notes are unsecured and guaranteed by certain wholly-owned subsidiaries (page:134). The financing is part of a layered structure: recourse parent debt, parent-guaranteed single-purpose facilities, and non-recourse debt; liquidity effect was cash of 5,524 million at June 30, 2026 against 2,244 million at March 31, 2026 (CP-1). After the quarter, in August 2026, the company signed the DDTL 5.5 facility of 2.6 billion with JPMorgan as agent at SOFR plus 5.5%, maturing September 2031, drawable to December 2026, and drew 1.2 billion; it also drew 1.2 billion on the revolver (page:46). Uses: delayed draw facilities finance computing equipment purchases, subject to debt-sizing limits tied to the cost of eligible assets; notes and convertibles fund general corporate purposes. Status of each: completed and drawn, except undrawn commitments (DDTL 4.0, DDTL 5.0 and DDTL 5.5 balances), which depend on eligible asset purchases. Off-balance-sheet lease and joint venture commitments (35.5 billion of leases not yet commenced, 1.7 billion of joint venture funding) sit alongside the debt and are in the financial base handoff. Conflicts and limits: the DDTL 5.0 maturity is printed as November 2031 in the debt table and as an expected final payment in May 2031 in the text, logged below and not reconciled; the sources do not contain the indentures or credit agreements, so use-of-proceeds restrictions, guarantor lists and covenants are Not supplied.
