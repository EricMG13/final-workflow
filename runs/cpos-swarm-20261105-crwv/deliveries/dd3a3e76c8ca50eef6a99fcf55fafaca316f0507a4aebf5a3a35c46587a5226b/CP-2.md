### Credit view

CoreWeave, Inc. is a high-risk credit: its contracted demand is strong, but its balance sheet depends on continued access to new debt and equity. The strongest support is revenue visibility, with committed contracts at 98% of Q2 2026 revenue and remaining performance obligations of USD 103.7 billion. The weakest point is financial: principal debt of USD 35,551 million at June 30, 2026 is 8.08x LTM adjusted EBITDA, and six months 2026 free cash flow was USD (10,454) million. The outcome will be decided by delivery and funding: whether CoreWeave brings contracted capacity online on time, collects from a concentrated customer base, and refinances USD 4,413 million due in the remainder of 2026 and USD 6,184 million in 2027 while delayed-draw commitments stay conditional.

### Key findings

First, leverage is high and rising faster than earnings. CoreWeave had principal debt of USD 35,551 million at June 30, 2026 against USD 21,615 million at December 31, 2025, and LTM adjusted EBITDA of USD 4,400 million, giving 8.08x gross and 6.82x net of unrestricted cash and marketable securities, against 6.99x and 5.97x at year end [Documentary Fact; financial base]. Cash interest cover on adjusted EBITDA is 3.35x, or 2.35x against net interest expense of USD 1,874 million. Both ratios flatter the position: adjusted EBITDA adds back stock compensation, cash interest is net of capitalized interest of USD 322 million over the twelve months, and on operating income plus depreciation and amortization gross leverage is 9.46x [Analyst Interpretation]. Mechanism: debt funds assets that are not yet earning, so period-end debt over trailing earnings overstates run-rate leverage, but operating income is already negative after interest-bearing growth, with an LTM operating loss of USD (231) million and the LTM net loss is USD (1,928) million.

Second, cash flow does not cover investment. Six months 2026 operating cash flow was USD 3,663 million against purchases of property and equipment of USD 14,117 million, leaving free cash flow of USD (10,454) million; the twelve-month figure is USD (13,655) million [Documentary Fact]. The gap is funded by new debt, equity placements of USD 2.0 billion (NVIDIA, January 2026) and USD 1.0 billion (April 2026), and customer prepayments, with deferred revenue at USD 9,692 million [Documentary Fact]. Implication: the credit relies on markets staying open; a funding pause would force a cut in capital spending and slow the delivery that converts backlog to revenue.

Third, liquidity is large on paper and conditional in practice. Company-reported total liquidity was USD 15,553 million at June 30, 2026: cash USD 5,524 million, marketable securities USD 15 million and availability USD 10,014 million, which rebuilds as USD 1,967 million on the revolver, USD 385 million on DDTL 3.0, USD 5,663 million on DDTL 4.0 and USD 1,999 million on DDTL 5.0 [Documentary Fact]. Draws on the delayed-draw facilities are limited to eligible asset purchases, the DDTL 3.0 and DDTL 5.0 draw periods ended before the as-of date, and restricted cash of USD 1,380 million sits outside the figure. In August 2026, after the quarter, CoreWeave drew USD 1.2 billion on the revolver and USD 1.2 billion on the new DDTL 5.5 facility, which lowers revolver availability (USD 767 million on the financial base's indicative basis) and means cash and availability at the as-of date are Not calculable [Analyst Interpretation].

Fourth, the maturity profile is front-loaded relative to cash. Scheduled principal is USD 4,413 million in the remainder of 2026, USD 6,184 million in 2027, USD 4,416 million in 2028, USD 2,421 million in 2029, USD 3,221 million in 2030 and USD 14,896 million after that; current debt is USD 7,513 million against cash of USD 5,524 million [Documentary Fact]. Much of the near-term amount is scheduled amortization on asset-level facilities and equipment financing, which is likely to be serviced from contracted cash flows rather than refinanced [Analyst Interpretation], but DDTL 4.0 payment timing is a management estimate. Implication: near-term refinancing risk is real but concentrated in amortizing secured debt; the unsecured and convertible notes mature from 2030 onward.

Fifth, revenue visibility is strong but concentrated and delivery-dependent. Committed contracts have a weighted-average duration of about five years, remaining performance obligations are USD 103.7 billion with 41% due within 24 months, and management cites a wider backlog of about USD 104 billion plus more than USD 25 billion of commitments added in early Q3 [Documentary Fact and Management Language, kept apart]. Three customers supplied 36%, 26% and 10% of Q2 2026 revenue (72% together), customers A and B were 32% each of receivables, and customer labels change between periods, so the fall from 71% for one customer a year earlier is not a like-for-like trend [Documentary Fact]. Mechanism: take-or-pay terms fix revenue, but revenue is recognized only as capacity is delivered (393 MW undelivered at one leased site), and service credits reduce the backlog. Implication: counterparty quality of a few customers drives default risk for the asset-level facilities.

Sixth, claims outside the debt figure are large. Operating lease liabilities are USD 16,319 million, finance leases USD 221 million, leases signed but not commenced USD 35.5 billion undiscounted, joint venture funding commitments up to USD 1.7 billion, and accrued purchases USD 5,520 million [Documentary Fact]. Debt plus lease liabilities would be USD 52,091 million; no lease-adjusted ratio is offered because adjusted EBITDA is stated after operating lease cost. Implication: fixed charges will rise as leases commence, ahead of the revenue they support.

Seventh, structure and control limit what a creditor can verify. About 89.5% of principal debt is recourse (USD 31,832 million) and USD 3,719 million is non-recourse; delayed-draw facilities are secured on subsidiary assets and guaranteed by the parent except DDTL 4.0, while the notes are unsecured and guaranteed by unnamed subsidiaries [Documentary Fact]. Founders hold all Class B shares, which carry about 66.9% of votes on the cover-page counts, and no sponsor is named [Analyst Interpretation]. Mechanism: asset-level lenders hold first-priority liens on the financed equipment and cash flows, so value available to unsecured holders depends on residual value after those claims; this is a mechanic, not a recovery conclusion. Material weaknesses in internal control continued at June 30, 2026 and securities suits are pending with no accrual.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. CoreWeave has long contracted revenue: committed contracts are 98% of Q2 2026 revenue and remaining performance obligations are USD 103.7 billion with a weighted-average contract duration of about five years [Documentary Fact]. It has grown fast, with Q2 2026 revenue of USD 2,575 million against USD 1,212 million a year earlier, up 112.5%, and LTM revenue of USD 7,590 million [Documentary Fact]. It has shown access to several funding sources in 2026, including secured delayed-draw loans, unsecured notes, convertibles, a euro bond and two equity placements, which supports refinancing capacity while markets stay open [Analyst Interpretation]. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 58.0% over the last twelve months [Documentary Fact; management measure]. The asset-level structure ties each facility to financed assets and contracted cash flows, with the parent guaranteeing all but DDTL 4.0 [Documentary Fact]. Counterparties named in the sources include Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta and Jane Street, with commitments of up to about USD 6.5 billion, USD 21.0 billion and USD 6.0 billion for the last three [Documentary Fact].

Weaknesses. Leverage is high: 8.08x gross and 6.82x net on adjusted EBITDA, and 9.46x gross on operating income plus depreciation and amortization [Documentary Fact]. Free cash flow is deeply negative at USD (10,454) million for six months 2026, so the business consumes external capital [Documentary Fact]. Earnings after interest are negative: the LTM operating loss is USD (231) million and net interest expense USD 1,874 million [Documentary Fact]. Refinancing and amortization are near term, with USD 4,413 million and USD 6,184 million due in the remainder of 2026 and 2027 [Documentary Fact]. Customer concentration is high, with three customers at 72% of Q2 2026 revenue [Documentary Fact]. Off-balance-sheet lease commitments of USD 35.5 billion and joint venture commitments of up to USD 1.7 billion add to future fixed charges [Documentary Fact]. Control is with founders through a ten-vote share class and no sponsor support is documented [Analyst Interpretation]. Material weaknesses in internal control continue, which weakens the reliability of figures used in lender tests [Documentary Fact]. Stockholders' equity of USD 5,024 million is small against principal debt of USD 35,551 million [Documentary Fact]. Covenant levels, guarantors and financing documents are not supplied, so headroom and legal protections cannot be tested [Insufficient Information].
