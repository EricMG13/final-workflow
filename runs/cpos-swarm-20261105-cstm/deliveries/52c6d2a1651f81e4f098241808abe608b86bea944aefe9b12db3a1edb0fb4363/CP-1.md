### Financial base view

The financial base is solid and fully sourced from filed statements for FY2025 and for the nine months to September 30, 2026, with the quarter taken from the filed statements and the release. On the group's own leverage definition, which divides net debt by last-twelve-months Segment Adjusted EBITDA that excludes non-cash metal price lag, leverage moved from 2.53x at FY2025 to 1.56x at September 30, 2026 by our calculation, close to the 1.6x the company reports. The improvement came mostly from earnings, not from deleveraging: total debt fell by 127 while earnings excluding metal price lag rose by 345 between FY2025 and the LTM to September 30, 2026. On Adjusted EBITDA as reported, which includes metal price lag, the same ratios are lower (1.23x net at September 30, 2026), so the basis chosen matters and is shown. Cash interest cover, liquidity and the maturity profile are comfortable in the near term, with the main structural facts being fixed-rate unsecured notes that mature mainly in 2029 and 2032, a secured asset-based revolver that is undrawn, and sizable off-balance-sheet receivables factoring. The view is limited because covenant levels, the credit agreement and the indentures are not in the pack, and because Adjusted EBITDA and net debt are management measures.

### Leverage and coverage reading

Evidence: net debt to Adjusted EBITDA excluding metal price lag moved from 2.53x (FY2025) to 1.56x (LTM Q3 2026), and total debt to the same measure from 2.70x to 1.71x. The company reports 2.2x at March 31, 2026, 1.8x at June 30, 2026 and 1.6x at September 30, 2026 (Release Q1, Q2 and Q3 page:1).

Risk mechanic: the improvement is mostly numerator-driven. Earnings excluding lag rose by 345 between the FY2025 and LTM windows (720 to 1,065) while net debt fell by 165 (1,824 to 1,659). A leverage ratio that falls on earnings is exposed to a reversal in the earnings drivers: the company attributes the quarter to improved aerospace and transportation conditions, supply shortages of automotive rolled products in North America and strong recycling, and the P&ARP segment rose 98% in Q3 on favorable metal costs and price and mix (10-Q Q3 page:24, 36). Treating the factoring programs as debt-like lifts the ratio: total debt plus the 417 derecognized receivables over LTM earnings excluding lag is 2.10x (calculated; not a company or covenant measure).

Credit implication: leverage sits inside management's stated 1.5x to 2.5x target range at the low end (Release Q3 page:2), so there is limited room to reduce further by design; the company is also returning cash to shareholders (78 of repurchases in 9M 2026 against free cash flow of 223) rather than only repaying debt. Cash interest cover of 10.65x is comfortable. Covenant headroom cannot be assessed because levels are not supplied.

### Cash flow quality

Evidence: cash from operations was 420 in 9M 2026 against 271 in 9M 2025, but inventories absorbed 527 and receivables 267, partly offset by payables of 310 (10-Q Q3 page:5), and the company ascribes the movements to higher metal prices and activity (10-Q Q3 page:39). The working capital lines sum to an outflow of 488 in 9M 2026 against 161 in 9M 2025 (calculated).

Risk mechanic (analyst interpretation): when aluminum prices rise, metal price lag adds to reported earnings (non-cash, 222 in 9M 2026) while inventory and receivables absorb cash. The lag turned negative in Q3 2026 (-4), which the company ties to primary aluminum prices decreasing (Release Q3 page:6). Metal price lag was 21% of 9M 2026 Adjusted EBITDA as reported (222 of 1,074, calculated), so in a rising price phase reported Adjusted EBITDA runs ahead of cash earnings.

Credit implication: free cash flow of 223 in 9M 2026 (68 in 9M 2025) is reported cash. The 2026 guidance of free cash flow above 300 needs more than 77 in the fourth quarter (300 less 223, calculated), against Q3 free cash flow of 128; the guidance is management's, not a filed fact. Cash conversion remains dependent on metal price direction and on factoring programs continuing, since 417 of receivables are sold.

### Covenants and definitions

The senior note indentures carry incurrence-style limits on debt, restricted payments, liens, asset sales and mergers, and a 101% change-of-control offer (10-K page:35, 108). The only maintenance covenant located is the fixed charge coverage ratio on the Pan-U.S. ABL, tested only if availability falls below 10% of commitments. The company states compliance with all applicable financial debt covenants at March 31, June 30 and September 30, 2026 (each quarterly debt note) and at December 31, 2025 and 2024 (10-K page:110). The covenant level, the definition and the reported ratio are Not supplied. Definition differences that matter: Adjusted EBITDA adds back share-based compensation, factoring expenses, restructuring, impairment, unrealized derivative gains and losses and non-operating pension items, and includes metal price lag; the company's leverage uses Segment Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes lag; net debt as defined by the company adjusts for cross-currency basis swaps net of margin calls and for cash pledged for guarantees (Release Q3 page:12); and factoring expense (15 in 9M 2026) sits in other gains and losses, outside finance costs. The metric computed here (carrying-value debt less cash over EBITDA excluding lag) differs from the company's only by the 1 swap adjustment at September 30, 2026.
