### Credit view

Constellium SE has a sound fundamental credit profile, with moderate and falling leverage, adequate liquidity and no senior notes maturing before 2028. Net debt was USD 1,659 million, or 1.56x Adjusted EBITDA excluding metal price lag, at September 30, 2026, against 2.53x at FY2025, and total liquidity was USD 992 million. The strongest support is qualification-protected, mostly multi-year customer relationships in aerospace, packaging and automotive. The weakest point is earnings quality: 20.7% of nine-month 2026 Adjusted EBITDA as reported was non-cash metal price lag, and management links part of the uplift to supply shortages. What decides the outcome is whether margins hold as metal prices and those shortages normalize, and how the USD 841 million of 2029 notes is refinanced.

### Key findings

Leverage has fallen mainly because earnings rose, not because debt shrank (Documentary Fact from the financial base, with an Analyst Interpretation). Net debt to Adjusted EBITDA excluding metal price lag moved from 2.53x at FY2025 to 1.56x on the last twelve months to September 30, 2026, and total debt to the same measure from 2.70x to 1.71x. Earnings excluding lag rose by USD 345 million between those windows while total debt fell by USD 127 million. A ratio that falls on the numerator reverses if the earnings drivers reverse, so Constellium SE carries leverage that sits inside its 1.5x to 2.5x target range but has little demonstrated durability. Treating USD 417 million of receivables derecognized under factoring as debt-like lifts total debt to Adjusted EBITDA excluding lag to 2.10x, an analyst sensitivity rather than a company measure.

Debt service is comfortable at current earnings (Documentary Fact). Adjusted EBITDA excluding lag covered cash interest paid 10.65x on the last twelve months to September 30, 2026, against 6.92x for FY2025, and the senior notes carry fixed coupons, so rate moves do not change cash interest on the notes. The cover is earnings-dependent and shares the earnings quality caveat above.

Earnings quality is the central analytical issue (Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation). Metal price lag was USD 222 million of USD 1,074 million of nine-month 2026 Adjusted EBITDA as reported, 20.7%, and it turned negative at USD 4 million in Q3 2026 when aluminum prices fell. Revenue rose 26% in the nine months on higher revenue per ton while shipments were down 1%. In a rising price phase, inventories (USD 1,905 million at September 30, 2026, up from USD 1,407 million at December 31, 2025) and receivables absorb cash, which is why nine-month 2026 working capital lines were an outflow of USD 488 million even as free cash flow of USD 223 million exceeded the USD 68 million of the prior-year nine months. Management attributes part of Q3 2026 to supply shortages of automotive rolled products in North America; that is Management Language and cannot be assumed to persist.

Liquidity is adequate in the near term and the facility profile improved after quarter-end (Documentary Fact). Total liquidity of USD 992 million comprises USD 158 million of cash, USD 541 million of asset-based facility availability, USD 114 million of French facility availability and USD 179 million of factoring availability. Cash plus committed undrawn facilities is USD 813 million. The asset-based facility is undrawn and was amended on October 22, 2026 to raise commitments from USD 550 million to USD 700 million and extend maturity to October 22, 2031 (subsequent event; effect on availability not disclosed). The French facility, which matures in December 2027 per the annual report, is the nearest facility maturity.

The maturity wall is concentrated in 2029 and 2032 (Documentary Fact, with Analyst Interpretation on refinancing cost). Face value of USD 225 million falls due in 2028, USD 841 million in 2029 (46.4% of USD 1,812 million total face value) and USD 691 million in 2032 (38.1%). The 2029 notes carry coupons of 3.750% and 3.125%, against 6.375% and 5.375% on the 2024 issues, so refinancing the 2029 notes at coupons like the 2024 issues would raise annual interest cost. The USD 100 million partial redemption of the 2028 notes in July 2026 shows a willingness to retire debt early when notes are callable at par.

Business risk rests on qualification-based relationships and is offset by concentration (Documentary Fact). Packaging and automotive rolled products supply 60% of revenue and 49% of segment earnings; the 10 largest customers are approximately 56% of FY2025 revenue; contracts in aerospace, packaging and automotive are mostly multi-year while general industrial contracts are one year or less. Metal cost is largely passed through, so margin risk sits in conversion margin, volumes and mix; the group has single-site loss precedent in the 2024 Valais flood.

Capital allocation is mixed (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on direction). Nine-month 2026 share repurchases of USD 78 million were alongside the partial note redemption; a new repurchase program of up to USD 300 million was authorized in March 2026 with about USD 257 million remaining, and no dividend is intended. Leverage inside the target range gives management room to continue returning cash, which competes with further debt reduction. Whether the indentures restrict that capacity cannot be tested.

Claims outside the debt figure add to the creditor burden (Documentary Fact). Pension and other benefit obligations were USD 341 million on the balance sheet at September 30, 2026, close-down and environmental provisions were USD 86 million, and the group is party to factoring programs under which USD 417 million of receivables were derecognized. None of these is in the USD 1,817 million of total debt.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Customer relationships protected by qualification (Documentary Fact): switching costs and mostly multi-year contracts in the largest end markets support volume visibility and retention. Leverage and coverage (Documentary Fact): net leverage of 1.56x and cash interest cover of 10.65x leave debt service comfortable at current earnings, and a cushion against a moderate earnings decline. Liquidity and maturity runway (Documentary Fact): USD 992 million of total liquidity, an undrawn asset-based facility with a longer tenor after the October 2026 amendment, and no senior notes maturing before 2028 give time to manage a downturn. Fixed-rate funding (Documentary Fact): fixed coupons on the senior notes limit cash interest sensitivity to rates. Diversified end markets and footprint (Documentary Fact): three segments across aerospace, packaging and automotive and 23 manufacturing facilities spread the operating base across regions.

Weaknesses. Earnings quality (Documentary Fact with Analyst Interpretation): a fifth of nine-month Adjusted EBITDA as reported is non-cash metal price lag, and revenue is price-led. Cash conversion sensitivity (Analyst Interpretation): working capital absorbs cash when metal prices rise and the group relies on factoring. Concentration (Documentary Fact): 10 customers are approximately 56% of revenue and the largest segment is 60% of revenue. Refinancing (Analyst Interpretation): the 2029 maturities are 46.4% of face value and carry coupons below those of the 2024 issues. Capital allocation (Analyst Interpretation): repurchases compete with deleveraging while leverage sits near the low end of the target range. Unknown creditor protections (Insufficient Information): covenant levels, guarantors, indenture baskets, ownership and ratings are not available, so recovery mechanics, structural subordination and distribution capacity cannot be assessed.
