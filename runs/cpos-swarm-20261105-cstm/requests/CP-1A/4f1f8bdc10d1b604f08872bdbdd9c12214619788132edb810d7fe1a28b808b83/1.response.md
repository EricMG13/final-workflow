### Business and transaction view

Constellium SE is the French parent of a global converter of aluminum into rolled and extruded products for aerospace, packaging and automotive customers, organized in three segments. The business model is metal pass-through plus a conversion margin, so long-run credit performance depends on end-market volumes, product mix and plant efficiency rather than on the aluminum price itself, although metal prices drive revenue, working capital and the non-cash metal price lag that sits inside reported Adjusted EBITDA. Revenue is spread across products and regions but the 10 largest customers are a majority of sales, qualification requirements create switching costs that support retention, and contracts in the largest end markets are multi-year. There is no transaction under review in the context: the recent corporate actions are a partial redemption of the 2028 notes, share repurchases under a renewed program, a cancellation of treasury shares, a small disposal of a joint venture interest, a plan settlement in the US pension and an amendment of the US asset-based facility after quarter-end. Ownership, control rights, guarantor perimeter and distribution capacity are not documented in the pages supplied, so governance conclusions are limited to documented actions and sponsor behavior is not assessed.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: the 10 largest customers are approximately 56% of FY2025 revenue and contracts in the largest end markets are multi-year with rigorous qualification (10-K page:13, 7, 8). Risk mechanic: qualification and switching costs support retention and volume visibility, but concentration means a loss or repricing at one account moves volumes and margin materially; general industrial contracts are one year or less. Credit implication: revenue visibility supports debt service in the near term, while a downturn in packaging or automotive volumes would weaken coverage; the concentration risk factor is stated by the company (10-K page:30). Counter-evidence: the cover-page error-correction mark versus the immaterial revision narrative (see Conflicts) does not bear on revenue but bears on the reliability of segment earnings.

2. Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: metal price lag was 222 of 1,074 of 9M 2026 Adjusted EBITDA as reported, and inventories rose to 1,905 from 1,407 (CP-1; 10-Q Q3 page:4, 41). Risk mechanic: in rising metal prices reported earnings include non-cash lag while inventories and receivables absorb cash; a price decline reverses the lag. Credit implication: leverage and coverage should be read on the basis excluding lag, and free cash flow of 223 in 9M 2026 can fall short of earnings in a price spike.

3. Concentration risk. Evidence: the largest segment supplies 60% of revenue and 49% of segment earnings; the top 10 metal suppliers are approximately 49% of purchases by volume (10-K page:53, 12). Risk mechanic: dependence on two concentrated sides, customers and metal suppliers, with internal casthouses partly mitigating supply. Credit implication: an operational disruption at a key rolling site (the 2024 Valais flood is an example of site-level loss) can affect a large share of earnings; insurance proceeds offset part of that loss (10-K page:68, 61).

4. Sponsor and ownership alignment. Evidence: no sponsor, parent or controlling holder is named; the proxy statement is not supplied (10-K page:126, 128). Risk mechanic: without ownership data, willingness to support or extract cannot be judged. Credit implication: Insufficient Information; this is a monitoring item, not an adverse conclusion. Documented actions point to a mixed capital allocation policy: partial debt redemption alongside repurchases.

5. Key uncertainties. Evidence: indentures, credit agreements and the guarantor list are not in the pack (CP-0). Risk mechanic: covenant headroom, restricted payment capacity and the guarantor perimeter drive creditor protection and structural subordination. Credit implication: these limit any conclusion on recovery prospects or priority; recovery prospects can appear only as a mechanic, and no expected recovery outcome is given.
