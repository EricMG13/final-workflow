### Business and transaction view

CVR Energy is a controlled holding company with two operating legs. The Petroleum segment runs two mid-continent refineries, Coffeyville in Kansas and Wynnewood in Oklahoma, supported by crude gathering and logistics. The Nitrogen Fertilizer segment is CVR Partners, which the parent consolidates with about 37 percent of the units and all of the general partner. Earnings are driven by refined product margins against crude cost, by the cost of complying with the Renewable Fuel Standard and by fertilizer prices and plant utilization. The latest quarter was exceptionally strong, which management attributes to the conflict in the Middle East and to a regulatory waiver; both are external and reversible. On November 2, 2026, after the balance sheet date, the company signed an agreement to buy two refineries and related assets from Marathon Petroleum for about 4.2 billion in cash. The agreement is pending regulatory clearance, is to be financed by new secured debt, a bridge facility if needed, an enlarged asset based loan and preferred and common equity commitments, and carries a minimum cash condition and termination fees. For a creditor, the transaction is the dominant fact: it would roughly change the scale of the business, add substantial secured debt ahead of existing unsecured notes, and rests on documents and target earnings that are not in the source set. Governance is concentrated in Icahn Enterprises, which both controls the company and has committed equity to the acquisition. The standalone credit profile in the prior module is therefore a starting point, not the post-transaction picture.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Pending acquisition. On November 2, 2026 the company entered into an equity and asset purchase agreement with indirect subsidiaries and affiliates of Marathon Petroleum Corporation to acquire the St. Paul Park refinery in Minnesota, the Mandan refinery in North Dakota, a 17 percent interest in each of Minnesota Pipe Line Company and MPL Investments, and associated wholesale marketing and logistics assets, assuming certain liabilities, for aggregate cash consideration of approximately 4.2 billion, subject to working capital and inventory adjustments. It is a Documentary Fact in the third quarter 10-Q, accounted for as a business combination with the company as acquirer, not closed at the cutoff, and a subsequent event after the September 30, 2026 balance sheet. Closing needs expiry of the antitrust waiting period and other approvals. Management expects closing before the end of 2026 (Management Language), while the risk factors state the outside date is 270 days after signing, extendable to 360 days in specified circumstances. A termination fee of 6%, 4% or 2% of the aggregate base purchase price applies depending on the circumstances; the dollar amounts are Not supplied because the base price is not separately stated from the cash consideration.

Financing. The company intends to fund the purchase with cash on hand, borrowings under its asset based loan, new senior secured debt (the permanent financing), the equity investments and the proceeds of a registered common stock offering. A debt commitment letter from Barclays Bank PLC and others provides a 364-day senior secured bridge loan of up to 2.5 billion to the extent permanent financing proceeds fall short, and consents to raise the asset based loan commitments by 550 to 1.1 billion at closing, or alternatively a new 1.1 billion facility refinancing the existing one. A commercial counterparty has committed to buy 800 million of non-voting common equivalent convertible preferred stock at closing. Icahn Enterprises committed up to 500 million of equity at the same price per share, terminating on completion of a common offering of at least 500 million. The company must hold at least 500 million of unrestricted cash until closing or termination, rising by up to 500 million of equity offering proceeds. Affiliates of the commercial counterparty will also supply crude, take product offtake and purchase initial crude and feedstock inventories at the two target refineries for ten years from closing, which is both a funding and a supply-side link. The size of the permanent financing, the total sources and uses and the target's earnings are Not supplied, so pro forma leverage and interest cover are Not calculable. The sum of committed sources cannot be compared with uses from the supplied text.

Creditor reading (Analyst Interpretation). Existing senior notes are unsecured; new financing is described as senior secured and an enlarged secured asset based loan ranks ahead of them on shared collateral, so effective subordination of the existing unsecured holders would rise on closing. Equity of 800 plus up to 500 reduces but does not remove the debt need. The 500 minimum cash and the bridge's 364-day tenor make the permanent financing market a near-term event. These are mechanics, not a view on outcome.

New debt issues in 2026. On February 12, 2026 the company issued 1 billion of notes, 600 of 7.500 percent senior notes due February 2031 and 400 of 7.875 percent senior notes due February 2034, with net proceeds of 993. Proceeds repaid the petroleum term loan, redeemed all 8.500 percent notes due 2029 at 104.250 percent of principal (a loss on extinguishment of 28 in interest expense) and redeemed 217 of the 5.750 percent notes due 2028 at par, leaving 183. On the same date the asset based loan was amended to raise commitments from 345 to 550, extend maturity from June 2027 to February 2031 and change the borrowing base and negative covenants. The effect was to push the first principal maturity to 2028 and to extend the maturity profile, with a changed coupon mix. The 6.125 percent senior secured notes of CVR Partners due June 2028 were not changed. Guarantee text for the new notes is in the 10-K and excludes CVR Partners and its subsidiaries.
