### Credit view

CVR Energy's standalone credit position is strong today, but the view for creditors depends on a pending debt-funded acquisition and on cycle-high earnings. The strongest support is the balance sheet at September 30, 2026: total debt of USD 1,781 million against trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 919 million, or 1.94x gross and 0.55x net, liquidity of USD 1,853 million and no note principal due before 2028. The weakest point is that earnings are at a cyclical high from elevated crack spreads and regulatory waivers, while the acquisition for about USD 4.2 billion in cash, with a bridge of up to USD 2.5 billion, is not in any ratio. What decides the outcome is the permanent financing structure, the target refineries' earnings and whether current margins last.

### Key findings

First, leverage has improved through earnings, not repayment. CVR Energy's total debt including finance leases moved from USD 1,765 million at December 31, 2025 to USD 1,781 million at September 30, 2026, while trailing Adjusted EBITDA rose from USD 393 million to USD 919 million, taking gross leverage from 4.49x to 1.94x and net leverage from 3.19x to 0.55x. Because the improvement comes from the numerator, a normalization of crack spreads would move these ratios back toward the earlier levels, which is why the debt levels, not the ratios, are the steadier anchor. [Financial base; Business context, market drivers]

Second, the maturity and liquidity position is comfortable on a standalone basis. The February 2026 refinancing repaid the term loan, redeemed the 2029 notes and part of the 2028 notes, and moved the first principal maturity to 2028, when USD 733 million falls due (USD 183 million at CVR Energy and USD 550 million of secured notes at CVR Partners). Cash was USD 1,278 million and undrawn facilities USD 575 million, for USD 1,853 million, but USD 158 million of the cash sits in the consolidated CVR Partners entity and USD 500 million must stay unrestricted until the acquisition closes or the agreement ends, so usable liquidity is lower than the headline. [Financial base liquidity and maturities; Business context transaction summary]

Third, cash interest cover is high and well above the FY2025 level: trailing Adjusted EBITDA was 6.96x trailing cash interest of USD 132 million, against 2.77x in FY2025. This covers the debt as it stood, including notes issued in February 2026 at 7.500 percent and 7.875 percent; it does not cover acquisition debt. [Financial base interest cover]

Fourth, the pending acquisition is the dominant credit event. On November 2, 2026, after the balance sheet date, CVR Energy agreed to buy two refineries and related logistics from Marathon Petroleum for about USD 4.2 billion in cash. The committed funding includes a senior secured bridge of up to USD 2.5 billion, a USD 550 million increase in the asset based loan to USD 1.1 billion, USD 800 million of convertible preferred stock from a commercial counterparty and up to USD 500 million from Icahn Enterprises. Because new debt is described as senior secured and the asset based loan is secured, existing unsecured noteholders would be effectively subordinated to a larger secured pool after closing. The permanent financing size, the target's earnings and total sources and uses are not supplied, so no post-closing ratio can be stated. [Business context transaction summary and gaps]

Fifth, claims outside debt and the definition of Adjusted EBITDA qualify the headline ratios. At September 30, 2026 the accrued RFS obligation was USD 230 million, derivative liabilities USD 160 million current and USD 18 million long-term, and accrued income taxes USD 132 million, none of which is in total debt, and the December 2025 contractual table showed transportation agreements of USD 995 million and purchase commitments of USD 165 million. Adjusted EBITDA adds back unrealized derivative losses of USD 278 million for nine months 2026 and removes favorable RFS changes, so it differs from reported trailing EBITDA of USD 964 million; the basis must be named whenever a ratio is used. [Financial base claims outside debt and definitions]

Sixth, governance is concentrated and in transition. Icahn Enterprises holds about 71 percent of the stock, the company relies on controlled-company exemptions, a separation and a new employment agreement for the chief executive are dated June 18, 2026, and the chief financial officer role is interim. Control is a funding positive, since Icahn Enterprises committed equity at the same price as a third party, but it also means transactions can be directed by one holder, and the legal protections for creditors sit in documents that are not supplied. [Business context ownership and governance]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Low standalone leverage (Documentary Fact): total debt of USD 1,781 million over trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 919 million gives 1.94x gross and 0.55x net, which leaves capacity to absorb a margin downturn before leverage returns to the FY2025 level of 4.49x. Liquidity and maturity profile (Documentary Fact): USD 1,853 million of liquidity, undrawn asset based facilities (USD 20 million of letters of credit issued against the CVR Energy facility), and no note principal due before 2028. Cash generation (Documentary Fact): nine months 2026 free cash flow after capital expenditure and turnaround of USD 872 million against negative USD 238 million in FY2025, which provided the cash build. Sponsor funding support (Analyst Interpretation): the Icahn Enterprises commitment of up to USD 500 million of equity at a third party's price aligns the controlling holder with closing and reduces the debt required. Regional logistics advantage (Documentary Fact): the company gathers about 67 percent and 94 percent of the crude for Coffeyville and Wynnewood.

Weaknesses. Cyclical, elevated earnings (Management Language carried, Analyst Interpretation on effect): management ties the margin improvement to the Iran war and calls the third quarter its best in company history; trailing figures include a quarter that is not a through-cycle level, so leverage is understated relative to mid-cycle. Pending acquisition (Documentary Fact): about USD 4.2 billion in cash, financed by new secured debt, a bridge of up to USD 2.5 billion and equity of USD 800 million plus up to USD 500 million; post-closing metrics are Not calculable and secured debt would rank ahead of the existing unsecured notes. Regulatory and hedge exposure (Documentary Fact): an accrued RFS obligation of USD 230 million under court challenge, derivative liabilities and a large Adjusted EBITDA add-back for unrealized hedge losses. Concentration (Documentary Fact): two refineries in one region, one top petroleum customer at about 12 percent of segment sales and fertilizer top two customers at about 28 percent. Structure and control (Documentary Fact, with Analyst Interpretation on control): about USD 569 million of consolidated debt sits at CVR Partners and its USD 158 million of cash is not freely available to the parent; the controlling holder can direct strategic transactions, and covenant terms are Not supplied. Management transition (Documentary Fact): chief executive change in June 2026 and an interim chief financial officer during a large transaction.
