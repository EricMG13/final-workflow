### Business and transaction view

Carvana Co. is a holding company whose operating business sits in Carvana Group, LLC, a vertically integrated online retailer of used vehicles: it buys cars from consumers and wholesalers, reconditions them in owned sites, sells and delivers them nationally, finances customers, and sells the loans it originates. Management calls itself the leading platform in its market, which is Management Language; the filings document the model but not the ranking. The creditor-relevant mechanics are these. Revenue is anchored on retail units, and every other stream (wholesale, loan sale gains, complementary products) is created by a retail sale, so volume, used-vehicle pricing and consumer credit availability move together. Profit depends heavily on selling loans into committed and market channels at a gain; that links earnings to the appetite of a few buyers and to benchmark rates, and management says it chose to give back margin to customers through lower loan rates. Funding is layered: corporate secured and unsecured debt at the holding company, plus asset-backed lines at operating and special purpose subsidiaries, as set out in the CP-1 handoff.

Ownership and governance matter to creditors more than usual. The Garcia Parties control most of the voting power through ten-vote Class B shares, the company is a controlled company, and DriveTime, owned by the same parties, supplies services on which revenue depends. The documented conduct since 2023 is mostly supportive (an equity purchase to fund a note tender, equity issuance to redeem secured notes, no dividends), but the agreements that limit what the controlling group could do are not in the pack, so capacity to act against creditors cannot be assessed. The only transaction in the latest quarter is the August 2026 refinancing, which left the debt amount unchanged and moved cost, maturity and rate profile. Subsequent to the quarter, one revolving facility was extended.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The only new debt issue in the latest quarter is the term loan. On August 14, 2026, Carvana Co. entered into a credit agreement for a senior secured term loan B, fully drawn at closing at an issue price of 99.75% of principal [10-Q Q3 2026, page:26, "which was fully drawn at closing at an issue price of 99.75%"]. The agreement is listed with the lenders and Barclays Bank PLC as administrative agent [10-Q Q3 2026, page:88, "Barclays Bank PLC, as Administrative Agent and Syndication Agent"]. Use of proceeds: together with cash on hand, the proceeds redeemed in full the 2030 Senior Secured Notes [10-Q Q3 2026, page:25, "to redeem in full its 2030 Senior Secured Notes"]. Status: closed and drawn at the balance sheet date; the redemption is complete and treated as an extinguishment with a loss recognised in the quarter. Debt raised and retired are equal in principal (the amounts are in the CP-1 handoff); the premium paid on redemption came from cash, so the liquidity effect is a cash outflow equal to that premium with no change in debt. The CP-1 handoff logs a conflict on the accrued interest component of the redemption payment (1 in Note 9 against 4 in the management discussion); it is carried here as logged and not reconciled.

Terms that matter to creditors: the term loan is floating (Term SOFR plus a margin), amortizes 1.00% a year from March 2027, matures in 2033, ranks pari passu with the 2031 secured notes on the same first-priority collateral, and has no financial maintenance covenant [10-Q Q3 2026, page:27, "ranking pari passu with the Company's outstanding Senior Secured Notes"]. An interest rate swap hedges only to July 31, 2028, so rate exposure returns for the remaining life [10-Q Q3 2026, page:52, "terminates on July 31, 2028"]. Prepayments through February 14, 2027 in connection with certain repricing transactions carry a 1.00% premium. The effect on the secured group is that the new lenders share the collateral equally with the remaining noteholders; the pro rata share of each depends on the principal amounts in the CP-1 handoff, and no intercreditor agreement is supplied. The preferred unit mechanism mirrors the debt: for each note retired one preferred unit of Carvana Group is retired, and one is issued for the term loan [10-Q Q3 2026, page:33, "issued 1.66 million of Class A Non-Convertible Preferred Units in conjunction with the incurrence of the Term Loan B"].

Earlier new financings in the evidence window: a fleet financing agreement with Citizens Bank in October 2025, a trade floor plan line with Stellantis Financial Services tied to franchise dealership purchases, and a seventh finance receivable facility in May 2026. Sale-capacity amendments with Ally (to a larger commitment through July 2027) and a loan purchase agreement upsize are receivable sales, not debt. No financial sponsor is involved, and no equity was raised through the at-the-market program in 2026; it had no activity in the first nine months [10-Q Q3 2026, page:32, "There was no activity pursuant to the ATM Program during the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026."]. Subsequent to the quarter, the third finance receivable lender's maturity was extended to October 15, 2027; this is a subsequent event and is not folded into the September 30 facts.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: all four revenue lines flow from retail units, which grew 39.4% in Q3 2026 [10-Q Q3 2026, page:58, "increased by 39.4% to 217,385"]. Mechanism: growth has been volume-led while unit gross profit declined; durability depends on consumer demand, used-car prices and funding availability. Implication: revenue is strong but not contracted; a downturn would hit volume and gain-on-sale together. Conflicts that counter it: none logged.
- Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: lower gross profit per unit and rising fuel and wage costs (cited above). Mechanism: management chose lower customer loan rates, which can reverse, but benchmark-rate and fuel moves are outside control. Implication: margin is sensitive to rates; cash conversion is lower than EBITDA suggests (CP-1 handoff).
- Concentration risk. Evidence: Ally lends on the floor plan and buys loans; DriveTime administers product contracts. Mechanism: failure or renegotiation by either would hit funding or revenue. Implication: counterparty dependence is a monitoring item; shares are in `## Calculations`.
- Sponsor alignment. Evidence: Garcia Parties equity support in 2023, company equity issuance in 2025, no dividends. Mechanism: controlling holders have injected equity and kept cash in the business. Implication: alignment is supportive on the record, but the record is short and the controlling group can influence debt incurrence and distributions through its votes.
- Key uncertainties: the 2023 exchange terms, the terms of the tax receivable agreement and related-party contracts, covenant levels, and the guarantor perimeter.
