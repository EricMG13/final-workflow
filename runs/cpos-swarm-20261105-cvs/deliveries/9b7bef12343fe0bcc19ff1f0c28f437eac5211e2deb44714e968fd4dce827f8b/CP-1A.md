### Business and transaction view

CVS Health is a diversified, listed health care company with four reportable segments: Health Care Benefits (the Aetna insurer), Health Services (the CVS Caremark pharmacy benefit manager plus clinical and care delivery businesses including Signify Health and Oak Street Health), Pharmacy & Consumer Wellness (the retail and infusion pharmacy business) and Corporate/Other. For a creditor the mechanics are: a very large, low-margin revenue base dominated by drug pass-through; an insurance segment whose results swing on the medical benefit ratio and on government rate and risk-adjustment policy; a pharmacy segment under reimbursement and price pressure; and a group that carries a large debt stack with unsecured senior notes and two junior subordinated series. The recent history that matters is a reset in 2025 (a goodwill impairment in the care delivery business, exits from accountable care programs, an individual exchange premium deficiency and the Omnicare bankruptcy) followed by a stronger 2026 in which Health Care Benefits recovered and the group repaid debt while leaving the dividend unchanged. The leading uncertainty is the announced separation: it is a proposed transaction, not a completed one, and nothing in the pack says how existing notes, facilities, guarantees or the junior subordinated notes would be allocated. There is no sponsor, no leveraged buyout and no sponsor behaviour to assess, so the governance review rests on documented issuer actions only, and ownership and control detail is Insufficient Information because the proxy statement is incorporated by reference and not supplied.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The only announced transaction is a proposed separation of CVS Health into two independent, publicly traded companies, announced on November 4, 2026 and disclosed as a subsequent event to the September 30, 2026 financial statements (Q3 10-Q page:40 and page:44). It is intended to be tax-free to the Company and its shareholders for U.S. federal income tax purposes. One company would hold Aetna, CVS Caremark, CVS Specialty, Oak Street Health and Signify Health and would carry the CVS Health name in the interim; the other would hold retail pharmacy, mail pharmacy, MinuteClinic including virtual care and the Health100 technology services business and would be named CVS Pharmacy. Completion is expected in the first half of 2028, subject to customary conditions including final approval by the Board, effectiveness of a Form 10 registration statement and a customary confirmation of tax-free treatment. This is a labelled future date, not a fact. The pack gives no purchase price, use of proceeds, new debt, debt allocation, guarantee, change-of-control or covenant treatment for the separation, so its liquidity and leverage effect is Insufficient Information. The creditor mechanic is that two entities will replace one obligor and the existing indentures and facilities will have to be dealt with; whether that is by assumption, guarantee, exchange or retained parent obligations is unknown, and that is the main monitoring item.

On new debt issues, no debt was issued in nine months 2026 (the cash flow statement shows no proceeds from long-term debt and repayments of long-term debt, Q3 10-Q page:4). Earlier issuance in the notes: on May 9, 2024 senior notes of about 5.0 billion dollars in five series for general corporate purposes; on December 10, 2024 two junior subordinated series of 2.25 billion and 750 million dollars with fixed-to-fixed rate resets, whose proceeds funded a cash tender in which about 2.6 billion dollars of senior notes were bought for about 2.0 billion dollars in cash with a gain on early extinguishment of 491 million dollars; and on August 15, 2025 senior notes in four series of 750 million, 1.5 billion, 1.25 billion and 500 million dollars due 2032, 2035, 2055 and 2065 with total proceeds of about 4.0 billion dollars used to repay existing indebtedness including commercial paper and for general corporate purposes (10-K page:151 to page:152). The creditor reading is that the Company has used long-dated issuance and a liability management tender to extend and reshape the stack, and has since moved to repayment. Other transactions relevant to credit are the Omnicare matters and the Rite Aid asset acquisitions below.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: Health Care Benefits revenue from the federal government is approximately 20 percent of consolidated revenues, and Medicare contracts with CMS are the large majority of it (10-K page:11); Medicare Advantage membership was 4,229 thousand at September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:55). Mechanic: government contracts are annual and subject to rate notices and risk adjustment, so revenue is visible but earnings depend on policy. Implication: revenue durability is good but margin resilience depends on government rates and medical cost trend, so leverage tolerance should be tested for a reversal in the Government business medical benefit ratio. No logged conflict counters this.
2. Margin and cash flow risk. Evidence: the medical benefit ratio was 87.8 percent for nine months 2026 against 90.0 percent, with lower favorable prior period development (Q3 10-Q page:55), and Health Care Benefits adjusted operating income rose strongly (financial base handoff). Mechanic: small changes in the ratio on a premium base of this size move group earnings. Implication: the improvement in leverage reported in the financial base handoff rests on one segment and is not yet a through-the-cycle measure; monitoring should centre on medical cost trend and prior period development.
3. Concentration risk. Evidence: no single Pharmacy & Consumer Wellness payor is 10 percent or more of revenue (10-K page:29), while Health Services revenue growth is driven by drug mix and brand inflation with client price improvements (Q3 10-Q page:58). Mechanic: PBM contracts reprice at renewal and legislation targets PBM activities (Q3 10-Q page:48). Implication: the largest client terms are not disclosed, so concentration inside the PBM cannot be judged.
4. Sponsor alignment. There is no sponsor; ownership alignment cannot be assessed beyond the dividend and repurchase record. Implication: no sponsor extraction or support risk applies; public shareholder distributions are modest and documented.
5. Key uncertainties. Evidence: separation announced for completion in the first half of 2028 (Q3 10-Q page:40); opioid accrual of about 3.4 billion dollars remaining (Q3 10-Q page:28); Omnicare guarantee (page:39). Mechanic: each is a claim or a structural change that the debt figure does not capture. Implication: refinancing and guarantor analysis should wait for the Form 10 and any indenture amendments; the committee should not assume that existing creditors keep their current obligor.
