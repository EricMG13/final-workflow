### Credit view

CVS Health Corporation is an improving, moderate-leverage credit whose 2026 progress rests heavily on one segment. The strongest support is deleveraging: total debt to adjusted EBITDA fell to 3.06x on LTM Q3 2026 from 3.78x at FY2025, adjusted EBITDA to cash interest rose to 6.51x, and undrawn back-up facilities of USD 7,500 million sit beside cash of USD 11,738 million. The weakest point is that Health Care Benefits supplied more than the whole net rise in adjusted operating income, while covenant levels, guarantor terms and the debt allocation under the announced separation are not disclosed. What decides the outcome is whether the medical benefit ratio holds, and what the separation does to the obligor and guarantee structure before completion, expected in the first half of 2028.

### Key findings

First, leverage and cover have improved on both numerator and denominator. Total debt carrying value fell from USD 64,570 million at December 31, 2025 to USD 60,680 million at September 30, 2026 while LTM adjusted EBITDA rose from USD 17,073 million to USD 19,837 million, taking total debt to adjusted EBITDA from 3.78x to 3.06x and net debt to adjusted EBITDA from 3.29x to 2.47x [CP-1]. The mechanic is repayment of USD 4,057 million of long-term debt with no issuance in nine months 2026 and higher earnings, so debt service is better covered. The implication is lower refinancing and leverage risk, but the EBITDA is our construct because the company reports none and discloses no covenant definition.

Second, the earnings recovery is concentrated. Adjusted operating income by segment moved from USD 14,443 million at FY2025 to USD 17,098 million on LTM Q3 2026; Health Care Benefits rose by USD 2,764 million while Health Services rose by USD 100 million, Pharmacy & Consumer Wellness by USD 178 million and Corporate/Other fell by USD 387 million (Calculations). Health Care Benefits recorded an implied loss of USD 676 million in Q4 2025 [CP-1], so the LTM window replaces a weak quarter, and its medical benefit ratio of 87.8 percent for nine months 2026 against 90.0 percent reflects the absence of 2025 premium deficiency reserves and lower favorable prior period development [CP-1A]. The mechanic is underwriting leverage on a large premium base; the implication is that a reversal would raise leverage quickly (see the sensitivity below).

Third, liquidity is adequate but less deep than the consolidated cash suggests. Consolidated cash of USD 11,738 million includes about USD 5,900 million held by the parent or nonrestricted subsidiaries, plus USD 7,500 million of undrawn facilities that now expire in May 2029, 2030 and 2031, and no commercial paper [CP-1]. Against that, the next twelve months include a current portion of long-term debt of USD 3,457 million, a dividend of about USD 3,423 million on an LTM basis and an opioid settlement obligation whose current portion was USD 648 million at December 31, 2025, together USD 7,528 million; parent-level liquidity of USD 13,400 million covers that 1.78x. The weak spots are that letters of credit on the facilities are not supplied and that nine months 2026 operating cash flow of USD 15,318 million was helped by working-capital timing.

Fourth, claims outside the debt figure and a structural change sit over the credit. The opioid accrual was about USD 3.4 billion at September 30, 2026, operating lease liabilities were USD 14,617 million, the Omnicare guarantee charge was USD 193 million, and the separation announced on November 4, 2026 would create two companies [CP-1, CP-1A]. The mechanic is competing calls on cash and a possible change of obligor or guarantors; the implication is that leverage on the debt figure alone understates claims, and the amendment, assumption or guarantee route for the notes is the main monitoring item.

Fifth, the information that would let a committee set protections is missing. Covenant levels and headroom, issuer and guarantor by series, indenture terms and letters of credit are not supplied, and ownership detail is not in the pack [CP-0, CP-1, CP-1A]. The ratings (BBB at Fitch with a Negative outlook, Baa3 at Moody's with a Positive outlook, BBB at S&P with a Stable outlook, company statements) are described by the company as investment grade, and one agency holds a Negative outlook. The implication is that the view is a fundamental one and the protection analysis is unfinished.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The group has scale and diversification across insurance, pharmacy benefits and retail pharmacy, with USD 420,614 million of LTM revenue and adjusted operating income positive in three of four segments [CP-1]. Deleveraging has been funded from internal cash flow, with USD 12,144 million of LTM free cash flow after dividends, no 2026 issuance and no share repurchases in nine months 2026 [CP-1, CP-1A]. Liquidity is supported by USD 7,500 million of undrawn facilities with the earliest expiry in May 2029, so no facility matures within twelve months [CP-1]. The maturity profile is manageable in the near term, with USD 3,379 million due in 2027 and USD 5,008 million in 2028 per the schedule at December 31, 2025, against LTM free cash flow of USD 15,567 million and the facilities [CP-1]. Reporting is regular and discloses segment reconciliations and subsequent events [CP-1A].

Weaknesses. Earnings are concentrated: Health Care Benefits drove the improvement and about 20 percent of consolidated revenue comes from the federal government, mainly Medicare contracts [CP-1A]. Protections cannot be assessed because covenant levels, guarantors and indentures are not supplied [CP-0, CP-1]. Cash is partly outside the parent, and letters of credit usage is unknown [CP-1]. The debt stack includes USD 3,000 million of junior subordinated notes in two series and unsecured senior notes, so structural and ranking questions arise on a separation [CP-1]. Claims outside debt are large: the opioid accrual, lease liabilities and the Omnicare guarantee [CP-1, CP-1A]. The separation introduces an unresolved change of obligor, and pharmacy segments face reimbursement and price pressure, with Pharmacy & Consumer Wellness revenue down 2.0 percent in Q3 2026 [CP-1A].
