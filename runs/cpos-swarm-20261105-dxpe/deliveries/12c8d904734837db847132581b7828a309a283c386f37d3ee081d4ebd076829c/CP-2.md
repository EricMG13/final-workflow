### Credit view

DXP's credit profile is moderately leveraged and improving, and the filings support an acceptable view with active monitoring. Its strongest support is liquidity of USD 411.0 million, a single term loan of USD 839.5 million that is not due until 2030, and gross leverage of 3.30x on last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA, down from 3.76x at FY2025. The weakest point is that growth in pumping and water relies on cash-funded acquisitions that last-twelve-month free cash flow of USD 105.0 million does not cover, while cash taxes have been unusually low. What decides the outcome is whether organic growth holds, whether acquisitions and the new USD 150.0 million buyback authorisation are paid for without lifting net leverage, and how floating interest rates move, since the term loan is unhedged as of FY2025.

### Assessment and thesis

Assessment: DXP's fundamental credit view is acceptable and improving on gross leverage, flat on net leverage, with business risk below financial risk in importance. Category: Analyst Interpretation. Confidence: Medium. Basis: the CP-1 leverage, liquidity, maturity and covenant tables and the CP-1A operating model, acquisition and governance rows. Medium rather than High because the covenant EBITDA bridge, the covenant level for two 2026 test dates, ownership and the credit agreement are missing.

Thesis: A diversified, replacement-driven distributor with ample liquidity and no maturity before 2030 can carry its single floating-rate term loan, provided acquisition spending and distributions stay within cash generation. Category: Analyst Interpretation.

### Key findings

First, DXP's gross leverage fell from 3.76x at FY2025 to 3.30x at Q3 2026 on last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA, but net leverage barely moved, from 2.41x to 2.44x, because total debt fell by only USD 7.3 million while cash fell from USD 303.8 million to USD 218.8 million after cash-funded acquisitions [CP-1 leverage table; calculations]. The improvement is therefore earnings-driven, and last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA is not pro forma for acquired businesses, which is a modest conservatism in the ratio.

Second, DXP has no maturity before October 13, 2030, when USD 812.0 million of term loan falls due, and the revolver, enlarged to USD 225.0 million and undrawn, runs to July 2, 2031 [CP-1 debt maturities and debt tables]. Liquidity of USD 411.0 million is about 6.1 times the next twelve months of scheduled amortisation plus last-twelve-month cash interest, so near-term refinancing and debt service risk is low. Interest cover is 4.33x on cash interest.

Third, DXP's cash generation is flattered and does not fund its growth. Last-twelve-month free cash flow of USD 105.0 million benefits from cash taxes of USD 7.9 million against a provision of USD 35.4 million; with taxes at the provision, free cash flow would be USD 77.4 million. Acquisitions over the same twelve months absorbed USD 163.5 million, so the gap was met from cash, which the USD 205.0 million term loan add-on of December 2025 had raised (an analyst reading, since the filings give no use of proceeds) [CP-1 financials table and gaps; CP-1A transaction summary]. That pattern holds only while cash and availability remain ample.

Fourth, DXP's growth in Innovative Pumping Solutions is mostly purchased. In Q3 2026 that segment grew 43.3 percent in total but only 1.6 percent organically, and the same quarter's gross margin was lower than a year earlier, which management attributes in part to input costs [CP-1A operating model rows]. Service Centers, the largest segment, grew 11.3 percent organically.

Fifth, DXP's covenants leave wide room on reported numbers. Secured leverage is 2.30 to 1.00 against levels of 5.50 or 5.75 for the September 30, 2026 test, and the fixed charge ratio on the revolver is 2.98 to 1.00 against a minimum of 1.00 that only applies in a compliance period [CP-1 covenant table]. Covenant EBITDA of USD 270.0 million exceeds last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA of USD 254.4 million with no bridge in the filings, and the two filings disagree on the covenant level for two 2026 test dates [CP-0 conflicts]. Even a 30 percent fall in covenant EBITDA with net debt unchanged would leave secured leverage near 3.28x, well inside either level.

Sixth, DXP has begun a new repurchase authorisation of USD 150.0 million after the prior programme lapsed with authorisation unused, while it keeps spending on acquisitions [CP-1A transaction summary]. The term loan limits distributions and investments through leverage-linked baskets whose levels are not printed, so the capacity to do both cannot be assessed.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. DXP's revenue is spread across many accounts and product lines: over 90 percent of sales are products, no customer reached 10 percent of revenue, and the largest segment grew organically in Q3 2026 [CP-1A operating model]. Category: Documentary Fact. DXP's liquidity is strong and its debt structure simple: USD 218.8 million of cash plus USD 192.2 million of revolver availability, one term loan of USD 839.5 million due in 2030, and no drawn revolver [CP-1 liquidity and maturities]. Category: Documentary Fact. DXP's leverage and cover are moving the right way on a last-twelve-month basis, with gross leverage at 3.30x and cash interest cover at 4.33x against 3.76x and 4.20x at FY2025 [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact. DXP has wide covenant headroom on reported ratios, and its issuer credit rating was raised by S&P from B to B+ on July 20, 2026 according to the company [CP-1A profile; no rating report supplied]. Category: Documentary Fact (issuer statement).

Weaknesses. DXP's debt is a single floating-rate term loan with no hedge disclosed as of FY2025, so rate moves flow straight into interest cost; the 10-K itself sized a 100 basis point move at USD 8.5 million before tax [10-K page:64; CP-1 debt table]. Category: Documentary Fact. DXP's growth in Innovative Pumping Solutions depends on acquisitions that are paid in cash and carry USD 30.7 million of contingent consideration, while organic growth in that segment is small [CP-1A]. Category: Documentary Fact with Analyst Interpretation of the dependence. DXP's free cash flow quality is weaker than the headline because 2026 cash taxes were far below the provision and working capital absorbed cash as sales and acquisitions grew [CP-1 gaps; calculations]. Category: Analyst Interpretation. DXP's capital allocation path is undocumented in the filings: legal room for distributions, the covenant EBITDA bridge and ownership percentages are missing [CP-0 and CP-1A gaps]. Category: Insufficient Information. DXP's end-market exposure includes energy, which the 10-K lists as a covenant risk, and the latest backlog figure is from December 31, 2025 [CP-1A]. Category: Documentary Fact.

## Calculations

All inputs are from CP-1 and CP-1A unless a source locator is given. Amounts in USD thousands unless stated.

- Net debt change from FY2025: 620,691 - 543,002 = 77,689, an increase of 14.3 percent (77,689 / 543,002). Gross debt fell by 846,785 - 839,525 = 7,260. Last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA rose 254,413 - 225,302 = 29,111, or 12.9 percent.
- Run-rate interest on the term loan at the September 30, 2026 rate of 7.15 percent (Q3 10-Q page:11): 839,525 x 0.0715 = 60,026. Adjusted EBITDA over this: 254,413 / 60,026 = 4.24x. Fees and amortisation excluded.
- Rate sensitivity, 100 basis points: 839,525 x 0.01 = 8,395. Cash interest 58,803 + 8,395 = 67,198; cover 254,413 / 67,198 = 3.79x. The increase is 14.3 percent of last-twelve-month cash interest (8,395 / 58,803).
- EBITDA stress (illustrative, not a forecast), cash and debt held at September 30, 2026: Adjusted EBITDA down 10 percent = 228,972, gross 839,525 / 228,972 = 3.67x, net 620,691 / 228,972 = 2.71x; down 20 percent = 203,530, gross 4.12x, net 3.05x, cash interest cover 203,530 / 58,803 = 3.46x; down 30 percent = 178,089, gross 4.71x, net 3.49x, cover 3.03x. Covenant basis: 270,000 x 0.70 = 189,000; 620,691 / 189,000 = 3.28x against 5.50 or 5.75.
- Scheduled obligations in the next twelve months: amortisation 2,120 (Q4 2026) + 3 x 2,120 (first three quarters of 2027) = 8,480 (Q3 10-Q page:11 and page:14), plus last-twelve-month cash interest 58,803 = 67,283. Liquidity 411,005 / 67,283 = 6.1x.
- Maturity concentration: 811,965 / 839,525 = 96.7 percent of debt due in 2030.
- Free cash flow after acquisitions, last twelve months: acquisitions net of cash acquired 61,675 (FY2025, 10-K page:70) + 126,234 (nine months 2026) - 24,448 (nine months 2025) = 163,461 (Q3 10-Q page:3). 104,961 - 163,461 = -58,500. Buybacks over the period: 17,109 + 0 - 1,999 = 15,110.

- Other debt-like claims for context only (dates differ): term loan 839,525 + contingent consideration 30,711 (Q3 10-Q page:8) + finance leases 20,883 (December 31, 2025, 10-K page:79) = 891,119; 891,119 / 254,413 = 3.50x. Operating leases of 74,724 are excluded because Adjusted EBITDA is after rent.
