### Business and transaction view

DXP is a listed industrial distributor with no sponsor, leveraged buyout structure or bond issue in the supplied sources; its only funded debt is a single Senior Secured Term Loan B plus an undrawn asset-based revolver, both described in the accepted financial base. The business is a first-tier distributor of maintenance, repair and operating products through Service Centers, supplemented by Innovative Pumping Solutions (engineered pump packages and water and wastewater equipment) and Supply Chain Services (outsourced procurement and inventory management). Over 90 percent of sales are products, no customer reached ten percent of revenue in the last three fiscal years, and the Service Centers segment is the largest contributor to sales and operating income. These features spread default-relevant demand risk across many accounts and end markets, which supports the leverage tolerance seen in the financial base, but the product mix is cyclical with industrial production and energy activity, which the 10-K lists as risk factors.

The credit-relevant change is the shift to acquisition-led growth in pumping and water. Five acquisitions closed in nine months 2026 with cash as the main consideration, and acquisition sales supplied almost all of the Innovative Pumping Solutions growth in Q3 2026 while organic growth in that segment was small. That growth is paid for from the cash balance, which fell over the period even as the revolver was enlarged, and it carries earn-out obligations. The company then authorised a larger share repurchase programme after the prior one lapsed with authorisation unused. The filings show capacity for both uses of cash (undrawn revolver, large cash balance, low reported covenant leverage) but do not show how the term loan's restricted payments and investments baskets limit either one, so the capital allocation path is a monitoring item rather than a documented constraint. Ownership is not a documented sponsor structure: the chief executive has held his roles since 1996 and the 10-K says he acquired a controlling interest in the predecessor in 1986, but current holdings are not in the supplied sources, so control rights and economic interest cannot be stated. Governance evidence is limited to documented actions: reconciled non-GAAP reporting, a related-party lease policy with a small disclosed amount, and no common dividends in the cash flow statements.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no leveraged buyout, sponsor, dividend recapitalisation or new note issue in the supplied sources, and none of the context fields for deal terms, quotes or ratings is populated, so the transaction row is Insufficient Information for any sponsor-related item; this is not an adverse finding. The financing events that do exist are bank debt amendments, all documented in the filings. On December 16, 2025 the company amended its Term Loan and Security Agreement with Goldman Sachs Bank USA as agent: the amendment adjusted certain financial ratio covenant compliance dates and added 205.0 US$m of incremental term loan commitments, leaving 848.0 US$m outstanding under the agreement, which matures October 13, 2030 (10-K page:83). The 10-K cash flow statement shows borrowings of 848,005 and repayments of 643,005 on the term loan in FY2025 (page:70), so the net new debt in FY2025 was 205,000 (calculated); the stated use of proceeds is not given in the sources, and the year-end cash balance of 303,783 followed by cash-funded acquisitions in 2026 is an analyst observation, not a documented use of proceeds. On July 2, 2026 the revolver was replaced by a Second Amended and Restated Loan and Security Agreement with Bank of America, N.A. as agent: commitments rose to 225.0 US$m and the maturity moved to July 2, 2031, with nothing drawn at September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:12). The promissory note issued with a 2024 acquisition was repaid in full in 2026 (page:14). No new debt was issued in Q3 2026 other than the revolver amendment; debt issuance costs paid in nine months 2026 were 1,179 (page:4). The detailed debt terms, maturities and covenants are in the accepted financial base.

Acquisitions are the active transaction flow. In FY2025 the company completed six acquisitions for total consideration of 79.2 US$m (10-K page:94). In nine months 2026 it completed five: total consideration 153,633, of which cash 130,121, stock 1,485 and contingent consideration 22,027, with goodwill of 76,439 assigned to Innovative Pumping Solutions (Q3 10-Q page:18). Named deals in the sources are PREMIERflow, LLC (January 1, 2026), Mid Atlantic Storage Systems Inc. (January 1, 2026), Ambiente H2O Inc. (February 1, 2026) and Mequipco Ltd. (August 1, 2026, Canadian water and wastewater, paid in cash and 9,456 shares); individual prices are not given and the Q2 2026 deal is not named. After the quarter, on November 1, 2026, the company agreed to acquire MDS of Michigan, LLC from balance sheet cash, amount not disclosed (Q3 10-Q page:21); this is a subsequent event and is not part of any Q3 2026 figure. Maximum contingent consideration payable is 34.0 US$m over three years (page:8). Pro forma sales and net income for the acquired businesses are given, but pro forma EBITDA is not.

Capital return is the second use of cash. The 85.0 million share repurchase programme announced August 28, 2024 is described as completed in August 2026, yet the Q3 10-Q table shows 68,006 of authorisation still unpurchased at July 31, 2026 and no shares bought in nine months 2026 (pages 19 and 35); the 24-month term ended in late August 2026, so the programme lapsed with authorisation unused. On October 28, 2026 the company authorised up to 150.0 million or 1.0 million shares over 24 months (page:21). The cash flow statements show no common stock dividend and list only preferred dividends. The term loan limits restricted payments and investments by baskets that open only below certain leverage levels (10-K page:84) but the levels are not printed, so legal capacity for buybacks or further acquisitions cannot be stated. Willingness (documented actions: acquisitions, a new authorisation, no 2026 repurchases) and feasibility (cash and availability in the financial base) are separate from capacity.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: over 90 percent of sales are products, no customer reaches 10 percent of revenue, and Service Centers organic growth was 11.3 percent in Q3 2026 (10-K page:13; Q3 10-Q page:27). Risk mechanic: many small accounts and replacement-type demand reduce the chance that one customer loss moves cash flow, but volume still follows industrial and energy activity, and Supply Chain Services contracts can be cancelled at short notice. Credit implication: supports tolerance for the leverage shown in the financial base; monitor organic growth rather than reported growth. The repurchase status conflict does not counter this.
2. Acquisition dependence in pumping. Evidence: Q3 2026 IPS sales growth of 43.3 percent against organic growth of 1.6 percent, with 48,832 of acquisition sales (Q3 10-Q page:27). Risk mechanic: purchased revenue needs cash and earn-outs (contingent consideration 30,711 at September 30, 2026, Q3 10-Q page:8) and acquired sales leave the acquisition-sales measure after twelve months of ownership, so later organic growth will show whether they hold. Credit implication: the cash balance, not operating cash flow alone, is funding growth; liquidity and net leverage in the financial base should be read alongside continued M&A, and organic IPS growth is a key uncertainty.
3. Capital allocation. Evidence: nine months 2026 acquisitions of 126,234 net of cash acquired, no repurchases, a new 150.0 million authorisation (Q3 10-Q pages 4, 19 and 21). Risk mechanic: two competing uses of cash against a lender regime whose restricted payment baskets depend on a leverage level that is not printed; a deterioration in earnings or a large buyback could move cash and secured leverage in opposite directions. Credit implication: monitoring posture should track cash, availability and reported secured leverage each quarter; capacity cannot be assessed without the credit agreement. The covenant level conflict counters any precise headroom statement.
4. Governance and related party. Evidence: lease expense of about 2.3 US$m to entities controlled by the chief executive and family (10-K page:100). Risk mechanic: a fixed cash leak to related parties under an audit committee policy; scale is small beside the Adjusted EBITDA in the financial base. Credit implication: no evidence of extraction; control concentration cannot be assessed because holdings are undisclosed.
