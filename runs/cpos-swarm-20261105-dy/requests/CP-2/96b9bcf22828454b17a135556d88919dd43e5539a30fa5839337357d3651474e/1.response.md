### Credit view

The fundamental credit view is that Dycom is a scaled, cash-generative specialty contractor with solid liquidity, whose financial risk was raised sharply by a debt-funded step into data-center electrical work and which carries meaningful customer concentration; the view is held with Medium confidence (Analyst Interpretation). It rests on three links from evidence to mechanism to implication.

First, business risk. Evidence: 94.0% of Q2 2027 revenue came from multi-year master service agreements and other long-term contracts, backlog was 12,242 million at August 1, 2026 with 6,472 million expected in the next twelve months, but customers are generally not committed to specific volumes and can terminate for convenience, and AT&T, Verizon and Lumen supplied 25.4%, 14.0% and 10.8% of FY2026 revenue (Documentary Fact, business and transaction handoff and financial base). Mechanism: recurring maintenance and program work gives revenue durability, while the absence of volume commitments and the concentration make realized revenue track a few carriers' capital budgets, and customer mergers (Verizon with Frontier, AT&T with Lumen's fiber business) reshuffle those budgets. Implication: revenue visibility is better than for a project contractor but is not a contractual floor, so default risk through the cycle depends on the carriers' continued build programs and on working capital discipline.

Second, financial risk. Evidence: notional debt rose from 950.0 million at FY2025 to 2,840.0 million at FY2026 and 2,842.7 million at August 1, 2026; constructed leverage was 3.85x gross and 2.89x net at FY2026 and 2.96x gross and 2.61x net on LTM Q2 2027 Adjusted EBITDA of 959.8 million, against 1.65x gross and 1.49x net at FY2025 (Documentary Fact inputs, constructed ratios; financial base). Mechanism: the acquisition debt arrived before a full year of acquired earnings, so the constructed ratios are not pro forma and are not covenant ratios, and about 82% of debt was floating-rate with no interest-rate hedge disclosed. Cash interest cover of 11.10x on LTM cash interest of 86.4 million is overstated by the timing of the new debt; the run-rate view of about 148.9 million of interest gives about 6.45x and the Q2 2027 annualized interest view of about 151.9 million gives about 6.32x. Implication: leverage tolerance is adequate on reported figures, debt service remains adequately covered even on run-rate interest, but the cushion is thinner than the headline cash cover suggests and is exposed to Term SOFR, with a 50 basis point move changing annual interest by about 11.7 million.

Third, liquidity and refinancing. Evidence: cash of 340.1 million plus 746.4 million of undrawn revolver gives liquidity of 1,086.5 million at August 1, 2026, with 53.6 million of standby letters of credit and no revolver draw; the only scheduled amortization visible is 4.0 million in FY2027 and the first large maturity is the 500.0 million 4.50% notes due April 2029, then Term Loan A and the revolver in December 2030 and Term Loan B in January 2033 (financial base). Mechanism: back-ended maturities with a large undrawn revolver limit near-term refinancing pressure. Counter-evidence: cash fell from 709.2 million at FY2026 to 340.1 million at August 1, 2026, free cash flow for the six months was negative 54.2 million after a 520.8 million receivables build, supplier finance of 367.4 million sits inside accounts payable, and the revolver availability computation and the composition of the 28.4 million current portion of debt are not printed. Implication: liquidity is the strongest mitigant, but it is working-capital sensitive and not backed by visible covenant headroom.

The view would weaken if combined-business earnings run materially below the LTM base, if a major carrier cuts or defers programs (management already deferred about 150 million of Communications wireless revenue into fiscal 2028, Management Language), or if buybacks outpace deleveraging. It would strengthen if full-year pro forma EBITDA confirms leverage near the constructed range and covenant compliance computations show wide headroom. Neither can be confirmed from the pack.

### Key findings

First, the leverage story is a step-up and a partial reversal. Debt of 2,840.0 million at FY2026 against 950.0 million a year earlier reflects the Power Solutions acquisition, and constructed gross leverage of 2.96x at August 1, 2026 is below the 3.85x at FY2026 only because the LTM earnings base now includes Building Systems and strong Communications growth; the ratios are not pro forma, not covenant ratios and sensitive to the 53-week FY2026 (Documentary Fact inputs, Analyst Interpretation). Second, interest burden is understated by cash interest: LTM cash interest of 86.4 million sits well below the run-rate of about 148.9 million, so the reported cover of 11.10x is a ceiling and about 6.45x the cautious figure. Third, liquidity is large but not unconditional: 1,086.5 million of liquidity, no revolver draw and a maturity wall that starts in April 2029, offset by cash that fell from 709.2 million at FY2026 to 340.1 million at August 1, 2026, a negative six-month free cash flow of 54.2 million and 367.4 million of supplier finance. Fourth, customer concentration and uncommitted volumes make revenue sensitive to a few carriers, with backlog of 12,242 million an indicator rather than a commitment. Fifth, Building Systems is both the growth driver and the newest risk: Q2 2027 revenue of 397.5 million and Non-GAAP Adjusted EBITDA of 97.2 million on fixed-price and modified-fixed-price work, with a preliminary purchase price allocation for National Technology Integrators and goodwill plus intangibles of 2,534.3 million exceeding equity of 2,063.9 million. Sixth, the information that would settle the credit view is missing: the executed credit agreement and indenture, covenant EBITDA and ratios, pro forma acquired EBITDA, ownership and restricted-payment capacity.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Recurring contract base and backlog: 94.0% of Q2 2027 revenue from multi-year master service agreements and other long-term contracts and backlog of 12,242 million support revenue durability (Documentary Fact). Liquidity and maturity profile: 1,086.5 million of liquidity, 746.4 million of undrawn revolver, and a first major maturity in April 2029 limit refinancing risk (Documentary Fact inputs). Earnings and cash generation: Adjusted EBITDA of 737.7 million in FY2026 against 576.3 million in FY2025 and 959.8 million on LTM Q2 2027, with LTM free cash flow of 485.3 million (Documentary Fact inputs, constructed LTM). Equity used in acquisitions and no dividends: part of the Power Solutions and National Technology Integrators consideration was paid in shares and no cash dividend is paid, which limits cash leakage (Documentary Fact; the behavior reading of Neutral or Mixed is Analyst Interpretation). Higher-margin diversification: Building Systems Adjusted EBITDA of 97.2 million on revenue of 397.5 million in Q2 2027 broadens the earnings base beyond carrier fiber work (Documentary Fact for the figures; durability of the margin is Analyst Interpretation because the contracts are fixed-price and the allocation is preliminary).

Weaknesses. Customer concentration: AT&T, Verizon and Lumen supplied 50.2% of FY2026 revenue combined by customer name, customers may terminate for convenience and are not committed to volumes (Documentary Fact). Leverage step-up and floating-rate exposure: debt of 2,842.7 million at August 1, 2026, about 82% floating-rate at FY2026, no interest-rate hedge disclosed (Documentary Fact; constructed ratios are Analyst Interpretation). Interest cover cushion thinner than headline: about 6.45x on run-rate interest against 11.10x on cash interest (Analyst Interpretation on constructed inputs). Working capital intensity: days sales outstanding of 101, unbilled receivables of 1,334,390 thousand of 2,281,043 thousand gross receivables, and a negative six-month free cash flow of 54.2 million (Documentary Fact). Claims outside the debt figure: supplier finance of 367.4 million, performance and other surety bonds of 1,215.8 million and operating lease liabilities of 228.7 million (Documentary Fact; sensitivity to supplier finance withdrawal is Analyst Interpretation). Asset base is largely intangible: goodwill plus intangible assets of 2,534.3 million exceed total stockholders' equity of 2,063.9 million, so tangible net worth is negative, which is a mechanic for loss absorption rather than a recovery conclusion (Documentary Fact inputs, constructed comparison). Financial policy opacity: new 150.0 million buyback authorization on August 26, 2026 beside rising debt, with restricted-payment capacity, covenant EBITDA and ownership information unavailable (Documentary Fact and Insufficient Information).
