### Credit view

Eldorado Gold's credit view is cautiously constructive: profitable mines at high gold prices support debt, but debt is rising and cash falling while two new mines are commissioned. The strongest support is operating earnings and low illustrative leverage, financed partly by shares issued for Foran. The weakest point is nine-month free cash flow of USD negative 654.3 million and liquidity down from USD 976.0 million to USD 671.5 million since December 2025. The outcome depends on Skouries and McIlvenna Bay reaching commercial production in late Q4 2026 at or near the revised budgets, on gold prices holding, and on the senior notes and revolver refinancing from 2029. Confidence is Medium because trailing earnings and covenant headroom are not supplied.

### Key findings

1. Eldorado Gold's cash is being consumed by growth spending rather than by operations. In the nine months to September 30, 2026 it generated USD 425.7 million of operating cash flow from continuing operations but spent USD 1,210.0 million on property, plant and equipment and USD 45.9 million on capitalised interest paid, a shortfall of about USD 830.2 million before acquisition cash, dividends, buybacks and borrowings (CP-1, Calculations). Company-defined free cash flow was negative USD 654.3 million; excluding Skouries and McIlvenna Bay it was positive USD 202.1 million (Q3 release page:13). Mechanic: the operating mines are cash generative and the two projects absorb it, so default risk is driven by project completion, not by the operating base. Documentary Fact.

2. Liquidity has fallen but covers near-term maturities. Unrestricted cash was USD 396.8 million and the undrawn revolver USD 274.7 million at September 30, 2026, together USD 671.5 million against USD 976.0 million at December 31, 2025 (CP-1). Principal falling due within a year is USD 141.1 million, so liquidity is about 4.8 times that amount (Calculations). Restricted cash of USD 71.9 million for the Skouries term loan reserve and the undrawn euro 30 million VAT facility are excluded. Mechanic: the cushion is adequate for scheduled repayments but not for a prolonged slip in the start-ups combined with a lower gold price. Analyst Interpretation.

3. Debt rose by USD 710.4 million to USD 1,985.4 million between December 31, 2025 and September 30, 2026 (carrying amount). The increase came from USD 321.6 million of revolver borrowings, USD 68.1 million from the final overrun draw on the Skouries loan, and USD 350.5 million of Foran-related debt (the Sprott facility net of its redemption option derivative asset, the equipment facility and government contributions), partly offset by repayments and movements on the Skouries project loans and the VAT facility (CP-1 debt table; Calculations). Foran itself was paid for with shares (USD 2,385.6 million of share consideration), which kept the acquisition from adding to leverage through the purchase price (CP-1A). Documentary Fact.

4. Reported leverage looks moderate only on an illustrative basis. Total debt to annualised nine-month Adjusted EBITDA is 1.67 times and net debt to the same measure 1.34 times; neither is a trailing twelve-month figure because FY2025 EBITDA is not supplied, and the base includes Foran only from April 14, 2026 and no Skouries contribution (CP-1). Cash interest cover (Adjusted EBITDA over interest paid plus capitalised interest paid) was 11.96 times for nine months 2026 and 9.81 times for Q3 2026. These do not use the covenant definitions, so headroom against the revolver tests of at least 3.00 times interest cover and at most 3.50 times net leverage is Not calculable. The illustrative figures sit well inside those levels, which is a reading and not a compliance finding; the Company states it complied at September 30, 2026 (Q3 statements page:14). Analyst Interpretation.

5. Skouries and McIlvenna Bay execution is the pivotal risk. First concentrate was reached at Skouries on September 8, 2026 and at McIlvenna Bay in June 2026, with commercial production for both expected late in Q4 2026 (CP-1A). The Skouries project capital estimate rose from about USD 1.315 billion in the Q1 release to about USD 1.425 billion, the McIlvenna Bay estimate from USD 952 million in the Q2 release to USD 975 million, and the 2026 production outlook was cut to 425,000 to 475,000 ounces from 495,000 to 600,000 ounces. On the release's own figures, remaining Skouries project capital is about USD 46 million and accelerated operational capital about USD 104 million, with up to USD 30 million more possible (Calculations); the remaining McIlvenna Bay spend is Not calculable because spending before the acquisition date is not in the pack. Mechanic: further slippage extends the period of negative free cash flow and delays the debt reduction that management says it expects. Management Language on funding ("fully funded") is not adopted because no funding model is supplied.

6. Refinancing is concentrated and amortisation starts soon. Principal due by year after September 30, 2026 is USD 141.1 million, 142.5 million, 958.8 million, 162.4 million, 354.7 million and 258.2 million beyond year five (CP-1). The third year contains the USD 500.0 million 6.25% senior notes due September 1, 2029 and the USD 325.0 million revolver drawn to March 1, 2029, the largest bucket by a wide margin. The Skouries term loan, non-recourse to the parent and secured on the Skouries and Hellas Gold assets, begins repaying on December 31, 2026 in 13 semi-annual instalments (Q3 statements page:13). Mechanic: if the start-ups deliver, cash flow and the equity-funded balance sheet should support refinancing; if not, the 2029 wall arrives with higher debt. Documentary Fact for the schedule, Analyst Interpretation for the refinancing reading.

7. Shareholder returns continued during the build. Dividends of USD 54.1 million and cancelled buybacks of USD 83.9 million in nine months 2026, about USD 138.0 million together, were paid while free cash flow was negative (CP-1A, Calculations). The senior notes restrict distributions in certain circumstances and the Company reports compliance. No creditor-adverse action is documented. Mechanic: these returns draw on the same liquidity that funds the projects and can be reduced by the board; they are a monitoring point, not a finding of extraction. Analyst Interpretation.

8. Earnings quality needs care. Net earnings from continuing operations were USD 426.2 million for nine months 2026 against USD 270.0 million a year earlier, but the 2025 period included a deferred tax recovery of USD 89.4 million and the 2026 effective tax rate was 37.6% against 16.8% (CP-1 Calculations). The Adjusted EBITDA measure of USD 892.0 million adds back a USD 97.6 million realised loss on gold and copper derivatives and USD 21.3 million of acquisition costs, and removes a USD 100.6 million unrealised derivative gain (Q3 release page:24). Mechanic: the realised swap loss is a real cost of the Skouries financing hedges that the adjusted figure leaves out; those swaps have expired with financial settlement (Q3 statements page:22), so the item should not recur, but the adjusted measure is higher than the unadjusted result for 2026. Documentary Fact.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, the operating base earns at high gold prices: the average realised gold price was USD 4,326 per ounce in Q3 2026 against USD 3,527 a year earlier, and Adjusted EBITDA for nine months 2026 was USD 892.0 million against USD 571.1 million (Documentary Fact, CP-1). Second, diversification across Turkiye, Canada and Greece with Canada now the largest segment at about 45% of nine-month revenue, and several mines at different cost levels, with Lamaque cash costs of USD 897 per ounce in Q3 2026 (CP-1A, Calculations; Analyst Interpretation). Third, an equity-funded Foran purchase that added roughly 24.7% to shares outstanding rather than debt to fund the price, and an extended and enlarged revolver of USD 600.0 million maturing March 1, 2029 (Documentary Fact). Fourth, a Skouries project loan that the notes describe as non-recourse to Eldorado Gold Corporation, which limits parent exposure to that lender if the project falls short (Documentary Fact for the description; the legal documents are not supplied, so the extent of recourse is not verified). Fifth, interest cover on cash interest of about ten to twelve times on matched bases (Analyst Interpretation, not a covenant measure).

Weaknesses. First, negative free cash flow and falling liquidity, with debt up USD 710.4 million in nine months (Documentary Fact). Second, cost and guidance deterioration: all-in sustaining cost guidance raised and 2026 production outlook cut, with Kisladag and Olympias weaker (Documentary Fact). Third, start-up risk and rising project capital at Skouries and McIlvenna Bay, with commercial production dates still forward-looking (Documentary Fact and Management Language). Fourth, a concentrated maturity profile and the start of Skouries term loan repayments in December 2026 (Documentary Fact). Fifth, a leadership transition on September 30, 2026 during the overrun period and shareholder distributions during the build (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on the credit effect). Sixth, high effective tax in 2026 and exposure to a high gold price: the earnings strength depends on a gold price the company does not control, and the pack contains no sensitivity (Analyst Interpretation; sensitivity Not supplied).
