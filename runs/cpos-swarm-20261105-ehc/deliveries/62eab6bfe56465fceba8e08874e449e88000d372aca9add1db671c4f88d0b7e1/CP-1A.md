### Business and transaction view

Encompass Health is the largest owner and operator of inpatient rehabilitation hospitals on its own description, with 177 hospitals in 39 states and Puerto Rico at 30 September 2026, 67 of them joint ventures. There is no leveraged buyout, sponsor, acquisition or restructuring in the pack: the transactions in the period are financings, a small equity-method disposal, a litigation recovery and shareholder distributions. The business is a single segment whose revenue is set largely by government reimbursement, so the creditor risk mechanics run through Medicare rate updates, claim review and denial rules, labor cost inflation and the pace of capacity additions, rather than through sponsor behaviour. The sources show a company that has funded growth with operating cash flow and the revolver while raising dividends and buybacks, and that refinanced half of its 2028 notes into 2034 notes at a higher coupon. Credit-relevant strengths are the nondiscretionary patient base, referral dependence on acute-care hospitals that is also a joint-venture channel, and long-dated fixed-rate debt. Credit-relevant weaknesses are payor concentration, regulatory review expansion from 2027, a billing disruption that lifted bad debt reserves in Q3 2026, geographic concentration in Florida and Texas and a growth plan that consumes more cash than operations generate after distributions. Ownership, control rights and sponsor alignment cannot be assessed beyond the public-company facts, because the proxy statement is not in the pack.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no sponsor transaction, acquisition or restructuring in the sources, so the transaction fact pack is a record of 2026 financing and capital events, all executed and all disclosed in the Q3 10-Q (Documentary Fact). On 9 March 2026 the company signed the 2026 Credit Agreement with Truist Bank as administrative and collateral agent, replacing the 2022 agreement that had Barclays as agent. Terms are described as substantially the same except a maturity of 9 March 2031, an undrawn fee lowered by five basis points, relaxed limits on investments, debt, liens and restricted payments, removal of a 0.10% adjustment on Term SOFR borrowings and a swingline sublimit raised from $25 million to $40 million; a $250.0 million revolver draw repaid the 2022 agreement in full (10-Q Q3 page:18). The agreement is secured by personal property of the company and its subsidiary guarantors. The agreement itself is not in the pack, so the relaxation of restricted payment and debt limits cannot be sized.

In May 2026 the company issued $500 million of 5.875% Senior Notes due 2034 at par for about $491 million of net proceeds, redeemed $400 million of the $800 million 4.50% Senior Notes due 2028 at par and repaid $100 million of revolver borrowings, recording a $3.2 million loss on early extinguishment in Q2 2026. In August 2026 it issued a further $100 million of the 2034 notes at 98.75% for about $97 million of net proceeds, used with cash to repay revolver borrowings (10-Q Q3 page:19). The liquidity effect is that revolver drawings fell to 75.0 at 30 September 2026 and availability rose to about $871 million, while total debt at carrying value rose by 108.3 over nine months 2026 (CP-1 reference; deck Q3 page:8). The coupon effect is that the weighted coupon on senior notes rises to 4.99% from 4.63% (Analyst Interpretation, see Calculations): the remaining 2028 notes at $400 million face are callable at par, and the 2034 notes are callable beginning June 2029 per the deck chart (deck Q3 page:11). The use of proceeds to retire lower-coupon debt rather than to fund distributions shows a refinancing motive, though total debt rose in the same period that dividends and buybacks rose.

Other 2026 events: a $17.9 million sale of the 50% equity interest in Gamma Knife Center at Barnes-Jewish Hospital agreed on 31 December 2025 and effective 1 January 2026, with a $17.5 million pre-tax gain in Other income (10-Q Q3 page:12); and a $21.9 million collection in February 2026 from defendants in litigation against former executive officers of the home health and hospice business spun off on 1 July 2022, producing a $16.3 million after-tax gain in discontinued operations (10-Q Q3 page:14). Both are non-recurring and are excluded or separately shown in management's adjusted measures. Subsequent events after 30 September 2026 and before the cutoff are: a dividend of $0.21 per share declared on 22 October 2026 for payment on 15 January 2027 (10-Q Q3 page:43); the Bangor, Maine and San Antonio, Texas hospitals opened in October 2026 (10-Q Q3 page:28); and raised 2026 guidance on 28 October 2026 (deck Q3 page:7). No new debt issue after 30 September 2026 appears in the sources. Ratings and pricing of the new notes against comparables cannot be assessed because no rating or quote is supplied.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: Medicare 66.3% and Medicare Advantage 15.7% of Q3 2026 revenue and 92% of patients admitted from acute-care hospitals (10-Q Q3 page:33; 10-K page:9). Mechanic: nondiscretionary demand supports volume, but nearly all price-setting is administrative, so a rule change reprices most revenue at once and referral channels depend on acute-care partners. Implication: revenue visibility is high on volume and low on rate; debt service capacity depends on regulatory outcomes more than on competition. Counter-evidence: the pack contains no logged conflict against this.
2. Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: Medicare rate update of about 2.3% against assumed labor cost growth per FTE of 3.5% to 4.0% (10-Q Q3 page:31; deck Q3 page:7); bad debt reserves up to 2.7% of revenue from 1.9% (deck Q3 page:5); review expansion to 108 hospitals from 1 Jan 2027 (10-Q Q3 page:31). Mechanic: a price-cost gap and slower collections reduce EBITDA and cash conversion together. Implication: leverage of 1.88x leaves cushion, but a combined margin and collection shock would reduce the cash available for growth capex and distributions first. The conflicting account-receivable figures logged below limit how precisely cash conversion can be traced.
3. Concentration risk. Evidence: Florida and Texas beds of 3,710 out of 11,465 and a single segment (10-K page:110, page:216). Mechanic: state-level events hit a third of capacity. Implication: modest diversification within a single reimbursement system.
4. Sponsor alignment. Not applicable as a sponsor concept: the company is listed with no named sponsor. Alignment is with public shareholders through dividends and buybacks, which were partly debt-funded in nine months 2026. Implication: monitor distribution pace against free cash flow after growth capex.
5. Key uncertainties. Control and ownership detail, covenant definitions, guarantor list and the duration of the billing disruption are not evidenced.
