### Credit view

Encompass Health Corp is a moderately leveraged, cash-generative inpatient rehabilitation operator whose credit is sound but exposed to government reimbursement. Its strongest support is financial: total debt was 1.88x trailing Adjusted EBITDA at Q3 2026, liquidity was USD 947.2 million, and the first bond maturity is USD 400 million face of 2028 notes. Its weakest point is that Medicare and Medicare Advantage supplied 82.0% of Q3 2026 revenue, so price updates and claim-review rules can move earnings quickly, while cash flow after capex, partner distributions, dividends and buybacks was negative USD 62.9 million in nine months 2026. The outcome depends on whether review expansion from 1 January 2027 and the billing disruption erode cash conversion before the issuer slows growth capex or shareholder returns.

### Key findings

Encompass Health carries USD 2,599.1 million of total debt at carrying value at 30 September 2026, equal to 1.88x trailing four quarter Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,379.0 million, or 1.83x net of USD 76.2 million of cash; these are analyst figures on carrying values and company-defined EBITDA, not the covenant ratios, and the covenant maximum is 4.50x [CP-1 Chart data: leverage; Covenants] (Documentary Fact for the inputs, Analyst Interpretation for the ratios).

The issuer has USD 947.2 million of liquidity: USD 76.2 million of cash plus about USD 871 million available under a USD 1,000 million revolver that matures on 9 March 2031. Its first bond maturity is USD 400 million face of 4.50% notes due 2028, followed by USD 800 million face of 4.75% notes due 2030 [CP-1 Liquidity; Debt by instrument; Chart data: debt maturities] (Documentary Fact).

Revenue is set mainly by government payors. Medicare was 66.3% and Medicare Advantage 15.7% of Q3 2026 revenue, 92% of patients are admitted from acute-care hospitals, and the 2027 Medicare rate update of about 2.3% sits against guided labor cost growth per FTE of 3.5% to 4.0% [CP-1A Company and operating model] (Documentary Fact for the mix and update, Management Language for the guidance).

The issuer funds a large capital programme and rising shareholder returns from operating cash flow and debt. For nine months 2026, operating cash flow of USD 957.7 million less capex of USD 617.2 million, partner distributions of USD 141.8 million, dividends of USD 57.9 million and buybacks of USD 203.7 million left negative USD 62.9 million, and carrying debt rose USD 108.3 million [CP-1 Cash flow quality; CP-1A Ownership, governance and capital allocation] (Documentary Fact for the components, Analyst Interpretation for the net). A correction recorded in the business pack means a Q3 2026 buyback of USD 57.9 million is supplied, which the financial base had listed as not supplied.

The 2026 refinancing pushed half of the 2028 notes out to 2034 at a higher coupon: the weighted coupon on senior notes rose to 4.99% from 4.63%, and the revolver maturity moved from October 2027 to March 2031 [CP-1A Transaction summary and new debt issues; CP-1 Debt by instrument] (Analyst Interpretation for the coupon, Documentary Fact for the dates).

Review Choice Demonstration will apply to 108 of the issuer's hospitals from 1 January 2027, and bad debt reserves reached 2.7% of Q3 2026 revenue from 1.9%, attributed by management to a billing disruption after a system conversion [CP-1A Company and operating model] (Documentary Fact; the cause is Management Language). Pre-claim review delays or denies payment, so these two items bear directly on cash conversion.

Interest cover is high: Adjusted EBITDA covered interest excluding amortization 11.7x on a trailing basis at Q3 2026 against a 3.0x covenant minimum [CP-1 Chart data: interest cover] (Analyst Interpretation); cash paid for interest in FY2025 gave 9.5x on the annual report figure, so the basis matters.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The issuer has modest leverage, with total debt at 1.88x trailing Adjusted EBITDA at Q3 2026, and wide room under the 4.50x covenant maximum (Analyst Interpretation, CP-1 Chart data: leverage). It has ample liquidity of USD 947.2 million and no revolver expiry until March 2031 (Documentary Fact, CP-1 Liquidity). Its notes carry fixed coupons, with the first bond maturity in 2028 (Documentary Fact, CP-1 Debt by instrument). Demand is nondiscretionary, with 92% of patients admitted from acute-care hospitals, and 67 of 177 hospitals are joint ventures that tie the issuer to referring systems (Documentary Fact, CP-1A Company and operating model). Growth capex is largely discretionary, since only about USD 225 million to USD 240 million of 2026 capex is nondiscretionary against a total of about USD 920 million to USD 995 million (Documentary Fact, CP-1A Company and operating model), which gives the issuer a lever to protect cash in a downturn. Adjusted EBITDA reached USD 346.6 million in Q3 2026 against USD 300.1 million (Documentary Fact, CP-1 Financials and credit metrics).

Weaknesses. The issuer depends on one payor class for most revenue, with Medicare and Medicare Advantage at 82.0% of Q3 2026 revenue, so a single rule change reprices most of the book (Documentary Fact, CP-1A Company and operating model). Review Choice Demonstration coverage will reach 108 hospitals from 1 January 2027 and bad debt reserves are higher, which together put cash conversion at risk (Documentary Fact). Cash flow after growth capex and distributions was negative USD 62.9 million in nine months 2026, so debt rose while leverage stayed modest (Analyst Interpretation, CP-1 Cash flow quality). Adjusted EBITDA deducts net income attributable to noncontrolling interests while debt is consolidated in full, and only part of the group guarantees the notes, with the parent and guarantors producing USD 3,016.4 million of the USD 4,793.5 million nine-month 2026 revenue (Documentary Fact, CP-1 Perimeter and Findings). Florida and Texas hold 32.4% of licensed beds at 31 December 2025 (Analyst Interpretation, CP-1A Calculations). Ownership, control rights, covenant ratios, guarantor list and ratings are not evidenced (Insufficient Information, CP-0 Gaps), and missing evidence is not read as an adverse conclusion.
