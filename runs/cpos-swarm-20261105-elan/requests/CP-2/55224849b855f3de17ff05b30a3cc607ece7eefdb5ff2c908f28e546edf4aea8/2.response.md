### Credit view

Elanco Animal Health Incorporated is a moderately leveraged but improving credit whose liquidity and maturity profile are adequate and whose main weakness is the quality and definition of its leverage. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 1,445 million at 2026-06-30 and required principal payments of only USD 63 million in each of 2026 and 2027, with no large maturity before 2028. The weakest point is that total debt including the finance lease is 3.91x trailing Adjusted EBITDA, against management's 3.1x net measure, while Adjusted EBITDA includes net add-backs of USD 236 million and claims outside the debt table sit beside it. What decides the outcome is whether trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,008 million converts to cash after restructuring, interest and a rising floating-rate exposure, and whether the USD 1,236 million falling due in 2028 is refinanced on similar terms.

### Key findings

First, liquidity and the near maturity profile are strong relative to obligations. Elanco Animal Health Incorporated held cash of USD 530 million and about USD 915 million of undrawn facilities at 2026-06-30, a total of USD 1,445 million, which is 11.5 times the USD 126 million of required principal payments in 2026 and 2027 [CP-1]. No facility matures before June 2028, so liquidity is not a near-term constraint on this evidence.

Second, leverage has improved but its level depends on the definition. Total debt including the finance lease fell from 4.49x trailing Adjusted EBITDA at 2025-12-31 to 3.91x at 2026-06-30 and net leverage on the company's definition fell from 3.6x to 3.1x [CP-1]. Most of the improvement came from higher Adjusted EBITDA rather than repayment: debt fell by USD 101 million while trailing Adjusted EBITDA rose from USD 901 million to USD 1,008 million [CP-1]. On reported EBITDA the same total debt is 5.10x.

Third, the quality of Adjusted EBITDA is the main analytical risk. Net add-backs of USD 236 million equal 31 percent of reported trailing EBITDA of USD 773 million, special charges have run at USD 127 million, USD 150 million, USD 237 million and USD 252 million in FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 and the trailing twelve months, and part of the restructuring is cash with a reserve of USD 86 million still to be paid [CP-1]. The removal of sold royalty revenue works in the other direction and makes the measure more conservative on that item [CP-1].

Fourth, important claims sit outside the leverage ratio. The liability for sale of future revenue to Blackstone is USD 315 million with an effective interest rate of 18.3 percent, the headquarters finance lease has a USD 250 million purchase option or landlord put in 2030 that management expects to exercise, the AHV acquisition carries USD 100 million of deferred cash and up to USD 140 million of contingent payments, and pension funded status is USD (150) million [CP-1; CP-1A]. The pack does not show how these rank against lenders or whether the royalty rights were collateral.

Fifth, 2028 is the refinancing event. USD 1,236 million of required principal falls due in 2028, which is 33 percent of the scheduled total and includes the USD 750 million senior notes due 2028-08-28, an incremental term facility due 2028-08-12 and the securitization facility due June 2028 [CP-1]. The company refinanced its 2027 term loan in October 2025 into facilities maturing in 2029 and 2032, which shows market access, and management targets net leverage below 3x in 2027 [CP-1A].

Sixth, interest sensitivity is rising. Interest rate swaps of USD 2,300 million notional mature in August 2026 against forward-starting swaps of USD 1,450 million, so unhedged floating-rate debt rises from USD 639 million to USD 1,489 million on the pack's figures, about USD 15 million a year for each 100 basis points before any later hedging that the pack does not show [CP-1]. Fiscal year 2025 cash interest cover on Adjusted EBITDA was 4.37x, or 3.62x before interest received on net investment hedges [CP-1]; trailing cash interest is not supplied.

Seventh, the information base limits confidence. Covenant levels are printed but achieved ratios are not, the credit agreement, indenture, guarantor list and collateral are not supplied, there are no ratings, and nothing after 2026-08-05 is in the pack [CP-0; CP-1]. On the proxies in the financial base, trailing Adjusted EBITDA could fall by about 59 percent before the leverage covenant level is reached and by about 54 percent before the interest cover level is reached, but covenant definitions differ and these are not headroom.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Elanco Animal Health Incorporated has a diversified revenue base across pet health and farm animal products, about 200 brands and more than 90 countries, with Q2 2026 revenue up 10 percent reported and 8 percent in organic constant currency, driven by new products [CP-1; CP-1A]. Liquidity of USD 1,445 million and a light maturity schedule before 2028 provide time. Gross margin rose to 58.3 percent in Q2 2026 from 57.5 percent, and operating cash flow was USD 290 million in six months 2026 against USD 233 million, with net capital spending falling to USD 84 million from USD 122 million, so operating cash flow less net purchases was USD 379 million trailing [CP-1]. There is no sponsor, no dividend and no buyback beyond employee tax withholding, and the company repaid a net USD 563 million of borrowings in 2025 [CP-1A]. The October 2025 refinancing extended maturities. Covenant levels of 7.71 to 1.00 and 2.00 to 1.00 sit far from the proxies computed from the pack [CP-1].

Weaknesses. Leverage remains high at 3.91x on total debt and 5.10x on reported EBITDA, the trailing reported result is a loss of USD 199 million, and tangible equity is negative at USD (1,461) million because goodwill and intangibles exceed total equity [CP-1]. Adjusted EBITDA relies on add-backs of USD 236 million, including cash restructuring. Claims outside the debt table, hedge roll-off, customer concentration (two distributors at about 19 percent of FY2025 revenue) and product concentration (five families at about 38 percent) add cash flow volatility, and the first half carries most of the parasiticide sales [CP-1; CP-1A]. The senior notes coupon of 4.900 percent has reset to 6.40 percent after earlier downgrades, which shows the cost of credit events [CP-1]. Governance documents, guarantor and collateral structure, achieved covenant ratios and ratings are missing, and the stated capital allocation flexibility at below 3x leverage is a monitoring point [CP-1A].
