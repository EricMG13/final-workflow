### Credit view

Entegris, Inc. is a cyclical, customer-concentrated supplier of semiconductor materials whose credit profile improved sharply in 2026 and is now adequate for its debt load. The strongest support is deleveraging with cash generation: total debt over Adjusted EBITDA is 3.27x on an LTM Q3 2026 basis against 4.23x for FY2025, the term loan has been repaid, the remaining USD 3,295.0 million of debt is fixed-rate notes, and nothing matures before 2028. The weakest point is the quality and durability of earnings: demand is volume-led from a few very large customers with no purchase commitments, and 2026 margins are helped by an accounting change and one-off refunds. What decides the outcome is whether advanced logic and memory fab demand holds through the 2028 to 2030 maturities. [CP-1 leverage data; CP-1A operating model]

### Key findings

The first finding is that Entegris, Inc. has deleveraged on both sides of the ratio. Par debt fell to USD 3,295.0 million at September 26, 2026 from USD 3,745.0 million at December 31, 2025 through term loan repayment of USD 450.0 million, while LTM Adjusted EBITDA rose to USD 1,008.2 million from USD 886.2 million in FY2025, so total debt over Adjusted EBITDA moved from 4.34x at FY2024 to 4.23x at FY2025 and 3.27x LTM Q3 2026, and net debt over Adjusted EBITDA from 3.99x to 3.82x to 2.91x. The mechanism is repayment from operating cash flow, which also removed floating-rate exposure, so the remaining debt service is fixed coupons of about USD 161.3 million a year and cash interest cover is 5.57x. [CP-1 leverage and interest cover data, debt schedule]

The second finding is that the cash generation behind this is strong but partly timing-supported. LTM free cash flow after capital spending was USD 616.0 million, equal to 18.7% of par debt, helped by capital spending of USD 182.0 million LTM against USD 299.2 million in FY2025 and by a working-capital inflow from payables and incentive accruals that the filings describe as timing. If capital spending returns toward the roughly USD 250.0 million planned in the annual report, or payables normalise, free cash flow would fall. [CP-1 financials and cash flow quality]

The third finding is that liquidity is ample and the maturity profile is back-ended but concentrated. Cash of USD 356.5 million plus the undrawn USD 750.0 million revolver gives USD 1,106.5 million before letters of credit, which are not supplied, and the revolver runs to April 29, 2031. Maturities are nil to 2027, USD 400.0 million in 2028, USD 2,000.0 million in 2029 (60.7% of par debt, including the USD 1,600.0 million secured notes) and USD 895.0 million in 2030, so refinancing risk sits in 2029 and depends on market access then. Only USD 58.3 million of the cash is held in the U.S. [CP-1 liquidity and debt maturities]

The fourth finding is that business risk is the weak side of the credit. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company was 16% of FY2025 net sales and the top ten customers were 50%; customers provide non-binding forecasts and no long-term purchase commitments. In Q3 2026 net sales rose 24.6% year on year, mainly on volume, but sales to Equipment and Engineering customers rose 66.8% against 19.3% for fabs, so part of the growth follows equipment shipments, which are more cyclical. A volume reversal would hit operating leverage first and then cash flow and leverage. [CP-1A operating model]

The fifth finding is that reported 2026 profitability flatters the run-rate. Depreciation was USD 55.6 million lower over nine months 2026 from a change in useful lives, and USD 8.3 million of tariff refunds helped Q3 2026 gross margin, though both are excluded from or neutral to Adjusted EBITDA. Adjusted EBITDA is management-defined; on unadjusted EBITDA, total leverage is 3.36x LTM Q3 2026 against 3.27x, so definition risk is small today. Covenant EBITDA is not supplied: the first lien net leverage covenant of 5.20x, tested only when revolver utilisation exceeds a threshold that is not printed, cannot be compared with any ratio, and a purely illustrative first lien net leverage of 1.23x (secured notes less cash over Adjusted EBITDA) is not covenant headroom. [CP-1 definition review, covenants]

The sixth finding concerns creditor control. No sponsor, parent or controlling holder is identified, dividends are a steady USD 0.10 per share each quarter, there were no buybacks in FY2025, and the documented actions are debt repayment, so no extraction behaviour is documented. The legal capacity to add secured debt is wide in principle, with incremental amounts up to the greater of USD 1.1 billion or 100% of EBITDA, and the financing documents, guarantor list and ratings are not supplied, so structural priority and any creditor-protection conclusion are left open. [CP-1A governance and capital allocation]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Debt reduction with falling leverage is a documented fact: total debt over Adjusted EBITDA is 3.27x LTM Q3 2026, down from 4.34x at FY2024, with all remaining debt fixed-rate notes and no maturity before 2028 (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 leverage data and debt schedule). Cash generation is strong: LTM free cash flow of USD 616.0 million exceeds the sum of the roughly USD 161.3 million of annual note coupons and LTM dividends of USD 61.3 million, which is USD 222.6 million, by USD 393.4 million (Analyst Interpretation of CP-1 cash flow figures; the coupon figure excludes revolver fees). Liquidity of USD 1,106.5 million before letters of credit, with no facility expiring within twelve months, gives room to absorb a downturn (Documentary Fact for the inputs; the sum excludes unsupplied letters of credit). The business is embedded in customer processes through recurring consumables and no platform exceeds 3% of net sales, which management describes as supporting revenue that is predominantly unit-driven or recurring (Management Language; basis CP-1A operating model). Capital allocation is creditor-friendly on documented actions: debt repayment, steady dividends and no repurchases, with no sponsor (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1A governance).

Weaknesses. Revenue is concentrated and uncommitted: one customer is 16% of FY2025 sales and ten customers are 50%, with no long-term purchase commitments (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1A customers). Demand is cyclical and the recent growth leans on equipment-linked categories, which may reverse (Analyst Interpretation; basis CP-1A end markets). Earnings quality needs care: depreciation was reduced by an accounting estimate change, tariff refunds were one-off, Adjusted EBITDA is management-defined and covenant EBITDA is unknown (Documentary Fact for the items; Analyst Interpretation for the effect). Refinancing is concentrated in 2029, at USD 2,000.0 million or 60.7% of par debt (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 debt maturities). Supply and trade exposure is real: single-source inputs, 26% of Q3 2026 sales to Taiwan and flat sales to China (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1A operating model). Contract, covenant, letter-of-credit, guarantor and rating evidence is Not supplied, so headroom, structural priority and market view cannot be assessed (Insufficient Information; the missing items would decide covenant and structural conclusions).
