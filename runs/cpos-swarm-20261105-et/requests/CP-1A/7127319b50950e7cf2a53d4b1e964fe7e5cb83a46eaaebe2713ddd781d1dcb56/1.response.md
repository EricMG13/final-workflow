### Business and transaction view

Energy Transfer LP is a large, diversified midstream operator whose eight reportable segments cover natural gas gathering, processing and transportation, NGL and refined products, crude oil, and two publicly traded consolidated subsidiaries: Sunoco LP (fuel distribution and terminals) and USAC (compression). The 10-K states that no customer individually accounted for more than 10 percent of revenue in FY2025, and in Q3 2026 no segment contributed more than a quarter of Adjusted EBITDA on the figures in CP-1, so concentration risk by customer or segment is limited on the documents read. The creditor-relevant features are three. First, change: Sunoco LP acquired Parkland on October 31, 2025, which enlarged that segment's revenue and earnings (it supplied 560 US$m of the 1,193 US$m increase in Q3 Adjusted EBITDA in CP-1) and added subsidiary debt, so the reported leverage improvement depends on a subsidiary with separate creditors. Second, a continuing programme of acquisitions and growth capital: J-W Power, TanQuid, Delta and Offen in 2026, Vaquero agreed on October 6, 2026 for about 2.63 billion US$ (1.95 billion US$ cash plus about 33.3 million new common units), growth capital guided at 5.4 billion to 5.6 billion US$ for 2026 and about 7 billion US$ for 2027. Third, structure and control: the parent's senior notes are unguaranteed by subsidiaries, a single individual controls the general partner, and the issuer's state of organisation and exchange changed in 2026 without a supplied explanation.

What is documented is stated as such below; management language (for example "exceptional product and geographic diversity") is quoted and not adopted. Nothing here is an opinion on creditworthiness. The sponsor field in the context is blank, which is not evidence that no controlling holder exists: the 10-K identifies one.

### Ownership, governance and capital allocation

Control. "Mr. Warren owns an 81.2% membership interest in our general partner and controls our general partner" and "owns all of the outstanding Energy Transfer Class A Units, which represents an approximately 20% voting interest in the Partnership"; the general partner "has the right to appoint and replace the members of the board, including all of its independent directors"; common unitholders "have no right to elect our general partner or the board of directors" (10-K page:132). The filing requires a vote of at least 66 2/3 percent of outstanding common units to remove the general partner and notes that the Class A units vote with the common units, so his holdings make removal harder (Analyst Interpretation). The 851,420,597 Class A units at December 31, 2025 are 19.8 percent of Class A plus common units outstanding (C6), which agrees with the filing's approximate 20 percent. His direct common unit holding is Not supplied in the pages read; the proxy statement is not in the pack. Ownership percentages are not inferred beyond what is stated.

Governance disputes. Unitholders have filed derivative actions against past and current officers, directors, LE GP, LLC and the Partnership as nominal defendant, alleging breach of fiduciary duties related primarily to pipeline construction in Pennsylvania and Ohio, and seeking damages and changes to governance (Q3 10-Q page:32). They are pending and unquantified.

Legal capacity, willingness and feasibility are kept apart. Legal capacity: the senior notes carry covenants including "a limitation on liens, a limitation on transactions with affiliates, a restriction on sale-leaseback transactions and limitations on mergers and sales of all or substantially all of the Partnership’s assets" and are unguaranteed (10-K page:325); the Five-Year Credit Facility has a maximum leverage ratio of 5.00 to 1.00, generally 5.50 to 1.00 in a Specified Acquisition Period (10-K page:330). The documents read do not show financial maintenance covenants on the notes; executed indentures are not supplied. Willingness (documented actions): the Partnership distributes all available cash quarterly and raised the distribution for 2026 quarters from 0.3350 to 0.3425 US$ per unit (Q3 10-Q page:19); it made no unit repurchases in the nine months to September 30, 2026, with 880 US$m remaining under the 2015 programme (Q3 10-Q page:19); it redeemed preferred units with proceeds of junior subordinated notes; it suspended the Lake Charles LNG liquefaction project in December 2025 (10-K page:9). Feasibility: liquidity, leverage and refinancing capacity are in CP-1 (liquidity 8,692 US$m including subsidiary facilities, with 4,406 US$m at the parent).

Behaviour category (issuer level, documented actions only): Neutral or Mixed. Distributions are paid from available cash and increased while growth capital is large, and growing distributions coexist with a falling reported covenant leverage ratio (CP-1) and debt-funded growth. No priming, uptier, drop-down, non-pro-rata exchange or collateral release was found in the pages read. Evidence quality: High for the filings; the scoring dimensions informed are control concentration, distribution policy and capital allocation.

Capital allocation mechanics. Cash acquisitions at the parent and at Sunoco LP and USAC are funded by cash, commercial paper and facility borrowings or new notes, raising consolidated debt unless offset by EBITDA; unit consideration reduces cash needs but increases distributions. Paying 100 percent of available cash leaves growth to be financed by debt and subsidiary-level capital, as CP-1 shows a negative LTM free cash flow after distributions of 873 US$m. Preferred redemption funded by junior subordinated notes raises debt-ranking claims while lowering the preferred coupon base (the notes' initial rates are 6.550% and 6.700%, against 6.500% on Series H, 10-K page:336); this is an Analyst Interpretation.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: no customer above 10 percent of revenue (10-K page:39); demand-fee and take-or-pay contracts (10-K page:8 and page:11). Mechanic: contracted fee streams cover a share of margin, while marketing, processing margins and fuel distribution vary with commodity spreads. Implication: earnings are more stable than the product-sales revenue suggests, but the proportion of margin that is contracted is Not supplied, so the coverage of fixed charges by contracted income cannot be stated. Counter-evidence: working capital and unrealized commodity swings in cash flow (CP-1).
2. Subsidiary leverage and structural subordination. Evidence: separate Sunoco LP and USAC facilities and notes, and parent notes unguaranteed (10-K page:325; Q3 10-Q page:18). Mechanic: subsidiary creditors rank ahead of the parent in subsidiary assets and cash, while Sunoco LP supplied 560 US$m of the Q3 Adjusted EBITDA increase (CP-1). Implication: the parent's creditors benefit from growth in those subsidiaries only through distributions, so parent leverage measured on consolidated EBITDA overstates parent-available coverage; this is a consideration for CP-2, not a conclusion.
3. Acquisition-led growth and funding. Evidence: Vaquero, Offen, J-W Power, TanQuid and Parkland (Q3 10-Q page:57 and page:58; 10-K page:313). Mechanic: cash components add to borrowings or draw on facilities; unit components dilute distributions per unit. Implication: leverage tolerance depends on EBITDA growth outpacing debt; the Vaquero funding plan is Not supplied, so pro forma leverage is Not calculable.
