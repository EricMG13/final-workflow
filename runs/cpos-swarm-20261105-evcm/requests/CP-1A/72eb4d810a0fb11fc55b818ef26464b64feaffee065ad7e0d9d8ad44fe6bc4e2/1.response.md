### Business and transaction view

EverCommerce Inc. is a US-listed, sponsor-controlled provider of vertically tailored software-as-a-service to service-based small businesses in home, health and wellness services, with recurring and re-occurring revenue near all of the total and a large, granular customer base. The evidence runs from the 10-K business description and the revenue model through sponsor control rights to the credit facilities: a business that is diversified and cash generative but dependent on third-party payment processors, with a founder-chief executive and two financial sponsors who can block a change of control, a chief executive replacement or large asset deals. For creditors the mechanics are favourable on diversification and the absence of any documented debt-funded distribution, with the term loan repriced and extended rather than increased. The strongest contrary evidence is that sponsors control board composition and consent rights, share repurchases have been running above free cash flow on an LTM basis, a pending Delaware case attacks the sponsors' veto over the chief executive, and the August 2026 release signals a chief executive change without a date. No transaction in the sources raises new debt. The limits are the absent credit agreement and the absence of any sponsor-level history before the sources start.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No new debt issue appears in the sources for the period under review. The credit facilities were repriced and extended rather than upsized: a December 13, 2024 amendment cut the term loan margin and removed the credit spread adjustment, a June 10, 2025 amendment reduced revolver commitments from 190.0 to 155.0 US$m and extended 125.0 US$m of them, and a July 29, 2025 amendment refinanced the term loan at par, extended its maturity to July 6, 2031 and cut the margin by 25 basis points (10-K page:147, page:148; 10-Q June 2026 page:19, page:20). On July 6, 2026 the remaining 30.0 US$m of the original revolver tranche expired, which is after the quarter end and before the cutoff (10-Q June 2026 page:52). Portfolio changes in the period were the sale of Fitness Solutions to Jonas Software, signed March 13, 2024 with the UK part closing July 1, 2024; the acquisition of ZyraTalk on September 15, 2025 for approximately 36.0 US$m in cash plus up to 6.5 US$m of contingent consideration tied to post-combination employment services; and the sale of the marketing technology business to Ignite Visibility on October 31, 2025 for approximately 45.0 US$m in cash subject to closing adjustments, which became discontinued operations and was followed by a transition services agreement that ran to April 30, 2026 (10-K page:9, page:10; 10-Q June 2026 page:12 to page:14). The sources give no use-of-proceeds statement for the marketing technology sale; the cash flow statement shows net disposal proceeds of 37,051 thousand in FY2025 (EX-99.1 March 2026 page:10), and any link to buybacks is not stated. A search of the 10-K, the two 2026 10-Qs and the 2026 releases for merger, take-private, tender offer and secondary offering found no such transaction. The Delaware case challenging the sponsors' chief executive approval right is the only pending matter bearing on control. The terms and parties of any further sponsor transaction are Not supplied.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: about 97% recurring or re-occurring revenue and a customer base of more than 745,000 with very small average revenue (10-K page:9; 10-Q June 2026 page:33). Mechanic: granularity and subscription terms (contracts with 30-day termination rights exist, per the remaining performance obligations note at 10-Q page:16) mean low concentration but quick churn if small businesses weaken. Implication: revenue is durable in the near term but net revenue retention of about 94% means growth must come from new customers; this counters any assumption of automatic deleveraging through earnings growth. Counter-evidence logged: none conflicting.
- Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: operating costs excluding D&A of 79.8% of H1 revenue against 78.1% and management's stated plan to increase product and sales spending (10-Q June 2026 page:41, page:42, page:44). Mechanic: investment-led cost growth compresses Adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow while add-backs remain large (CP-1). Implication: leverage tolerance rests on management's ability to hold the cost base; monitor quarterly margin.
- Concentration and dependency. Evidence: payments about 20% of revenue and reliance on Worldpay, PayPal and card networks (10-K page:35, page:38). Mechanic: loss of processor or network access, or a compliance failure, interrupts transaction revenue. Implication: operational single-point exposure that is not visible in leverage metrics.
- Sponsor alignment and control. Evidence: sponsor consent rights and approximately 85.7% ownership (10-K page:95); no dividend and cash buybacks. Mechanic: the sponsors can steer capital allocation and block a sale or insolvency filing, while the company's own repurchases raise the sponsors' percentage ownership as shares are retired if the sponsors do not sell, an analyst inference. Implication: creditors rely on the covenant package, which is not supplied; governance quality is Neutral or Mixed on documented actions.
- Key uncertainties. The outcome of the Delaware case, the identity and timing of the chief executive transition, covenant capacity for restricted payments and debt incurrence, and the effect of the unremediated material weakness on reported revenue.
