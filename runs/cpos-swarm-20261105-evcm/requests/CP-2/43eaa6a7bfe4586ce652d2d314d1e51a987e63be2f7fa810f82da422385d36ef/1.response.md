### Credit view

The fundamental credit view for EverCommerce Inc. is stable on current metrics with a negative earnings bias, at a confidence of Medium. Evidence from the financial base shows a single secured term loan of 523.875 US$m maturing in July 2031, an undrawn revolver, LTM leverage of 2.98x gross and 2.22x net and cash interest cover of 5.31x; the business handoff shows a diversified base of more than 745,000 small service customers with recurring revenue near all of the total. The mechanics supporting the view are low customer concentration, modest amortization, a maturity profile with no principal due before 2030 beyond amortization, and cash on hand that exceeds a year of mandatory debt service by a wide margin. The mechanics pulling against it are a net revenue retention of about 94% for the June 2026 quarter, H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA below H1 2025, investment-led cost growth, a management signal that 2026 will land at the low end of guidance, add-backs of 29.7% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA and an unremediated material weakness in revenue accounting. Credit implication: default risk over the next twelve months is low on these inputs because debt service and amortization are covered several times by operating cash flow and liquidity; leverage tolerance is the issue, because earnings are flat to down while shareholders receive cash through buybacks that exceeded free cash flow over the last twelve months, under sponsor control whose limits in the credit agreement are not visible. The view is bounded by the absent credit agreement, the unreported letters of credit and covenant EBITDA, and the cutoff of the sources at August 5, 2026.

### Key findings

The first finding a committee must read is that the debt structure is simple and conservative on its face: one term loan of 523.875 US$m, secured and guaranteed, repriced and extended in 2024 and 2025, with 425,000 thousand of swaps fixing about 81.1% of the balance until October 31, 2027 and no principal wall before the 2030 revolver and 2031 term loan maturities. Gross leverage of 2.98x and net leverage of 2.22x on LTM company Adjusted EBITDA are below FY2024's 3.24x and 2.41x, and cash interest cover rose from 3.61x to 5.31x. Evidence of mechanism: lower base rates and margin cuts reduced cash interest, and scheduled amortization of 5.500 US$m a year is small against free cash flow, so debt service is covered. Implication: near-term payment risk is low.

The second finding is that the headline overstates the earnings base. Adjusted EBITDA adds back stock compensation, amortization of contract costs and non-recurring costs of 52.275 US$m on an LTM basis, 29.7% of the total, and non-recurring costs have been positive in every period shown. Without the stock compensation add-back leverage is 3.47x gross and 2.58x net. H1 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of 85.211 US$m is below H1 2025's 89.902 US$m, LTM is below FY2025, and free cash flow fell from 57.3% to 40.8% of Adjusted EBITDA between FY2024 and LTM. Implication: the leverage cushion is real but narrower than the headline implies, and a continuing earnings decline would move leverage toward the 3.7x area under a 20% stress.

The third finding is the guidance arithmetic. Management said on August 5, 2026 that results are expected toward the lower end of the 183 to 191 US$m Adjusted EBITDA range, yet reaching even the low end after the third quarter range of 44 to 46 US$m requires a fourth quarter of 51.789 to 53.789 US$m against 44.155 US$m a year earlier. Implication: either the fourth quarter steps up materially or full-year results land below the stated range; creditors should expect LTM Adjusted EBITDA near the first-half run rate absent evidence to the contrary, which is a monitoring position and not a forecast.

The fourth finding is capital allocation and control. Providence Strategic Growth and Silver Lake parties hold approximately 85.7% of the common stock with consent rights over change of control, asset deals above 500.0 US$m, equity issuance to non-strategic financial investors and chief executive changes. The company returned 85.141 US$m through buybacks in FY2025 and 82.105 US$m on an LTM basis against LTM free cash flow of 71.687 US$m. No dividend or debt-funded distribution is documented. Implication: willingness to return cash is demonstrated; capacity to do more depends on credit agreement baskets that are not supplied, so restricted-payment headroom is a priority diligence item.

The fifth finding is the set of near-term events: the 30.0 US$m revolver tranche expired on July 6, 2026 leaving 125.0 US$m, a Delaware case challenging the sponsors' chief executive approval right has oral argument scheduled for October 19, 2026 with no outcome in the sources, and a chief executive transition is signalled by the August release without date or full name while the same-day 10-Q is signed by Eric Remer as chief executive. Implication: the pack should be refreshed for third quarter 2026 reporting before a committee relies on it.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Business durability: the customer base is granular, with approximately 93% of customers each contributing under $2 thousand of revenue, recurring or re-occurring revenue near all of the total, so no single-customer concentration is indicated; the mechanic is low idiosyncratic loss risk, and the implication is stable revenue visibility in the near term (Documentary Fact in the business handoff). Liquidity and maturity profile: cash of 133.5 US$m, an undrawn revolver of 125.0 US$m after July 6, 2026, scheduled amortization of 5.500 US$m a year and no maturity of principal beyond amortization until the revolver in July 2030 and the term loan in July 2031 mean refinancing risk is distant, about 56 months to the term loan maturity from the cutoff (Documentary Fact and calculation). Interest rate protection: swaps fixing 425,000 thousand of notional until October 31, 2027 reduce earnings volatility from base rates, and cover of 5.31x is above FY2025's 5.06x (Documentary Fact). Cash generation: operating cash flow after interest covered capital spending, capitalized software and amortization, with LTM free cash flow of 71.687 US$m (Documentary Fact).

Weaknesses. Earnings momentum and quality: Adjusted EBITDA is lower year on year in H1 2026, add-backs are large and recurring, net revenue retention fell to about 94% from about 97%, and the company-defined measure is the only EBITDA available (Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation). Sponsor control and shareholder returns: the sponsors can block a sale or an insolvency filing and approve the chief executive, buybacks have exceeded free cash flow over twelve months, and covenant restrictions are unseen (Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation). Controls and disclosure: an unremediated material weakness in order-to-cash revenue accounting and disclosure controls that were not effective at June 30, 2026 reduce confidence in reported revenue timing (Documentary Fact). Dependency: payments are about 20% of revenue and rely on card networks, Worldpay and PayPal, a single-point exposure not captured in leverage (Documentary Fact). Information limits: no credit agreement, no letters of credit amount, no covenant-basis EBITDA and no filing after August 5, 2026 (Insufficient Information; missing evidence is not treated as adverse).

Thesis. EverCommerce is a diversified, recurring-revenue software credit with moderate leverage, long-dated and modest debt service, and ample liquidity, whose credit quality is held back by flat earnings, large add-backs and sponsor-directed shareholder returns whose limits cannot yet be tested.
