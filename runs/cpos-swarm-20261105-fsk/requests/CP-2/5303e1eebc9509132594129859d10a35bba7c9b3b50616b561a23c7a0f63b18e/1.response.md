### Credit view

FS KKR Capital Corp's creditors are adequately protected in the near term, but the credit is weakening because the equity value beneath the debt is shrinking. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 3,308 million against USD 900 million of notes maturing in 2027, asset coverage of 179% on debt against the 150% minimum, and a lender-agreed lower equity floor of USD 3,750.0 million. The weakest point is the fall in net asset value per share from USD 20.89 to USD 18.28 over nine months 2026, with investment income down 24.2% as the portfolio shrinks, distributions above net investment income and five stockholder suits with no loss estimate. What decides the outcome is whether portfolio marks stabilise and non-accruals, at 3.4% of fair value, keep falling, because the covenants and the asset coverage test all run off net asset value. [Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 figures; CP-1A governance and litigation rows]

### Key findings

The equity cushion under the debt is eroding even as debt falls. Over nine months 2026 common equity fell by USD 850 million to USD 4,999 million, net asset value per share fell from USD 20.89 to USD 18.28, and realised and unrealised losses of USD 766 million outran net investment income of USD 366 million, while debt principal fell 16.8% to USD 6,337 million. Because the debt reduction came from asset sales and repayments rather than retained earnings, debt principal to common equity eased only from 130.3% to 126.8%, so creditors still depend on portfolio marks to hold the cushion. [Documentary Fact; CP-1 Financials and credit metrics and Calculations]

Liquidity is adequate for the next two years but the later wall is larger. The issuer holds USD 111 million of cash and USD 3,197 million of availability, subject to a borrowing base that is not printed, which is 3.68 times the USD 900 million of notes due in January and July 2027 and 2.14 times unfunded commitments of USD 1,545.4 million. No committed facility expires within twelve months. Maturities then rise to USD 1,000 million in 2029, USD 1,644 million in 2030 and USD 1,300 million in 2031, USD 3,944 million in total or 62.2% of principal debt, and the 7.500% coupon on the USD 900 million of 2031 notes issued on June 8, 2026 compares with 3.400% on the notes that reached maturity in January 2026, so refinancing will cost more. [Documentary Fact for the schedule; Analyst Interpretation for the wall; CP-1 debt maturities; CP-1A transaction summary]

The nearest covenant constraint is the equity floor, not the leverage ratio. On May 8, 2026 lenders agreed to cut revolver commitments to about USD 4,051.7 million from USD 4,700.0 million, raise margins and reset the minimum shareholders' equity floor to USD 3,750.0 million from about USD 5,048.6 million. Common equity of USD 4,999 million is USD 1,249.0 million, or 25.0%, above the new floor on a proxy basis, which equals a fall of about 11.0% in investment fair value; asset coverage would take roughly a 15% to 16% fall in its base to reach 150%. Equity at 2026-09-30 was USD 49.6 million below the former floor, so, on this proxy, the amendment avoided a breach. The credit agreement's definition of equity is not supplied, so this ordering is an interpretation. [Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 Covenants and headroom]

Earnings no longer cover the shareholder distribution without sponsor support. Investment income fell 21.2% in Q3 2026 against Q3 2025 and net investment income was USD 127 million, after a USD 12 million incentive fee waiver that runs to the quarter ending March 31, 2027 with no recoupment. Net investment income covered 105.0% of Q3 common distributions of USD 121 million with the waiver and 95.0% without it, and 98.1% over nine months, while a special distribution of USD 0.20 per share and a regular USD 0.44 were declared for the fourth quarter. When the waiver ends, the same income would leave distributions about USD 12 million a quarter less covered, so either the distribution, the portfolio size or net asset value must give; each reduces the cushion or the cash available to reduce debt. [Documentary Fact for the figures; Analyst Interpretation for the consequence; CP-1 Earnings and income quality; CP-1A fee waiver row]

The debt structure puts the unsecured notes behind secured claims that can grow. The issuer has USD 4,650 million of unsecured notes against USD 1,687 million of secured debt, 73.4% and 26.6% of principal debt. The revolver is secured on substantially all assets of the Company and certain subsidiaries and has USD 3,134 million of availability, so secured borrowing ahead of the notes can rise subject to the borrowing base and the 150% asset coverage test; the 7.500% notes state they rank effectively junior to secured debt and are structurally junior to subsidiary debt. As a balance sheet mechanic and not a recovery estimate, investments at fair value less secured debt are 2.07 times the unsecured notes today. The joint venture, worth 32.7% of common equity, also carries USD 2,330.9 million of its own debt that ranks ahead of the issuer in its assets. [Documentary Fact for the structure; Analyst Interpretation for the mechanics; CP-1 Debt schedule; CP-1A Company and operating model]

Sponsor support is real but small against the decline, and governance carries open items. A KKR affiliate paid USD 150 million for convertible preferred stock that ranks junior to debt, a KKR subsidiary ran a tender offer, and the adviser waived half its subordinated income incentive fee, but the USD 150 million is 17.6% of the USD 850 million fall in equity and the preferred carries two board seats, a change-of-control put and a holder put after six years. Share repurchases of USD 90 million through October 30, 2026 use the same cash that could reduce debt. Five stockholder suits allege misleading statements on valuations and distributions, two challenge adviser fees, and the Company states it cannot estimate a loss. No priming, uptiering, collateral release or non-pro-rata exchange is documented, but the executed agreements are not supplied, so legal capacity for such steps is not assessed. [Documentary Fact for the actions; Analyst Interpretation for the weighting; CP-1A governance, ownership and conflicts rows]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity and maturity profile: USD 3,308 million of cash plus availability, no committed facility expiring within twelve months and USD 900 million due in 2027, so near-term debt service and refinancing are covered unless the borrowing base falls sharply. [Documentary Fact; CP-1 liquidity and maturities]. Loan book quality and seniority: 63.2% senior secured across 234 portfolio companies, top ten exposure of 21%, a 10.0% yield on accruing debt and non-accruals down from 4.2% to 3.4% of fair value since March 31, 2026, which supports interest income and marks. [Documentary Fact; CP-1A Company and operating model; CP-1 Financials]. Statutory and covenant headroom with lender support: asset coverage of 179% on debt against a 150% minimum, a lender-agreed lower equity floor and compliance reported at 2026-09-30, which show lenders willing to adjust terms rather than restrict the facility. [Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation for the lender reading; CP-1 Covenants]. Sponsor alignment: preferred capital, a tender, a four-quarter fee waiver with no recoupment and an adviser jointly run by KKR Credit and Future Standard give creditors an engaged sponsor, though the support is limited in size. [Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation; CP-1A governance rows]. Rate balance: a 100 basis point rise adds USD 27 million to twelve-month net interest income and a fall removes the same, with USD 2,600 million of fixed-rate notes swapped to floating, so rate moves do not threaten debt service on their own. [Documentary Fact; CP-1 debt schedule and earnings notes].

Weaknesses. Shrinking cushion and income: net asset value per share is down 12.5% over nine months, investment income is down 24.2%, and debt principal is still 126.8% of common equity, so the earnings base and the cushion both shrink together. [Documentary Fact; CP-1 Calculations]. Distribution not covered without help: net investment income covered 98.1% of nine-month common distributions and would cover 95.0% in Q3 without the waiver, which ends after the quarter ending March 31, 2027; a special distribution and share repurchases continue. [Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 and CP-1A]. Equity-based covenant is the nearest limit: the USD 3,750.0 million floor is about 11.0% of investment fair value from current equity and its definition is not supplied. [Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 Covenants]. Concentration in affiliated and structured holdings: the joint venture is 32.7% of common equity with USD 2,330.9 million of its own debt, asset based finance is 11.6% and equity and other 9.9% of fair value, and affiliated dividend income was USD 84 million of Q3 income of USD 294 million. [Documentary Fact; CP-1A Company and operating model]. Unsecured ranking and growing secured capacity: USD 4,650 million of notes rank effectively junior to USD 1,687 million of secured debt plus up to USD 3,134 million of revolver availability. [Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 Debt schedule]. Legal and valuation scrutiny: five suits about valuations and distributions with no loss estimate, in a business where reported net asset value drives every test. [Documentary Fact; CP-1A Claims and governance rows]. Disclosure limits: no ratings, no common stock ownership table, no covenant certificate and no quantified target leverage, so monitoring relies on management statements. [Insufficient Information; CP-0 and CP-1A gaps].
