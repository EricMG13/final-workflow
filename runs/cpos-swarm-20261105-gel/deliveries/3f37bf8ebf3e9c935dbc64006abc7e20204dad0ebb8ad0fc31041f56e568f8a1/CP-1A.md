### Business and transaction view

Genesis Energy, L.P. is a Delaware master limited partnership that earns most of its margin from fee-based midstream services in the Gulf of America and the Gulf Coast: offshore crude oil and natural gas pipelines, a Jones Act marine fleet, and onshore refinery-linked pipelines, terminals and sulfur removal. The view for a creditor is mixed but constructive on durability. Offshore pipelines operate under life-of-lease commitments and, for the Shenandoah development, contractual minimum volume commitments on the 100% owned SYNC Pipeline and the 64% owned CHOPS Pipeline; marine is mostly time charters; onshore marketing is back-to-back and adds revenue with little margin. Counter-evidence is also on the page: in Q3 2026 Shenandoah volumes were below the minimum, management says production shortfalls continue into early 2027 and that 2026 Adjusted EBITDA will likely be near or below the low end of guidance. The transaction record is dominated by deliberate balance-sheet repositioning: the 2025 sale of the Alkali Business, the March 2026 refinancing into 2034 notes and a new revolver, the June 2026 asset sale, a new receivables facility and repeated purchases of high-cost preferred units. Ownership and control are concentrated: Class B common units elect the board, and the preferred units carry senior distribution and liquidation rights. Facts are tagged below as Documentary Fact, Management Language, Analyst Interpretation or Insufficient Information.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transactions that matter to a creditor all sit between February 2025 and September 2026 and share one purpose, stated in the 10-K strategy as "reducing debt in absolute terms and opportunistically redeeming our high-cost Class A Convertible Preferred Units". On February 28, 2025 the partnership completed the sale of the Alkali Business for a gross purchase price of $1.425 billion; the buyer assumed $413.4 million of 5.875% senior secured notes due 2042, and the proceeds paid down the revolver, bought 7,416,196 preferred units at $35.40 on March 6, 2025 and redeemed the remaining $406.2 million of the 8.000% notes due 2027 on April 3, 2025. That sale is why 2025 comparatives separate discontinued operations. On March 4, 2026 the partnership issued $750.0 million of 6.750% senior unsecured notes due March 15, 2034 with net proceeds of about $735.9 million, used to buy $416.1 million of the 7.750% notes due 2028 in a tender and to redeem the remaining $263.3 million on March 22, 2026. The same day it entered the Eighth Amended and Restated Credit Agreement, raising the revolver from $800 million to $900 million and moving expiry to March 4, 2031, with springing earlier maturities if more than $150 million of the 2029 notes remain outstanding on October 16, 2028 or more than $150 million of the 2030 notes on January 14, 2030. The mechanics are credit-relevant: the new notes carry a lower coupon than the retired notes (calculated 50.625 million a year on 750.0 million against 52.650 million a year on 679.360 million) but 70.640 million more principal, and the 2028 wall was replaced by a 2034 maturity. Preferred purchases in 2026 were 741,620 units on February 3 at $33.71 for $25.0 million, 3,263,127 units on March 6 at $34.38 for $112.2 million and 2,454,445 units on June 8 at $34.38 for $84.4 million, a total of 6,459,192 units for $221.6 million (calculated), funded, according to the 10-Q, by the remaining 2034 note proceeds, operating cash flow and revolver borrowings (the March purchase) and by the $95.0 million proceeds of the June 3, 2026 sale of non-core natural gas pipeline and platform assets (the June purchase), while the funding of the February purchase is not stated; the repurchased units carried distributions of $3.7892 per unit a year, or about $24.5 million a year in total (calculated), at an annual coupon of 11.24%. On June 26, 2026 the partnership entered a three-year $99.5 million AR Facility at SOFR plus 1.375%, through a bankruptcy-remote subsidiary that is not a guarantor; it is fully drawn. Debt service and liquidity effects are in CP-1: total debt principal rose to $3,249.5 million, no maturity falls before January 15, 2029, and revolver availability was $894.4 million at September 30, 2026. The creditor mechanics are therefore a lower cost of capital and a longer maturity profile at the price of more senior debt: the preferred units are equity (mezzanine) that ranks senior only to the common units, so each purchase exchanges junior capital for senior debt. Management says these steps "eliminated any near-term refinancing risk"; that is management language, and the revolver's springing maturities and covenants remain. No sponsor transaction, dividend recapitalization or non-pro-rata exchange appears in the sources; the seller of the repurchased preferred units is described only as one of the holders and is not named.

### Ownership and control

Ownership is held through publicly traded Class A common units, a small class of Class B common units with exclusive right to elect the board, and the preferred units. The general partner owns a non-economic interest and has "sole responsibility for conducting our business and managing our operations" (10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl page:8), so unitholder-level checks depend on the Class B holders. Documented legal capacity: the partnership agreement authorizes the general partner to issue additional limited partner interests (10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl page:200), and Class B transfers do not need unitholder consent (10-K_2025-12-31_gel-20251231.htm.jsonl page:65). Willingness, shown by documented actions only: distributions to common units rose from 0.165 to 0.20 per unit, a 21.2% increase (calculated) matching the release's "increase of approximately 21%" for Q3 2026 against Q3 2025 (EX-99.1_2026-11-05_a90ex9918kearnings_q32026.htm.jsonl page:2), and 250,000 units were repurchased in 2026 at about $14.57 each for $3.6 million (10-Q_2026-09-30_gel-20260930.htm.jsonl page:19). Feasibility: leverage, cover and liquidity are in CP-1. Tag: Neutral or Mixed. Reasoning: documented actions include deleveraging with asset-sale proceeds in 2025, but 2026 preferred purchases were partly debt-funded, common unit repurchases restarted in 2026 and distributions rose while total debt rose, and there is no evidence of priming, drop-downs or non-pro-rata exchanges. Evidence quality: High (primary, dated, issuer-specific) for actions; Low for intent.
