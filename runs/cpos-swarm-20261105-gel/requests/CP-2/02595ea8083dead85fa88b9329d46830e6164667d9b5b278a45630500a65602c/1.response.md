### Credit view

Genesis Energy, L.P. is a leveraged but liquid Gulf of America midstream credit: contracted offshore pipeline cash flows and a refinanced maturity profile support its debt service, while leverage of 5.18x and interest cover of 2.36x leave limited room for error. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 41.477 million of cash and USD 894.4 million of undrawn revolver, with no maturity before January 2029. The weakest point is that debt rose to USD 3,249.5 million in 2026 as preferred units were bought back, and that offshore volumes at Shenandoah were below the contractual minimum in the third quarter. Producer volumes, covenant headroom and the 2029 refinancing decide the outcome.

### Key findings

The first finding is liquidity and maturities. Genesis ended the third quarter of 2026 with USD 41.477 million of unrestricted cash and USD 894.4 million of revolver availability, no revolver borrowings, and no debt maturing until the 8.250% notes of USD 600.0 million fall due on January 15, 2029 [CP-1, Chart data: liquidity and debt maturities]. The mechanism is the March 2026 refinancing, which replaced the 2028 notes with USD 750.0 million of 2034 notes and extended the revolver to March 4, 2031 [CP-1A, Transaction summary]. The implication is that default risk from refinancing in the next two years is low; the condition is that revolver availability is stated as subject to covenant compliance.

The second finding is that leverage has not fallen. Total debt principal was USD 3,249.5 million at September 30, 2026 against USD 3,085.760 million at December 31, 2025, and computed total debt to EBITDA was 5.18x on a last-twelve-months basis against 5.74x for FY2025, with net debt to EBITDA of 5.12x [CP-1, Chart data: leverage]. The ratio improved because EBITDA grew, not because debt fell: the debt increase came from the 2034 notes, the AR Facility of USD 99.5 million and USD 221.6 million of preferred unit purchases funded partly by debt [CP-1A, Transaction summary]. The implication is that the partnership is trading junior, higher-cost capital for senior debt, which lowers cash costs but raises the claims ahead of equity and leaves leverage tolerance dependent on EBITDA.

The third finding is debt service capacity. EBITDA to cash interest was 2.36x on a last-twelve-months basis and 1.90x for FY2025, and the notes alone carry a run-rate coupon of USD 247.625 million a year, equal to 39.5% of last-twelve-months computed EBITDA of USD 626.898 million [CP-1, Chart data: interest cover; Calculations]. The mechanism is high-coupon unsecured notes against a business whose margin is concentrated in offshore pipelines. The implication is that a modest EBITDA shortfall consumes interest cover quickly; the covenant minimum of 2.00x through December 31, 2026 rising to 2.15x in 2027 is not reported by management and cannot be tested here.

The fourth finding is covenant headroom. Management reports a covenant leverage ratio of 5.03x against a 5.75x maximum through December 31, 2027, and the release's own inputs imply an EBITDA cushion of USD 76.041 million or 12.5% [CP-1, Covenants and definitions review]. The mechanism is that covenant EBITDA includes pro forma adjustments of USD 11.405 million and covenant debt excludes the AR Facility; an illustrative fall of about 9.9% in computed EBITDA would lift computed leverage to 5.75x (Calculations; not a covenant test because the bases differ). The implication is that headroom is adequate today but is the variable that governs revolver access, and it cannot be verified without the executed credit agreement.

The fifth finding is business risk. Offshore pipelines produced 67.7% of nine-month 2026 Segment Margin under life-of-lease and minimum-volume arrangements, and in the third quarter Shenandoah volumes were below the minimum so only the minimum was recognized; management expects Adjusted EBITDA near or below the low end of guidance [CP-1A, Credit translations]. The mechanism is that minimum volume commitments floor margin but cap upside when third-party wells underperform, and Gulf weather adds interruption risk (about four days of disruption in October 2026). The implication is that the contracted base supports debt service while growth depends on producers.

The sixth finding is cash conversion. Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow of USD 340.898 million included a working capital inflow of USD 58.619 million, and cash after capital payments and distributions was USD 101.661 million, before USD 221.594 million of preferred unit redemptions [CP-1, Credit narrative by dimension]. The mechanism is crude marketing, which moves receivables and payables together, with receivables partly financed through the AR Facility. The implication is that reported cash flow overstates the underlying run-rate and that free cash after distributions is small relative to debt, so deleveraging depends on EBITDA growth or asset sales rather than free cash flow.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, contracted and fee-based offshore cash flow: pipelines under life-of-lease commitments and minimum volume commitments provided 67.7% of nine-month 2026 Segment Margin (Documentary Fact; CP-1A Company and operating model). Second, liquidity and maturity profile: USD 894.4 million of revolver availability, USD 41.477 million of cash and no maturity before January 2029 (Documentary Fact; CP-1). Third, diversified customers and assets: no customer above 10% of 2025 revenue, a marine fleet mostly on time charters and a sulfur services business with refinery contracts (Documentary Fact; CP-1A). Fourth, lower funding costs from the 2026 actions: the 2034 notes carry a lower coupon than the retired 2028 notes and the AR Facility prices at SOFR plus 1.375%, while repurchased preferred units carried an annual coupon of 11.24% (Documentary Fact; CP-1A Transaction summary). Management describes these steps as having eliminated near-term refinancing risk, which is Management Language and is not adopted here.

Weaknesses. First, leverage and cover: 5.18x total debt to EBITDA and 2.36x EBITDA to cash interest, with debt higher than at year end (Documentary Fact for the figures; Analyst Interpretation for the reading). Second, earnings concentration and volume risk: offshore volumes depend on third-party producers, Shenandoah was at the minimum in Q3 2026 and management guides to the low end of the range (Documentary Fact; Management Language for the guidance). Third, control and capital allocation: Class B units elect the board and a family group holds 77% of them, and the partnership bought back preferred units with debt while raising common distributions (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation for the creditor reading). Fourth, definition and documentation gaps: the management covenant ratio uses a different debt and EBITDA perimeter and the executed credit documents are not supplied (Insufficient Information for covenant headroom verification).
