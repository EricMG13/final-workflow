### Credit view

GFL is a scaled, price-led North American waste operator whose credit has become more leveraged, and the fundamental view is cautious but not adverse. The strongest support is recurring collection revenue and a Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA margin of 31.5% with no named maturity until 2028. The weakest point is leverage: total debt is 5.18x trailing Adjusted EBITDA on an unadjusted basis against management's 3.8x run-rate figure, which relies on CAD 730.9 million of management adjustments. The outcome is decided by whether SECURE earnings and synergies arrive, how the revolver covenant defines its debt and EBITDA, and what the open take-private review brings.

### Key findings

Debt has stepped up faster than earnings, and the leverage reading depends on the definition. GFL carried CAD 11,696.4 million of debt before deferred costs at September 30, 2026, up from CAD 7,426.7 million at year-end 2025 [CP-1 financial table], while trailing Adjusted EBITDA is CAD 2,257.1 million and includes SECURE for only the month after its September 1, 2026 closing [CP-1 chart data: leverage]. Unadjusted, that is 5.18x total and 5.05x net; management's run-rate basis adds CAD 730.9 million to reach 3.8x [CP-1]. The revolver test is 5.00x for four quarters after a Material Acquisition and management states compliance, but the agreement's own definitions are not supplied, so headroom is Not calculable and the unadjusted figure should not be read as a breach [CP-1 liquidity and covenants]. This is Analyst Interpretation resting on Documentary Fact figures.

Operating performance is solid but growth is bought and priced rather than volume-led. In Q3 2026 revenue rose 27.2%, of which acquisitions were 18.7% and organic 7.4%, with price 5.7% and volume (0.1)% [CP-1A company and operating model]. Adjusted EBITDA was CAD 678.7 million, up from CAD 535.1 million, at a 31.5% margin against 31.6% a year earlier, while the USA segment margin fell 2.3 points and Canada rose 2.1 points. Net loss from continuing operations was CAD 409.8 million for nine months 2026 against income of CAD 123.5 million, driven by CAD 482.9 million of interest and other finance costs and non-cash foreign exchange and fair-value charges, so earnings quality rests on the management measure [CP-1].

Cash flow covers interest on a management basis but with less room than the headline. Interest paid in cash was CAD 371.7 million for nine months 2026 against Adjusted EBITDA of CAD 1,748.4 million, a 4.70x ratio, and Q3 cash interest of CAD 172.5 million was 46.1% above CAD 118.1 million a year earlier [CP-1 chart data: interest cover; Calculations]. Adjusted EBITDA excludes CAD 172.1 million of cash costs (transaction, integration and Founder and CEO cash remuneration) for nine months 2026, and Adjusted Free Cash Flow of CAD 484.9 million is a management measure after add-backs; operating cash flow was CAD 950.1 million after a CAD 193.1 million working-capital outflow [CP-1].

Liquidity and the maturity profile are manageable near term. Cash is CAD 296.5 million and the derived revolver availability is CAD 809.7 million to CAD 1,157.0 million, depending on how letters of credit are allocated; the revolver matures on April 29, 2030 and the Term Loan B Facility on August 28, 2033 [CP-1 liquidity]. The named maturities cluster in 2028 to 2031, totalling CAD 7,621.4 million, or 65.2% of debt before deferred costs (Calculations), so refinancing capacity in those years will matter [CP-1 chart data: debt maturities].

Claims outside Note 7 debt add to the burden: lease obligations of CAD 705.0 million, closure and post-closure obligations of CAD 1,503.8 million, letters of credit of CAD 535.1 million, performance bonds of CAD 2,324.1 million, and new crude oil transportation commitments of CAD 561.4 million with onerous storage commitments of CAD 51.0 million that did not exist at year-end 2025 [CP-1 claims outside the debt figure; CP-1A company and operating model]. Pensions, factoring and litigation funding were not found in the interim sources.

Ownership and governance are unresolved rather than adverse. A Special Committee is reviewing unsolicited take-private proposals, with updated proposals due on November 9, 2026, after the cutoff, and no terms are supplied [CP-1A transaction summary]. Documented actions are mixed: nine-month 2025 share repurchases of CAD 2,758.5 million against CAD 71.0 million in 2026, and a cash payment to the Founder and CEO of CAD 74.2 million in place of share-unit settlement [CP-1A ownership, governance and behaviour]. No sponsor, ratings or control-rights information is supplied.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, recurring route-based revenue: collection is 60.9% of gross revenue for nine months 2026, which gives revenue visibility even though contract terms are not supplied (Analyst Interpretation on Documentary Fact rows [CP-1A company and operating model]). Second, pricing power in the reported numbers: Q3 2026 price of 5.7% and surcharges of 1.5% drove organic growth of 7.4%, and the nine-month Adjusted EBITDA margin was 30.4% against 29.9% (Documentary Fact [CP-1A; CP-1]). Third, funding diversification and a staggered profile: a revolver to April 2030, a Term Loan B Facility to August 2033 and eight series of notes with maturities from 2028 to 2034, with no named instrument due before 2028 (Documentary Fact [CP-1 debt instruments]). Fourth, cash conversion improved on the management measure, with Adjusted Free Cash Flow of CAD 484.9 million against CAD 331.3 million (Management Language [CP-1]). Fifth, covenant compliance is stated by the issuer at September 30, 2026 (Documentary Fact, issuer assertion [CP-1 liquidity and covenants]). Sixth, an independent committee process governs the take-private review (Documentary Fact [CP-1A ownership, governance and behaviour]).

Weaknesses. First, leverage: debt before deferred costs rose by CAD 4,269.7 million in nine months and unadjusted leverage is 5.18x total and 5.05x net, both above the 5.00x covenant level although the covenant uses its own definitions, with the run-rate figure of 3.8x resting on CAD 730.9 million of management adjustments (Analyst Interpretation on Documentary Fact figures [CP-1]). Second, acquisition dependence and integration risk: acquisitions were 18.7% of Q3 growth, SECURE accounting is provisional, and Canada segment goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles rose from CAD 1,942.0 million to CAD 6,902.2 million (Documentary Fact [CP-1A]). Third, soft volume and a falling USA margin: volume was (0.1)% in Q3 2026 and (0.6)% for nine months 2026, and the USA margin fell 2.3 points (Documentary Fact [CP-1A]). Fourth, earnings quality: CAD 172.1 million of cash costs are added back and the nine-month net loss from continuing operations was CAD 409.8 million (Documentary Fact [CP-1]). Fifth, a rising interest burden: Q3 cash interest of CAD 172.5 million against CAD 118.1 million a year earlier, while Adjusted EBITDA rose 26.8% (Documentary Fact [CP-1]). Sixth, new energy-linked commitments: crude oil transportation commitments of CAD 561.4 million coincide with the SECURE closing but the sources do not tie them to it (Analyst Interpretation [CP-1A]). Seventh, unresolved ownership: the take-private review could change financing and covenants, and control rights are Insufficient Information (Management Language on the review; Insufficient Information on control [CP-1A]).
