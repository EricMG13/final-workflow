### Financial base view

The consolidated Group moved from no borrowings (September 30, 2025) to $4,992 million of borrowings after the December 2025 Merus financing, so the reported base now has two regimes: a pre-acquisition year with net cash and a post-acquisition year with net debt. Debt is carried at amortized cost in four instruments (Term A Loans, Term B Loans, Secured Notes, Unsecured Notes), all disclosed with current and non-current carrying values, and the Group had $1,860 million of cash and no marketable securities at September 30, 2026. On a carrying-value basis net debt was $3,132 million, down from $3,559 million at December 31, 2025, with principal repayments of $288 million in nine months 2026. The income base is royalty-led (royalties $2,699 million of $3,238 million revenue) and operating profit is $986 million for the nine months, with adjusted operating profit of $1,108 million on the company's own non-IFRS definition. The issuer reports no EBITDA, and the depreciation and amortization needed for a last-twelve-months EBITDA is not supplied for FY2025, so the leverage chart cannot be populated; EBIT-based reference ratios are given instead and are labelled as such. Interest cover is calculable for Q1 2026 and nine months 2026 only. Undrawn committed facilities, covenant levels and contractual maturities are Not supplied. The base is therefore sufficient for reported-fact extraction, cash and net debt, cash conversion and interest cover on a nine-month basis, and restricted for LTM leverage, liquidity headroom, maturity profile and covenant analysis.

### Evidence, risk mechanic and credit implication

#### Acquisition leverage rests on one intangible asset

Evidence: borrowings of 4,992 against shareholders' equity of 6,333, and goodwill and intangibles of 9,197 including acquired IPR&D of 8,237, which is 63.7% of total assets of 12,936 (Q3 2026 report page:5, page:34, page:40). Risk mechanic: the Merus purchase was accounted for as an asset acquisition concentrated in a single identifiable asset, petosemtamab (Q3 2026 report page:39), so the balance-sheet value that backs the debt depends on one pipeline asset that is not yet generating revenue; an adverse trial or regulatory outcome would impair the IPR&D and equity but would not by itself change cash debt service. Credit implication: asset-based recovery cover is weak relative to debt, and creditor protection rests on royalty cash flows and the covenants, not on the asset base. October 2026 topline results for petosemtamab were reported as meeting both primary endpoints (Q3 2026 report page:4); that is a post-quarter event and a management announcement, with no financial effect quantified in the supplied sources.

#### Margin compression reflects launch investment and is guided to continue

Evidence: adjusted operating expenses of 1,896 versus 1,489 (up 27%) against revenue up 22%, giving operating margin of 30.4% versus 37.8% (Q3 2026 report page:2, page:5). The full-year guidance of adjusted operating profit of 1,065 to 1,385 and revenue of 4,325 to 4,525 implies a fourth quarter below the third: adjusted operating profit of minus 43 to 277 (midpoint 117) versus 452 in Q3 2026, because 9M adjusted operating profit is 1,108. Risk mechanic: spending ahead of launches in 2027 is front-loaded while royalty growth is tied to partners' sales. Credit implication: EBITDA available for interest is not going to grow in line with revenue in the near term; the interest cover ratios above should be read as strong-quarter outcomes, not as a floor. Management language: guidance assumes no significant new agreements (page:6).

#### Cash flow covers debt service, but quality is mixed

Evidence: operating cash flow of 574 after interest paid on borrowings of 229, a working-capital outflow of 354 and taxes paid of 97; capital expenditure only 19; principal repayments 288; buybacks 96 (Q3 2026 report page:29, page:35). Risk mechanic: nine-month free cash flow of 555 funded the 288 repayments with surplus, but 185 of the working-capital outflow was other payables, including year-end accruals settled in 2026 (page:31), and interest paid (229) was below interest expense on borrowings (256) because 48 of interest was accrued but unpaid at September 30, 2026 (page:48). Credit implication: the next semi-annual coupon dates will pull more cash than the accrual-lagged nine-month figure suggests; debt service coverage should be tested on interest expense, not interest paid, when covenant definitions are available.

#### Revenue concentration and partner dependence

Evidence: DARZALEX royalties 2,057 are 63.5% of revenue, and J&J accounts for 2,169 or 67.0% of revenue (Q3 2026 report page:39, page:40). Risk mechanic: royalties are set by J&J's net sales and are reduced by the Halozyme royalty share and by territories without Genmab patent coverage (page:19, page:20); the DARZALEX royalty rose 18% versus J&J net sales up 19% (page:19). Credit implication: repayment capacity depends on one partner's product, with limited issuer control over sales, pricing or patent life; patent expiry terms are outside this handoff.

#### Interest-rate exposure and hedges

Evidence: floating-rate term loans indexed to 3-month SOFR, four swaps of 406 each (1,624 in total) at fixed rates between 3.3355% and 3.4885% to 6/30/2028, and a Term B margin cut from 3.00% to 2.00% in June 2026 on a nominal 1,900 (Q3 2026 report page:46, page:47). Risk mechanic: the swaps hedge part of the floating exposure for approximately two years (Q3 2026 report page:39); the margin cut lowers the contractual spread cost by about 19 a year on the nominal (1,900 x 1.00%), before any floor or step features that are not supplied. Credit implication: rate resets matter for the part of the floating-rate term loans not covered by swaps (term loans carry 2,600 at amortized cost against swap notional of 1,624; the bases differ, and the hedged allocation is not supplied), and the coupons on the secured and unsecured notes are not supplied.

#### Contingent claims and covenants

Evidence: patent and trade-secret litigation (Chugai in Japan; AbbVie in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington) with no provisions recorded and no amounts stated (Q3 2026 report page:33, page:51); Term A Loans subject to financial covenants with compliance stated and no levels (page:46). Credit implication: contingent liabilities cannot be sized and covenant headroom cannot be calculated; both are open items, not zero.

### Definition review

- EBITDA: not reported by the issuer; the company reports adjusted operating profit excluding acquisition and integration charges and amortization of acquired intangibles (Q3 2026 report page:2, page:3). The analyst EBITDA above adds back depreciation (including right-of-use depreciation, so it is before lease depreciation but lease interest 5 is separately in financing costs) and amortization from the cash flow statement; it does not add back share-based compensation 124, impairment 27 or acquisition and integration charges 87. A variant adding back the latter two (87 + 27 = 114) gives 1,182 and cover of 5.16x on 229.
- Total debt: borrowings at amortized cost from the balance sheet, net of unamortized fees; nominal amounts are only partly supplied (Term B nominal 1,900; Notes principal as issued). Lease liabilities (206) are shown separately.
- Cash interest: "Interest paid on borrowings" in operating activities; other interest paid (2) and lease interest (5) are shown separately.
- Covenant EBITDA and total net leverage definitions are in the unsupplied credit agreement; the analyst EBITDA is not a covenant measure.
