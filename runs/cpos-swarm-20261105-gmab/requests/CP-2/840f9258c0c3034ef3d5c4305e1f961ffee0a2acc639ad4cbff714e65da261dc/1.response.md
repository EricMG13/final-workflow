### Credit view

Genmab's fundamental credit is sound but now leveraged, and it can only be partly assessed. Its strongest support is a high-margin royalty stream: royalties were USD 2,699 million of USD 3,238 million revenue in the nine months to September 30, 2026, and operating cash flow of USD 574 million covered USD 288 million of principal repayments, taking net debt to USD 3,132 million. Its weakest point is that the largest royalty, DARZALEX, has a finite horizon, with Genmab's patents expiring between 2029 and 2031, while the new debt financed a pipeline asset that earns no revenue yet. The outcome will be decided by whether petosemtamab, Rina-S and epcoritamab launch in 2027 before royalties fade, tested against debt maturities and covenants that are not supplied.

### Key findings

1. Genmab moved from net cash to net debt and is paying it down. Borrowings at amortized cost were USD 4,992 million against cash of USD 1,860 million at September 30, 2026, so net debt was USD 3,132 million, down from USD 3,559 million at December 31, 2025, with USD 288 million of principal repaid in nine months 2026 [CP-1 Financials table; CP-1 Calculations]. Genmab had no borrowings before December 2025 [CP-1 Financial base view]. Category: Documentary Fact for the balances, Analyst Interpretation for the direction.
2. Leverage on EBITDA cannot be stated. FY2025 depreciation and amortization is only in the unsupplied audited statements, so LTM EBITDA is Not supplied [CP-1 Chart data: leverage]. EBIT-based references are 4.78x total debt and 3.00x net debt to LTM operating profit, which is a conservative upper reference because EBITDA exceeds operating profit. Management's stated aim of gross leverage below 3x within two years of closing cannot be tested without its definition [CP-1A F5]. Category: Insufficient Information for EBITDA leverage; Management Language for the target.
3. Interest cover is comfortable on the measure available. EBITDA of USD 1,068 million covered interest paid on borrowings of USD 229 million 4.66x over nine months 2026, and 3.54x in the first quarter; interest paid was below interest expense on borrowings of USD 256 million, consistent with USD 48 million of accrued interest on borrowings at September 30, 2026 [CP-1 Chart data: interest cover; CP-1 Evidence section]. Cover should be read as a strong-period figure, not a floor. Category: Analyst Interpretation (EBITDA is an analyst definition, not an issuer measure).
4. The cash stream behind the debt has a finite horizon. DARZALEX royalties were USD 2,057 million, 63.5% of nine-month revenue, J&J accounts for 67.0% of revenue, the royalty term runs to the later of 13 years after first sale or the last relevant patent expiry, the daratumumab patents expire in 2029 (US), 2031 (Europe) and 2030 (Japan), and J&J may terminate on 150 days' notice [CP-1 Financials table; CP-1A F6, F7]. Debt maturities are Not supplied, so overlap cannot be tested. Category: Documentary Fact.
5. Margins are compressing and guidance points lower for the fourth quarter. Operating margin was 30.4% against 37.8% a year earlier, adjusted operating expenses rose 27% while revenue rose 22%, and the guidance ranges imply fourth-quarter adjusted operating profit between negative USD 43 million and USD 277 million (midpoint USD 117 million) against USD 452 million in the third quarter [CP-1 Evidence section; CP-1 Calculations]. Category: Documentary Fact for the guidance; Analyst Interpretation for the implied quarter. Management attributes the spending to launch readiness; that is Management Language [CP-1A F15].
6. Balance-sheet cover is concentrated in one asset. Goodwill and intangibles were USD 9,197 million, 71.1% of total assets, of which acquired IPR&D was USD 8,237 million; equity less goodwill and intangibles was negative USD 2,864 million [CP-1 Financials table]. The purchase was an asset acquisition concentrated in petosemtamab [CP-1A F4]. No recovery conclusion is drawn. Category: Documentary Fact.
7. Liquidity is visible only as cash. Cash was USD 1,860 million with no marketable securities, and current borrowings were USD 318 million; a USD 500 million revolver exists [CP-1A F16] but its availability is Not supplied [CP-1 Chart data: liquidity]. Category: Insufficient Information for committed liquidity.
8. Structure and covenants are only partly visible. The debt is secured Term A and Term B loans, secured notes of USD 1.5 billion and unsecured notes of USD 1.0 billion [CP-1A F3]; Term A Loans are subject to financial covenants with compliance stated and no levels [CP-1 Contingent claims section; CP-1A F10]. Guarantors, coupons and maturities are Not supplied. Category: Documentary Fact for what exists; Insufficient Information for terms.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. (1) High-margin, partner-funded royalty income that covered debt service and principal repayments: royalties USD 2,699 million, operating cash flow USD 574 million, net debt reduction USD 427 million in nine months [CP-1 Financials table; Documentary Fact]. (2) Low capital intensity, with only USD 19 million of tangible and intangible investment in nine months 2026, so cash generation is not absorbed by capex [CP-1 Financials table; Documentary Fact]. (3) Royalties beyond DARZALEX: Kesimpta USD 408 million and TEPEZZA USD 93 million in nine months 2026, with Novartis and Amgen as licensees [CP-1A operating model; Documentary Fact]. (4) Interest-rate hedges: four swaps totalling USD 1,624 million of notional to June 30, 2028 and a Term B margin cut from 3.00% to 2.00% [CP-1 Evidence section; Documentary Fact]; the saving is an upper bound near USD 19 million a year because the Term B balance has since fallen [CP-1A Transaction section, correcting CP-1]. (5) Ownership and distributions: no controlling shareholder, no sponsor, no dividends in three years, and distributions restricted by the debt documents [CP-1A F8, F9, F13; Documentary Fact]. (6) Deleveraging in practice: USD 288 million of repayments in nine months 2026 and a stated leverage target [CP-1A F5; Management Language, not adopted].

Weaknesses. (1) Concentration: DARZALEX royalties are 63.5% of revenue and J&J 67.0% [CP-1 Financials table; Documentary Fact]. (2) Finite royalty horizon from 2029 to 2031 for the issued patents, with a licensee termination right [CP-1A F6, F7; Documentary Fact]. (3) Leverage built for an acquisition: borrowings of USD 4,992 million against equity of USD 6,333 million, with the asset base dominated by an unproven pipeline asset [CP-1 Financials table; CP-1A F1, F2, F4; Documentary Fact]. (4) Rising cost base: adjusted operating expenses up 27% and operating margin down to 30.4%, with fourth-quarter guidance implying lower profit [CP-1 Evidence section; Analyst Interpretation]. (5) Floating-rate and refinancing exposure that cannot be sized: term loans of USD 2,600 million at amortized cost against USD 1,624 million of swap notional, on different bases, with maturities Not supplied [CP-1 Calculations; Insufficient Information]. (6) Unquantified contingent claims: AbbVie, a profit-sharing partner on epcoritamab, is the plaintiff in a trade secret case over Rina-S, with no provision recorded and no amount stated, and patent suits by Chugai in Japan [CP-1A F14; Documentary Fact; exposure is Insufficient Information].
