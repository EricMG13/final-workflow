### Business and transaction view

Gray Media is the largest owner of top-rated local television stations in the United States by its own description, with stations in 117 full-power markets at August 2026, a Telemundo affiliate group and a small production business. It earns money in three ways: core advertising that follows the economy and the audience, political advertising that arrives in even-numbered years and peaks in the fourth quarter, and retransmission consent fees paid by pay-television distributors, less the fees it pays to the four major networks. In FY2025 retransmission consent was 46% of revenue and core advertising 47%, with political at 1%; in FY2024, an election year, political was 14%. That structure gives the credit two features a creditor must read together: a large, contractual-looking fee stream that is shrinking in subscribers and exposed to blackouts and to network renewals through 2028, and a cyclical advertising stream whose largest swing is political.

The transaction story is a station-portfolio reshaping financed with debt and cash. In 2025 the company replaced term loans and unsecured notes with 1,150 of second lien notes and 775 of first lien notes, so that, after the January 2026 repayment of the last 2026 notes, no principal falls due before December 1, 2028. In 2026 it closed purchases from Allen Media Group, Block Communications, Sagamore Hill and Bahakel, swapped three stations to Scripps for two, amended and restated its credit agreement without changing commitments, issued 70 of additional first lien notes, repurchased 50 of preferred stock liquidation value for 30, and completed first closings of the American Spirit Media and WHPM purchases on July 1, 2026. Management says the WBBJ-TV acquisition and other station transactions will contribute to reducing leverage; the filings show the full acquisition cost is already in cash and debt while only part of the earnings is in the last twelve months. The context names no sponsor and the sources describe none: control runs through a dual-class common structure, and a senior preferred stock has a holder the pack does not name.

For a creditor the mechanism is straightforward. Political and retransmission revenue drive cash flow; acquisitions and refinancings drive leverage and the secured share of debt; Class A voting control and the preferred stock's change-of-control redemption shape who decides and who is paid first outside the debt. Documents that would show how much of this the creditor group can control, the credit agreement, indentures and purchase agreements, are not in the pack.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

In 2025 the company refinanced most of its term loans and its unsecured 2027 notes. On July 18, 2025 it issued 900 of 9.625% senior secured second lien notes due 2032 at par under an indenture of that date, and on December 12, 2025 it added 250 at 102% of par; interest is payable January 15 and July 15. On July 25, 2025 it issued 775 of 7.25% senior secured first lien notes due 2033 at par, payable February 15 and August 15. The second lien proceeds, with 50 borrowed under the revolver, were used to redeem the 2027 notes at par, repay 403 of the 2024 term loan, redeem 125 of the 10.5% first lien notes due 2029 at 103% of par, and pay expenses and for general purposes; the first lien proceeds repaid 630 of the 2021 term loan, 80 of the 2024 term loan and all 50 then drawn under the revolver. The revolver commitment was raised to 750 and extended to December 1, 2028 by the fifth amendment of July 18, 2025. The 2025 refinancing produced a loss on early extinguishment of debt of 10. The result is more secured debt with a later first maturity: second lien notes rank behind the credit agreement and both first lien note series, and all of these benefit from guarantees of substantially all subsidiaries and a pledge of substantially all assets excluding real estate.

In 2026 the company amended and restated its credit agreement on March 31, 2026 with no change in commitments, term loan principal or stated maturities and no new borrowings, repaid its remaining 2026 notes of 2 on January 20, 2026, and repaid the remaining 10 term loan balance on April 2, 2026. On June 30, 2026 it issued 70 of additional 7.25% first lien notes due 2033 at par in a private placement, using 40 for the first closing of the American Spirit Media purchase on July 1, 2026 and 30 to repurchase 50 of Series A preferred stock liquidation preference. On July 21, 2026 it repurchased 100 of its 2029 first lien notes and 20 of its 2031 notes at par plus accrued interest with cash and revolver borrowings. On August 6, 2026 the board authorized up to 250 of debt repurchases through December 31, 2027. The acquisitions that closed in the first half of 2026 were Bahakel's WBBJ-TV (25, license assets February 13, 2026), the Allen 3 (56, March 27, 2026), the Allen 7 (115, May 1, 2026), Block (80, May 6, 2026) and Sagamore Hill (2, May 8, 2026), plus a non-cash station swap with Scripps on May 15, 2026 recorded at a fair value of 70. The American Spirit Media (anticipated total 50) and WHPM (anticipated total 4) purchases had first closings on July 1, 2026; second closings are subject to regulatory approval and are not assured.

The creditor read-through is mixed and is labelled as analyst interpretation. First, secured debt grew and unsecured debt fell: first lien and second lien instruments now carry 65.8% of principal against 55.2% at December 31, 2024 (C3). Mechanism: new collateralised paper replaced unsecured notes and term loans, so unsecured claims rank behind more secured debt; the second lien tranche is itself junior to 2,709 of first lien debt. Second, the acquisitions were funded by cash that fell from 368 to 176 and by 70 of first lien debt, while the earnings contribution is only in the months after closing. Third, a debt repurchase program of up to 250 was authorised against liquidity of 921 and a fully drawn receivables facility, and the July repurchase drew on the revolver by an undisclosed amount. No sponsor, acquirer or merger is described. The company says "we anticipate that the WBBJ-TV acquisition will contribute to reducing our leverage" ([S-R-Feb page:3]); that is management language about one deal and station transactions announced in the second half of 2025, and no pro forma leverage is supplied.
