### Credit view

Gray Media is a stretched credit with adequate liquidity: the company has no principal maturity before December 2028, but it carries USD 5,867 million of debt against USD 709 million of last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA. Its strongest support is liquidity of USD 921 million and a maturity profile pushed out by the 2025 refinancing. Its weakest point is earnings coverage: debt is 8.28x Adjusted EBITDA, cash interest cover is 1.84x, and free cash flow after dividends is USD 59 million. What decides the outcome is whether 2026 election-year earnings and the acquired stations lift Adjusted EBITDA toward the USD 994 million denominator the company uses for its covenants, and whether retransmission renewals and the 2028 to 2031 refinancing are managed through the 2027 off-year.

### Credit thesis

Gray Media can service its debt and has time to refinance, but only if election-year earnings and acquired stations lift Adjusted EBITDA before 2028; on today's last-twelve-months earnings it is a highly levered credit with thin coverage.

### Key findings

Gray Media's reported covenant leverage of 5.73x total net understates the burden shown by its own earnings. The company's covenant denominator is USD 994 million, an eight-quarter average that includes the third and fourth quarters of the 2024 election year and USD 144 million of acquisition, financing and synergy adjustments; its last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA is USD 709 million, and the same net debt of USD 5,696 million is 8.03x on that figure. Both are correct on their own bases and neither is a maintenance test. The mechanism is that incurrence capacity and covenant optics depend on a smoothed, pro forma denominator while cash interest of USD 385 million is paid out of actual earnings. This is Analyst Interpretation over CP-1 figures.

The company's earnings are cyclical and its fee income is eroding at the same time. Retransmission consent was 46% of FY2025 revenue and political advertising 14% of FY2024 revenue and 1% of FY2025, and USD 133 million of the last twelve months; retransmission consent fell 4% in 2025 and 7% in the first half of 2026 on subscriber loss, one station going independent and a satellite blackout between March and May 1, 2026, with network affiliation fees falling faster so that net retransmission revenue rose to USD 292 million from USD 281 million. Network affiliation agreements expire between mid-2027 and December 31, 2028, just before the December 2028 term loan and revolver maturity. The implication is that FY2026 will flatter cash flow, and FY2027 and FY2028 carry the renewal and refinancing risk.

Liquidity is adequate but depleted by the 2026 transactions. Cash fell from USD 368 million to USD 176 million in the first half, after USD 264 million of acquisition spending; revolver availability is USD 745 million; and on July 21, 2026 the company repurchased USD 100 million of 2029 first lien notes and USD 20 million of 2031 notes using cash and an undisclosed revolver draw. Reading two issuer passages together, a first lien net leverage cap of 4.25x applies when the revolver is drawn or more than USD 50 million of letters of credit are uncollateralized; the company reports 2.55x, so a draw outstanding at a quarter-end would activate a test with headroom of about 40% of the denominator (Analyst Interpretation, issuer-described terms only).

The refinancing in 2025 moved debt up the structure. Principal secured by a lien rose to 65.4% of the total at December 31, 2025 and 65.8% at June 30, 2026, from 55.2% at December 31, 2024, with the 10.5% first lien notes due 2029 and the 9.625% second lien notes due 2032 carrying the highest coupons. Coupon arithmetic gives an annual cost of about USD 443 million on the instruments listed, against an issuer estimate of USD 465 million of interest payments over the next twelve months (the coupon sum excludes receivables-facility interest and fees). The implication for creditors is that unsecured noteholders hold USD 2,008 million behind USD 3,859 million of secured debt, and that the cost of debt leaves little earnings cushion; no recovery conclusion is drawn.

Distributions continue ahead of deleveraging. In the first half of 2026 the company paid USD 17 million of common dividends and USD 27 million of preferred dividends, and it used USD 30 million of new first lien debt to repurchase USD 50 million of preferred liquidation value at a USD 20 million discount. The issuer's USD 465 million interest estimate plus USD 83 million of guided 2026 dividends together equal 77.3% of last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA. Class A shareholders hold 51.5% of the votes and the holders are not identified in the pack, so alignment with creditors cannot be assessed beyond the documented actions.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Gray Media has scale: stations in 117 full-power markets reaching about 37% of US households, with its own count of 78 markets with the top-rated station (Management Language, Nielsen data not verified by the issuer). Its customer and market concentration is low: no advertiser above 5% of broadcast advertising and the largest market, Phoenix, at 5% of revenue (Documentary Fact). Its liquidity is USD 921 million and no principal is due before December 1, 2028, after refinancings that the company says extended maturities beyond the next two political cycles (Documentary Fact; the claim about political cycles is Management Language). The first lien net leverage ratio of 2.55x is well inside the 3.5x incurrence level and the 4.25x cap (Documentary Fact, issuer-reported). The company has reduced debt in the past: USD 520 million of net principal payments in 2024 (Documentary Fact). Net retransmission revenue held at USD 547 million in FY2025 against USD 550 million in FY2024 despite a USD 53 million fall in retransmission fees, because affiliation fees fell USD 50 million (Documentary Fact).

Weaknesses. Gray Media's leverage is 8.28x on last-twelve-months Adjusted EBITDA and 8.67x in FY2025, against 4.90x in FY2024, and cash interest cover is 1.84x (Documentary Fact, calculated). Its free cash flow is thin: USD 146 million before and USD 59 million after dividends over the last twelve months (calculated). Its earnings swing with the election cycle and with a high fixed-cost base, and Adjusted EBITDA is a release-only management measure (Documentary Fact). Its revenue base faces subscriber erosion and a cluster of affiliation expiries in 2027 and 2028 (Documentary Fact). A growing share of debt is secured, USD 400 million of receivables financing is fully drawn and outside reported debt, and USD 600 million of senior preferred stock carries a cash dividend of about USD 48 million a year and a change-of-control redemption (Analyst Interpretation over Documentary Facts). Class A shares hold most of the votes and the identities of holders, of the preferred holder and any related parties are not supplied (Insufficient Information). Key legal documents and every figure after June 30, 2026 are missing (Insufficient Information).
