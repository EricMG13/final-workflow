### Business and transaction view

Hudbay is a Canadian-incorporated, listed copper and gold producer with three operating regions (Constancia in Peru, the Snow Lake operations in Manitoba and Copper Mountain in British Columbia), one care-and-maintenance site (Flin Flon) and a US development pipeline (Copper World and Cactus in Arizona, Mason in Nevada). For a creditor the operating model has three features that matter. First, revenue is concentrate and doré sold at provisional prices that are finally set up to about three months later, so quarterly revenue and working capital move with metal prices and shipment timing, as the second-quarter port closures in Peru showed. Second, gold and silver by-product credits lower reported unit costs and add a stream obligation: part of revenue is non-cash amortisation of deferred revenue from precious metals streaming agreements. Third, the group is moving from de-leveraging to growth: the 2026 events are the sale of 30% of Copper World to Mitsubishi, the all-share acquisition of Arizona Sonoran, the repayment of the 2026 notes with cash and a revolver draw, and 52.0 of municipal bonds issued by the Copper World entity. The structural point is that Copper World, Mason and now Cactus are excluded from the guarantee of the senior notes and from the security for the revolving facilities, yet the municipal bonds issued by Copper World LLC carry a parent guarantee. Project cash of 334.5 is contractually restricted to Copper World. So value and cash are being built in assets that sit outside the existing creditor collateral, while the parent keeps a contingent exposure to their debt. Ownership: no controlling or sponsor holder is disclosed in the sources; the context names no sponsor. The behaviour record is supportive on documented actions (debt repurchases below par, repayment at maturity, no distributions beyond a small dividend), with a policy statement of net debt to adjusted EBITDA below 1.0x through the investment cycle, which is management language and not a covenant. Legal capacity cannot be assessed because no indenture or credit agreement is supplied. The Wheaton enhanced stream for Copper World is described as agreed in terms but, in the latest release, still as a definitive agreement to be consummated, so project funding sources remain partly unfixed.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Copper World joint venture. Hudbay sold a 30% minority interest in Copper World to Mitsubishi Corporation under a subscription agreement dated 2025-08-12; the transaction closed in January 2026. Mitsubishi paid about 420 at closing (422.7 was recorded as received) and will pay a further 180.0 within 18 months of closing, plus 30% of future equity contributions. Hudbay keeps 70% and consolidates Copper World. The deal is accounted for as an equity transaction: a non-controlling interest of 461.6 was recognised, a deferred consideration receivable of 169.8 accretes toward 180.0, and the closing cash is restricted to the project. Stated use of the closing proceeds is to fund the remaining feasibility study, pre-sanction costs and initial development. Status: closed; the second payment is outstanding. Creditor effect: the closing cash (334.5 still held at 2026-06-30 by the project entity) is not available to other creditors, and the receivable belongs to the project.

Copper World feasibility and sanction. The feasibility study is described as progressing well with 95% of engineering complete and a sanction decision on track for late 2026. The study "is expected to reflect higher capital expenditures as compared to the 2023 pre-feasibility study primarily due to typical cost inflation along with new capital related to project scope changes that would allow for future mill expansion optionality" (PR-Q2 page:18). Earlier releases gave a feasibility completion date of mid-2026 (PR-Q1 page:16; PR-FY25 page:23); the July release gives no completion date. The 2023 pre-feasibility funding assumptions included 350 of project-level financing and 230 from a precious metals stream (Q3MDA page:9, footnote 4), which are management assumptions that predate the joint venture and the new study. Status: pending. Creditor effect: sanction will draw on project cash, partner contributions, the stream and possibly project-level debt; any project debt that carries a parent guarantee would be a new claim on the group, as the municipal bonds already are.

Wheaton enhanced stream. In August 2025 Hudbay agreed terms to amend the Copper World streaming agreement, adding up to 70 of contingent payment on a mill expansion alongside the initial 230 deposit, with ongoing gold and silver payments set at 15% of spot prices (Q3MDA page:13). The latest release still lists the definitive agreement as to be consummated (PR-Q2 page:32). Status: terms agreed; definitive agreement not confirmed in the pack. Creditor effect: a stream is a prior claim on project revenue; its signing, size and delivery obligations are not supplied.

Arizona Sonoran acquisition. Hudbay acquired all shares of Arizona Sonoran Copper Company Inc. not already owned, at 0.242 of a Hudbay share per share, by plan of arrangement completed 2026-06-24. It issued 46,794,082 shares and cancelled its existing stake valued at 123.1, recorded net identifiable assets of 1,267.0 and treated the purchase as an asset acquisition because substantially all value is in the Cactus project (Q2FS page:8). It assumed deferred land payments of 102.8 at 6% interest that are secured vendor carryback loans with deeds of trust (Q2FS page:16): 5.0 a year in 2026 to 2028 and 82.2 on 2029-08-28, prepayable without penalty. Hudbay expects to spend about 30 at Cactus in the second half of 2026 on an updated pre-feasibility study and site work (PR-Q2 page:4). Status: closed. Creditor effect: no corporate debt was issued and 46,794,082 new shares dilute equity, but the project is outside the guarantee and security perimeter, adds secured land debt, and competes with Copper World for capital after sanction.

Repayment of the 2026 notes and revolver draw. The 4.50% notes of 472.5 were repaid at maturity on 2026-04-01 using cash and a 272.0 draw on the Canadian revolving credit facility (Q2FS page:18-19). Status: closed. Creditor effect: refinancing risk before 2028 was removed, but undrawn revolver availability fell to 153.7 and the drawn revolver now matures with the facility in November 2028.

New debt issue: municipal bonds. On 2026-06-24 the Arizona Industrial Development Authority issued Series 2026A solid waste disposal revenue bonds of 52.0 at 4.50%, interest paid semi-annually, with an initial mandatory tender date of 2036-07-02; Copper World LLC is the obligor and the parent and primary operating subsidiaries guarantee on a senior unsecured basis (Q2FS page:14, page:20). The proceeds of 49.8 are held in a trust account for qualifying Copper World solid waste disposal expenditure and are expected to be used within twelve months. Status: issued. Creditor effect: low-cost long-dated project debt, with a parent guarantee and restricted proceeds.

Copper Mountain minority purchase.
