### Credit view

Hudbay Minerals Inc. has a sound fundamental credit profile today, with low leverage and strong cash generation, but the profile is exposed to thin committed liquidity and to a large Arizona capital programme. The strongest support is total debt of USD 860.2 million against trailing adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,271.6 million (0.68x), no funded maturity before 2028, and earnings spread over three mines, two countries of operation and several metals. The weakest point is liquidity: undrawn revolver of USD 153.7 million after the April 2026 draw, and USD 334.5 million of cash restricted to Copper World, so that cash available to the rest of the group is USD 556.4 million. The outcome will be decided by the Copper World sanction decision expected in late 2026 and how it is funded, by the gold price that supports by-product credits, and by covenant terms that are not supplied.

### Credit thesis

Hudbay's low reported leverage, long-lived diversified mines and no funded maturity before 2028 support a sound credit profile, provided that the Copper World and Cactus capital programme is funded without eroding the thin committed liquidity or pushing project debt back onto the group (Analyst Interpretation).

### Key findings

Hudbay Minerals Inc. reported total long-term debt of USD 860.2 million at 2026-06-30, down from USD 1,008.6 million at 2025-12-31, while trailing adjusted EBITDA rose to USD 1,271.6 million, the highest of the five trailing quarter-ends in the financial base. Total debt over trailing adjusted EBITDA was 0.68x, against 0.95x at 2025-12-31, and the company's own net debt was negative USD 80.5 million (Documentary Fact). The measure is the company's non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA, which adds back share-based compensation and deducts non-cash stream amortisation, and no covenant definition is supplied (Documentary Fact).

The headline net cash position needs three qualifications. USD 334.5 million of the USD 890.9 million cash is held by Copper World LLC and restricted to that project. USD 49.8 million of municipal bond proceeds are also restricted. The USD 52.0 million of bonds is Copper World debt that the parent guarantees. Setting those aside, total debt is 0.64x and net debt USD 253.1 million, or 0.20x, still low (Analyst Interpretation).

Liquidity changed shape in the second quarter. The company repaid USD 472.5 million of 4.50% notes at maturity on 2026-04-01 using cash and a USD 272.0 million revolver draw, so undrawn revolver availability fell from USD 425.2 million at 2026-03-31 to USD 153.7 million. Headline liquidity of USD 1,044.6 million is now mostly cash. The revolver split between its Canadian and Peruvian facilities is not printed, so the USD 153.7 million is an aggregate that may overstate what is usable on the facility that carries the draw (Documentary Fact; Insufficient Information on the split).

The next funded maturities are the USD 272.0 million revolver draw at its November 2028 facility maturity and USD 542.4 million of 6.125% notes due April 2029, with nothing earlier. Together they equal about two years of trailing free cash flow of USD 413.0 million (1.97x, derived), which is manageable if earnings hold, but the two maturities fall within five months of each other (Analyst Interpretation).

Cash conversion is strong but sensitive. First-half 2026 cash taxes paid were USD 262.8 million, equal to 35.4% of adjusted EBITDA, and the effective tax rate was 43.1%. Capital spending of USD 293.1 million took more than half of operating cash flow of USD 508.3 million. Free cash flow on the company definition was USD 204.1 million for the half, but the free cash flow history differs between releases (Documentary Fact; derived ratios in Calculations).

The business is diversified but exposed. Revenue comes from three operating regions with gold by-product credits that lower unit cost, and mine lives to 2040, 2041 and 2045 are management estimates. Recent interruptions include Peruvian unrest and Manitoba wildfires in 2025 and port closures in the second quarter of 2026 that delayed about 10,000 dry metric tonnes of concentrate (Documentary Fact; Management Language on mine lives).

The structure is the main creditor point. Copper World, Mason and Cactus are excluded from the note guarantees and from the revolver security, Copper World cash is restricted, and the parent guarantees the Copper World bonds. The Copper World sanction decision is expected in late 2026 after a feasibility study that management says will reflect higher capital cost than the 2023 study, the Wheaton enhanced stream is not confirmed as signed, and the Arizona Sonoran acquisition added USD 102.8 million of secured deferred land payments (Documentary Fact; Management Language on timing). Whether creditors' collateral and cash are diluted by this programme depends on documents that are not supplied.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Low leverage: total debt at 0.68x trailing adjusted EBITDA and a trend from 1.12x at 2025-09-30 (Documentary Fact for inputs). Cash generation: trailing free cash flow of USD 413.0 million on the company definition, with a first-half operating cash flow of USD 508.3 million (Documentary Fact). Maturity runway: no funded maturity before 2028 after the 2026 notes were repaid (Documentary Fact). Diversification: three operating regions, copper plus gold, with by-product credits and long reserve-based mine lives (Documentary Fact and Management Language). Documented behaviour: repurchases below par, repayment at maturity, an equity-funded acquisition, partner funding of 30% of Copper World, a very small dividend and no buyback purchases under the current authority (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation that behaviour is supportive to neutral).

Weaknesses. Committed liquidity: undrawn revolver of USD 153.7 million, 65.8% of the USD 450.0 million facilities used including letters of credit, and facility-level availability not printed (Documentary Fact; derived). Ring-fenced cash: USD 334.5 million not available to the wider group (Documentary Fact). Structural position: project assets outside collateral with a parent guarantee of project bonds (Documentary Fact). Project and capital intensity: Copper World sanction, higher study cost, the Cactus programme and New Ingerbelle spending ahead (Management Language). Earnings sensitivity: dependence on gold by-product credits, and a 43.1% effective tax rate on first-half income (Analyst Interpretation on sensitivity). Evidence quality: FY2025 figures from a management release, a misprinted trailing cell in the July release, a conflicting share count and no covenant levels (Insufficient Information on covenants).
