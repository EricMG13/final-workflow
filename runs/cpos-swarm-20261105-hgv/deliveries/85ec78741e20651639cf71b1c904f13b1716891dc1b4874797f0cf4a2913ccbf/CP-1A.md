### Business and transaction view

HGV is a timeshare operator that sells vacation ownership interests, finances most of those sales through consumer loans, and manages the resorts and clubs under a long-term licence from Hilton. The business has two parts. Real estate sales and financing is cyclical and driven by tour flow and volume per guest; in Q3 2026 contract sales of 807 were down from 907 on a 12.5 percent lower volume per guest despite higher tour flow, and management cut full-year Adjusted EBITDA guidance. Resort operations and club management is a cost-plus and fee business that is steadier but smaller than the real estate segment by segment Adjusted EBITDA. Financing converts sales into long-dated consumer receivables that are funded with non-recourse warehouse and securitisation debt, so the creditor reads two balance sheets in one filing: a recourse corporate stack and a receivable-backed non-recourse stack. The 2024 Bluegreen acquisition and 2021 Diamond acquisition expanded scale and are still being rebranded, which links future licence fees to rebranding milestones. In 2026 the company bought out the remainder of Elara for cash, upsized and added receivable facilities, refinanced a term loan to 2033 at unchanged pricing, repurchased stock beyond free cash flow and authorised a further repurchase plan. Ownership is public with a significant holder, Apollo, whose board and consent rights persist while it holds defined share thresholds. There is no sponsor-style dividend recapitalisation history in the sources and no dividends are planned. For the creditor, the transmission is: weaker sales conversion reduces Adjusted EBITDA and cash conversion; the corporate debt stack is covered by recurring fee income but with limited cushion if sales fall further; buyback appetite and acquisitions compete with deleveraging; and the legal permissions that govern distributions and additional debt cannot be read because the financing documents are not in the pack.

Evidence: management and club fees, financing revenue and servicing, plus rental revenue form the non-sales part of income (`10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl page:7`: "Our management agreements with HOAs provide for a cost-plus management fee"), and a member base of 715,888. Risk mechanic: cost-plus fees move with resort operating costs and club fees with members, not with sales cycles, so they cushion a downturn in contract sales; rental and ancillary services run at a loss, which offsets part of that cushion. Credit implication: debt service coverage is better supported by the resort segment and financing margin than by sales, but the CP-1 corporate leverage of 3.88x net excluding net construction leaves limited tolerance for further sales declines. No logged conflict counters this.

Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: Q3 2026 contract sales down on lower volume per guest, guidance cut, reported results lifted by a net construction recognition of 296 and an allowance estimate change (`10-Q_2026-09-30_hgv-20260930.htm.jsonl page:16`). Risk mechanic: reported EBITDA recognition is timing driven; cash conversion depends on sales and on receivable funding. Credit implication: monitor construction-adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow and revolver drawn balance rather than reported Adjusted EBITDA.

Concentration risk. Evidence: a single licensor and brand; a licence fee of 5 percent of gross revenues and rebranding milestones (`10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl page:22`). Risk mechanic: loss of or default under the licence would remove the brand underpinning sales; milestone misses raise fixed costs. Credit implication: a fixed senior call on revenue and brand dependence; the licence runs to December 31, 2116 for its initial term (`10-K_2025-12-31_hgv-20251231.htm.jsonl page:21`: "The initial term of the License Agreement will expire on December 31, 2116").

Sponsor alignment. Evidence: Apollo holds board and consent rights while above thresholds and has been a seller of shares; the company repurchased shares alongside the June 2026 offering. Risk mechanic: a holder reducing its stake has less influence and the board controls capital allocation. Credit implication: capital allocation is set by the board; creditor protection rests on the financing documents, which are not in the pack.

Key uncertainties. Covenant levels, the intercreditor and guarantor package, current Apollo holding, Elara purchase accounting, and the effect of the allowance change on Adjusted EBITDA are not determinable from the sources.
