### Credit view

Hilton Grand Vacations is a leveraged but adequately liquid timeshare credit whose direction is weakening, so a cautious view is warranted. The strongest support is recurring income from resort management, club fees and loan servicing, a long-dated Hilton licence and corporate maturities that are small before 2028, with liquidity of USD 684 million. The weakest point is earnings quality: reported net leverage of 3.29x rises to 3.88x once net construction recognitions are removed, while third quarter sales conversion weakened and guidance was cut. What decides the outcome is whether sales conversion recovers enough to keep recurring leverage from rising, and whether the board favours debt reduction over buybacks of USD 410 million in nine months. Covenant headroom cannot be tested from the sources, which limits confidence to Medium.

### Assessment

Category: Analyst Interpretation. Confidence: Medium. Basis: financial base figures (reported and construction-adjusted leverage, liquidity, free cash flow against repurchases, maturity profile) and the business context rows on contract sales, recurring fee streams, licence terms and capital allocation. The view is Medium and not High because the business context handoff judged this module ready only with limitations, and the covenant package is Not supplied.

Thesis (Analyst Interpretation): recurring fee income and a back-ended corporate maturity profile support the debt, but the reliance on construction recognition in reported earnings, the thinner liquidity and the buyback pace leave less room for a further sales decline than reported leverage suggests.

### Key findings

First, the reported leverage understates the debt burden on recurring earnings. Hilton Grand Vacations has corporate debt of USD 4,946 million at September 30, 2026 against last twelve months Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,434 million, giving total leverage of 3.45x and net leverage of 3.29x. That EBITDA includes net construction recognitions of USD 218 million; excluding them, EBITDA is USD 1,216 million and net leverage is 3.88x, close to the 3.9x management states on an undefined basis. The mechanism is timing: revenue and costs on units under construction are deferred and then recognised on completion, so a third quarter recognition of USD 296 million lifted reported earnings without new cash sales. For creditors this means leverage tolerance should be judged on the construction-adjusted figure, and the same adjustment changes interest cover from 5.07x to 4.30x.

Second, liquidity has thinned. Unrestricted cash of USD 227 million and undrawn revolver capacity of USD 457 million give corporate liquidity of USD 684 million, down from USD 1,048 million at December 31, 2025. The revolver drawn balance rose from USD 130 million to USD 490 million, and corporate debt rose by USD 343 million in nine months. Nine-month free cash flow of USD 273 million was below share repurchases of USD 410 million, and the revolver draw coincided with both the repurchases and the Elara purchase, though the sources do not state the draw's purpose. The implication is lower capacity to absorb a sales shortfall without further borrowing; the revolver matures in January 2030 and no facility expires within twelve months.

Third, sales conversion is the swing factor. Contract sales fell to USD 807 million in the third quarter from USD 907 million on a 12.5% lower volume per guest despite higher tour flow, and management reduced full-year Adjusted EBITDA guidance to a range of USD 1.165 billion to USD 1.180 billion from USD 1.225 billion to USD 1.265 billion. Management attributes the shortfall to slow ramp of its execution initiatives; that is Management Language and is not adopted. The recurring resort and financing streams cushion the effect, but rental and ancillary services run at a loss and a larger owned-inventory mix needs more capital.

Fourth, the non-recourse stack and other claims sit beside the corporate debt. Non-recourse debt of USD 2,923 million is payable from pledged receivables and is excluded from the ratios; including it would put total borrowings at USD 7,869 million, a figure shown for information only. Further claims outside corporate debt include inventory purchase commitments of USD 304 million, marketing and licence commitments of USD 257 million and surety commitments of USD 346 million. The licence fee of 5% of gross revenues to Hilton is a fixed senior call on revenue. These claims reduce the cash available to the corporate creditor and make the receivable funding markets part of the credit risk.

Fifth, control and capital allocation are board-driven. Apollo retains board and consent rights while above share thresholds and has been selling stock, with its holding after the June 2026 offering not disclosed. No dividend is planned, and the company repurchased shares alongside the offering. Governance behaviour is documented as Neutral or Mixed: no priming, drop-down or extraction is evidenced, but legal capacity under the credit agreement and indentures is Not supplied, so what the documents permit cannot be assessed.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Recurring income is a genuine support (Documentary Fact): resort management fees are cost-plus, club fees follow the member base, and financing and servicing income comes from a receivable book that carries a double-digit weighted average interest rate, so these streams do not depend on new sales. The maturity profile is back-ended (Documentary Fact): corporate debt maturing is small until 2028 and the largest block falls due after 2030, which limits near-term refinancing need. Liquidity remains adequate (Documentary Fact): USD 684 million of cash and undrawn revolver capacity, with no corporate facility expiring within twelve months, plus separate non-recourse warehouse capacity that funds receivables. The Hilton licence is long dated (Documentary Fact): the initial term runs to 2116, protecting the brand that supports sales. Corporate interest cover on the reported basis is 5.07x and on the construction-adjusted basis 4.30x (Analyst Interpretation), which suggests debt service is covered by current earnings.

Weaknesses. Earnings quality is lower than reported (Analyst Interpretation): a third quarter construction recognition and an allowance estimate change raised reported results, and the allowance effect on Adjusted EBITDA is not separately stated. Sales conversion is weakening (Documentary Fact): contract sales declined on lower volume per guest and guidance was cut. Capital allocation competes with deleveraging (Documentary Fact): repurchases of USD 410 million exceeded free cash flow of USD 273 million in nine months, and a further USD 600 million plan was authorised. The Hilton licence is a fixed senior call (Documentary Fact): a fee of 5% of gross revenues and rebranding milestones that, if missed, add up to 1% to the fee. Visibility on creditor protections is absent (Insufficient Information): covenant levels, guarantor identities, collateral and permitted-payment terms are not in the sources, which matters because the Senior Notes due 2032 are secured and the others unsecured. The Elara acquisition accounting is preliminary and its price is logged differently in two filings (Documentary Fact), a minor source conflict that does not change the view.
