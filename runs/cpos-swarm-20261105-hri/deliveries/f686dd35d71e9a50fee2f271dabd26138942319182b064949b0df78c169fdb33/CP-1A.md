### Business and transaction view

The credit story is a scaled, fragmented-market equipment renter that doubled its footprint in one transaction and is now working through the leverage that transaction created. Operating facts are well documented and issuer-specific: one reportable segment, a fleet with original equipment cost of about US$10.0 billion at Q3 2026, 615 locations, a diversified end-market mix with national accounts at 49% of FY2025 rental revenue, and demand that management ties to large projects. The transaction facts are also well documented: the H&E acquisition, its funding mix, the December 2025 refinancing of the 2027 notes, the Cinelease sale and the 2026 AR Facility upsizing. Ownership is the thinnest area: the filings identify no sponsor, parent or controlling holder, but the holder detail sits in a proxy statement that is not supplied, so control and alignment are Insufficient Information rather than a conclusion.

The mechanisms that matter to a creditor are these. Rental revenue is earned on a fleet that must be continually replaced, so cash conversion is set by the gap between EBITDA and fleet spending, not by EBITDA alone (CP-1 shows EBITDA after net rental capex of US$1,075m on LTM EBITDA of US$1,932m). The acquisition was paid for mostly with new secured and unsecured debt, and the unsecured Senior Notes rank behind secured facilities that can reach a maximum of US$4,000m under the ABL plus the US$750m term loan, so the pool of assets that cover the notes depends on how much of the ABL is drawn. Management states the integration is complete and that revenue and cost synergies "are tracking to plan" (Q2 release page:1), but no synergy target or bridge is supplied, so the earnings uplift that deleveraging needs cannot be tested from the filings. Cyclical and seasonal demand, fuel and delivery cost inflation, and floating-rate exposure are the stated operating pressures.

Not asserted here: any view on sponsor behaviour (there is no sponsor), any recovery outcome, any peer comparison, or any ownership percentage.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

H&E Equipment Services, Inc. acquisition (Documentary Fact, completed). On June 2, 2025 Herc Holdings completed the acquisition of all outstanding H&E common stock under a merger agreement dated February 19, 2025, paying "$78.75 in cash and 0.1287 shares of Company common stock on a per-H&E share basis" for a total purchase price of 4.8 billion US dollars, made up of 2.9 billion in cash and about 4.7 million Herc shares valued at 584m, with a further 1.4 billion of H&E debt repaid in cash and not assumed (Q3 10-Q page:15; 10-K page:97). There is no sponsor and no seller other than H&E's public shareholders. H&E brought approximately 160 branches in over 30 states and 2,500 team members (10-K page:8, page:97). Management calls it "the largest in the history of our industry" (Q1 release page:1; Management Language, not adopted). Funding was new debt: 2.8 billion in senior unsecured notes (the 7.00% Senior Notes due 2030 at 1.65 billion and the 7.25% Senior Notes due 2033 at 1.1 billion), a 750m Term Loan Facility and 2.5 billion of borrowings under a new ABL Credit Facility, of which approximately 1.6 billion repaid the prior ABL (10-K page:97, page:57). A senior secured 364-day bridge facility of up to 4.5 billion was committed in February 2025, undrawn, and terminated after the notes, term loan and ABL were in place (10-K page:118). Transaction expenses tied to H&E were 193m in FY2025, including a 64m termination fee paid on H&E's behalf, 27m of advisory fees and 21m of bridge commitment fees (10-K page:98). The purchase price allocation recorded customer relationships of 1,190 with a ten-year life and goodwill of 2,171 after a 12m measurement-period reduction in Q2 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:15-16); the 10-K showed goodwill of 2,183 before that adjustment (logged under Conflicts). Liquidity and leverage effect: total debt as printed rose from 4,086 at December 31, 2024 to 8,047 at December 31, 2025 (10-K page:103); CP-1 gross debt was 8,103 at FY2025 and 8,080 at Q3 2026 (Analyst Interpretation: the acquisition debt is carried in full against earnings that became a full-year contribution only in the LTM to Q3 2026). Integration status is Management Language: Q1 release page:1 states the company "successfully completed the integration of our H&E acquisition" and Q3 10-Q page:25 states "We have completed the integration of H&E and continue to focus on realizing the anticipated operational synergies". The synergy plan is described as running "over our three-year synergy plan" (Q1 release page:1) without a stated amount; this is Insufficient Information and is a gap, not an adverse finding.

New debt issues (Documentary Fact). On June 2, 2025 the 7.00% Senior Notes due 2030 (1.65 billion) and 7.25% Senior Notes due 2033 (1.1 billion) were issued, with the Term Loan Facility (750m, SOFR plus 1.75% after a December 2025 repricing amendment) and the new ABL (maximum 4.0 billion; matures June 2, 2030). On December 16, 2025 the company issued 600m of 5.75% Senior Notes due 2031 and 600m of 6.00% Senior Notes due 2034 and, with other borrowings, redeemed the 1.2 billion 5.50% Senior Notes due 2027 at 100.00% of principal, recording a 2m loss on extinguishment (10-K page:104, page:108, page:112). The June 7, 2024 6.625% Senior Notes due 2029 (800m) remain outstanding. Mechanism and implication: the December refinancing removed the nearest unsecured maturity and moved the next notes maturity to 2029, so the near-dated debt is the AR Facility and the revolver rather than the notes; CP-1 shows the debt maturing schedule at December 31, 2025. All notes are senior unsecured, guaranteed by domestic subsidiaries, with a change of control repurchase offer at 101% (10-K page:105); the filings describe the ABL as permitting "future secured debt on a basis either pari passu with, or subordinated to, the liens securing the New ABL Credit Facility" (10-K page:114), which is legal capacity that could dilute the unsecured notes' asset coverage; no such issuance is documented.

Other 2025 and 2026 events (Documentary Fact). Otay Mesa Sales was acquired on July 16, 2024 for about 273m using cash and ABL drawings (10-K page:98). Cinelease was sold on July 31, 2025 for initial cash consideration of 100m plus earnouts, after a 49m pre-tax loss on assets held for sale in Q2 2025, and "the net proceeds from the sale of Cinelease" were used "to repay indebtedness" (10-K page:57, page:101); an earnout receivable of 32m is carried at fair value and depends on Cinelease revenue in 2027 and 2028 (Q3 10-Q page:22). The AR Facility was extended in August 2025 and increased from 400m to 475m in December 2025, to 500m in May 2026, and in August 2026 to 550m with maturity extended to August 31, 2027 (10-K page:57; Q3 10-Q page:18). In July 2026 the company replaced certain insurance-related bank letters of credit with surety-backed letters of credit, adding about 25m of ABL capacity (Q3 10-Q page:32). Subsequent event after the Q3 period end: on October 27, 2026 the company raised its FY2026 outlook (Q3 release page:4); no other post-quarter event is stated in the sources.
