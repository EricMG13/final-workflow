### Credit view

Herc Holdings is a large, well-diversified equipment renter with an adequate but thin credit profile: it is highly levered after paying for H&E mostly with debt. Its strongest support is demand spread across end markets, with national accounts at 49% of FY2025 rental revenue, no customer above 3%, USD 2,100 million of liquidity, no maintenance covenants and no Senior Notes maturing before 2029. Its weakest point is cash conversion: gross debt of USD 8,080 million is 4.18x LTM reported EBITDA but 7.52x EBITDA after net rental capex, and free cash flow turned negative in Q3 2026. What decides the outcome is whether earnings growth and synergies reduce leverage faster than fleet spending, rates and fuel costs absorb it, with the ABL borrowing base as the buffer [CP-1, CP-1A].

### Key findings

First, the issuer's leverage is falling because of earnings, not repayment. Gross debt moved from USD 8,103 million at FY2025 to USD 8,080 million at Q3 2026 while LTM reported EBITDA rose from USD 1,497 million to USD 1,932 million, taking gross debt to EBITDA from 5.41x to 4.18x and net debt to EBITDA from 5.38x to 4.15x [CP-1 leverage chart]. Mechanism: the acquisition debt was carried in full against earnings that only became a full-year contribution in the LTM to Q3 2026, so the earlier ratios were inflated by the part-year earnings base and the later ratio is the first clean one. Implication: further deleveraging has to come from continued EBITDA growth or from using free cash flow to repay debt, and the filings show gross debt essentially flat so far (Analyst Interpretation).

Second, fleet reinvestment absorbs much of the earnings that leverage ratios count. Net rental equipment spending was USD 857 million over the LTM, leaving USD 1,075 million of EBITDA after fleet reinvestment, so gross debt is 7.52x that residual [CP-1 calculations]. Mechanism: rental depreciation is added back in EBITDA while the fleet must be replaced in cash. Implication: the credit's repayment capacity is set by the spread between EBITDA and fleet spending, and the raised outlook for net rental capex of USD 1,000 million to USD 1,100 million for FY2026 against USD 737 million spent in 9M 2026 implies USD 263 million to USD 363 million more in Q4 2026 (Documentary Fact for the figures, Management Language for the outlook).

Third, liquidity is adequate but concentrated. Cash was USD 66 million and ABL availability under the borrowing base was USD 2,034 million at Q3 2026, for USD 2,100 million, up from USD 1,932 million at FY2025; the AR Facility is fully drawn at USD 550 million and matures on August 31, 2027, within twelve months of the balance sheet date [CP-1 liquidity chart]. Mechanism: the revolver is the buffer for seasonal swings and for unpaid fleet purchases of USD 252 million. Implication: a borrowing-base shortfall would cut liquidity even without any covenant breach, because the only financial maintenance test is a springing fixed charge ratio of 1.00:1.00 whose trigger is not quantified.

Fourth, interest is a heavy fixed charge. Cash interest paid over the LTM was USD 530 million against EBITDA of USD 1,932 million, giving 3.65x cover; Senior Notes coupons add up to USD 318.75 million a year and floating debt of USD 3,244 million is 40.1% of gross debt [CP-1 calculations]. Mechanism: coupon dates make single quarters lumpy, with cash interest of USD 169 million in Q2 2026 and USD 81 million in Q3 2026. Implication: trailing measures are the reliable ones, and short-term rates feed directly into a large share of the debt.

Fifth, the business context lowers cyclical risk without removing it. National accounts were 49% of FY2025 equipment rental revenue and no customer exceeded 3%, but demand is construction-linked, seasonal and, per management, project-led [CP-1A company and operating model]. Management's own net leverage of 3.90x uses pro forma adjusted EBITDA that cannot be rebuilt from the pack, and the synergy plan has no stated amount; both are Management Language and are not adopted.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Demand is diversified and project-supported (Documentary Fact): contractors, industrial, infrastructure and government, commercial facilities and other customers all contribute, national accounts were 49% of FY2025 equipment rental revenue and no customer exceeded 3% [CP-1A company and operating model]; this supports revenue visibility and bounds single-name loss. Liquidity is sound (Documentary Fact): USD 2,100 million at Q3 2026 with no financial maintenance covenant active and the company stating compliance [CP-1 liquidity and covenants]; this supports seasonal and capex funding without capital-market access. The nearest Senior Notes maturity is 2029 (Documentary Fact): the December 2025 refinancing redeemed the 2027 notes at par, so the 2026 to 2028 maturities in the year-end schedule are USD 26 million, USD 21 million and USD 497 million [CP-1 maturity chart; CP-1A transaction summary]; this limits refinancing pressure on the unsecured stack until 2029, subject to the AR Facility (USD 550 million, due August 31, 2027) and the ABL (due June 2, 2030). Earnings are rising (Documentary Fact): LTM reported EBITDA of USD 1,932 million against USD 1,497 million at FY2025 and a Q3 2026 dollar utilization of 43.5% (a management operating measure) [CP-1; CP-1A operating model]; this supports debt service. Capital allocation conduct is neutral to supportive (Analyst Interpretation): no repurchases in FY2025, Cinelease proceeds applied to debt and the 2027 notes refinanced at par [CP-1A governance behaviour]; this limits leakage, although the dividend continued at USD 0.70 per share each quarter.

Weaknesses. Leverage is high (Documentary Fact for the ratios, which are CP-1 calculations on unadjusted EBITDA): 4.18x gross and 4.15x net at Q3 2026 and USD 8,080 million of gross debt [CP-1]; this leaves limited tolerance for a downturn. Fleet capital intensity is heavy (Documentary Fact): EBITDA after net rental capex of USD 1,075 million, 7.52x gross debt, and negative Q3 2026 free cash flow of USD 69 million with USD 252 million of fleet purchases unpaid [CP-1]; this slows repayment and increases revolver use. Liquidity depends on one facility (Documentary Fact): ABL availability of USD 2,034 million is borrowing-base dependent and the AR Facility is fully drawn [CP-1]. Interest burden and floating-rate exposure (Documentary Fact): cash interest of USD 530 million over the LTM, cover of 3.65x and floating debt of USD 3,244 million [CP-1]; this exposes cash flow to short-term rates and the company says it is reevaluating its approach to floating-rate debt [CP-1A operating model], but no hedging program is described. Balance sheet quality (Documentary Fact): goodwill and intangible assets exceed equity, leaving tangible equity of negative USD 2,434 million [CP-1]; asset coverage rests on the fleet rather than on book equity. Structural position of the unsecured notes (Analyst Interpretation): USD 4,750 million of Senior Notes are unsecured and effectively junior to the secured ABL, term loan and AR Facility (USD 3,244 million drawn) to the extent of the value of collateral, and the ABL permits future pari passu or subordinated secured debt [CP-1 debt by instrument; CP-1A transaction summary]; no priming action is documented. Unverified management claims (Management Language): synergies tracking to plan and the 3.90x pro forma leverage, neither testable from the pack.
