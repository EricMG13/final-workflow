### Credit view

IRON MOUNTAIN INC is a large, diversified and cash-generative REIT whose debt load, at 6.22x LTM Adjusted EBITDA, is high and still growing, so the credit view is cautious: sound operations on a stretched balance sheet. The strongest support is a recurring storage business with Global RIM revenue of USD 5,291.5 million and no customer above about 3%, plus a data center segment growing 39.6% with a 53.6% margin. The weakest point is that nine-month 2026 operating cash flow of USD 1,203.6 million fell short of capex of USD 1,724.2 million and dividends of USD 790.0 million. The outcome is decided by whether data center leases commence on schedule and whether the company keeps refinancing, including USD 1,000.0 million of notes due September 15, 2027, at acceptable cost. [Confidence: Medium; Analyst Interpretation.]

### Key findings

IRON MOUNTAIN INC carries total debt of USD 17,972.2 million at September 30, 2026, which is 6.22x LTM Adjusted EBITDA of USD 2,888.2 million and 6.16x on a net basis, compared with 6.38x and 6.32x at FY2025. Leverage fell against FY2025 because Adjusted EBITDA grew faster than debt, but it rose slightly from 6.19x at LTM Q2 2026, and a stricter measure that does not add back stock-based compensation, restructuring or acquisition costs (operating income plus depreciation and amortization) gives 6.81x. Adjusted EBITDA is a management measure that adds back items such as stock-based compensation, so the headline ratios understate leverage on a stricter basis.

The company does not fund its investment programme from operations. Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow of USD 1,203.6 million was below capex of USD 1,724.2 million, a shortfall of USD 520.6 million before USD 790.0 million of dividends. Debt rose from USD 16,432.0 million at December 31, 2025, and the cash flow statement shows senior note proceeds and noncontrolling interest contributions as sources. Data center growth capex dominates the spending, so the credit improves only as leased capacity commences and converts to revenue.

Liquidity is ample but moving down. Cash of USD 188.2 million and revolver availability of USD 2,218.2 million give USD 2,406.4 million, against USD 2,912.4 million at June 30, 2026, because revolver borrowings rose by USD 489.5 million in Q3 2026. Interest cover is steady: Adjusted EBITDA is 3.16x cash interest paid on an LTM basis against 3.12x at FY2025, and the weighted average interest rate is 5.7%.

Near-term refinancing is the main event risk. The current portion of long-term debt is USD 1,232.9 million, which includes the USD 1,000.0 million 4 7/8% notes due September 15, 2027. The last company-printed maturity table (December 31, 2025) shows USD 2,330.8 million due in 2027, USD 1,663.7 million in 2028, USD 2,137.5 million in 2029 and USD 3,035.7 million in 2030; some of that has since been extended or refinanced, but no later table is printed. The Credit Agreement tests show 4.8x lease adjusted leverage against a 7.0x maximum and 2.5x fixed charge coverage against a 1.5x minimum, so reported headroom is wide, although those ratios use other definitions than Adjusted EBITDA and cannot be rebuilt from the sources.

On the business side, Global RIM generated USD 5,291.5 million of revenue and USD 2,363.5 million of segment Adjusted EBITDA in FY2025, and nine-month 2026 data center revenue was USD 791.2 million, up 39.6%. Management reports leasing of 137 megawatts year to date, which is signed capacity, not commenced revenue. The board raised the quarterly dividend by 15% to USD 0.993 per share on November 5, 2026, and the indentures do not treat a leveraged recapitalization as a change of control, so event protection for noteholders is limited. No sponsor or controlling holder is disclosed, which removes sponsor extraction as a driver but also leaves ownership unassessed.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The recurring storage base is the first strength: Global RIM contributes most segment profit, management states a majority of revenue is recurring, storage contracts run one to five years and no customer exceeds about 3% of revenue, so cash flow is not exposed to a single name. The data center segment is a second strength, because long leases with a weighted average expiration of 10.3 years at December 31, 2025 and a segment margin that rose to 53.6% give visibility on a growing earnings layer. Third, reported covenant headroom is wide and the revolver, which matures in 2030, has USD 2,218.2 million available, which supports liquidity for the next twelve months despite near-term maturities. Fourth, funding is diversified across notes, bank debt, project loans and a receivables program, and the 2026 issuance extended the note stack to 2035. All are tagged Documentary Fact except the recurring-revenue share and the leasing pace, which are Management Language.

Weaknesses. Leverage of 6.22x on a management EBITDA measure, and 6.81x on a stricter measure, leaves limited tolerance for an earnings shortfall. Free cash flow is negative before dividends and the company has raised its dividend while borrowing, so debt rises each year while the data center build runs; the REIT distribution rules and a stated commitment to dividend growth increase financing needs. Refinancing exposure is concentrated in 2027 to 2030. Project debt and a new joint venture add structurally senior and noncontrolling claims at subsidiaries that sit ahead of parent noteholders, and the parent notes depend on subsidiary guarantors and cash upstream. The ALM business relies on a limited number of clients and China-based buyers, and Corporate and Other carries negative segment Adjusted EBITDA after corporate costs. Ownership, contract backlog and ratings are not supplied, which limits the assessment of business risk; missing evidence is not treated as adverse.
