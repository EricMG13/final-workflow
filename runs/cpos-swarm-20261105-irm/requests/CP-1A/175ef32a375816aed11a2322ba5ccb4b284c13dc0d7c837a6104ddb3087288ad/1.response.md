### Business and transaction view

IRON MOUNTAIN INC is a listed US real estate investment trust with no sponsor or controlling owner disclosed in the pinned sources. Its credit rests on three operating blocks that behave differently. Global RIM (records management, data management, digital solutions, shredding, media and consumer storage) is the recurring core: FY2025 segment revenue was 5,291,481 thousand and segment Adjusted EBITDA 2,363,498 thousand. Management says a majority of revenue is recurring, storage contracts run one to five years and no customer exceeds about 3% of revenue; all three are management or filing statements and the sources give no volume data beyond stating that volume is expected to be relatively stable, so growth in this segment comes from revenue management (pricing). Global Data Center is the growth engine: FY2025 revenue 803,429 thousand, Adjusted EBITDA 416,326 thousand, with nine-month 2026 revenue up 39.6% on lease commencements. It also absorbs most of the capital: nine-month 2026 data center growth capex was 1,344,097 thousand of 1,589,574 thousand total growth capex. Corporate and Other (ALM, Fine Arts and corporate costs) grew revenue 55.9% in nine months 2026 partly through acquisitions and reports negative segment Adjusted EBITDA (109,611 thousand loss) because corporate costs sit in it.

For a creditor the transmission mechanism is: recurring storage cash flow plus long data center leases (weighted average lease expiration 10.3 years at December 31, 2025) give revenue visibility; the same data center growth consumes cash ahead of revenue, so debt rose to 17,972,202 thousand while operating cash flow of 1,203,634 thousand trailed capex of 1,724,205 thousand (CP-1). The 2026 transactions were financings, not a change of control: a 1,500,000 thousand note issue, two data center project financings, a receivables program upsize and one new joint venture with an unnamed partner. The credit implication is refinancing and market-access dependence rather than sponsor or ownership risk, and the sources do not allow an ownership or sponsor-behaviour assessment beyond documented issuer actions. Governance of creditor-relevant behaviour is Neutral or Mixed: distributions are tied to REIT status and are rising while free cash flow is negative, but there is no documented priming, uptiering, drop-down or collateral release.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no sponsor transaction, leveraged buyout or change of control in the sources, so the transaction view is the set of financing and investment events in the period. All items below are completed unless stated, and each is a Documentary Fact from the cited filing.

In Q2 2026, on June 26, 2026, IMI completed a private offering of 1,500,000 thousand of 6 1/4% Notes due 2035, issued at par with a maturity of January 15, 2035, interest paid January 15 and July 15 and a par call date of July 15, 2031; net proceeds of about 1,481,800 thousand repaid part of the revolving credit facility borrowings and funded general corporate purposes (10-Q Q3 page:22-23). The Q2 2026 quarterly report prints the par call date as July 15, 2031 in its debt note (10-Q Q2 page:21) and as July 15, 2029 in its MD&A table (10-Q Q2 page:47); both spans are quoted in the conflicts table and the conflict is logged, not reconciled. In Q1 2026, on January 9, 2026, two wholly-owned subsidiaries entered into mortgage and mezzanine loans with a total original principal of 433,000 thousand (Virginia 3 Term Loans due 2031, maturing January 9, 2031, weighted average rate 6.3%), used to repay the earlier Virginia 3 loans and part of the revolver (10-Q Q3 page:21). On June 9, 2026, Iron Mountain Data Centers Virginia 9, LLC entered into a credit agreement allowing term loans up to 298,000 thousand at SOFR plus 3.00%, maturing June 9, 2029 with two one-year extension options, with 138,627 thousand outstanding at September 30, 2026 (10-Q Q3 page:21-22). In the same quarter the company formed Iron Mountain Data Centers Virginia 9 JV, LP with an unnamed partner, creating initial noncontrolling interests of about 49,900 thousand (10-Q Q3 page:24). On May 28, 2026 the receivables securitization program was amended to include certain Canadian entities, increase capacity from 400,000 thousand to 450,000 thousand and extend maturity from July 1, 2027 to July 1, 2029 (10-Q Q3 page:22).

Other items in the period: a finance lease expected to commence in the fourth quarter of 2026 with a 31-year term and about 223,400 thousand of undiscounted minimum payments (10-Q Q3 page:10); acquisitions in the ALM business, with cash paid for acquisitions of 8,033 thousand in nine months 2026 (10-Q Q3 page:6) and a 31.7 million increase in Corporate and Other service revenue attributed to acquisitions (10-Q Q3 page:44); and the deferred purchase obligation for the 2024 Regency Technologies acquisition, which has an earnout of up to 200,000 thousand payable in 2027 if earned and a fair value of 210,188 thousand at September 30, 2026 (10-K page:134; 10-Q Q3 page:10-11). The creditor effect is that the 2026 issuance extended the bond stack to 2035 and moved some bank borrowing into fixed rate notes, while project debt at subsidiaries and a new joint venture add structurally senior and noncontrolling claims; the stated use of proceeds was repayment of existing revolver borrowings, which in my reading restored revolver capacity rather than adding committed liquidity (Analyst Interpretation). Subsequent events up to the cutoff: the board declared a dividend of 0.993 per share on November 5, 2026, payable January 6, 2027 to holders of record on December 15, 2026, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance; both are subsequent to the September 30, 2026 balance sheet and are not folded into period facts (10-Q Q3 page:24; Release Q3 page:2). No new debt issue after September 30, 2026 appears in the sources.
