### Credit view

JetBlue Airways Corporation is a weak but currently funded credit: operating earnings do not cover its debt service, so it is living on its balance sheet. The strongest support is USD 2,226 million of liquidity at September 30, 2026, which includes a USD 600 million undrawn revolver to October 2029, together with only USD 629 million of debt maturing through 2027 and continued access to aircraft-secured funding. The weakest point is earnings: computed last-twelve-months EBITDA excluding special items of USD 149 million covers cash interest of USD 493 million only 0.30x, and free cash flow was an outflow of USD 1,193 million. What decides the outcome is whether fares keep recapturing unhedged fuel cost through 2027, and whether unencumbered collateral remains available as cash falls.

### Key findings

First, the earnings base is thin relative to the debt load. Computed EBITDA excluding special items was USD 350 million in FY2025 and USD 149 million for the last twelve months to September 30, 2026, against debt of USD 8,393 million, which gives 56.33x gross and 45.42x net leverage [CP-1]. The issuer reports no EBITDA, so the ratios are our definition and are very sensitive to a small denominator; the direction is not in doubt, because the operating loss widened from USD 268 million to USD 509 million between the nine months of 2025 and 2026 [CP-1; Q3 10-Q page:6].

Second, the cash burn is funded by the liquidity cushion rather than by operations. Operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 273 million and free cash flow an outflow of USD 897 million in the nine months of 2026, while unrestricted cash and investments fell by USD 851 million to USD 1,626 million and net debt rose from USD 6,021 million to USD 6,767 million [CP-1]. The USD 495 million of new secured borrowing in 2026 offset only part of the USD 658 million of principal repaid.

Third, the structure is predominantly secured. Secured instruments are USD 7,455 million of the USD 8,450 million gross debt and finance lease obligations, and unsecured instruments are USD 995 million (Q3 10-Q page:11; calculation below). Of the secured amount, USD 2,733 million is the TrueBlue notes and term loan, secured by a first lien on loyalty program collateral and co-issued with JetBlue Loyalty, LP (10-K page:129, page:130). The 10-Q states that "Approximately 66% of our owned property and equipment and intangible assets at net book value were pledged or committed to be pledged as security under various loan agreements" (Q3 10-Q page:47). Mechanic: each new secured borrowing moves unencumbered value to secured lenders ahead of the USD 995 million of unsecured claims and any future unsecured lender; management's claim of about USD 7 billion of unencumbered assets is attributed and not reconciled (Q3 release page:2).

Fourth, the maturity profile is back-ended. Debt maturing is USD 152 million for the remainder of 2026, USD 477 million in 2027, USD 582 million in 2028, USD 1,835 million in 2029, USD 656 million in 2030 and USD 4,691 million thereafter (Q3 10-Q page:11). The 2029 amount includes the maturity of the USD 460 million 2.50% convertible senior notes and of the TrueBlue term loan facility, and coincides with the October 21, 2029 revolver expiry. Liquidity covers the fixed claims through 2027 (see Calculations) provided the revolver remains available and its minimum liquidity covenant is met.

Fifth, covenant headroom cannot be measured. Management states "we were in compliance with the material covenants of our debt and lease agreements" (Q3 10-Q page:47), but no level, step-down or reported ratio is printed for the TrueBlue financings or the Citibank revolver. The revolver "includes covenants that require us to maintain certain minimum balances in unrestricted cash, cash equivalents, and unused commitments available under revolving credit facilities" (10-K page:94). With unrestricted cash and investments falling, this is the test most likely to bind first; its level is Insufficient Information.

Sixth, operating exposures are unhedged and open-ended. The issuer had no fuel hedging contracts at September 30, 2026, and a 10% fuel price increase would add approximately USD 376 million of expense over twelve months (Q3 10-Q page:53). The pilot contract has been amendable since February 2025 and the inflight contract becomes amendable on December 13, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:19, page:20), so labor cost can step up without a revenue offset. Management says it recaptured approximately 87% of higher third-quarter fuel cost through fares (Q3 release page:1, Management Language); that recapture is the central assumption in any recovery of coverage.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity: USD 1,626 million of unrestricted cash, cash equivalents and investment securities plus the USD 600 million undrawn Citibank revolver gives USD 2,226 million, with no facility used in that figure expiring within twelve months [CP-1; Q3 10-Q page:14]. Documentary Fact. Funding access: the issuer borrowed USD 500 million against owned aircraft in 2026 and expects a further USD 250 million in the fourth quarter of 2026, which shows secured market access for aircraft collateral (Q3 10-Q page:13, page:14). Documentary Fact; the further amount is forward-looking. Governance: no sponsor or controlling holder is identified, no dividends are paid and no repurchase program exists [CP-1A; 10-K page:80], so no sponsor extraction channel is documented. Documentary Fact; beneficial ownership is Insufficient Information. Revenue momentum: Q3 2026 revenue of USD 2,812 million rose from USD 2,322 million in Q3 2025 [CP-1], with management attributing the growth to higher yields. Documentary Fact for the figures; Management Language for the attribution.

Weaknesses. Earnings and coverage: the 0.30x cash interest cover and negative free cash flow above are the core weakness. Documentary Fact for the inputs; Analyst Interpretation for the credit reading. Rising net debt and a thinning equity base: stockholders' equity fell from USD 2,120 million to USD 1,337 million and retained earnings turned to a deficit of USD 106 million between December 31, 2025 and September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:6). Documentary Fact. Encumbrance: about 88% of debt is secured and a large majority of fixed assets are pledged or committed to be pledged. Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation. Concentration and operating shocks: 95% of routes touch six focus cities in a congested Northeast corridor, and disruption plus engine groundings (two aircraft grounded at September 30, 2026) reduce completed capacity [CP-1A]. Documentary Fact. Contingencies: the Northeast Alliance antitrust class action cannot be estimated (Q3 10-Q page:21) and the American Airlines contract claim carries an accrual of unstated size (Q3 10-Q page:22). Documentary Fact; loss is Insufficient Information.

- Last-twelve-months free cash outflow 1,193 against unrestricted cash and investments 1,626: 1,193 / 1,626 = 73.37 percent [CP-1].
