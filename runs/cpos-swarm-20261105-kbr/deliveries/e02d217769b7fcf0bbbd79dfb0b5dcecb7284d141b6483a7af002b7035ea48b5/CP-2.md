### Credit view

KBR, INC. is a moderately levered, well-funded defense and sustainable-technology services group whose credit is sound on today's numbers but is about to be reset by the planned spin-off of its larger segment. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 1,046 million, net leverage of 2.1x on management's measure and a backlog of USD 17,718 million. The weakest point is that adjusted EBITDA changed definition in 2026, current maturities rose to USD 554 million, and no post-spin debt, covenant or guarantor terms are disclosed. The outcome turns on how much debt the Mission Technology Solutions cash distribution retires, how the August 2027 term loan is refinanced, and whether the remaining sustainable technology group can carry the capital structure it inherits.

### Credit assessment and thesis

Category: Analyst Interpretation. Confidence: Medium, held below High because the covenant, indenture and credit agreement are not supplied, the post-spin capital structure is Not supplied, and the business context was ready only with limitations. Basis: financial base leverage, liquidity, maturity and definition rows; business context transaction, customer concentration, backlog and governance rows.

Thesis: KBR, INC. can service and refinance its present debt from liquidity and recurring government and technology programs, but its credit quality will be decided by the debt, covenants and earnings base it carries after the Mission Technology Solutions spin-off, which the evidence does not yet show.

### Key findings

1. The spin-off, not the pre-spin ratios, drives the credit view (Documentary Fact for the facts, Analyst Interpretation for the reading). The planned separation of Mission Technology Solutions into Trinzic, Inc. is targeted for January 4, 2027, after the cutoff, and is conditional on Board approval, tax treatment, a Form 10 filed on September 30, 2026, financing and regulatory approvals [business context, transaction register]. That segment carried USD 3,870 million of USD 5,935 million nine-month revenue and USD 441 million of USD 777 million nine-month adjusted EBITDA [business context, calculations], so what remains is the sustainable technology segment plus corporate costs, with USD 336 million of nine-month adjusted EBITDA on the same definition. Management intends to use cash distributed by the departing segment to reduce KBR's debt, but the amount, the allocation of existing debt, and the fate of guarantors and covenants are Not supplied [business context, gaps]. Credit implication: pre-spin leverage cannot be carried forward, and no post-spin leverage claim is made here.

2. Reported leverage is moderate but flattered by a definition change (Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation). Net debt of USD 2,118 million over trailing adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,015 million is 2.1x, against 2.2x at January 2, 2026, and gross leverage is 2.52x [financial base, leverage chart]. Since the first quarter of fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA adds back joint venture items of USD 44 million over nine months without recasting history; without them net leverage is 2.18x, almost level with 2.19x at the end of fiscal 2025, and on operating income plus depreciation and amortization it is 2.37x [financial base, definition review]. Credit implication: most of the apparent improvement is definitional, so covenant judgments should use the lower-quality measures as well.

3. A refinancing and amortization step falls inside twelve months (Documentary Fact). Current maturities of long-term debt are USD 554 million at October 2, 2026 against USD 49 million at January 2, 2026, because Term Loan A-2 matures in August 2027; the USD 250 million Senior Notes mature in September 2028, and the revolver and Term Loan A-1 and A-3 in February 2029 [financial base, debt schedule]. Credit implication: the tranche can be repaid from liquidity or refinanced, but its timing coincides with the separation, when lender consent, ratings and covenant terms are being reset (Amendment No. 14 already permitted the reorganization).

Interest coverage floor 3.00x on the memo interest expense of 145: required EBITDA 3.00 x 145 = 435, a decline of 580, or 57.1%. Defined terms are not supplied, so these are not covenant headroom.

C3. Liquidity against the nearest maturity. Liquidity 439 + 607 = 1,046; current maturities 554; 1,046 / 554 = 1.89x.

C4. Scale of the perimeter change (illustrative, not a forecast or pro forma). Nine-month adjusted EBITDA excluding the departing segment: 407 - 71 = 336, or 336 / 777 = 43.2% of the consolidated figure. Applying that share to trailing net debt gives 2,118 x 336 / 777 = 916, so holding net leverage at 2.09x would require net debt to fall by about 2,118 - 916 = 1,202. The share is from nine-month figures and net debt is at a point in time; corporate cost allocation after separation, stranded costs, the debt the new company assumes and the size of the cash distribution are Not supplied.

C5. Sensitivity of leverage to debt repayment. Each 100 of net debt repaid reduces net leverage by 100 / 1,015 = 0.10x on the printed basis.

C6. Cash flow and investment sums. Nine-month operating cash flow 506 - 331 = 175 lower. Minority and joint venture investments 115 + 49 + 13 = 177. Letters of credit outside the debt figure: 18 on the revolver and 305 on bilateral lines.
