### Business and transaction view

KBR, INC. is a listed company with no named sponsor and no parent: the context leaves those fields blank, the cover shows 126,108,987 shares outstanding at October 21, 2026, and the beneficial ownership table is incorporated by reference to a proxy statement that is not in the pack, so holder concentration and control rights are not supplied. The business has two core segments. Mission Technology Solutions (MTS) serves defense, intelligence, space and civil government customers, mainly in the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia, largely on cost-reimbursable terms; U.S. government customers were 57% of fiscal 2025 revenue and U.K. government customers 9%. Sustainable Technology Solutions (STS) licenses proprietary process technology and provides engineering and advisory services to energy and chemicals customers, with a large equity-method joint venture component. The operating model is capital light, project and program based, and dependent on backlog conversion, working capital and joint venture distributions; backlog was 17,718 at October 2, 2026, plus 5,404 of award options, of which 38% is expected to be executed within one year, and nearly all contracts allow termination for convenience.

The transaction that dominates the credit view is the Planned Spin-Off of MTS into a separate listed company, to be named Trinzic, Inc., first announced in September 2025 and now targeted for January 4, 2027, the first business day of fiscal 2027, after the evidence cutoff. Completion is conditional on Board approval, a tax opinion or private letter ruling, effectiveness of a Form 10 filed on September 30, 2026, satisfactory completion of financing, and regulatory approvals. The mechanism that matters to creditors is that MTS is the larger segment by revenue, adjusted EBITDA and backlog, so after separation the remaining KBR group is the STS business plus corporate costs, while management says it will use cash distributed by MTS from its own financing to reduce KBR's debt. The size of that distribution, which debt stays with which company, whether the Senior Credit Facility and the Senior Notes remain with KBR, and the covenant and guarantor perimeter after separation are not supplied. The July 29, 2026 Amendment No. 14 to the credit agreement permits the internal reorganization; its text is not supplied. Behaviour to date is that of a company preparing a separation while keeping its dividend, cutting buybacks, and making small acquisitions and minority investments, with no documented priming, non-pro-rata exchange or distribution to insiders. The creditor view is therefore: a business with diversified government and commercial exposure, moderate leverage and large pending structural change, where the post-spin perimeter, debt allocation and covenant package, not the pre-spin credit statistics, will decide the credit quality, and where those facts are not yet in the pack.
