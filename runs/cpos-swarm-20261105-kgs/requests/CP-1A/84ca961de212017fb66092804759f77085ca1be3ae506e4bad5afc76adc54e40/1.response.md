### Business and transaction view

Kodiak's credit story is a contract compression operator with fixed-fee, multi-year contracts, high fleet utilisation and customers described as large upstream and midstream companies, that is in the middle of a strategic widening into distributed power. On the evidence, the compression core is stable and gaining visibility: remaining performance obligations rose sharply in the second quarter of 2026 and utilisation was 98.2%. The power build is the new variable. The acquisition closed on April 1, 2026, the acquired business has produced one quarter of results, only one customer lease is disclosed in detail, and purchase commitments rose to $2.5 billion. The financial handoff shows an equity offering, a refinanced notes stack and a largely undrawn ABL alongside this growth, which supports near-term liquidity but moves the credit from a steady-state contract business toward a capital-intensive build in which the pace of customer contracting, not demand commentary, matters. Ownership is public: the former sponsor affiliate exited in 2025, so no sponsor is documented as supporting or controlling the credit. Documented capital allocation shows both shareholder distributions and equity issuance; no priming, drop-down, non-pro-rata exchange or collateral release is documented in the pack. Governance evidence is limited to filing summaries because the proxy statement and executed financing documents are not supplied.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transaction that matters most is the acquisition of Distributed Power Solutions, LLC, a provider of turnkey distributed and behind-the-meter power generation, by Kodiak Gas Services, LLC. The type is an acquisition of 100% of the equity interests for cash and registrant shares. Signing was on February 5, 2026 at a value of approximately $675.0 million at signing, comprising $575.0 million of cash and 2,401,278 shares valued at approximately $100.0 million on a $41.64 five-day average price, with a $37.1 million termination fee payable by the registrant in certain circumstances (10-K pages 25 and 139). Closing was April 1, 2026 on cash of $587.3 million, adjusted for additional power generation assets bought before closing, indebtedness and working capital, and 2.4 million shares with an estimated fair value of $139.0 million at the $57.90 closing price, so the fair value of consideration transferred was 726,354 thousand USD (10-Q 2026-06-30 pages 12 and 13; 10-Q 2026-03-31 page 24). The sellers are not named in the acquisition note; the registration rights agreement entered at closing names Mustang PRS, LLC and Louisiana Machinery Company, L.L.C. as holders of the registrant shares issued in the transaction (10-Q 2026-03-31 page 24); the pack does not otherwise name the sellers. The purchase price allocation is preliminary and recognises goodwill of 344,782 thousand USD, all in the Power Infrastructure reporting unit (10-Q 2026-06-30 pages 13 and 19). The pack contains no bridge between the signing and closing values, so the difference is logged as a conflict below and not reconciled. The stated rationale, which is management language, is to expand beyond contract compression into distributed power generation and enhance revenue growth through scale and complementary services (10-Q 2026-06-30 page 12). Liquidity effect and use of proceeds: the notes and equity transactions that followed are described below; the pack does not state which source funded which part of the cash consideration, and the financial handoff shows cash paid net of cash acquired of 575,965 thousand USD in the first half of 2026. The status is complete, with the allocation open for up to one year from closing.

New debt in the period. On March 20, 2026 Kodiak Services issued $1.0 billion of 5.875% senior unsecured notes due 2031, with fees of $14.3 million, and used a portion of the net proceeds to redeem all $750.0 million of its 7.25% notes due 2029 at 103.625 percent, through a defeasance on March 30, 2026 that required a $785.5 million cash outlay and produced a loss on extinguishment of 36,512 thousand USD (10-Q 2026-06-30 pages 21 to 23). The 2029 notes were redeemed April 10, 2026. On April 2, 2026 Kodiak and Kodiak Services signed the Fifth Amendment to the ABL credit agreement, which modifies the leverage ratio calculation by allowing net proceeds of the 2031 notes to be deducted from total indebtedness, in addition to the $50.0 million cash netting cap, through June 30, 2026 (page 21). In the prior year, on September 5, 2025 Kodiak Services issued $600.0 million each of 6.50% notes due 2033 and 6.75% notes due 2035, with add-ons of $170.0 million and $30.0 million on September 22, 2025, using the proceeds to repay part of the ABL, and a Fourth Amendment to the ABL reduced commitments to $2.0 billion and extended maturity to September 2030 (10-Q 2026-06-30 page 23; 10-K page 72), the amendment date not being stated in the text read. The equity side: an underwritten offering of 10.6 million shares at $71.00, with a further 1.6 million shares under the option, closed May 15, 2026 for net proceeds of approximately $836.1 million (10-Q 2026-06-30 page 26). The pack does not state a use of proceeds for that offering, and the financial handoff shows ABL drawings fell in the quarter; the link is an inference, not a documented fact. After quarter end Kodiak exercised options to buy about 43,000 horsepower of leased compression equipment for about $32.8 million in July 2026 and declared a $0.49 per share dividend on August 5, 2026 (10-Q 2026-06-30 pages 29 and 35). The financial handoff carries the resulting leverage, liquidity and maturity figures: total debt to LTM adjusted EBITDA of 3.63x, liquidity of 1,742.0 US$m, and purchase commitments of $2.5 billion. Parties, status and amounts above are Documentary Facts; the stated rationales are Management Language; the possible link between the equity offering and ABL repayment is Analyst Interpretation.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability (Analyst Interpretation). Evidence: fixed monthly fees with primary terms of one to seven years (10-K page:16), 9.0% of revenue-generating horsepower on month-to-month terms, and performance obligations of 2,643,245 thousand USD at June 30, 2026 against LTM compression revenue of 1,220,890 thousand USD (Calculation A2). Mechanic: take-or-pay-style fees convert fleet utilisation into near-term revenue regardless of volumes, and the rise in obligations extends the contracted period. Implication: supports near-term revenue visibility and debt service; the step up is unexplained, and the tail beyond 2027 was thin until the second quarter. Counter: the shorter-term tail and month-to-month contracts are the exposure if basin activity weakens.
2. Margin and cash-flow risk (Analyst Interpretation). Evidence: Compression adjusted gross margin of 70.0% in the second quarter against Other Services at 11.3%, below 15.9% in the first quarter of 2026 and 24.5% a year earlier (release 2026-08-06 page:6), with receivables and working capital absorbing cash in the first half (financial handoff, cash flow quality). Mechanic: the high-margin compression stream carries the credit; Other Services margin erosion and working capital drag reduce cash conversion. Implication: credit metrics on adjusted EBITDA overstate cash earnings in 2026; the financial handoff shows operating cash flow at 62.5% of LTM adjusted EBITDA.
3. Concentration risk (Analyst Interpretation). Evidence: 82.8% of compression assets in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford (10-K page:13), four largest customers about 32% of revenue and one customer 14%, and a limited number of suppliers. Mechanic: basin activity, a single large customer's decisions and equipment lead times drive utilisation and growth timing. Implication: loss of the largest customer or a supplier disruption would have a disproportionate effect on utilisation and growth timing; no peer comparison is made.
4. Transformation and execution risk in power (Analyst Interpretation). Evidence: one disclosed lease with initial term to April 1, 2028, revised power growth capital spending guidance, purchase commitments of $2.5 billion against $276.4 million at March 31, 2026 (10-Q 2026-03-31 page:34), and a one gigawatt turbine order (release 2026-08-06 page:2). Mechanic: equipment is bought before multi-year contracts are shown, so cash is committed ahead of revenue; power lease revenue was 8.4% of second quarter revenue (Calculation A3). Implication: liquidity of 1,742.0 US$m in the financial handoff must absorb a 12-month commitment of $587.9 million plus negative implied free cash flow; delays in contracting would raise leverage because EBITDA from the new assets lags the financing. Counter: management states customer discussions are advanced, a Management Language claim that is not backed by executed contracts in the pack.
5. Sponsor alignment (Analyst Interpretation). Evidence: the sponsor affiliate holding fell to zero by December 2, 2025 with no proceeds to Kodiak. Mechanic: with no sponsor, distributions and acquisitions are decided by a public-company board under the covenant package; ABL-funded dividends require $100,000,000 pro forma availability. Implication: no sponsor is documented as available to provide support, and equity issuance at market is the demonstrated support route; monitoring should track distributions against free cash flow, which in 2025 were 263,525 thousand USD against company free cash flow of 229,581 thousand USD (Calculation A4).
6. Key uncertainties (Analyst Interpretation). Preliminary purchase accounting; an unquantified sanctions-related matter; unexplained allowance write-offs; and no executed financing documents. Each is a gap below.
