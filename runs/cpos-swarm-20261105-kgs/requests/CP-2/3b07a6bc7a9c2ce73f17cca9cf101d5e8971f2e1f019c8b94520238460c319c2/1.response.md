### Credit view

Kodiak Gas Services is a moderately leveraged contract compression credit whose strength is contracted, high-margin revenue and ample liquidity, and whose main exposure is a power build financed ahead of contracts. Compression remaining performance obligations of USD 2,643.2 million, utilisation of 98.2% and total debt of 3.63x LTM adjusted EBITDA after an equity raise of USD 836.1 million support the credit, with liquidity of USD 1,742.0 million and no scheduled debt maturity of size before 2030. The weakest point is that growth spending outruns cash generation: purchase commitments are USD 2.5 billion, guidance implies negative 2026 free cash flow, and only one power lease is disclosed. Delivery of power contracts against that spending, and covenant headroom that cannot be recomputed here, decide the outcome.

### Thesis

Kodiak's cash-generative, contracted compression fleet and equity-supported balance sheet carry the credit through a power build whose repayment capacity is not yet evidenced by contracts (Analyst Interpretation, resting on the contract, leverage and commitment rows below).

### Key findings

The first finding is revenue durability. Kodiak sells compression on fixed monthly fees under primary terms of one to seven years, with 9.0% of revenue-generating horsepower on month-to-month terms, and its compression remaining performance obligations were USD 2,643.2 million at June 30, 2026 against USD 1,632.9 million at March 31, 2026, with 49.9% falling in 2028 or later (Documentary Fact, from the business-context handoff). The mechanism is that fixed fees turn 98.2% utilisation into revenue largely independent of volume, so contracted obligations of 2.17 times LTM compression revenue protect near-term coverage of debt service. The implication is stronger revenue visibility than the previous quarter showed, tempered because the filings do not explain the jump and the month-to-month tail would reprice quickly in a weak basin.

The second is financial risk and leverage. Total debt was USD 2,780.9 million, or 3.63x LTM adjusted EBITDA of USD 766.1 million, and net debt was 3.45x, against 4.31x total and 4.30x net at FY2024 and 3.66x total at FY2025 (Documentary Fact for inputs, Calculated in the financial-base handoff). The mechanism is that the May equity offering of USD 836.1 million arrived in the same period as the USD 576.0 million cash acquisition and a notes refinancing, so debt rose by USD 166.3 million since December 2025 while LTM adjusted EBITDA rose by USD 51.1 million. The implication for leverage tolerance is a stepped-down but not yet clearly falling ratio; the figure overstates leverage on a full-year perimeter because EBITDA from the acquired power business before April 1, 2026 is Not calculable, and management's covenant-basis ratio of 3.2x (Management Language) cannot be recomputed.

The third is funding and liquidity. Liquidity was USD 1,742.0 million (cash of USD 137.6 million and undrawn ABL capacity of USD 1,604.5 million, an upper bound because the borrowing base is not supplied), against purchase commitments of USD 2.5 billion, of which USD 587.9 million is expected within twelve months, up from USD 276.4 million at March 31, 2026. Revised 2026 guidance implies discretionary cash flow less growth and other capital spending of negative USD 235.0 million to negative USD 125.0 million, before dividends of about USD 49.5 million a quarter. The mechanism is that the spending is discretionary in timing but contractually committed once ordered, and the ABL, which matures September 5, 2030 and is secured on essentially all assets, is the funding backstop. The implication is that refinancing risk is low through 2029 but that net leverage, on the indicative sensitivity below, ends 2026 at about 3.27x to 3.52x, roughly where it is today, unless adjusted EBITDA beats guidance or equity is raised again (Analyst Interpretation).

The fourth is earnings and cash quality. Add-backs were USD 162.7 million, or 21.2% of LTM adjusted EBITDA, and unadjusted leverage on the same debt would be 4.61x; operating cash flow was 62.5% of LTM adjusted EBITDA against 83.9% in FY2025, as working capital used USD 154.6 million in the first half (Documentary Fact and Calculated). The mechanism is that equity compensation, a settled sales tax reserve and refinancing and acquisition costs sit outside adjusted EBITDA while the sales tax settlement and working capital absorbed cash. The implication is that adjusted-EBITDA coverage of 4.14x on cash interest paid is a ceiling on debt-service capacity, and monitoring should use operating cash flow.

The fifth is the power transition and ownership. The acquisition closed April 1, 2026 with fair value of consideration of USD 726.4 million (signing value was about USD 675.0 million, logged as a conflict, not reconciled), goodwill of USD 344.8 million on a preliminary allocation, one disclosed lease agreement whose initial term ends April 1, 2028, and no sponsor, since EQT affiliates sold their entire stake by December 2, 2025 (Documentary Fact). The mechanism is that equipment is bought before multi-year contracts are shown, and no sponsor is documented as available to cure a shortfall. The implication is that the next two to three quarters of power contract announcements and utilisation are the main evidence that will move the credit view either way.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Contracted revenue visibility is the first: fixed-fee compression contracts, 98.2% utilisation, performance obligations of USD 2,643.2 million, and customer cost pass-throughs for fuel and taxes (Documentary Fact; business-context handoff). Margin resilience is the second: compression adjusted gross margin was 70.0% in the second quarter and adjusted EBITDA margin was 55.4% (Documentary Fact; financial-base handoff), which cushions a revenue decline. Liquidity and maturity profile is the third: USD 1,742.0 million of liquidity, no scheduled maturity of size before the ABL in 2030, and notes of USD 1,000.0 million due 2031, USD 770.0 million due 2033 and USD 630.0 million due 2035 (Documentary Fact). Balance sheet response is the fourth: Kodiak issued USD 836.1 million of equity alongside a debt-financed acquisition (Documentary Fact), which is documented equity support. Secured debt is the fifth: only the USD 380.9 million drawn on the ABL is secured, equal to 0.50x LTM adjusted EBITDA on this analysis (Analyst Interpretation, indicative only because the covenant definition of senior secured debt is not supplied).

Weaknesses. Growth spending against cash flow is the first: commitments of USD 2.5 billion and guidance that implies negative free cash flow in 2026 (Analyst Interpretation on Documentary Fact inputs). Earnings quality is the second: add-backs of 21.2% of LTM adjusted EBITDA and weaker cash conversion of 62.5% (Documentary Fact and Calculated). Concentration is the third: 82.8% of compression assets in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford, four customers at about 32% of revenue and one at 14%, and dependence on a limited number of suppliers (Documentary Fact). Power visibility is the fourth: one disclosed lease agreement, so most power revenue is Not disclosed in contract terms, and a power fleet utilisation of 89.6% in its first quarter (Documentary Fact; the conclusion that visibility is thin is Analyst Interpretation). Covenant opacity is the fifth: the maximum Leverage Ratio of 5.25 to 1.00 printed in the annual report is on a defined basis not supplied, and the amended calculation applied only through June 30, 2026 (Documentary Fact; headroom is Insufficient Information). Unquantified contingencies are the sixth: a sanctions-related payments matter with no estimated penalty and a sales tax liability disclosed on two bases (Documentary Fact; exposure is Insufficient Information, not an adverse conclusion).

### Monitoring posture

Four items should be tracked each quarter, in this order: free cash flow against the USD 587.9 million of twelve-month commitments and the ABL balance; announcements of signed power leases and power utilisation against the 89.6% first-quarter level; the covenant leverage ratio and any amended terms, once the credit agreement is supplied; and conversion of performance obligations, to see whether the second-quarter jump reflects durable new contracts.
