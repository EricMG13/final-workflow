### Financial base view

View: leverage on the company-defined Adjusted EBITDA basis stepped up through 2026 because debt principal rose from 506,767 thousand to 768,478 thousand while LTM Adjusted EBITDA rose from 145,302 thousand to 179,068 thousand, and cash generation does not yet support deleveraging without earnings growth. Net debt (notes payable principal less cash) over LTM Adjusted EBITDA was 3.25x at FY2025 and 4.00x at Q2 2026; including finance lease liabilities (which sit outside notes payable and whose costs fall below EBITDA) it was 3.81x and 4.57x. The increase has a mechanical cause that the sources show: the Third Amendment of the credit agreement raised the term loan by $265.0 million (10-Q Q2 2026 page:18) to fund the Seemann acquisition completed February 3, 2026 (10-Q Q2 2026 page:15), while LTM EBITDA carries Seemann only from that date and the filer states that pro forma information was impracticable (10-Q Q2 2026 page:16), so the ratio is probably overstated relative to a pro forma figure that the sources do not allow us to compute. Operating cash flow was negative for FY2025 and for the six months to June 30, 2026, because contract assets and receivables grew with revenue; with capital expenditure included, free cash flow was negative in every period shown except FY2024. Interest cover is moderate (3.37x LTM on Adjusted EBITDA versus cash interest paid) and falls to 2.74x after deducting LTM capex. Liquidity is adequate: cash and an undrawn $150.0 million revolver. The debt has no maturity before the term loan's April 1, 2032 date other than scheduled amortization of 5.050 US$m a year in the December 31, 2025 table and a current portion of 5.610 US$m at June 30, 2026, but the credit agreement itself is not supplied, the term loan size is stated two ways, and the filer reports material weaknesses in internal control. These points limit confidence in the base but do not stop the analysis. The figures below are reported facts; every ratio is a calculation shown in the Calculations section.

### Covenant and definition review

Maintenance covenant: none while the revolver is undrawn. 10-Q Q2 2026 page:19 states the credit agreement "requires the Company to comply with a springing financial covenant that is tested on the last day of any testing fiscal quarter if and when the outstanding principal amount of revolving credit loans exceeds an applicable threshold." The level is "less than or equal to 6.50 to 1.00" for the Consolidated First Lien Net Leverage Ratio. The filer says the covenant "was not required" at June 30, 2026 and that it was in compliance. The applicable threshold, the definition of the ratio, its EBITDA add-backs and cash-netting limits are not supplied, so headroom against the covenant is Not calculable. For orientation only, net debt over Adjusted EBITDA is 4.00x and over unadjusted EBITDA 4.54x; neither uses the covenant definition, and net debt here includes the term note and other notes.

Definitions that differ from the computed metric: the company's Adjusted EBITDA adds back transaction related expenses, integration and restructuring costs, lender and administrative agent fees, share-based compensation and other non-recurring costs (10-Q Q2 2026 page:35). Depreciation and amortization in the reconciliation differs from the income statement line because it includes depreciation allocated to cost of goods sold (10-K FY2025 page:102 states $11.3 million for FY2025). The add-backs recur alongside the acquisition programme (transaction costs in every period shown) and lender fees recur with each credit agreement amendment, so the adjusted measure is a weaker guide to cash earnings than the reported EBITDA beside it. EBITDA also excludes finance lease interest and right-of-use amortization (10-Q Q2 2026 page:19), which is why the lease-inclusive ratio rows are shown.
