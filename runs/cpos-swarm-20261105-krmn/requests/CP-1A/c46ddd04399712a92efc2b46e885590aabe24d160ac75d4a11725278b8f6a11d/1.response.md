### Business and transaction view

View: Karman is a supplier of mission-critical structures, interstage systems and propulsion components to prime contractors on US missile, hypersonic, space-launch and, since February 2026, maritime defense programs. The credit story is demand-led growth that management is funding largely with acquisitions and term debt. Revenue visibility is high on paper, because the company says it often holds sole or single source positions on long-lived programs (10-K FY2025 page:17) and reports a large backlog (EX-99.1 Aug 2026 page:1). Three features limit how far that visibility supports debt service. First, concentration: the three largest customers were about half of FY2025 revenue (10-K FY2025 page:25), so a single prime's resourcing or funding decision moves the group. Second, the backlog definition was widened in 2026 (10-Q Q2 2026 page:33) and remaining performance obligations are a narrower measure (10-Q Q2 2026 page:7), so backlog growth cannot be read as the same kind of coverage as in the annual report. Third, the growth rate depends on acquired businesses: Seemann contributed revenue from its acquisition on February 3, 2026 (amounts in the transaction section), and the filer states that pro forma information is impracticable to obtain (10-Q Q2 2026 page:16). The ownership position is not documented in the pack: the 10-K incorporates ownership, related-party and director information by reference to a proxy statement that is not supplied (10-K FY2025 page:156), the run context names no sponsor, and a July 2025 secondary offering shows existing stockholders selling (10-K FY2025 page:107). Governance evidence is mixed: documented actions show equity raised at IPO, no dividends and no debt-funded distributions in the periods covered, but also five credit agreement amendments in the sixteen months from April 1, 2025 to August 3, 2026, and disclosed material weaknesses in internal control (10-K FY2025 page:153; 10-Q Q2 2026 page:41). Leverage and liquidity consequences are those in the financials handoff; this module adds the business mechanics behind them.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

This section is prose only. The facts are ordered by date and each states its status at the cutoff.

Financing history. Before April 2025 the company borrowed under a TCW financing agreement, which was extinguished when the company entered the Citibank credit agreement on April 1, 2025 with a new $300.0 million term loan and a $50.0 million revolver, maturing April 1, 2032 and April 1, 2030 respectively (10-K FY2025 page:140). That agreement has been amended five times to the cutoff. The First Amendment on May 27, 2025 added $75.0 million of term loan to fund the ISP acquisition; the Second Amendment on October 24, 2025 added $130.0 million to fund Five Axis and repay revolver borrowings (10-K FY2025 page:140). The Third Amendment on February 2, 2026 refinanced $502.8 million of existing term loans to a margin 75 basis points lower and added $265.0 million (10-Q Q2 2026 page:18). The Fourth Amendment on March 9, 2026 raised revolver commitments by $100.0 million to $150.0 million and removed the cap on incremental revolving commitments (10-Q Q2 2026 page:18). The Fifth Amendment on August 3, 2026 lowered the term loan margin to SOFR plus 2.25% and revolver pricing by 50 basis points (10-Q Q2 2026 page:19). All five are completed. Each of the three term loan increases coincided with an acquisition (ISP, Five Axis, Seemann), and MTI was funded by a $30.0 million revolver draw (10-K FY2025 page:141). That is the transmission mechanism: acquisition funding is arranged by amending the existing facility, whose springing covenant is written on a first lien ratio, and not by a new issue to bond investors, so no new notes were issued in the period. No bond, note issue or ratings event appears in the sources, and the run context lists no ratings.

Equity events. The IPO was completed on February 14, 2025: 26.5 million shares at $22.00, of which 8.4 million were sold by the company (10-K FY2025 page:116). On July 23, 2025 existing stockholders priced a secondary sale of 21,000,000 shares at $49.00, with an option for 3,150,000 more that was fully exercised, and the company "did not sell any shares or receive proceeds" (10-K FY2025 page:107). The company expects to retain earnings and has no current plans to pay dividends (10-K FY2025 page:87).

Acquisitions. MTI closed April 2, 2025 for $82.3 million in cash; ISP closed May 28, 2025 for approximately $52.9 million in cash and 147,842 shares; Five Axis closed October 28, 2025 for $90.7 million in cash and 68,625 shares (all 10-K FY2025 page:94). Seemann Composites and Materials Sciences, a maritime defense composites business, closed February 3, 2026 (10-Q Q2 2026 page:15). The Q2 2026 10-Q states cash of approximately $215.9 million, shares valued at $17.0 million and total consideration transferred of $232.9 million, funded with incremental term borrowing, while the 10-K described the signed agreement as $210.0 million in cash and $10.0 million in shares (10-K FY2025 page:152); this is logged as a conflict and not reconciled. Seemann revenue was $37.8 million for the three months and $63.0 million for the six months ended June 30, 2026 (10-Q Q2 2026 page:16). Walker Precision Engineering, a Glasgow-based maker of components for missile seekers and guidance systems, is the subject of a definitive agreement dated July 20, 2026 for aggregate consideration of approximately $94.0 million, expected to close in the third quarter of 2026 subject to regulatory approvals (10-Q Q2 2026 page:29). Its status at the cutoff is pending as far as the sources show; funding, its earnings and any financing are Not supplied. It would be the first non-US operation described in the sources read, since the listed subsidiaries are in US states (10-Q Q2 2026 page:4); analyst interpretation: that adds cross-border integration and export-control questions the filings do not address.

Effect on leverage and liquidity. Per the financials handoff, total debt principal rose to 768,478 thousand at June 30, 2026 from 506,767 thousand at December 31, 2025 and net debt over LTM Adjusted EBITDA reached 4.00x. The debt increase is the Seemann funding. Cash and the undrawn revolver gave 201.741 US$m of liquidity, and the handoff's illustration shows the pending Walker consideration would consume 46.6% of it if funded entirely from liquidity; the sources do not say how it will be funded. The expected annual interest saving from the repricing is described by management as "approximately $4 million" (EX-99.1 Aug 2026 page:1). Seller notes and contingent consideration: the 2024 seller note was repaid in April 2025 (10-K FY2025 page:141), and the Seemann acquisition has no contingent consideration (10-Q Q2 2026 page:15).

### Credit translations

1. Customer concentration. Evidence: the three largest customers were "approximately 51.5% of revenue" (10-K FY2025 page:25), yet no single program was more than 12% on average (10-K FY2025 page:10). Mechanic: demand and cash collection run through a few primes, so one customer's resourcing or payment timing changes revenue and contract assets together, which the working-capital growth in CP-1 already shows. Implication: cash conversion and revenue durability carry customer-specific risk that diversification by program does not remove; monitor customer mix and receivable and contract asset ageing. The conflict log below, on end-market shares, counters the use of the end-market split as a diversification argument.
2. Acquisition-led growth. Evidence: four closings and one pending agreement in sixteen months, each paired with a term loan increase (10-K FY2025 page:140; 10-Q Q2 2026 page:18). Mechanic: leverage is set by the pace of deals as well as earnings; LTM earnings include acquired businesses only from their closing dates, so reported leverage lags pro forma leverage while integration and transaction costs recur as add-backs. Implication: leverage tolerance depends on whether acquired earnings arrive on plan and whether further deals are debt-funded; the Walker funding is the next test. The Seemann consideration conflict counters any precise transaction-value statement.
3. Backlog visibility. Evidence: backlog is now described as including purchase orders, long term agreements and authorizations to proceed, and "Backlog was previously referred to as funded backlog" (10-Q Q2 2026 page:33). Mechanic: a broader backlog definition raises reported coverage without raising contracted, funded revenue, and government customers can terminate or not exercise options (10-K FY2025 page:35). Implication: use remaining performance obligations for revenue coverage and treat backlog as an attributed indicator.
4. Structure and ownership. Evidence: the company is a holding company whose sole asset is the stock of subsidiaries (10-K FY2025 page:3), and guarantors and security are not stated in the notes. Mechanic: cash and claims sit in subsidiaries, so structural position of the lenders depends on the guarantee and security package. Implication: committee readiness on recovery mechanics is limited until the credit agreement is read; no recovery conclusion is drawn here.

Effect: backlog across the FY2025 and 2026 filings is not like-for-like even though the company says historical amounts were not changed.
