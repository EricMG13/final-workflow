### Credit view

Karman is a leveraged but liquid defense and space supplier whose credit profile is weakening as acquisition debt outpaces cash generation. Its strongest support is demand-led earnings growth, with LTM Adjusted EBITDA of USD 179,068 thousand, USD 201.741 million of liquidity at June 30, 2026 and no scheduled maturity beyond about USD 5.050 million a year until the term loan matures on April 1, 2032. The weakest point is cash conversion: operating cash flow was USD (22,119) thousand in FY2025 and USD (2,975) thousand in the six months to June 30, 2026, while net leverage rose from 3.25x to 4.00x of Adjusted EBITDA. What decides the outcome is whether acquired earnings and working-capital releases reduce leverage before the pending Walker acquisition, priced at about USD 94.0 million and not yet shown to be funded, draws on liquidity or adds debt.

### Key findings

Net leverage has risen with each acquisition. The issuer's total debt principal was USD 768,478 thousand at June 30, 2026 against USD 506,767 thousand at December 31, 2025, almost all of it the term loan upsized to fund the Seemann acquisition, so net debt over LTM Adjusted EBITDA rose from 3.25x to 4.00x and, with finance lease liabilities that sit outside notes payable and whose costs fall below EBITDA, from 3.81x to 4.57x. Because LTM earnings include acquired businesses only from closing, the ratio probably overstates pro forma leverage, but the sources do not allow that to be measured. On unadjusted EBITDA the same net ratio is 4.54x, so the add-backs of USD 21,150 thousand (11.8% of LTM Adjusted EBITDA) matter to how leveraged the issuer looks. [Analyst Interpretation, based on CP-1 leverage and the CP-1A acquisition timeline.]

The issuer does not yet generate free cash. Operating cash flow was USD (22,119) thousand in FY2025 and USD (2,975) thousand in the six months to June 30, 2026, and operating cash flow less capex was USD (27,671) thousand on an LTM basis, as contract assets rose from USD 156,298 thousand to USD 187,120 thousand. Interest, capex and acquisitions were therefore paid from new debt and, in 2025, the IPO. The mechanism is revenue recognized over time ahead of billing, so growth consumes cash until contracts bill and collect. [Documentary Fact for the figures; Analyst Interpretation for the mechanism.]

Liquidity and maturities are the main support. Cash of USD 51.741 million plus an undrawn revolver of USD 150.0 million gave USD 201.741 million at June 30, 2026, scheduled amortization is about USD 5.050 million a year with USD 481.517 million shown as due thereafter in the December 31, 2025 schedule, the term loan matures on April 1, 2032 and the revolver on April 1, 2030. The one financial covenant is springing at 6.50x on a first lien net leverage basis and applies only when the revolver is drawn beyond a threshold the sources do not state, so lenders have no maintenance test while it is undrawn. [Documentary Fact.]

Customer and counterparty concentration is the main business risk. The three largest customers were approximately 51.5% of FY2025 revenue and no single program exceeded 12%, so revenue depends on a few primes even though the program base is broad. The filer's claim of sole or single source positions is management language and is not independently verified. [Documentary Fact for the percentages; Management Language for the positions.]

Reported information is less reliable than a clean filer's. Material weaknesses in internal control were reported as not remediated at June 30, 2026, Seemann purchase accounting was revised in the second quarter and judged immaterial, and the filings conflict on the Seemann consideration (cash of USD 210.0 million in the annual report against approximately USD 215.9 million cash and USD 232.9 million total consideration later), the term loan size (USD 767.8 million against USD 772 million) and end-market revenue shares. Each is logged and not reconciled. [Documentary Fact.]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths.

Demand and positioning. The issuer supplies payload protection, interstage and propulsion systems to US missile, hypersonic, launch and maritime programs, and management says it often holds sole or single source positions. The mechanism is that qualified suppliers are rarely requalified, which supports revenue durability, but the claim is management's. [Management Language, CP-1A company and operating model rows.]

Earnings scale and margin. LTM revenue was USD 589,552 thousand and LTM Adjusted EBITDA USD 179,068 thousand, a margin of 30.4%, with gross margin of 41.7% LTM against 38.3% in FY2024. Higher earnings are what reduces leverage if debt stays flat. [Documentary Fact, CP-1.]

Liquidity and maturity profile. Liquidity of USD 201.741 million at June 30, 2026, an undrawn revolver of USD 150.0 million, and a current portion of notes of USD 5.610 million with the term loan due April 1, 2032 mean no refinancing event is near. [Documentary Fact, CP-1.]

Program breadth. No single program was more than 12% of revenue, which limits single-program loss risk even with customer concentration. [Documentary Fact, CP-1A.]

Interest burden covered. LTM Adjusted EBITDA covered cash interest paid 3.37x, 3.24x in FY2025, and the August 3, 2026 repricing lowers future cash interest. [Documentary Fact for the ratios; Analyst Interpretation for the direction of future interest.]

Weaknesses.

Leverage drift. Net debt over Adjusted EBITDA rose from 3.25x to 4.00x and total debt over Adjusted EBITDA from 3.49x to 4.29x, because debt grew faster than LTM earnings. The mechanism is debt-funded acquisitions whose earnings enter LTM only from closing. [Documentary Fact for the ratios; Analyst Interpretation for the cause.]

Cash conversion. Negative operating cash flow and negative free cash flow after capex, with working capital absorbing cash as contract assets grow. Cover falls to 2.74x after deducting LTM capex, and 2.97x on unadjusted EBITDA. [Documentary Fact, CP-1.]

Concentration. The three largest customers were approximately 51.5% of FY2025 revenue and one supplier was 23.8% of accounts payable at December 31, 2025. A resourcing, funding or payment change at one prime would hit revenue and receivables together. [Documentary Fact, CP-1A.]

Acquisition dependence and add-backs. Four acquisitions closed between April 2025 and February 2026 and a fifth is pending; transaction, integration and lender fees recur as add-backs of USD 21,150 thousand LTM. The mechanism is that EBITDA quality depends on a recurring set of items. [Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation.]

Reporting reliability and thin lender protection in the sources. Unremediated material weaknesses, revised purchase accounting, five conflicts, an undisclosed owner base and no supplied credit agreement mean lender protections, guarantors and security cannot be confirmed. Missing evidence here is not an adverse conclusion; it limits confidence. [Insufficient Information for ownership and covenant terms.]

Floating-rate exposure. The term loan is priced at SOFR plus 2.25% from August 3, 2026, and no interest rate hedge was found in the sources searched, so cash interest moves with rates. [Documentary Fact; absence of hedges is Insufficient Information.]

## Calculations

All inputs are from the financials handoff (USD thousands unless stated) or the business handoff.

1. Change in leverage on Adjusted EBITDA, FY2025 to June 30, 2026: net 4.00x - 3.25x = 0.75x; total 4.29x - 3.49x = 0.80x; lease-inclusive net 4.57x - 3.81x = 0.76x.
2. Debt versus earnings growth: total debt principal 768,478 - 506,767 = 261,711, or 261,711 / 506,767 = 51.6%; LTM Adjusted EBITDA 179,068 against FY2025 145,302 gives (179,068 - 145,302) / 145,302 = 23.2%. Debt grew faster than earnings on these bases, consistent with leverage rising.
3. Rate sensitivity: term note principal 763,962 x 1% = 7,640 per year of additional interest for a one percent rise in the floating rate (an analyst calculation, not a company figure); against LTM cash interest paid of 53,144 this is 7,640 / 53,144 = 14.4%.
4. Interest cover after the August 2026 repricing is not calculated: the company's expected annual saving is described as approximately USD 4 million and a post-amendment cash interest run rate is Not supplied.
5. Liquidity use if Walker were funded entirely from cash and revolver: 94.0 / 201.741 = 46.6%, as in the financials handoff; the funding source is Not supplied.
