### Credit view

Magnera's creditors hold a leveraged but adequately liquid credit that is improving slowly and is not yet proven. Its strongest support is a long runway: the debt stack of USD 1,966 million has no maturity before October 2029, cash is USD 280 million, a USD 350 million revolver commitment is undrawn, and the issuer repaid USD 65 million in nine months without distributions. Its weakest point is the quality of the earnings and cash flow behind a 5.28x gross leverage figure: add-backs are USD 122 million of USD 372 million LTM adjusted EBITDA, operating income covers net interest only 0.42x, nine-month free cash flow was USD 32 million, and reporting controls are ineffective. The outcome turns on fourth-quarter cash delivery, on refinancing USD 500 million in 2029 and USD 1,466 million in 2031, and on revolver and covenant terms that are not yet visible.

### Key findings

First, leverage is improving on filed figures but is sensitive to the earnings definition. Magnera's gross debt was USD 1,966 million at June 27, 2026 against LTM adjusted EBITDA of USD 372 million, giving 5.28x gross and 4.53x net, down from 5.74x and 4.88x at September 27, 2025 (Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 Chart data: leverage). The reduction came from higher LTM adjusted EBITDA and USD 65 million of repayments, with earnings contributing somewhat more. Operating income plus depreciation and amortization was USD 250 million LTM, so leverage would be 7.86x gross on that basis; add-backs of USD 122 million are about one third of adjusted EBITDA (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 Financials and credit metrics). The mechanism is that every quarter carries restructuring and integration add-backs, so creditors should treat the adjusted figure as the issuer's own measure and rely on the unadjusted figure for downside testing.

Second, coverage is adequate only on the adjusted measure. Magnera's LTM adjusted EBITDA covers a cash-interest proxy 2.91x, but LTM operating income of USD 63 million covers net interest expense of USD 151 million only 0.42x, and the issuer reported a net loss in every period shown, USD 112 million LTM (Documentary Fact and calculation; CP-1 Chart data: interest cover). The cash-interest figure for interim periods is a proxy, not a reported amount. The implication is that interest is paid from depreciation and non-cash charges rather than from operating profit, so any EBITDA shortfall reaches liquidity quickly.

Third, cash conversion is the weak link in the near term. Magnera generated USD 32 million of free cash flow in nine months of fiscal 2026 and a negative USD 28 million in the third quarter, as working capital absorbed USD 54 million (calculated by difference in CP-1). Management guides to USD 90 million to USD 110 million for the year, which requires USD 58 million to USD 78 million in the fourth quarter (Management Language for the guidance; Analyst Interpretation for the requirement). Raw-material inventory rose in the third quarter and the price-cost spread helped earnings, so cash and margin both depend on how fast cost changes pass through (CP-1A Company and operating model, Pricing and pass-through). The implication is that deleveraging pace in the next two quarters is a function of working capital release, not of earnings growth.

Fourth, liquidity and maturities favour creditors, with one unverified element. Magnera had USD 280 million of cash, about 58% of it outside the U.S., plus a USD 350 million asset-based revolver commitment with no borrowings, a combined USD 630 million before any borrowing base or letters of credit (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Chart data: liquidity). The revolver's availability, drawn letters of credit and covenants are Not supplied (Insufficient Information). The term loan and the notes carry no financial maintenance covenant, which lowers the chance of a covenant-driven default (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Instruments, liquidity build and covenants). The implication is that near-term default risk is low on the information supplied, and the question moves to refinancing.

Fifth, refinancing risk is concentrated. The 4.75% notes of USD 500 million mature in October 2029, the revolver in November 2029, and the term loan of USD 706 million and 7.25% notes of USD 760 million in November 2031 (CP-1 Chart data: debt maturities). A quarter of debt falls due in 2029 and three quarters in 2031 (calc). Management says it intends to refinance prior to maturity, and the fair value of the debt sat USD 48 million below book at June 27, 2026 against USD 136 million at March 28, 2026 (CP-1 Interpretation). The mechanism is that a weak operating year in fiscal 2027 or 2028 would meet the 2029 maturity wall with leverage still above five times gross, narrowing refinancing options; the implication is a monitoring posture focused on deleveraging progress and market access ahead of 2028.

Sixth, the business gives limited revenue visibility. The top ten customers were about 42% of FY2025 net sales, the largest about 14%, and many customers have no long-term arrangements (Documentary Fact; CP-1A Company and operating model, Customers and Contract tenor). Organic volume fell 1% in nine months while currency and the merger lifted reported sales (CP-1A Volume and price drivers). The implication is that revenue can move quickly with account decisions, so creditors rely on cost pass-through and the Americas segment, which earns about two thirds of adjusted EBITDA, rather than on contracted revenue.

Seventh, reporting reliability and related-party dependence are open items. Material weaknesses and ineffective disclosure controls persist at June 27, 2026, and legacy IT still runs under a transition services agreement with the former parent (Documentary Fact; CP-1A Ownership and issuer-level governance behaviour). The implication is that interim figures carry control risk and that remediation status at the fiscal 2026 annual report is a monitoring trigger; the terms of the agreement are Not supplied.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. A long maturity runway is the first strength: Magnera has no debt maturing before October 2029, and its term loan and notes carry no financial maintenance covenant (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Chart data: debt maturities and covenants). Liquidity is the second: USD 280 million of cash and a USD 350 million undrawn revolver commitment total USD 630 million, with no facility expiring within twelve months (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Chart data: liquidity), though availability is unconfirmed. Deleveraging behaviour is the third: gross leverage fell from 5.74x to 5.28x, USD 65 million of debt was repaid in nine months and the equity statements show no dividends or buybacks (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Chart data: leverage and CP-1A Ownership and issuer-level governance behaviour). Rate exposure is the fourth: USD 1,260 million, about 64%, of debt is fixed-rate notes, and the filing puts a quarter-point move in floating rates at about USD 2 million of annual interest (Analyst Interpretation, calculated from the debt table; CP-1 Instruments). Operating scale and diversity are the fifth: 45 manufacturing facilities across two segments and consumer-oriented end markets, with the Americas earning a higher margin (Documentary Fact and Management Language for the stability description; CP-1A Company and operating model).

Weaknesses. Earnings quality is the first weakness: add-backs of USD 122 million LTM, recurring restructuring and integration charges, and unadjusted leverage of 7.86x gross (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1 Financials and credit metrics). Interest burden is the second: net interest expense of USD 151 million LTM against operating income of USD 63 million and a net loss every period (Documentary Fact and calculation; CP-1). Cash flow is the third: free cash flow of USD 32 million for nine months against guidance requiring a strong fourth quarter, and unquantified receivable programmes that sit outside reported debt (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Claims outside the debt figure). Customer concentration and short contract tenor are the fourth (Documentary Fact; CP-1A). Reporting controls and dependence on the former parent's systems are the fifth (Documentary Fact; CP-1A). Claims outside debt are the sixth: operating leases of USD 60 million, a cross-currency swap liability of USD 78 million, Brazilian tax claims of up to USD 66 million, an environmental liability of USD 17 million against a USD 9 million escrow asset and an unfunded pension obligation of USD 27 million at September 27, 2025 (Documentary Fact; CP-1 Claims outside the debt figure; treating all as debt would lift gross leverage to about 5.95x, calc). Equity quality is the seventh: book equity of USD 1,017 million is mostly goodwill and intangibles of USD 858 million, leaving tangible equity of about USD 159 million (Analyst Interpretation, calculated from the balance sheet in CP-1).
