### Business and transaction view

The supplied sources show a mid-sized, publicly listed manufacturer of nonwoven and film-based specialty materials for consumer-oriented end markets, formed in a combination in which a carve-out of the former parent's health, hygiene and specialties business merged with Glatfelter. The structure matters to a creditor in three ways. First, the combination was financed with new first priority secured debt and a cash distribution to the former parent, so opening leverage was set by the transaction rather than by operations; since closing the documented capital allocation is voluntary debt repayment, with no dividends or buybacks in the equity statements. Second, the integration is incomplete: the issuer reports material weaknesses tied to the merger and still depends on the former parent's legacy IT systems under a transition services agreement, so the reporting base is exposed to a counterparty that is also the former owner. Third, the operating model combines high input-cost pass-through with lag, a large customer concentration (top ten about 42 percent of FY2025 net sales) and a geographic mix in which Rest of World, the lower-margin segment, is the largest region in the nine-month geographic table. No sponsor, controlling holder or related-party financing is documented in the supplied text; that is a gap in the evidence, not a finding of absence. The view is bounded by what is not supplied: the merger agreements, the proxy statement, the indentures and credit agreements, and any source dated after August 6, 2026.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transaction that created the issuer closed on November 4, 2024: Treasure Holdco, Inc., a wholly owned subsidiary of Berry Global Group, Inc., merged with Glatfelter Corporation, which concurrently changed its name to Magnera Corporation [10-K_2025-09-27 page:5] "completed its merger (the “Transaction”) with the Glatfelter Corporation". The agreement is listed in the exhibit index as an RMT transaction agreement dated February 6, 2024 [10-K_2025-09-27 page:45] "2.1† RMT Transaction Agreement" (the agreement itself is not supplied; the label is the issuer's). Pre-transaction Treasure shareholders received shares representing 90 percent of the combined company and Glatfelter shareholders retained 10 percent [10-K_2025-09-27 page:5] "pre-Transaction Treasure shareholders received shares of Magnera representing 90% of the combined company". Treasure was the accounting acquirer, so pre-closing history is the carve-out of the former parent's business and the Glatfelter business is consolidated from the closing date. The purchase consideration for the Glatfelter equity was recorded at 74, with goodwill of 36 on the transaction [10-K_2025-09-27 page:32] "Fair value of GLT common stock concurrent with closing $ 74" and "Goodwill 36", and Glatfelter debt of 869 was assumed in the allocation [10-K_2025-09-27 page:32] "Debt (869 )". The Glatfelter contribution to FY2025 net sales was 1,145 [10-K_2025-09-27 page:32] "The net sales for the year ended September 27, 2025 included $1,145 million from GLT." and unaudited pro forma FY2025 net sales and net loss including Glatfelter for the full year were 3,316 and (165) [10-K_2025-09-27 page:32] "unaudited pro forma net sales and net loss were $3,316 million and $(165) million".

New debt raised at closing. The issuer consummated a 785 term loan due 2031, an 800 issuance of 7.25% first priority senior secured notes due 2031 and a 350 revolving credit facility [10-K_2025-09-27 page:34] "As part of the Transaction, the Company consummated a $785 million Term Loan due 2031". Use of proceeds: "The proceeds from the Term Loan and 7.25% Notes were used to retire a portion of GLT outstanding debt and fund a cash distribution to Berry." [10-K_2025-09-27 page:34]. The distribution to Berry was 1,111 [10-K_2025-09-27 page:25] "Cash distribution to Berry (1,111 ) — —", against proceeds from long-term borrowings of 1,556 in the cash flow statement, which is 29 below the 1,585 face amount of the term loan and notes (calc); the supplied text does not explain the 29. Berry also transferred its health, hygiene and specialties global nonwovens and films business to Treasure as part of the closing [10-K_2025-09-27 page:26] "Berry received a cash distribution and, in turn, transferred". A prepayment penalty of 15 was charged for retiring debt concurrently with the transaction [10-K_2025-09-27 page:16] "a $15 million prepayment penalty charge for retiring debt concurrently with the Transaction". The retained Glatfelter 4.75% first priority senior secured notes due October 2029 remain outstanding at 500 [10-K_2025-09-27 page:34] "retained GLT 4.75 % First Priority Senior Secured Notes"; the indentures and a first supplemental indenture are exhibits but not supplied, so issuer and co-issuer entities, guarantors and collateral are not documented here beyond the names in the exhibit index [10-K_2025-09-27 page:46] "4.1 Indenture, by and between Treasure Escrow Corporation".

Liquidity and status. The transaction brought cash of 37 into the group (acquired cash), and cash was 305 at September 27, 2025 and 280 at June 27, 2026 (CP-1). The transaction is complete; the 10-K presents the final allocation [10-K_2025-09-27 page:32] "The following table summarizes the final purchase price allocation:" and describes the measurement period adjustments. There was no new debt issuance in the first nine months of FY2026; the only financing flows are repayments of 65. Subsequent events: no new issuance or refinancing is reported in any supplied source dated after the closing, and nothing is supplied after August 6, 2026. Management says it intends to refinance long-term debt prior to maturity [10-Q_2026-06-27 page:19] "which we intend to refinance prior to maturity"; no refinancing is documented. Management's pro forma adjusted EBITDA includes a synergy add-on that fell across the three releases: [EX-99.1_2026-02-05 page:6] "Synergies and cost reductions 60", [EX-99.1_2026-05-07 page:6] "Synergies and cost reductions 48" and [EX-99.1_2026-08-06 page:6] "Synergies and cost reductions 35". These are attributed management figures, not filed results. The pro forma total moved from 423 to 412 to 405 [EX-99.1_2026-02-05 page:6] "Comparable Adjusted EBITDA (1)(6) $ 423", [EX-99.1_2026-05-07 page:6] "PF Adjusted EBITDA $ 412" and [EX-99.1_2026-08-06 page:6] "PF Adjusted EBITDA $ 405", while the filed LTM adjusted EBITDA built in CP-1 rose from 363 to 364 to 372; the direction of the two series differs because the synergy add-on shrank faster than LTM earnings grew. ### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: no long-term arrangements with many customers [10-K_2025-09-27 page:8] "since we do not have long-term arrangements with many of our customers" and a top-ten share of about 42 percent [10-K_2025-09-27 page:27] "our top ten customers represented approximately 42% of net sales". Risk mechanic: short-tenor demand and concentrated accounts reduce revenue visibility; the filing's own statement that customers can shift suppliers quickly links competition to volume. Credit implication: debt service cannot lean on contracted revenue, so leverage tolerance should be lower than for contract-backed businesses, and monitoring should watch organic volume (negative at nine months) and any top-account loss. Counter: the consumer-oriented end markets are management's description of stability and the Americas volume rose 1 percent at nine months.
2. Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: input costs are polymer resin and pulps passed on with lag; a favourable price-cost spread of 11 in Q3 helped, while selling prices fell by 117 at nine months. Risk mechanic: in rising-cost periods the lag compresses margin and absorbs working capital (raw-material inventory rose in Q3, CP-1 shows free cash flow of (28) in Q3). Credit implication: cash conversion is the weak link in the near term; fourth-quarter delivery of the free cash flow guidance range is the monitoring point. Counter: management reports that Project CORE and synergy benefits are being realised [EX-99.1_2026-08-06 page:2] "realized benefits from Project CORE and merger synergies".
3. Geographic and currency risk. Evidence: Rest of World is about 45 percent of nine-month net sales, 58 percent of cash is outside the U.S. (CP-1) and unremitted foreign earnings were 336 at fiscal 2025 year end [10-K_2025-09-27 page:18] "Our unremitted foreign earnings were $336 million at the end of fiscal 2025." Risk mechanic: dollar debt is serviced from cash flows generated partly in euro, sterling and Brazilian real; repatriation is described by management as without significant restriction but the tax cost is not estimated. Credit implication: net debt that nets all cash overstates readily available liquidity at the U.S. borrower level, and translation moves reported leverage. Counter: the euro cross-currency swaps hedge part of the net investment.
4. Transaction and integration risk. Evidence: material weaknesses tied to the merger, reliance on legacy Berry IT under a transition services agreement, restructuring and integration charges recurring each quarter (CP-1). Risk mechanic: integration is incomplete and a former parent provides services; a failure of control or service continuity could delay reporting or disrupt order-to-cash. Credit implication: reporting reliability is a standing limitation and the monitoring posture should include remediation status at the FY2026 annual report. Counter: the auditor's opinion on the FY2025 financial statements was unqualified and management states there were no material errors.
5. Sponsor and shareholder alignment. Evidence: no sponsor, no dividends or buybacks through June 27, 2026, voluntary debt repayment. Risk mechanic: absent a controlling holder, distribution policy is set by a public-company board; the one large distribution (1,111) occurred at closing to the former parent. Credit implication: documented willingness points to deleveraging, but legal capacity to distribute, incur debt or prime depends on covenant baskets that are Not supplied, so no alignment conclusion beyond the documented actions is drawn.
6. Key uncertainties. The identity and rights of the holders of the 90 percent, the terms of the transition services agreement, the revolver's covenants and availability, the receivable programme size, and anything after August 6, 2026. Credit implication: each limits a committee view of control, liquidity or refinancing; none is treated as adverse by itself.
