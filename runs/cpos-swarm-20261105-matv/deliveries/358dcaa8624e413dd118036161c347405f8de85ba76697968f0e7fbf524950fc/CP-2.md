### Credit view

Mativ Holdings, Inc. is a leveraged but liquid specialty materials credit whose fundamental strength is adequate and improving, not yet comfortable. Its strongest support is a refinanced capital structure with no material maturity before the USD 400.0 million 8.000% notes due October 1, 2029, USD 373.5 million of liquidity and compliance with a net leverage covenant that stands at 3.8x against a 5.00x maximum. Its weakest point is thin earnings coverage: on reported earnings net debt is 4.19x EBITDA and EBITDA covers cash interest only 2.62x, while the largest segment by revenue, Sustainable & Adhesive Solutions, earns the lower gross margin and lost volume in the third quarter of 2026. The outcome turns on whether price-led margin recovery and cost savings hold as volume weakens, and on refinancing the 2029 notes.

### Key findings

First, the refinancing changed the shape of the debt more than its size. On April 3, 2026 Mativ replaced its revolver, Term Loan A and Term Loan B and removed the delayed draw term loan, which took away the May 2027 maturity wall (about USD 514.2 million at year-end 2025) [CP-1 debt register; CP-1A transaction summary]. Total debt carrying value is USD 976.4 million and net debt USD 879.1 million, against USD 1,018.2 million and USD 934.0 million at December 31, 2025. The mechanic is that the nearest large repayment is now the notes, which with small amortization make up USD 406.2 million of the USD 1,011.1 million of principal due in 2029 on the maturity table, about 40.2 percent [CP-1 debt maturities chart; calculated below]. The implication is low near-term repayment risk and a concentrated 2029 refinancing, with an eight percent coupon already in the cost of debt. One wording in the credit agreement could pull the revolver and Term Loan A (USD 109.7 million drawn and outstanding) ahead of the notes, which would lift the 2029 bucket to about 51.0 percent of principal; the filing's own maturity table does not show this, and the amended agreement is needed to settle it [CP-0 and CP-1 conflicts].

Second, leverage depends on whose earnings are used, and the deleveraging has mostly come from earnings, not debt paydown. Management states net leverage of 3.8x at September 30, 2026 against 5.00x allowed, down from 4.2x at December 31, 2025 [CP-1 covenants]. On reported operating profit plus depreciation and amortization, net debt to EBITDA fell from 5.54x to 4.19x, and holding net debt at the year-end level would have given 4.45x, so about 1.09x of the 1.35x improvement came from higher trailing EBITDA (USD 168.5 million to USD 210.0 million) and about 0.26x from lower net debt [calculated below]. The mechanic is that trailing EBITDA rose partly because restructuring and impairment charges fell (nine months 2026 USD 4.7 million against USD 18.2 million) and partly on margin recovery; both can reverse. The implication is that leverage headroom today is comfortable (EBITDA could fall about 24.0 percent before 5.00x at constant net debt) but narrows toward the final 4.00x step, where the same measure gives about 5.0 percent, and the step dates are not disclosed [CP-1 covenants].

Third, interest cover is thin on reported earnings. EBITDA covers cash interest 2.62x over the last twelve months and interest expense 2.92x, only 0.12x above the lowest covenant level of 2.50x and below the 3.00x top level, although covenant EBITDA and interest are defined differently and the reported coverage ratio is not disclosed [CP-1 interest cover chart; CP-1 covenants]. Management's own nine-month adjusted EBITDA over cash interest is 3.52x. The mechanic is a fixed interest burden of about USD 80.1 million of cash interest on earnings that move with volume; the implication is limited cushion if the third-quarter volume decline persists.

Fourth, the earnings mix is shifting toward the higher-margin segment while the larger segment weakens. Filtration & Advanced Materials grew net sales 10.2 percent in Q3 2026 with a gross margin of 23.6 percent, while Sustainable & Adhesive Solutions fell 10.9 percent at a gross margin of 16.4 percent, and adjusted EBITDA of USD 62.3 million fell about 7 percent against USD 66.8 million a year earlier [CP-1 segments; CP-1A operating model]. Price added 3.7 percent to Q3 sales while volume and mix removed 5.9 percent, so margin recovery has come from pricing, not volume. The mechanic is dependence on input-cost pass-through and demand in cyclical end markets, with Healthcare weak. The implication is that a further volume loss would flow disproportionately into earnings and cover.

Fifth, liquidity is adequate but less deep than before and partly dependent on off-balance-sheet funding. Liquidity of USD 373.5 million is cash of USD 97.3 million (USD 72.7 million held abroad) plus printed undrawn revolver capacity of USD 276.2 million, down from USD 498.5 million in March 2026 because the revolver was reduced to USD 305.0 million; USD 8.8 million of commitment is unexplained [CP-1 liquidity]. Trailing free cash flow of about USD 98.5 million after USD 22.5 million of dividends leaves about USD 76.0 million a year, so if that were held flat to October 2029 it would retire about 57 percent of the USD 400.0 million notes [calculated below, assumption stated]. The company sold USD 837.3 million of receivables in nine months 2026 under a program extended to October 23, 2029, and carries leases, pensions and a foreign exchange contract liability outside reported debt [CP-1 claims outside debt]. The implication is that the notes will need refinancing and that withdrawal of the receivables program would need replacement from the revolver.

Sixth, the July 27, 2026 tornado left USD 38.0 million of insurance recoveries receivable (about 39 percent of cash) and a net recorded loss of USD 0.8 million; business interruption recoveries are not recognized. The mechanic is delayed cash on an insured loss and a single third-party distribution node that interrupted supply; the implication is a near-term liquidity watch item, with management expecting full recovery of the inventory loss [CP-1 events; CP-1A operating model, Management Language].

Seventh, creditor protection cannot be read directly. The maintenance covenants are stated to apply to the revolver and Term Loan A only, so the USD 498.7 million Term Loan B carries none; the credit agreement, indenture and receivables agreement are not in the evidence, so baskets, guarantor coverage, security after the amendment and distribution capacity are unknown. Documented actions show no related-party, priming or non-pro rata transactions and a smaller dividend than in 2023 [CP-1A governance]. The implication is a supportive willingness record but an untested legal capacity.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The business is diversified: no product category exceeds 35 percent of sales and no customer exceeds 10 percent, which supports revenue durability through cycles [CP-1A operating model; Documentary Fact]. The capital structure is refinanced, with the nearest large maturity in October 2029, covenant compliance stated at September 30, 2026 and liquidity of USD 373.5 million [CP-1 liquidity and debt register; Documentary Fact]. Cash generation covers capital spending and the dividend: nine-month free cash flow was USD 90.5 million (a management measure) against dividends of USD 17.0 million [CP-1 cash flow; Documentary Fact]. Filtration & Advanced Materials is gaining profitability, with adjusted EBITDA of USD 39.6 million in Q3 2026 against USD 32.9 million, and management targets further cost savings of USD 15.0 million to USD 20.0 million by end 2026 [CP-1 segments; CP-1A operating model; Documentary Fact for the first, Management Language for the target]. About two thirds of floating bank debt is hedged with interest rate swaps (USD 405.8 million notional against USD 608.4 million of floating balances), limiting rate sensitivity [CP-1A operating model; Analyst Interpretation on the ratio]. Reporting quality is sound, with effective internal control and no related-party or priming-type transaction found [CP-1A governance; Documentary Fact].

Weaknesses. Reported leverage and cover are tight: net debt to EBITDA of 4.19x and EBITDA to cash interest of 2.62x on reported earnings, with total debt to capital at 67.6 percent [CP-1 leverage and covenants; Documentary Fact for inputs, Analyst Interpretation for the reading]. Margins are low and recovered through price while volume and mix fell, with the larger segment, Sustainable & Adhesive Solutions, at a lower gross margin and showing a 10.9 percent sales decline in Q3 2026 [CP-1A operating model; Documentary Fact]. Refinancing is back-ended: the USD 400.0 million notes at 8.000% fall due on October 1, 2029, and a possible earlier maturity of the revolver and Term Loan A is unresolved [CP-1 debt maturities; Documentary Fact and Insufficient Information]. Funding relies on a receivables program that is off balance sheet and on claims outside reported debt, including operating leases of USD 56.2 million, pension and other postretirement liabilities of USD 49.1 million and a foreign exchange contract liability of USD 27.6 million [CP-1 claims outside debt; Documentary Fact]. Operating concentration was demonstrated by the tornado, and Healthcare volume is weak [CP-1A operating model; Documentary Fact].

## Calculations

All amounts USD millions unless stated. Inputs come from the CP-1 and CP-1A handoffs; each calculation is repeated here so it can be recomputed. The reported-basis EBITDA of 168.5 (FY2025) and 210.0 (twelve months to September 30, 2026) is the CP-1 construction: operating profit plus depreciation and amortization, with the FY2025 goodwill impairment of 411.9 added back.

- Deleveraging attribution: net debt to EBITDA at FY2025 is 934.0 / 168.5 = 5.54x; at Q3 2026 it is 879.1 / 210.0 = 4.19x; improvement 5.54 - 4.19 = 1.35x. Holding net debt at 934.0 and using the Q3 2026 EBITDA gives 934.0 / 210.0 = 4.45x. The EBITDA effect is 5.54 - 4.45 = 1.09x (from EBITDA rising by (210.0 - 168.5) / 168.5 = 24.6 percent); the net debt effect is 4.45 - 4.19 = 0.26x (net debt down 934.0 - 879.1 = 54.9, or 5.9 percent). The attribution depends on the FY2025 base, which carried 19.9 of restructuring and other impairment charges.
- Principal concentration in 2029: notes 400.0 / principal 1,011.1 = 39.6 percent; 2029 bucket on the table 406.2 / 1,011.1 = 40.2 percent. If revolver 20.0 and Term Loan A 89.7 (sum 109.7) matured in 2029, the bucket is 406.2 + 109.7 = 515.9, or 515.9 / 1,011.1 = 51.0 percent. Inputs from the debt register and maturity table (CP-1).
- Free cash flow capacity (assumption: trailing figures held flat, no growth, no working-capital swing): trailing management-basis free cash flow 98.5 less trailing dividends 22.5 = 76.0 a year; over the 3.0 years from September 30, 2026 to October 1, 2029 that is 76.0 x 3 = 228.0, or 228.0 / 400.0 = 57 percent of the notes. This is an illustrative projection, not a forecast; free cash flow benefited from a working-capital inflow and a tax refund (CP-1).
- Free cash flow to debt: 98.5 / 976.4 = 10.1 percent.
- Interest cover headroom over the covenant floor: reported-basis cover 2.62x less 2.50x = 0.12x, equal to an EBITDA fall of 1 - 2.50 / 2.62 = 4.6 percent at constant interest; this is a proxy only because the covenant uses different definitions.
- Covenant EBITDA cushion at constant net debt (CP-1): 1 - 3.8 / 5.00 = 24.0 percent; 1 - 3.8 / 4.00 = 5.0 percent.
- Insurance receivable relative to cash: 38.0 / 97.3 = 39.1 percent.
- Adjusted EBITDA change in Q3 2026: (62.3 - 66.8) / 66.8 = -6.7 percent (the release prints "down 7%").
