### Credit view

MAXIMUS, INC. is a moderately levered, covenant-compliant government services credit whose weak point is seasonal, customer-driven cash flow rather than balance sheet leverage. Its strongest support is a USD 15,327 million backlog at September 30, 2025, a reported covenant leverage ratio of 2.00 against a 4.00 maximum, and bank debt that matures between fiscal 2029 and 2031. The weakest point is that net debt to issuer-adjusted EBITDA rose to 2.25x from 1.60x in nine months, nine-month free cash flow was an outflow of USD 208.6 million, cash is USD 57.0 million, and about 60% of revenue comes from ten contracts. What decides the outcome is whether the fourth-quarter collections that guidance requires arrive, and whether management keeps funding buybacks with debt while a major customer's modification runs to December 31, 2026.

### Key findings

1. Leverage is moderate but rising. Total debt principal is USD 1,653.5 million and unrestricted cash USD 57.0 million, so net debt is USD 1,596.6 million, or 2.25x LTM issuer-adjusted EBITDA of USD 711.0 million (2.33x gross), against 1.60x at September 30, 2025. Debt principal rose USD 306.7 million and net debt USD 472.1 million in nine months [CP-1 C5, C11].
2. Covenant headroom is wide on the reported definition. Consolidated net total leverage is 2.00 against a 4.00 maximum, so Credit Agreement EBITDA of USD 798.4 million could fall 50.0% before the test binds, and net interest coverage is 8.72 against a 3.00 minimum, a 65.6% cushion. The Credit Agreement EBITDA exceeds the issuer-adjusted measure by USD 87.4 million of stock compensation and pro forma adjustments, and the agreement and its definitions are not supplied, so the headroom is the issuer's own figure [CP-1 C9; CP-0 gaps].
3. Cash flow is seasonal and delayed. Operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 179.9 million in nine months and USD 469.5 million for the LTM, 66.0% of issuer-adjusted EBITDA. Days sales outstanding were 98 days against 62 because a large federal contract paid late; USD 245 million was collected after June 30, 2026. Full-year guidance implies fourth-quarter free cash flow of USD 633.6 million to USD 683.6 million, against USD 641.8 million delivered in the prior year's fourth quarter [CP-1 C3, C13; CP-1A].
4. Shareholder returns are debt-funded. Nine-month buybacks of USD 204.9 million and dividends of USD 51.4 million, USD 256.4 million together, were met from net borrowing of USD 302.7 million and a USD 165.4 million fall in unrestricted cash, with other cash flows not itemised. Fiscal 2025 buybacks of USD 447.5 million were 6.5 times dividends. Management states a target leverage of 2x to 3x on the covenant definition, so the reported 2.00 sits at the bottom of the range [CP-1 C11; CP-1A governance].
5. Liquidity is adequate but thin in cash. Liquidity is USD 787.0 million: cash USD 57.0 million plus USD 730.0 million of revolver capacity as printed, with letters of credit not stated and the USD 350.0 million receivable-sale facility excluded. Scheduled principal payments are USD 19.0 million for July to September 2026 and USD 75.8 million in fiscal 2027, covered 8.3 times by liquidity; the largest single-year bucket is USD 690.2 million in fiscal 2029 [CP-1 C10, maturities chart].
6. Interest cost is exposed to floating rates. Swaps cover USD 675.0 million, 40.8% of principal; USD 978.5 million pays SOFR plus margin, so 100 basis points adds about USD 9.8 million a year. A USD 75.0 million Term Loan B swap expires in September 2026. The effective rate is 5.6% [CP-1 C12; C1 below].
7. Earnings are down and the outlook was lowered. Nine-month revenue was USD 3,930.0 million, 4.4% lower, and fiscal Q3 revenue fell 5.1%, but issuer-adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 14.0% from 13.2%. In August 2026 adjusted EPS guidance fell to USD 7.90 to USD 8.20 from USD 8.25 to USD 8.55 and free cash flow guidance to USD 425 million to USD 475 million from USD 450 million to USD 500 million, after a customer modification effective July 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026 that management expects to reduce diluted EPS by about USD 0.35 per quarter [CP-1 latest quarter; CP-1A timeline].
8. Revenue is concentrated and terminable. About 55% of fiscal 2025 revenue came from the U.S. federal government, about 60% from the ten largest contracts and about one-fifth from a single federal agency; almost all contracts permit termination for convenience. Backlog of USD 15,327 million is 2.82 times fiscal 2025 revenue but fell 5.2% in the year to September 30, 2025, and no later backlog is supplied [CP-1A operating model, C1, C2].
9. Claims outside debt are modest but unquantified in places. Operating lease liabilities are USD 94.9 million; receivables of USD 1.28 billion were sold to a bank in nine months with the outstanding balance not printed; performance bonds were USD 33.2 million at September 30, 2025; the MOVEit litigation accrual is not stated; no defined benefit pension is described. Goodwill and intangibles exceed equity by USD 517.0 million [CP-1 claims table, C14].
10. Governance is open on the facts that matter to creditors. There is no sponsor and no controlling holder disclosed (30 holders of record, about 151,000 beneficial owners), but the proxy statement, Credit Agreement, guarantors and change-of-control terms are not supplied, and three source conflicts are logged: the fiscal 2025 buyback amount, the federal revenue share definition and the Outside the U.S. margin outlook [CP-1A governance and conflicts].

### Thesis

MAXIMUS, INC. is an essential-services government contractor whose backlog, covenant headroom and long-dated bank facility support moderate leverage, provided fourth-quarter collections arrive, contract concentration does not produce a loss of a top contract, and shareholder returns do not outrun free cash flow.

## Calculations

- **C1, interest sensitivity (inference).** Unhedged principal 419,375 + 539,173 + 20,000 = 978,548 thousand (CP-1 C12); a 100 basis point rise adds 978,548 x 1% = 9,785 thousand, USD 9.8 million a year, which is 11.3% of LTM cash interest paid of 86,279 thousand (9,785 / 86,279). It assumes a full year at the June 30, 2026 balances and ignores the September 2026 swap expiry.
- **C2, liquidity cover of near-term principal.** Liquidity 786,953 thousand (CP-1 C10) / principal due July 2026 to September 2027, 18,952 + 75,809 = 94,761 thousand = 8.3x. Through fiscal 2028: 94,761 + 81,434 = 176,195 thousand, 4.5x.
- **C3, room to the top of management's range (Credit Agreement basis, illustrative).** At 3.00x on Credit Agreement EBITDA of 798,419 thousand, net debt would be 2,395,257 thousand against 1,596,595 thousand today, an increase of 798,662 thousand. At the 4.00 covenant maximum the increase is 1,597,081 thousand (CP-1 C9). This ignores EBITDA change and is not a forecast or a capacity statement.
- **C4, shareholder returns against cash generation.** LTM dividends 68,282 + LTM share purchases 345,958 = 414,240 thousand, 95.6% of LTM free cash flow of 433,230 thousand (CP-1 C1, C3). Nine months: returns 256,356 thousand against free cash flow of −208,615 thousand, a gap of 464,971 thousand; net borrowing of 302,740 and a fall in unrestricted cash of 222,351 − 56,953 = 165,398 together are 468,138 thousand, and other cash flows are not itemised here.
- **C5, capitalization.** Debt principal 1,653,548 / (1,653,548 + equity 1,740,551) = 48.7%. Goodwill plus intangibles of 2,257,574 exceed equity by 517,023 (CP-1 C14).
- **C6, leverage including operating leases (illustrative).** Principal 1,653,548 + operating lease liabilities 94,883 = 1,748,431 thousand; / LTM issuer-adjusted EBITDA 711,009 = 2.46x. Net of unrestricted cash: (1,748,431 − 56,953) = 1,691,478 / 711,009 = 2.38x. Lease expense is inside EBITDA, so this overstates leverage and is shown only as a sensitivity.
- **C7, collections already received against guidance.** Implied fourth-quarter free cash flow 633,615 to 683,615 thousand (CP-1 C13); the 245,000 thousand collected after June 30, 2026 is 38.7% to 35.8% of it (245,000 / 633,615 and 245,000 / 683,615).
