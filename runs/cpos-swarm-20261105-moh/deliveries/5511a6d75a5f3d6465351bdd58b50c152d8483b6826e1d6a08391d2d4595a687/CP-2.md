### Credit view

Molina Healthcare, Inc. is a deteriorating credit with adequate near-term liquidity: debt of USD 3,771 million equals 11.32x EBITDA for the twelve months to September 30, 2026, against 1.54x in FY2024 [CP-1 leverage table]. The strongest support is structure and timing: the debt is parent-level senior unsecured notes, no principal falls due before USD 800 million in June 2028, and the USD 1,250 million revolver is undrawn. The weakest point is earnings: the medical care ratio reached 92.1% in nine months 2026 against 90.8%, leaving LTM EBITDA of USD 333 million against interest expense of USD 213 million. The outcome depends on whether Medicaid rate updates restore margins before the interest coverage floor steps up in 2027, which the sources cannot confirm.

### Key findings

The first finding is the speed of the earnings decline. Operating income fell from USD 1,707 million in FY2024 to USD 781 million in FY2025 and to a calculated USD 172 million for the twelve months to September 30, 2026, while debt stayed near USD 3,771 million, so calculated EBITDA of USD 1,893 million, USD 976 million and USD 333 million puts total debt at 1.54x, 3.86x and 11.32x [CP-1 financials table]. The mechanism is a fixed premium rate set against a medical cost trend that outran it: the medical care ratio was 89.1% in FY2024, 91.7% in FY2025 and 92.1% in nine months 2026, and the implied fourth quarter of 2025 was an operating loss of USD 162 million [CP-1 calculations]. Because debt did not fall, each point of margin lost translates directly into higher leverage. On the parent cash basis net debt is 9.50x EBITDA for the same twelve months. This is a calculated, Documentary Fact finding with high confidence in the inputs and medium confidence in the EBITDA definition, since the company reports no EBITDA.

The second finding is that covenant headroom, the question that matters most for creditors, cannot be measured. The credit agreement requires minimum interest coverage, which the February 4, 2026 amendment reduced from 3.0 to 1.0 to 1.75 to 1.00 through December 31, 2026, then 2.00, 2.50 and 2.75 for the March, June and September 2027 quarters, and a maximum debt to capital ratio of 60%; the company states compliance at September 30, 2026 [CP-1 observations]. A simple EBITDA over interest expense measure is 1.56x for the twelve months to September 30, 2026, or 2.00x with the USD 93 million non-cash impairment added back, but the agreement defines the ratio and that definition and the reported ratio are not supplied. The mechanism is that relief was taken in advance and the floor rises as the amendment expires; the implication is that earnings must recover materially during 2027 or relief must be extended, and a simple-basis shortfall is illustrated under Calculations. Confidence is low because the decisive figure is absent.

The third finding is that liquidity is adequate for debt service but sits at the parent. Consolidated cash and cash equivalents were USD 4,479 million at September 30, 2026, yet most of it supports claims and statutory capital at regulated health plans; parent cash and investments were USD 608 million against USD 223 million at December 31, 2025, the USD 1,250 million revolver is undrawn, and no principal falls due before USD 800 million in June 2028 [CP-1 liquidity and maturity tables]. Subsidiary dividends to the parent were USD 985 million in 2025 and the parent received USD 329 million of dividends and return of capital in nine months 2026, against an expected decline in dividends payable without approval [CP-1 observations]. The mechanism is that creditors rely on subsidiary distributions that fall when earnings fall and that the parent must fund statutory capital first, so the revolver is the real backstop; letters of credit drawn on it are not printed, so availability net of letters of credit is unconfirmed.

The fourth finding is the maturity and refinancing profile. The five senior notes total USD 3,800 million in principal: USD 800 million in 2028, USD 650 million in 2030, USD 850 million in 2031, USD 750 million in 2032 and USD 750 million in 2033, at an annual coupon of about USD 191.4 million, or 5.04% of principal. Recent issuance priced at 6.500% in November 2025 and 6.250% in November 2024, so any refinancing at current levels raises cost, while LTM interest expense of USD 213 million already consumes a large share of the USD 333 million of EBITDA. The implication is limited near-term refinancing pressure but little capacity to absorb a further earnings shock before 2028.

The fifth finding concerns business risk. Medicaid is 75% of FY2025 premium revenue and four state contracts (California, New York, Texas and Washington) are about 54% of Medicaid premium revenue [CP-1A operating model]. Medicaid contracts run three to five years and either party may terminate, so re-procurement outcomes and annual rate setting drive revenue, and the Virginia contract loss shows the exposure. Management states that 2026 is the trough year for Medicaid margins and that recent rate updates are strong for 2027, which is Management Language that the results through the third quarter of 2026 do not yet support: the Medicaid medical care ratio was 93.0% in the third quarter against 92.0%. The sixth finding is that near-term earnings carry identified drags: guidance includes a loss of USD 1.50 per diluted share from the new Florida contract and a loss of USD 1.00 per diluted share from the Medicare product to be exited for 2027, and the GAAP guidance floor of USD 122 million net income implies only about USD 10 million for the fourth quarter. The sources conflict on the scale of the Florida contract (about 120,000 enrollees against USD 6 billion of annual premium revenue), so the size of that start-up cost cannot be verified [CP-1A conflicts].

The seventh finding is capital allocation and governance. The company repurchased USD 1,000 million of stock in FY2025, paused repurchases in nine months 2026 and retains USD 500 million of authorization through December 31, 2026, which is an Analyst Interpretation of Neutral or Mixed behaviour: the pause is supportive, but the authorization is a possible call on parent cash. The securities class action was dismissed with prejudice on October 20, 2026 and the derivative dismissal notice was filed on October 6, 2026, which removes an overhang. The only rating evidence is BB from S&P and Ba2 from Moody's as stated in the annual report.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The first strength is a favourable maturity and liquidity structure: no principal is due before USD 800 million in June 2028, the USD 1,250 million revolver is undrawn and runs to November 20, 2030, and parent cash and investments rose to USD 608 million from USD 223 million, so the debt can be serviced for some time even on thin earnings (Documentary Fact, CP-1). The second is an unsecured, single-issuer capital structure at the parent with no sponsor, no creditor-adverse transaction found and no dividend recapitalisation, which limits governance and structural-priming risk (Documentary Fact, CP-1A, with the caveat that indentures are not supplied). The third is an established government-programme franchise with recent state procurement wins and a large Medicaid base; the contract win record is a management claim and is tagged Management Language, but the wins themselves are documented (CP-1A). The fourth is the pause in share repurchases in nine months 2026 and the dismissal of the securities and derivative suits, which reduce competing calls on parent cash and a legal overhang (Documentary Fact, CP-1 and CP-1A). The fifth is that operating cash flow was positive in nine months 2026 at USD 629 million, giving an operating cash flow less capital expenditure figure of USD 535 million, although this reflects settlement timing (Analyst Interpretation, CP-1).

Weaknesses. The first weakness is leverage on collapsed earnings: 11.32x total debt to EBITDA for the twelve months to September 30, 2026 against 1.54x in FY2024, with a simple EBITDA over interest expense of 1.56x (Documentary Fact for inputs, calculated, CP-1). The second is unverifiable covenant headroom against a floor that rises from 1.75 to 1.00 to 2.75 to 1.00 by September 2027 (Insufficient Information on the executed definition and ratio; the relief obtained in advance is an Analyst Interpretation of limited headroom). The third is dependence on subsidiary dividends and on consolidated cash that is largely tied to statutory capital, so the headline net cash position does not measure parent debt-service capacity (Analyst Interpretation, CP-1). The fourth is concentration in Medicaid and in four states, with short and terminable contracts and an unproven margin recovery (Documentary Fact and Management Language, CP-1A). The fifth is settlement-driven cash flow: operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 535 million in FY2025 and an inflow of USD 629 million in nine months 2026, so cash conversion is volatile (Documentary Fact, CP-1). The sixth is the identified 2026 and 2027 earnings drags from the Florida start-up and the Medicare exit, together with policy risk from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, with the Florida contract size in conflict across the sources (Documentary Fact and conflict, CP-1A).
