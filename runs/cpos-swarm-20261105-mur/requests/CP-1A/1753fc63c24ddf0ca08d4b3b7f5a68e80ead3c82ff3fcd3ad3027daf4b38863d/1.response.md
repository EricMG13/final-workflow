### Business and transaction view

MURPHY OIL CORP is an independent, publicly listed oil and gas exploration and production company with no sponsor, no controlling owner identified in the sources and no acquisition or leveraged transaction driving the credit view. The decision drivers are therefore the operating model and recent change, not transaction structure. Evidence: crude oil and condensate is 86.1 percent of nine-month 2026 revenue from production, no commodity derivative contracts were in place at September 30, 2026, and realized prices rose sharply on the year (for example United States onshore crude at 86.48 dollars per barrel in Q3 2026 against 65.48 dollars in Q3 2025) while total hydrocarbon production fell 7 percent in Q3 2026 and 3 percent in nine months 2026, which management attributes mainly to lower Gulf of America volumes (natural decline) and to the timing of new onshore wells (10-Q Q3 page:34-36). Risk mechanic: an oil-weighted, unhedged revenue base converts price moves almost directly into cash flow, so the 2026 earnings recovery is a price result with lower volumes; sustaining production requires continuing capital in decline-prone Gulf of America fields (about half of nine-month revenue is United States offshore) while the company also funds exploration and appraisal in Côte d'Ivoire, Vietnam and the Gulf of America. Credit implication: leverage and liquidity are currently strong (CP-1 handoff), but they depend on commodity prices and on management choosing to keep exploration and shareholder returns within cash flow; the sources show that discretion being exercised conservatively on buybacks and with a refinancing that extended maturities, but they do not show the covenant or indenture limits that would constrain distributions or new debt. Credit-relevant change in the period: the January 2026 refinancing (500.0 million dollars of 6.500% notes due 2034, 227.5 million dollars of 2027 and 2028 notes redeemed, revolver upsized to 2.0 billion dollars and extended to January 2031), the first oil at Lac Da Vang in Vietnam with an 83.9 million dollar finance lease for its floating storage vessel, raised 2026 production guidance, and an October 2026 drillship agreement worth approximately 225 million to 325 million dollars gross. The view is limited by missing ownership percentages, customer concentration and capital allocation plan terms (see Gaps).
