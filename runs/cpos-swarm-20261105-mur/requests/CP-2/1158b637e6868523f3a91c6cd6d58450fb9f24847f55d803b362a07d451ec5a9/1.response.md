### Credit view

MURPHY OIL CORP is a financially conservative but price-exposed oil producer, and its credit is sound today. The strongest support is low leverage (total debt of USD 1,634.4 million is 0.89x calculated last-twelve-months EBITDA of USD 1,837.5 million), liquidity of USD 2,499.9 million and no note maturing before May 2029. The weakest point is that crude oil supplies 86.1 percent of revenue with no commodity derivatives in place, while exploration, development and a new drillship commitment absorb most operating cash flow, leaving little after dividends. What decides the outcome is the oil price and management's willingness to cut discretionary spending and buybacks when it falls; the covenant and indenture limits that would bound that discretion are not in the sources.

### Key findings

First, the company's leverage is low and has fallen even though debt rose. Total debt was USD 1,385.1 million at December 31, 2025 and USD 1,634.4 million at September 30, 2026, an increase of USD 249.3 million from a USD 500.0 million notes issue, a USD 83.9 million finance lease and the repayment of USD 227.5 million of notes and the USD 100.0 million revolver draw, while calculated EBITDA rose from USD 1,394.0 million (FY2025) to USD 1,837.5 million (LTM Q3 2026), so total debt over EBITDA moved from 0.99x to 0.89x [CP-1 Debt stack; Chart data: leverage]. The management-basis cross-check gives 0.92x on adjusted EBITDA attributable to Murphy of USD 1,784.8 million.

Second, the improvement is a price result. Revenue from production rose to USD 2,507.9 million for nine months 2026 from USD 2,076.8 million, with realized prices up about a third and production down 3 percent, and lease operating costs fell on lower Gulf of America workovers, which management itself describes as partly offset by activity elsewhere [CP-1 Financials and credit metrics; CP-1A Company and operating model: Cost structure; Management explanation of costs]. Part of the cost gain may not repeat, and none of the price gain is protected, because crude oil is 86.1 percent of revenue and there were no commodity derivatives at September 30, 2026.

Third, free cash flow is modest. Calculated free cash flow (operating cash flow less property additions and dry hole costs) was USD 184.6 million for nine months 2026, which after USD 150.5 million of dividends and USD 21.2 million of distributions to the minority holder of MP Gulf of Mexico, LLC leaves USD 12.9 million; management's own adjusted free cash flow was negative USD 32.2 million for the same period, excluding the minority share [CP-1 Free cash flow and shareholder distributions]. Cash still rose by USD 123.1 million because operating cash flow exceeded investing outflow by USD 155.9 million, while financing used USD 32.8 million.

Fourth, maturities and liquidity are comfortable. No note matures until May 2029 (USD 117.6 million), followed by USD 600.0 million in October 2032, USD 500.0 million in February 2034 and USD 339.8 million in December 2042; the USD 2.0 billion revolver matures January 2, 2031 and is undrawn apart from USD 0.4 million of letters of credit [CP-1 Chart data: debt maturities; Liquidity]. The 2026 refinancing moved the company from a revolver and shorter notes to this profile.

Fifth, claims outside the debt figure are large. Asset retirement obligations are USD 1,065.4 million, operating lease liabilities USD 653.8 million, and net pension and retiree medical liabilities were USD 195.8 million at December 31, 2025, before commitments of USD 1,648.0 million at that date and a new drillship agreement of about USD 225 million to USD 325 million gross from the first quarter of 2027 [CP-1 Claims outside the debt figure]. Adding the first three to total debt gives USD 3,549.3 million, or 1.93x calculated LTM EBITDA (see Calculations).

Sixth, creditor protections are unverified. The previous revolver's tests were a maximum leverage ratio of 3.25x and a minimum interest coverage ratio of 2.50x before an investment grade ratings date, and a 60 percent maximum debt to total capitalization after it; the amended facility's terms, the indentures and the ratings are not supplied, and which test applies is unknown [CP-1 Covenants and definitions; CP-0 Gaps]. The mechanical distance to the stated levels is wide (see Calculations) but is not covenant headroom.

## Calculations

Inputs are from the CP-1 and CP-1A handoffs unless a source is named; EBITDA means the calculated, consolidated figure defined in the CP-1 handoff (operating income plus DD&A plus impairment).

1. Illustrative oil price sensitivity (arithmetic, not a forecast): nine-month 2026 crude oil and condensate revenue of USD 2,158.6 million (CP-1A, from 10-Q Q3 page:8-9) times 10 percent = USD 215.9 million; divided by nine-month 2026 calculated EBITDA of USD 1,537.3 million = 14.0 percent. It assumes constant volumes, no cost or royalty offset and no tax effect.
2. Mechanical distance to the previous facility's stated levels (not covenant headroom): total debt USD 1,634.4 million divided by 3.25 = USD 502.9 million, so calculated LTM EBITDA of USD 1,837.5 million could fall by 72.6 percent before total debt over EBITDA reached 3.25x; interest paid of USD 86.3 million times 2.50 = USD 215.8 million, so EBITDA could fall by 88.3 percent before EBITDA over interest paid reached 2.50x. The covenant definitions are not supplied.
3. Claims-inclusive leverage (illustrative): total debt USD 1,634.352 million + operating lease liabilities USD 653.756 million (September 30, 2026) + asset retirement obligations USD 1,065.394 million (September 30, 2026) + pension and retiree medical net liabilities USD 195.814 million (USD 132.415 million + USD 63.399 million, December 31, 2025) = USD 3,549.316 million; divided by USD 1,837.474 million = 1.93x. It mixes dates, treats asset retirement obligations as debt-like although they are costs of operations, and excludes commitments and letters of credit. Asset retirement obligations over total debt: 1,065.394 / 1,634.352 = 65.2 percent.
4. Cash walk, nine months 2026 (USD million, 10-Q Q3 page:4-5): operating cash flow 1,517.609 - investing outflow 1,361.686 = 155.923; less financing outflow 32.834, plus exchange effect 0.016 = 123.105, equal to the increase in cash from 377.196 to 500.301.
5. Free cash flow after distributions, nine months 2026 (CP-1): 184.570 - 150.528 - 21.164 = 12.878, shown as USD 12.9 million.
6. Interest cost sensitivity of the 2042 notes coupon: 339.761 million times 0.5 percentage points = USD 1.7 million a year (1,698.8 thousand).
7. Figures carried unchanged from CP-1 and CP-1A, recomputed here as a check: total debt 1,634.352 / EBITDA 1,837.474 = 0.89x; net debt 1,134.051 / 1,837.474 = 0.62x; EBITDA 1,837.474 / interest paid 86.305 = 21.29x; liquidity 500.301 + 1,999.6 = 2,499.9; operating cash flow growth 1,517.609 / 998.162 - 1 = 52.0 percent.
