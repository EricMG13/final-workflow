### Business and transaction view

MaxLinear, Inc. is a Delaware, Carlsbad-headquartered fabless designer of communications systems-on-chip that sells through distributors, module makers, original equipment manufacturers and original design manufacturers, and it is publicly held with no sponsor, parent or controlling holder disclosed in the sources (45 record holders and about 34,000 beneficial holders at 2026-01-22). Its business is changing fast: infrastructure, mainly optical products for data centers, was 57% of Q3 2026 net revenue against 32% in Q3 2025, while broadband fell from 46% to 24% of the quarter's revenue. That change raises revenue and margin, but concentrates risk: the ten largest customers were 56% of nine-month 2026 revenue (65% in FY2025, when two customers were 28%), customers buy on purchase orders with no long-term commitments, shipments to Asia were 85% of nine-month 2026 revenue (Hong Kong 52% in Q3 2026), one supplier took 52% of Q3 2026 inventory purchases, and the company states it has curtailed business with certain customers in China because of export controls. The creditor-relevant transaction history is the terminated Silicon Motion merger of 2022 to 2023: the company paid a USD 18.3 million ticking fee when committed financing was terminated and now faces a confidential Singapore arbitration in which Silicon Motion seeks the termination fee, further damages and costs, plus stockholder suits in two courts and a derivative action. No amount is accrued or estimated, and the filings state that damages could require drawing all lines of credit. On the financing side the sources show a single secured bank structure that was extended and enlarged in 2026 (second amendment of 2026-04-22; third amendment of 2026-10-16, a subsequent event to the quarter) and no notes or new debt issues; the only other 2026 funding transactions are an USD 8.0 million sale of an interest in claims and a revolver draw of USD 40.0 million. Strongest contrary evidence: the filings are consistent and primary, but the facts that would test control and creditor treatment (proxy statement, executed credit agreement and amendments, customer identities, order backlog) are not supplied, so governance conclusions are limited to documented actions. Consequence: the business view supports a fast-growing but concentrated, order-driven semiconductor credit with an unresolved legal overhang; the monitoring posture should key on customer concentration, supplier prepayments and the Silicon Motion docket.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The terminated Silicon Motion merger is the defining transaction. On 2022-05-05 MaxLinear agreed to acquire Silicon Motion Technology Corporation, a Cayman Islands maker of NAND flash controllers, by statutory merger, with debt financing committed by Wells Fargo Bank and other lenders under a second amended and restated commitment letter dated 2022-10-24 (10-K `page:157`). MaxLinear terminated the agreement on 2023-07-26, stating it owed no break-up fee; Silicon Motion disputes the termination and filed a notice of arbitration with the Singapore International Arbitration Centre on 2023-10-05, seeking the termination fee, additional damages, fees and costs, in a confidential proceeding (third quarter 10-Q `page:14`, `page:38`). The financing commitments were terminated and the company paid Wells Fargo a ticking fee of USD 18.3 million in August 2023 (10-K `page:158`). Silicon Motion stockholders have sued MaxLinear and officers: a putative class action filed 2023-08-31, dismissed with prejudice on 2025-07-15 and on appeal to the Ninth Circuit with briefing completed on 2026-04-13; a second federal action filed 2024-06-13 by HBK funds in which all but one claim survived a motion to dismiss; and a third action filed by the same plaintiffs on 2026-05-14 in San Diego Superior Court alleging fraud and related claims; a derivative action filed 2025-02-12 is stayed (third quarter 10-Q `page:38`-`page:41`). The company accrues no loss, regards an unfavorable outcome as reasonably possible and says a reasonable range cannot be estimated (`page:43`). Its 10-K states that damages or settlement could be significant and could require drawing all existing lines of credit, and that financing on favorable terms may not be available (10-K `page:37`-`page:38`). This is an unresolved contingent claim, not a debt instrument.

The only debt documents are the June 23, 2021 Credit Agreement (Initial Term Loan, original USD 350.0 million, and a revolver) and its amendments: a benchmark replacement on 2023-06-29; the Second Amendment of 2026-04-22, which extended the revolver from 2026-06-23 to 2028-03-23, added USD 30 million of commitments and a USD 10.0 million letter of credit commitment, raised the revolver margin to 2.25% and set a total net leverage ceiling of 3.50 to 1.00 and a liquidity floor of USD 80.0 million; and the Third Amendment of 2026-10-16, a subsequent event after the quarter, which adds USD 70 million of commitments for a total of USD 200 million and lowers the liquidity floor to USD 60.0 million. Proceeds of the revolver finance working capital and general corporate purposes. No new notes or other debt issues appear in the sources. In nine months 2026 the company borrowed USD 60.0 million on the revolver and repaid USD 20.0 million, leaving USD 40.0 million drawn, and in March 2026 it sold an interest in certain claims for up to USD 10.0 million, receiving USD 8.0 million used to pay costs of those claims, recorded as a long-term liability (third quarter 10-Q `page:24`). Use of the revolver draw beyond working capital is not itemised; the cash flow statement points to wafer prepayments and inventory (`page:62`).

Other transactions: a USD 2.0 million convertible note receivable from a privately-held entity (2026-01-05, 3.87% interest, due 2029-01-05) and the supplier note receivable above (third quarter 10-Q `page:18`); a contingent consideration of USD 2.6 million from an acquisition in January 2023, unpaid pending resolution of certain matters (`page:17`); a share repurchase program of up to USD 75.0 million authorized on 2025-11-20, under which USD 20.0 million was repurchased in 2025 and none in nine months 2026, leaving about USD 55.0 million available (`page:44`). The executed credit agreement, amendments and the filing that carries the Third Amendment are not supplied; terms above are the issuer's summaries.
