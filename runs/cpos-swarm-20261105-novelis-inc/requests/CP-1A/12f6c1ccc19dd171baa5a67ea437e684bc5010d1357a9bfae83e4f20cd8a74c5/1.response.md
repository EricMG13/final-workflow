### Business and transaction view

Novelis Inc. is a Canadian-incorporated, Atlanta-headquartered producer of aluminum rolled products that is wholly owned indirectly by Hindalco Industries Ltd. and files as a voluntary filer without public equity (AR page:1; page:2). Its model converts purchased primary and recycled metal into sheet, plate and foil for beverage packaging, automotive, aerospace and specialty customers, and passes metal price through to customers under a three-part price of an LME base, a local market premium and a conversion premium (AR page:73), so margin per tonne rather than metal price is the economic driver, and the open exposure is the timing difference called metal price lag (AR page:74). The creditor-relevant business facts are three. First, revenue is concentrated: the 10 largest customers were 57% of fiscal 2026 net sales against 41% in fiscal 2025, and one customer, Ball, was 18% (AR page:33; page:188). Second, the company is in the middle of a capital programme centered on a greenfield plant in Bay Minette, Alabama that management sizes at approximately 5.0 billion (AR page:72) and that the company says is funded through cash flows from operations and "a combination of local financing, our senior secured credit facilities, equity contributions from our common shareholder, and senior notes" (Q2R page:61), which is consistent with the rise in leverage and revolver usage shown in the financial base. Third, two fires at the Oswego plant in September and November 2025 idled the Oswego hot mill until its restart in early June 2026 and management estimated a total negative cash flow impact of approximately 1.7 billion before insurance (AR page:70; Q2R page:36); cumulative net insurance recoveries were 519 through September 30, 2026 (Q2R page:48). Ownership behavior in the filings is supportive on equity (950 of subscriptions at 150 a share in fiscal 2026) and mixed on distributions (a 35 return of capital in fiscal 2026 and a new brand royalty to an affiliate), and the filings give no signal of creditor-adverse transactions such as priming or drop-downs; that absence is an observation about the pages read, not proof of the legal capacity to do them, which depends on indenture and credit agreement terms that were not supplied.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Financing activity over the last two fiscal years is a sequence of completed refinancings and new money for the Bay Minette programme, all described in the debt note. In fiscal 2025 the company issued 750 of 6.875% Senior Notes due January 2030 through Novelis Corporation and used the net proceeds to repay 738 of borrowings under the asset-based revolver (AR page:151), and entered a floating rate Term Loan Facility that provided 1,250 of proceeds and matures March 2032 (AR page:85; Q2R page:18); in September 2025 the term loan margin was cut from 2.00% to 1.75% over three-month Term SOFR (Q2R page:18). On August 18, 2025 Novelis Corporation issued 750 of 6.375% Senior Notes due August 2033 and used the net proceeds to buy 737 of the 750 outstanding 3.25% Senior Notes due November 2026 in a cash tender, leaving 13 outstanding (Q2R page:20; page:17). The Baldwin County Industrial Development Authority issued tax-exempt bonds loaned to Novelis Corporation to fund the Bay Minette plant: Series 2025A of 400 in June 2025 at 5.00%, Series 2025B of 100 in September 2025 at 4.625% and Series 2026A of 225 in March 2026 at 4.30%, each with a mandatory tender (June 2032 or March 2033) before final maturity (Q2R page:22 to page:24). The asset-based revolver was enlarged by 500 on February 19, 2026 to 2.5 billion and by a further 500 on June 16, 2026 to 3.0 billion, with maturity extended to June 16, 2031 (Q2R page:18). On July 23, 2026 the company signed an unsecured 500 term loan, fully drawn, due July 24, 2028 at Term SOFR plus 1.00% rising to 1.25% on certain rating events (Q2R page:21). Uncommitted trade and bank lines were added: a Citi facility of up to 300 after a January 2026 increase from 200, and an MUFG facility of up to 500 from May 26, 2026, both undrawn at September 30, 2026 (Q2R page:19). Use of proceeds disclosed: bonds and the Bay Minette related issuance for construction costs, the 2033 notes for the tender of the 2026 notes, the March 2025 term loan to repay earlier term loans, and the 6.875% notes to repay revolver borrowings (Q2R page:18; page:20; page:22; AR page:151). The use of proceeds for the July 2026 term loan is Insufficient Information in the pages read.

Equity and distributions: the parent subscribed 950 of new shares in fiscal 2026 for capital projects (AR page:86; Q2R page:8), the company returned 35 of capital to the parent in the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and has paid none in fiscal 2027 (Q2R page:16). The Aditya Birla brand license from July 1, 2026 is a new related-party cost of up to 24 for fiscal 2027 (Q2R page:16).

Operating events with transaction-like effects: the Oswego fires of September 16 and November 20, 2025, with the hot mill restarting in early June 2026 and cumulative net insurance recoveries of 519 through September 30, 2026 (Q2R page:36; page:48); the June 2024 Sierre flood in Switzerland, financed in part by a CHF 100 million loan from the Banque Cantonale du Valais whose interest is payable by the Canton of Valais (Q2R page:21); restructuring under the 2025 Efficiency Plan with cumulative costs of 185 and a target of approximately 350 to 400 of annualized savings by the end of fiscal 2028 (Q2R page:47); and the Bay Minette plant, whose cold mill produced its first coil in September 2026 with commercial shipments targeted for the first quarter of fiscal 2028 (RQ2 page:2). The annual report said the plant was expected to commission in fiscal 2027 and in the second half of calendar 2026 (AR page:2; page:72), while the Q2 release reports a first coil in September 2026 and targets commercial shipments in the first quarter of fiscal 2028. These are different milestones, commissioning and commercial shipments, so no delay is concluded and no conflict is logged.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: supply contracts of "one to five years", a stated "high degree of renewal business" and significant switching costs (AR page:23) against a top 10 customer share of 57% in fiscal 2026 from 41% (AR page:33). Mechanism: long contracts and qualification costs support volume visibility, but a few customers hold most of the volume, so one non-renewal or insourcing decision moves a large share of revenue. Implication: revenue is durable in the base case and lumpy in the downside; the leverage tolerance of the credit is lower than the stable sector profile suggests.
- Margin and earnings quality. Evidence: price pass-through under the conversion model (AR page:73) and adjustments for metal price lag, fire losses and start-up costs inside Adjusted EBITDA (RQ4 page:9). Mechanism: pass-through protects conversion margin against metal price moves, but timing gains and losses, fire items and start-up costs are excluded from the measure, so the measure and cash earnings can differ by large amounts in a period. Implication: leverage computed on Adjusted EBITDA should be read with the exclusions; the financial base carries both.
- Cash flow and liquidity. Evidence: capital spending guidance of 2.1 to 2.4 billion for fiscal 2027 (Q2R page:48), negative operating cash flow for the six months (Q2R page:4) and revolver availability of 606 (Q2R page:19). Mechanism: the build continues for the rest of the year and start-up costs continue until normal capacity, while insurance recoveries are uncertain in amount and timing. Implication: refinancing and liquidity depend on revolver access and on delivery of management's expected return to positive free cash flow, which is a management expectation (RQ2 page:3).
- Sponsor alignment. Evidence: 950 of equity contributions and no fiscal 2027 distribution, against a new affiliate royalty. Mechanism: the parent has put in cash when the programme needed it; there is no stated obligation. Implication: treat parent support as demonstrated behavior, not a credit enhancement; the pages read contain no parent guarantee.
- Key uncertainties: timing of Bay Minette commercial shipments, the amount and timing of remaining Oswego insurance recoveries, the effect of tariffs and duties (including a customs matter in which the company carries a cash deposit of approximately 127 for antidumping duties it is appealing, Q2R page:39), a union strike at the Yeongju plant that management says may affect beverage packaging shipments (Q2R page:47), and the absence of executed financing documents.
