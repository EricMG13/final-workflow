### Credit view

Novelis Inc. is a large, diversified aluminum rolling credit whose leverage and liquidity have weakened over the past six months, but it complies with its covenants and has no bond maturity of size before April 2029. The strongest support is a conversion pricing model that passes metal cost to customers, scale across four regions and a trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,831 million. The weakest point is USD 9,797 million of debt, net leverage of 4.7x against a stated goal of 3.5x, USD 2,631 million of short-term borrowings and liquidity down to USD 2,038 million. The outcome turns on whether Bay Minette reaches commercial shipments, capital spending falls and insurance recoveries arrive, so that free cash flow turns positive.

### Key findings

Novelis Inc. has become more leveraged because it is funding a USD 5.0 billion plant and recovering from two fires at the same time. Company-defined net leverage was 2.9x at March 31, 2025, 4.1x at March 31, 2026 and 4.7x at September 30, 2026, against a management goal of about 3.5x during the investment cycle, which management says tariffs and the fire impacts have pushed above target [financial base; Q2R p65]. Total debt rose by USD 1,819 million in six months to USD 9,797 million, and Adjusted Net Debt rose by USD 1,796 million, almost the same as the USD 1,804 million Adjusted Free Cash Flow outflow, so the extra debt is the cash burn of capital spending and working capital rather than shareholder returns [financial base; RQ2 p9, p10].

Liquidity has narrowed and rests on one facility. The company had USD 2,038 million of liquidity at September 30, 2026, down from USD 2,756 million at March 31, 2026, and the revolver availability of USD 606 million is the main committed backstop [financial base; Q2R p19, p61]. The reported figures reconcile to a revolver drawn about USD 2,266 million with USD 128 million of letters of credit against a USD 3,000 million commitment (calculated, see Calculations), which suggests the commitment rather than the borrowing base is the binding limit; that is an inference and the borrowing base is not printed. Short-term borrowings of USD 2,631 million are classified as due within one year, although the revolver itself matures on June 16, 2031 [Q2R p17, p18].

Bond maturities are not the near-term pressure. Only USD 13 million of 3.25% notes falls due in November 2026, and the next large maturities are USD 568 million of notes in April 2029, USD 2,350 million across two series in January 2030 and USD 750 million in August 2031 [financial base; Q2R p17]. The term loans mature in March 2032 and a USD 500 million term loan in July 2028. The risk is therefore the cost and availability of bank liquidity through the build, and then the size of the 2029 and 2030 refinancing if leverage has not come down.

Earnings are improving on a reported basis but are definition sensitive. Trailing Adjusted EBITDA is USD 1,831 million against USD 1,645 million for fiscal 2026, and the quarter included USD 39 million of business interruption recoveries and favorable scrap effects that management cites [financial base; RQ2 p2]. The measure excludes USD 69 million of trailing start-up costs and USD 1,140 million of trailing fire losses net of recoveries, and deducts USD 574 million of trailing metal price lag; unadjusted EBITDA is USD 1,058 million [RQ2 p8, p9]. Management says it will stop excluding start-up costs once Bay Minette reaches normal capacity [Q2R p68].

The business model is a source of resilience with one concentration risk. Metal price passes through under the conversion model and volumes are supported by contracts of generally one to five years, but the 10 largest customers were 57% of fiscal 2026 net sales and Ball alone was 18% [business context; AR p23, p33, p188]. The parent has supported the build with USD 950 million of equity in fiscal 2026 and paid no return of capital in fiscal 2027 so far, but the filing states there is no assurance of further funding [business context; Q2R p61].

Structure and documentation limit how firmly guarantee and covenant questions can be answered. The notes are issued by Novelis Corporation and Novelis Sheet Ingot GmbH and guaranteed by Novelis Inc. and certain subsidiaries; non-guarantor subsidiaries held 20% of six-month net sales, 18% of Adjusted EBITDA and 11% of assets [Q2R p19, p64]. The only financial maintenance test described is a springing fixed charge coverage ratio of 1.25 to 1.0 on the revolver, which applies when excess availability falls below a threshold; availability of USD 606 million is above the highest value that threshold can take on the filed wording, but the June 2026 amendment text was not supplied [AR p148; financial base].

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Scale and diversification support business risk: 29 facilities in nine countries, four segments that all contributed positive Adjusted EBITDA, and fiscal 2026 net sales of USD 18,434 million across beverage packaging, automotive, aerospace and specialty markets [business context; AR p8, p188]. The conversion model passes aluminum price to customers, so margin per tonne rather than metal price drives earnings [AR p73]. Recycling gives a metal cost lever: the company purchased or tolled about 2,155 kt of recycled aluminum against 1,298 kt of primary in fiscal 2026 [AR p18]. The company is current on all covenants and reports compliance with each facility at September 30, 2026 [Q2R p18 to p24]. The financial maintenance covenant package described is light: a single springing test on the revolver, with negative covenants in the notes and term loans and no other maintenance ratio described in the pages read [AR p146 to p148; Q2R p19]. Debt maturities are staggered, with no bond of size due before April 2029 [financial base]. The parent put in USD 950 million of equity in fiscal 2026 and has paid no return of capital this fiscal year [business context]. The efficiency plan had delivered over USD 200 million of run-rate savings at fiscal 2026 year end against a target of USD 350 million to USD 400 million by fiscal 2028 [Q2R p47].

Weaknesses. Net leverage of 4.7x is above management's 3.5x goal and gross debt to Adjusted EBITDA is 5.35x (calculated) [financial base]. Free cash flow is deeply negative: trailing Adjusted Free Cash Flow is an outflow of USD 3,682 million and operating cash flow was an outflow of USD 418 million in six months [financial base]. Liquidity is concentrated in a revolver that is mostly drawn, and short-term borrowings of USD 2,631 million exceed cash of USD 1,277 million [financial base]. Interim cash interest is not disclosed, and accrual interest expense rose to USD 80 million in the quarter from USD 68 million [Q2R p3]. Customer concentration is high and rose year on year [AR p33]. Execution risk is live: the Bay Minette cold mill produced its first coil in September 2026, but commercial shipments are only targeted for the first quarter of fiscal 2028, so start-up costs and capital spending continue before the plant earns [RQ2 p2]. Insurance recoveries of USD 519 million cumulatively are real but further amounts and timing are uncertain [Q2R p48]. Trade measures matter: tariffs reduced fiscal 2026 Adjusted EBITDA by USD 143 million per the release, and the company announced a temporary production and staffing reduction at its Kingston, Canada plant because of duties [RQ4 p1; Q2R p47]. Floating rate debt exposes pre-tax income to about USD 15 million for each 100 basis points, on the company's stated sensitivity [Q2R p73]. Finally, the evidence on guarantee scope, covenant capacity and ratings is summary-level because no executed agreements or rating reports were supplied [source readiness].

### Liquidity and refinancing

At September 30, 2026 cash was USD 1,277 million and availability under committed facilities USD 761 million, of which the revolver was USD 606 million with USD 128 million of letters of credit drawn on it [financial base; Q2R p19, p61]. The remaining USD 155 million of committed availability is not itemized in the pages read. Uncommitted Citi and MUFG facilities of up to USD 300 million and USD 500 million were undrawn and are not counted [Q2R p19]. Against this, USD 2,704 million of debt is due within one year, but USD 2,266 million of it is revolver borrowing (calculated) that matures in 2031 and USD 350 million is Brazil short-term loans, so the year-one figure is a rollover question rather than a scheduled repayment. Management's capital spending guidance of USD 2,100 million to USD 2,400 million for fiscal 2027 against USD 1,543 million spent in six months implies USD 557 million to USD 857 million in the second half (calculated). Operating cash flow for the second half is not guided in the filings, so no runway is computed. Seasonal or working capital swings matter: inventories rose to USD 5,145 million from USD 4,401 million and supplier finance programs hold USD 1.5 billion of confirmed invoices in payables [Q2R p3, p8].

Covenant position. The notes and the term loans carry negative covenants and a cross-acceleration or cross-default feature; the Term Loan Facility cross-defaults to the revolver regardless of amount [Q2R p19, p20; AR p146]. Compliance is reported for every facility at September 30, 2026. The revolver springing fixed charge test is 1.25 to 1.0 and the company's own reported figure for it is not given; headroom cannot be stated, only that availability is above the highest the trigger can be [AR p148; financial base].

### Structure and claims outside debt

Secured debt under the senior secured credit facilities is the term loans of USD 1,231 million and the revolver of about USD 2,266 million, together USD 3,497 million (calculated), secured by substantially all assets of the company and the guarantors [AR p146]. The senior notes of USD 4,431 million and the tax-exempt bonds of USD 725 million are senior unsecured and guaranteed by Novelis Inc. and certain subsidiaries (calculated) [Q2R p17, p19, p22]; the USD 500 million 2026 term loan is unsecured, and its guarantors are not stated in the pages read [Q2R p21]. Non-guarantor subsidiaries held USD 3.7 billion of assets and USD 2.2 billion of debt and other liabilities including intercompany balances at September 30, 2026 [Q2R p65], which rank structurally ahead of the notes as to those subsidiaries' assets, although the figure includes intercompany balances. Outside the debt figure the company has operating lease liabilities of USD 112 million at March 31, 2026, accrued postretirement benefits of USD 449 million, estimated benefit payments of USD 1.2 billion through 2036, purchase obligations of USD 17.2 billion at March 31, 2026 of which USD 7.6 billion fall due within 12 months, supplier finance of USD 1.5 billion inside payables, factoring whose volume is not printed, and a cash deposit of about USD 127 million held against disputed U.S. antidumping duties [financial base; AR p84; Q2R p8, p39].
