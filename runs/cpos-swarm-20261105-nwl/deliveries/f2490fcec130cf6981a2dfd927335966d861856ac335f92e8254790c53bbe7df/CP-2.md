### Credit view

NEWELL BRANDS INC. is a leveraged, turnaround-stage consumer brands credit that is improving but not yet self-funding. The strongest support is earnings momentum and liquidity: trailing-twelve-month normalized EBITDA is USD 1,030 million, net debt to that EBITDA is 4.46x at Q3 2026 against 5.07x at FY2025, the 2027 notes are redeemed, and liquidity is USD 729 million before factoring. The weakest point is cash conversion: operating cash flow less capital expenditure was USD 53 million over the last twelve months, well below net interest expense of USD 341 million and dividends of USD 127 million, and net debt rose by USD 122 million since FY2025. Outcomes turn on whether EBITDA gains become free cash flow and debt reduction before the USD 1,252 million 2028 notes fall due, since the new asset-based facility can mature earlier if they remain outstanding.

### Key findings

1. Leverage improved on earnings, not repayment. NEWELL BRANDS INC. reports net debt of USD 4,592 million against USD 4,470 million at FY2025, so the fall in net debt to normalized EBITDA from 5.07x to 4.46x comes from trailing-twelve-month normalized EBITDA rising from USD 882 million to USD 1,030 million. That EBITDA is a management measure; it includes tariff recoveries recorded in Q2 2026, and removing the roughly USD 126 million named for that quarter would put net leverage near 5.08x (a one-sided sensitivity, calculated below). On a filing-based EBITDA before impairment the ratio is 5.51x. The issuer reported a GAAP net loss of USD 323 million over the twelve months to Q3 2026. Mechanic: lenders and the market will test sustainability of EBITDA, not the reported ratio. Implication: debt service capacity is thinner than the headline ratio suggests.

2. Cash generation does not cover the obligations ahead of equity. Operating cash flow less capital expenditure was USD 53 million over the twelve months to Q3 2026, against net interest expense of USD 341 million and cash dividends of USD 127 million, leaving USD 74 million negative after dividends. First-quarter seasonality and an inventory build explain part of the profile, and Q3 2026 alone was strongly positive, but the year relies on the third and fourth quarters, and receivables factoring and tariff refunds have supported operating cash flow. Implication: refinancing, not internal cash, funds any maturity; the issuer's own full-year operating cash flow outlook of about USD 150 million is a management claim.

3. The capital structure is pushed out but not de-risked. The USD 500 million 2027 notes are gone and the secured revolver was replaced by an asset-based facility of up to USD 800 million, with USD 215 million drawn and USD 480 million of net availability at 2026-09-30. The next large maturity is USD 1,252 million in 2028, which exceeds liquidity of USD 729 million before factoring. Under the facility's terms its maturity springs to 91 days before the earliest material debt maturity if USD 125 million or more is still outstanding, so the 2028 notes can pull the facility's maturity forward (the 2028 notes' exact date is not printed in the pages read). Implication: access to the bond market in 2027 and early 2028 is the decisive event.

4. Priority claims on current assets are growing. The asset-based facility is secured on receivables, inventory, equipment, intellectual property and deposit accounts, and a receivables purchaser holds a first-priority lien on certain unsold receivables. At 2026-09-30 about USD 435 million of receivables were sold across two programs, and borrowing-base availability of USD 741 million means drawn secured borrowings could rise by USD 526 million from USD 215 million. Mechanic: more secured and sold claims on the same current assets reduce the unencumbered pool behind USD 4,626 million of other debt. This is a mechanic, not a recovery conclusion; lien priority between the lenders and the purchaser is not described in the pages read.

5. Business risk is concentrated. Amazon and Walmart supplied about 17% and 13% of FY2025 net sales, the 10-K says retailer power may limit price recovery, Learning and Development earned more than the whole nine-month 2026 segment operating income while Home and Commercial Solutions earned USD 9 million, and a USD 101 million tradename impairment fell in Q3 2026. Implication: a retailer decision or a weak category can move cash flow materially, and impairments signal that asset values already reflect lower expected earnings.

6. Debt-like funding sits outside the debt figure. Factoring of USD 435 million is not in reported debt; the issuer has cut both programs' limits and intends to reduce debt and factoring by a couple of hundred million dollars in 2026. Net debt plus factoring over normalized EBITDA is 4.88x (calculated below). Reducing factoring draws on liquidity unless replaced.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. (1) Brand breadth and reach: the issuer sells in over 150 countries with a portfolio including Rubbermaid, Sharpie, Graco, Coleman and Yankee Candle (Documentary Fact). (2) Earnings momentum: trailing-twelve-month normalized EBITDA rose from USD 882 million at FY2025 to USD 1,030 million at Q3 2026, and management reports two consecutive quarters of sales growth (Documentary Fact for the figures; Management Language for the growth characterisation). (3) Maturity runway: the 2027 notes were redeemed and the asset-based facility runs to 2031 on its stated terms (Documentary Fact). (4) A profitable core: Learning and Development earned a segment operating income of USD 553 million over nine months 2026 (Documentary Fact). (5) Seasonal cash generation: Q3 2026 operating cash flow of USD 297 million, derived from cumulative statements, shows the model produces cash in the second half (Analyst Interpretation). (6) Transparent reporting with net debt and EBITDA reconciliations each quarter (Documentary Fact).

Weaknesses. (1) Leverage: net debt to normalized EBITDA of 4.46x, and 5.51x on a filing-based basis before impairment, with GAAP losses over the last twelve months (Documentary Fact and calculation). (2) Thin cash conversion relative to interest and dividends (Documentary Fact and calculation). (3) Customer concentration and weak pricing power (Documentary Fact). (4) A weak largest segment: Home and Commercial Solutions had a segment operating loss of USD 138 million in FY2025 and carries the Q3 2026 impairment, and Outdoor and Recreation lost USD 17 million over nine months 2026 (Documentary Fact). (5) Structural subordination risk through secured borrowing-base debt and sold receivables, and a springing maturity (Analyst Interpretation). (6) Reliance on management measures and one-off tariff refunds in 2026 earnings and cash flow (Documentary Fact). (7) Covenant headroom, ownership and ratings cannot be assessed from the pack (Insufficient Information; not an adverse conclusion).
