### Credit view

OLIN Corp's fundamental credit is weakening but not impaired in the near term: leverage has stepped up while liquidity and the maturity profile give time to reach the Huntsman merger decision point. The strongest support is USD 1,244.4 million of cash and undrawn revolver, no maturity before the USD 465.0 million receivables facility in November 2027, and three segments that do not move together. The weakest point is trailing adjusted EBITDA of USD 536.6 million, which puts net debt to adjusted EBITDA at 5.21x against 4.08x at FY2025 and cash interest cover at 3.15x. What decides the outcome is fourth quarter 2026 delivery against guidance of USD 80 million to USD 100 million, closing of the merger in the first quarter of 2027, and the end of covenant relief by September 30, 2027. Confidence is Medium.

### Key findings

First, leverage has risen while earnings fell. Trailing twelve month adjusted EBITDA fell by USD 115.2 million to USD 536.6 million between FY2025 and Q3 2026 (a fall of 17.7 percent), while reported total debt rose by USD 185.8 million to USD 3,013.1 million and cash rose by USD 47.2 million, so net debt rose by USD 138.6 million to USD 2,798.3 million. Net debt to adjusted EBITDA moved from 4.08x to 5.21x, total debt to adjusted EBITDA from 4.34x to 5.62x and cash interest cover from 3.66x to 3.15x (Documentary Fact; CP-1 handoff). The mechanism is that fixed interest and capital costs are carried by a smaller EBITDA base, and the implication is less tolerance for further operating shocks before covenant relief ends. The ratios use the company's non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA, which excludes restructuring, acquisition-related costs, the legacy litigation charge of the first quarter of 2026 and, on the CP-1 reproduction, the FY2025 Shintech charge; the covenant ratio is defined differently and its levels are not supplied.

Second, the earnings decline is concentrated in Chlor Alkali Products and Vinyls. Segment income for the nine months of 2026 was USD 80.2 million against USD 270.8 million, with the Freeport vinyl chloride monomer outage costing USD 72.2 million, lower volumes USD 55.3 million, lower pricing USD 42.0 million and a legacy litigation charge USD 36.1 million, partly offset by USD 59.2 million of lower operating costs (Documentary Fact; CP-1A handoff). Winchester segment income was USD 77.1 million and Epoxy USD 13.7 million for the nine months, against USD 67.1 million and a loss of USD 84.3 million a year earlier. The mechanism is operating leverage in a commodity chlor alkali segment that holds most of the depreciation base; the implication is that recovery of credit metrics depends on that segment normalising after the outage, which management says is restored (Management Language), and on epoxy demand, which the filing describes as weak.

Third, cash generation is uneven. Nine month operating cash flow of USD 69.7 million did not cover capital spending of USD 106.6 million, leaving free cash flow before dividends of USD -36.9 million and, after dividends of USD 68.3 million, USD -105.2 million. Trailing twelve month free cash flow was USD 221.5 million, or USD 130.4 million after trailing dividends of USD 91.1 million, because the fourth quarter of 2025 alone produced USD 321.2 million of operating cash flow (Documentary Fact; derived from CP-1 handoff). The mechanism is working capital and timing: receivables rose USD 133.4 million on third quarter sales timing. The implication is that fourth quarter 2026 working capital release, not earnings alone, determines whether net debt falls by year end.

Fourth, liquidity is adequate and maturities are not near. Cash of USD 214.8 million plus USD 1,029.6 million undrawn revolver gives USD 1,244.4 million, equal to 2.68 times the USD 465.0 million receivables facility maturing November 19, 2027, the first maturity. The revolver and term loan mature March 14, 2030 and no covenant limited borrowing at September 30, 2026 (CP-1 handoff). The mechanism is that the nearest wall is small against liquidity, but the 2029 notes of USD 669.3 million and 2030 maturities of USD 1,213.4 million together are USD 1,882.7 million, or 62.1 percent of USD 3,030.7 million principal. The implication is that refinancing risk sits in 2029 and 2030 and depends on market access.

Fifth, covenant relief and ranking mechanics are time-limited. The February 19, 2026 amendment relaxed the net leverage and coverage covenants, added subsidiary guarantees and collateral for the bank facilities, and limits debt, non-regular distributions and asset sales until the earlier of September 30, 2027 and Olin's election, when guarantees and liens fall away (CP-1 handoff; CP-1A handoff). Numeric levels are not supplied, so headroom is not calculable. The mechanism is that bank debt of USD 1,163.1 million (38.4 percent of principal, comprising the term loan, drawn revolver and receivables facility) has collateral or receivables security during relief, while the senior notes of USD 1,784.6 million (58.9 percent) have security not stated in the pack; the implication is that creditor priority changes when relief ends, and the notes' position cannot be assessed without the indentures. This is a mechanic, not a recovery conclusion.

Sixth, the Huntsman merger replaces uncertainty with a different one. The all-stock merger is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027, with a new USD 2,500.0 million facility replacing Olin's bank facilities and Huntsman's revolver (Documentary Fact; CP-1A handoff). The mechanism is a reset of covenants, collateral, amortisation and pricing, with Huntsman obligations joining the guarantor group; the implication is that existing Olin creditors will hold claims on a combined company whose leverage cannot be computed because Huntsman financials, debt and synergy targets are not in the pack. Ratings reflect this: Fitch BB+ on Rating Watch Positive, S&P BB on Credit Watch Developing and Moody's Ba2 with a Negative outlook.

Seventh, claims outside reported debt are moderate but real: operating lease liabilities of USD 387.8 million, an environmental reserve of USD 156.8 million, a net balance sheet pension position of USD 63.6 million (accrued pension liability USD 196.5 million less pension assets USD 132.9 million) with a foreign plan deficit of USD 204.2 million at December 31, 2025, and the Shintech judgment on which about USD 114 million was paid in the nine months (CP-1 handoff). The implication is claims that rank alongside financial debt in cash terms but sit outside the debt ratios.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity runway is the first strength (Documentary Fact): USD 1,244.4 million of cash and undrawn revolver against a nearest maturity of USD 465.0 million, with no covenant restricting borrowing at September 30, 2026. Business diversification is the second (Documentary Fact; CP-1A handoff): three segments, no customer above 10 percent of 2025 sales, a captive chlorine outlet through epoxy and chlorinated organics, and a Winchester business with government contract revenue of about 13 percent of 2025 sales that improved to USD 77.1 million of segment income over the nine months. Third, covenant relief was obtained in February 2026 with the revolver and term loan maturity of March 14, 2030 maintained, and Olin reports compliance with no event of default (Documentary Fact). Fourth, issuer behaviour shows no creditor-adverse transfer: there were no buybacks in the nine months of 2026 after USD 50.5 million in 2025 and no priming or collateral release is documented, though the pack is limited (Analyst Interpretation). Fifth, structural cost actions are cited by management as lowering operating costs, and the nine month Chlor Alkali bridge shows USD 59.2 million of lower operating costs (Management Language qualified by Documentary Fact).

Weaknesses. Leverage is elevated and rising: net debt to adjusted EBITDA of 5.21x and total debt to adjusted EBITDA of 5.62x, with cover at 3.15x (Documentary Fact). Earnings are concentrated in a segment hit by an operating outage and by weaker pricing and volumes, and the outage impact in the third quarter of USD 32.1 million exceeded the July estimate of about USD 20 million (Documentary Fact). Epoxy remains challenged by subsidised Asian competition and weak demand (Documentary Fact). Free cash flow for the nine months was negative while the USD 0.20 quarterly dividend was maintained, so dividends of USD 68.3 million were covered by net borrowing (Analyst Interpretation). Ratings were lowered in February 2026 to BB+, Ba2 and BB and now carry mixed watch and outlook signals (Documentary Fact). Fourth quarter guidance of USD 80 million to USD 100 million implies a seasonally weak quarter, with management citing destocking, higher feedstock costs, Asian export pressure and turnarounds (Management Language). Finally, the credit depends on covenant levels, ranking and merger terms that the pack does not contain (Insufficient Information).
