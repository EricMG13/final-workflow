### Credit view

PBF Holding Company LLC has a sound credit profile at 30 September 2026 that has not yet been tested through a normal-margin year. Its strongest support is liquidity and leverage: USD 2,510.6 million of cash against USD 1,850.0 million of debt principal, net cash of USD 660.6 million, and a USD 4,000.0 million revolver running to 30 September 2031. Its weakest point is earnings durability: Adjusted EBITDA was USD 29.5 million in FY2025 and USD 3,505.2 million over the twelve months to Q3 2026, with USD 3,278.6 million earned in two quarters. What decides the outcome is whether refining margins hold while cash can leave for the parent and the open fire, legal and hydrogen plants items are resolved.

### Key findings

First, the issuer's balance sheet is strong at the latest reported date. Principal debt was USD 1,850.0 million against cash of USD 2,510.6 million, giving net cash of USD 660.6 million and leverage of 0.5x total debt and -0.2x net debt on twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA of USD 3,505.2 million [CP-1 net debt and leverage calculation]. After the redemption of USD 800.0 million of 9.875% notes on 26 October 2026 for about USD 853.4 million, principal falls to USD 1,050.0 million and cash to roughly USD 1,657.2 million before accrued interest and other flows, which is an illustration and not a reported balance [CP-1 illustrative effects]. Category: Documentary Fact for the reported figures, Analyst Interpretation for the illustration.

Second, the issuer's earnings swung from a trough to very high levels, and the trailing figure is concentrated. Adjusted EBITDA was USD 29.5 million in FY2025, USD 1,139.3 million in Q2 2026 and USD 2,139.3 million in Q3 2026 [CP-1 financials table]. The business is priced at spot market and has no contractual margin protection, so the issuer's debt capacity follows crack spreads, crude differentials, throughput and compliance costs [CP-1A operating model]. Management attributes the 2026 strength to conflict-driven market conditions and describes them as uncertain in duration; that is Management Language and is not adopted as a forecast. Category: Analyst Interpretation.

Third, the issuer's refinancing in 2026 removed near-term maturities and lowered stated coupon cost. The 6.00% notes due 2028 and 7.875% notes due 2030 were redeemed, USD 500.0 million of 7.25% notes due 2034 and USD 550.0 million of zero-coupon exchangeable notes due 2032 were issued, and the secured revolver was extended with a USD 4,000.0 million commitment [CP-1A transaction summary]. Stated coupons retired of about USD 166.5 million a year compare with about USD 36.3 million on the new fixed notes, excluding revolver fees and discount amortisation. The exchangeable notes settle principal in cash and are linked to the parent's share price, creating a fair-value liability of USD 197.6 million that moves earnings [CP-1 debt instruments; CP-1A transaction summary]. Category: Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation.

Fourth, claims outside the reported debt are material. They include operating lease liabilities of USD 712.3 million at 30 September 2026, pension and post-retirement medical deficits of USD 89.8 million and USD 16.4 million at 31 December 2025, fixed purchase commitments of USD 642.9 million, renewable energy credit and emissions obligations of USD 486.5 million and environmental liabilities of USD 148.4 million, plus unquantified investigations into the Martinez fire and catalyst release [CP-1 claims outside the debt figure]. These rank alongside debt in a downside case and are not in the leverage ratios. Category: Documentary Fact.

Fifth, the issuer's cash flows with its parent were supportive in the downturn and have turned outward as earnings recovered. Parent contributions exceeded distributions by USD 199.6 million in FY2025, but in Q3 2026 distributions exceeded contributions by USD 60.9 million, and the issuer may distribute cash to fund the parent's dividend [CP-1A ownership and behaviour]. Documented behaviour toward creditors is Neutral or Mixed: full redemptions at par or a premium and transparent reporting are supportive, while past asset distributions to affiliates and the parent-level secured note on the hydrogen plants are open. Category: Analyst Interpretation.

Sixth, the evidence has gaps that limit how far the view can be pushed: no forecast or through-the-cycle earnings case, no executed indentures or credit agreement, a liquidity figure that cannot be rebuilt, and an unexplained difference between issuer and parent debt figures.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The issuer has low reported leverage and net cash: 0.5x total debt and -0.2x net debt to twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA, and cash interest paid of USD 162.3 million against Adjusted EBITDA of USD 3,505.2 million, or 21.6x cover on a measure that excludes capitalised interest [CP-1 interest cover]. Documentary Fact for the figures, Analyst Interpretation for the reading. The issuer has liquidity depth, with USD 2,510.6 million of cash and about USD 3,100.0 million of rounded borrowing availability, and no revolver drawing at the quarter end [CP-1 liquidity]. Its maturity profile has no drawn debt maturing before 2032 after the October redemption [CP-1 debt maturities]. Its asset base is diversified across six refineries in four regions with one customer at about 13% of revenue, so customer loss is a smaller risk than a single refinery event [CP-1A operating model]. The parent supported the issuer with net contributions of USD 199.6 million in the FY2025 downturn and contributed the hydrogen plants in Q3 2026 [CP-1A ownership and behaviour].

Weaknesses. The issuer's earnings depend on external margins and uptime: Adjusted EBITDA of USD 29.5 million in FY2025 against USD 3,505.2 million over the latest twelve months, with USD 3,278.6 million from two quarters. Analyst Interpretation resting on the CP-1 figures. The issuer faces event risk at single sites: the Martinez fire investigations and the 8 May 2026 Chalmette fire have no quantified impact [CP-1A transaction summary]. Cash can leave to the parent chain and the exchangeable notes link the issuer to the parent's share price, with a fair-value liability of USD 197.6 million [CP-1A behaviour table; CP-1 debt instruments]. Large claims sit outside debt, notably leases, purchase commitments, emissions credits and environmental liabilities [CP-1 claims outside debt]. The revolver is secured and ranks ahead of the unsecured notes to the extent of its collateral; the unsecured notes are also structurally behind non-guarantor subsidiaries, a structural mechanic and not a recovery conclusion [CP-1 debt instruments]. Regulatory compliance cost is large and volatile: renewable fuel compliance cost was USD 522.8 million in Q3 2026 against USD 169.0 million a year earlier [CP-1A operating model].
