### Credit view

Precision Drilling is a moderately leveraged North American driller with sound reported credit metrics but a cyclical business and a thin cash cushion. Its strongest support is reported net debt of 1.28x LTM Adjusted EBITDA, covenant ratios far inside their limits and CAD 462.5 million of recomputed liquidity. Its weakest point is free cash flow: CAD 55.7 million for nine months 2026 against CAD 104.7 million a year earlier, while debt repayment and buybacks totalled CAD 121.6 million and cash fell to CAD 23.2 million. The outcome turns on oil-driven North American activity, the United States margin recovery management guides to, refinancing of the 2028 revolver and 2029 notes, and the Canada Revenue Agency reassessment.

### Key findings

1. Reported leverage is moderate and stable. Net debt to LTM Adjusted EBITDA was between 1.20x and 1.31x at the four quarter-ends from December 31, 2025 to September 30, 2026, and 1.28x at the latest; total debt to LTM Adjusted EBITDA was 1.33x [CP-1]. The issuer targets a sustained net debt to Adjusted EBITDA ratio of below 1.0x, so the calculated ratio is above its own target [CP-1A]. Because the debt is in US dollars and statements in Canadian dollars, the carrying value rose by CAD 22.9 million from exchange rates over the nine months even as CAD 75.1 million was repaid, so the Canadian dollar balance understates progress [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact for the reported items, Analyst Interpretation for the ratios.
2. Free cash flow weakened. The issuer's cash from operations of CAD 279.5 million for nine months 2026 funded capital expenditure of CAD 223.8 million, up 22.9% on the prior-year nine months, leaving free cash flow of CAD 55.7 million after CAD 104.7 million the year before; in Q3 2026 alone free cash flow was negative CAD 11.7 million [CP-1]. Mechanism: expansion and upgrade spending, reactivation costs and seasonal working-capital builds absorb cash while Adjusted EBITDA is lower than the prior-year nine months. Implication: shareholder returns of CAD 46.6 million and net debt repayment of CAD 75.1 million were funded partly by running cash down to CAD 23.2 million. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
3. Liquidity is adequate but depends on the revolver. The issuer reports more than CAD 460 million of available liquidity and the recomputation gives CAD 462.5 million, of which about CAD 382 million is undrawn revolver [CP-1]. Letters of credit consume revolver availability and US$43 million of commitments expire on June 28, 2027. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
4. Refinancing is concentrated in 2028 and 2029. US$46 million of revolver is drawn to October 31, 2028 and US$400 million of 6.875% unsecured notes mature on January 15, 2029, about 90% of principal. Nothing in the supplied sources shows redemption terms, the notes' pricing history or the terms of the revolver's extension option [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact for dates, Insufficient Information for terms.
5. Covenants are not constraining on reported numbers: senior secured debt to covenant EBITDA of 0.14x against a 2.50x ceiling, and covenant EBITDA to interest of 10.27x against a 2.50x floor. The covenant EBITDA definition and the notes' coverage value are not supplied, so the reported ratios cannot be recomputed [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact; limitation stated.
6. Business risk is cyclical with partial contract support. Revenue is almost all day-rate or hourly; term-contracted average rigs were 54 in Q3 2026 against 40 a year earlier, all seven international rigs are on five-year contracts running into 2027 and 2028, but in Q1 2026 term-contracted rigs generated only 26% of Canadian and 34% of United States utilization days [CP-1A]. Category: Documentary Fact.
7. A contested tax reassessment is a contingent cash call. The Canada Revenue Agency denied a deduction for the 2018 tax year and proposed the same for 2019 to 2022; the issuer estimates a maximum tax liability of about CAD 155 million excluding interest, recognizes no liability, and must pay half of any assessed amount while contesting [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact; outcome and timing are uncertain.
8. Governance behaviour is supportive on deleveraging and mixed on distributions: the issuer reduced debt and targets CAD 100 million of reduction for 2026, and also allocates up to 50% of free cash flow before debt repayments to buybacks, with cash at a low level; no extraction or creditor-adverse action is documented, but legal capacity under the debt agreements is not visible [CP-1A]. Category: Analyst Interpretation.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths.

- Moderate leverage with stability: net debt of 1.28x and total debt of 1.33x LTM Adjusted EBITDA, in a narrow range over four quarter-ends [CP-1]. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
- Wide covenant headroom on reported ratios, 0.14x against 2.50x and 10.27x against 2.50x [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact.
- Low secured debt: the secured revolver carries about 10% of principal and the unsecured notes about 90% (calculation below) [CP-1]. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
- Liquidity of CAD 462.5 million on recomputation, supported by a revolver with final maturity in October 2028 [CP-1]. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
- Improved coverage: nine-month Adjusted EBITDA to cash interest of 7.44x against 6.03x a year earlier, as interest paid fell with lower debt [CP-1]. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
- Contract support: rising term-contracted rigs and full contracting of international rigs [CP-1A]. Category: Documentary Fact.
- Deleveraging record: debt reduced by CAD 610 million since the start of 2022 against a target of CAD 700 million through 2027, per the issuer [CP-1A]. Category: Management Language.
- Flexible capital spending: expansion and upgrade spending of CAD 84.6 million for nine months 2026 is discretionary against maintenance and infrastructure of CAD 139.3 million [CP-1]. Category: Analyst Interpretation.

Weaknesses.

- Cyclical, commodity-driven activity with seasonality in Canada, the largest market, and exposure to customer budgets [CP-1A]. Category: Documentary Fact.
- Free cash flow decline and a negative Q3 2026, with a thin cash balance of CAD 23.2 million [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact.
- Revolver dependence for liquidity, with letters of credit consuming availability [CP-1]. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
- Refinancing concentration: about 90% of principal in one January 2029 maturity [CP-1]. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
- United States margin pressure from rig reactivation costs, with the fourth-quarter step change a management claim [CP-1A]. Category: Management Language.
- Earnings quality: Adjusted EBITDA is after cash-settled share-based compensation that follows the share price, and the covenant definition is not supplied [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact.
- Contingent tax liability of about CAD 155 million excluding interest, unrecognized [CP-1]. Category: Documentary Fact.
- Information limits: unaudited interim basis, no annual statements, no debt agreements, no ownership or customer data, and an unresolved auditor-name conflict [CP-0]. Category: Insufficient Information.
- Concentration: seven international rigs in two Middle East markets, with customer concentration not disclosed [CP-1A]. Category: Insufficient Information for customers.

### Thesis

Precision Drilling can service and refinance its moderate debt if North American drilling activity holds and free cash flow recovers after the 2026 capital programme, but returns to shareholders and debt reduction now run ahead of cash generation, so the credit rests on cyclical activity, the revolver and a single 2029 maturity (Analyst Interpretation).
