### Business and transaction view

The supplied sources describe a publicly listed Canadian contract driller with no sponsor, no parent and no disclosed controlling holder, and with no acquisition, disposal, recapitalisation or new debt issue in the period. The business is two segments: Contract Drilling Services, which earns day-rate revenue from drilling rigs in Canada, the United States and the Middle East, and Completion and Production Services, which earns hourly and rental revenue in Canada. Volume depends on oil and gas prices and customer capital discipline; Canadian volume is seasonal because spring break-up halts heavy equipment moves. Term contracts add visibility, and coverage rose during 2026, but contracted rigs are less than half of the average active fleet, and in Q1 2026, the only quarter for which the share of utilization days is disclosed, term-contracted rigs generated a minority of days in both Canada and the United States, where reactivation costs are also depressing 2026 margins. Management describes a step change in United States margins in the fourth quarter; that is management language, not a result. For a creditor the operating story matters through four mechanics: oil-price-driven activity swings feed through to cash flow quickly because costs are largely variable; capital spending is discretionary but was raised twice in 2026, first for contracted upgrades and then for long-lead deliveries and a pending upgrade; international rigs are fully contracted but concentrated in two markets near a conflict zone; and capital allocation splits free cash flow between debt repayment and buybacks, with cash drawn down. The pack cannot show who controls the company, how concentrated customers are, how the notes came to exist, or what the credit agreement permits in distributions, so legal capacity and willingness to prioritise creditors are only partly assessable.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability. Evidence: term-contracted average rigs rose for 2026 and all international rigs are on five-year contracts (R3 pages 7 and 8), but the United States term share of utilization days fell in Q1 2026 (R1 page:8). Mechanic: day-rate revenue follows activity, which follows oil prices and customer budgets; term contracts floor part of it and shortfall payments are small. Implication: a commodity downturn would cut United States and Canadian volumes faster than costs fall in the first months, so cash flow and revolver reliance would rise; term coverage limits but does not remove this. Counter-evidence: no conflict logged.
- Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: United States reactivation costs per day rose and guidance expects a step change in the fourth quarter (R3 pages 4 and 7); share-based compensation is cash-settled and share-price sensitive (R3 page:11). Mechanic: reactivation spending is front-loaded and is recovered only if utilization and day rates hold; compensation expense moves EBITDA with the share price. Implication: current-year EBITDA understates or overstates run-rate depending on both; the fourth-quarter guidance should be monitored, not assumed.
- Concentration risk. Evidence: Canada is the largest revenue market; seven international rigs are in two Middle East markets (S3 page:7; R3 page:7). Mechanic: weather, heavy oil economics and regional conflict drive large blocks of revenue; customer concentration is not disclosed. Implication: single-region shocks matter; the true counterparty concentration is unknown.
- Sponsor alignment. Evidence: no sponsor or controlling holder is identified; management allocates free cash flow between debt repayment and buybacks (R3 page:6). Mechanic: with no identified sponsor or controlling holder, distributions are a board policy choice, constrained by the covenants. Implication: willingness has been creditor-supportive on debt reduction, mixed on buybacks; the legal limits on distributions are not visible in this pack.
- Key uncertainties. The contested tax reassessment (S3 page:12), the covenant definitions, the cause of the Q4 2025 loss and the audit-firm conflict are open items carried to the next modules.
