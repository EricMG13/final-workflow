### Business and transaction view

PENN is a public company without a sponsor or controlling holder disclosed in the supplied text. It runs regional casinos and racetracks and an online sports betting and iCasino business across North America, and its real estate sits mostly with landlords under long-dated triple net leases. For a creditor the structure matters more than the brand: the retail segments produce the cash flow, rent is a fixed and escalating operating cost paid before debt service and a lease default is also a debt default, and the Interactive segment is moving toward break-even by cutting cost rather than by growing underlying revenue. The 2026 transactions were refinancings and development financings rather than a change of control: a new 600.0 unsecured note, extended bank maturities, and sale-leaseback funding from GLPI for the Aurora project. The history that matters is the 2023 Barstool round trip, the 2023 to 2025 ESPN relationship and its early end, the 2025 to 2026 activist contest settled by a cooperation agreement, and the shift from buybacks in 2025 to none in 2026. Creditor protections in the credit agreement and indentures, board composition detail and large-holder data are not supplied, so legal capacity to pay out cash, incur debt or release collateral is not assessed and the governance read relies on documented actions only.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On March 16, 2026 PENN issued 600.0 of 6.75% senior unsecured notes at par due April 1, 2031, callable from April 1, 2028 at declining premiums, interest payable each April 1 and October 1; the notes are not guaranteed by subsidiaries unless certain subsidiary-guaranteed debt securities are later issued, and net proceeds repaid revolver borrowings (10-Q Q3 page:18). The result at 2026-09-30 is total debt principal of 2806.6 (CP-1), with the nearest large maturity the 400.0 5.625% Notes due January 15, 2027 which are classified long-term on the Company's "intent and ability to refinance on a long-term basis" (page:19).

Use of proceeds and liquidity effect: the notes repaid revolver drawings, lifting undrawn revolver capacity; the amendments extended maturities; the Aurora sale-leaseback brought in 216.3 of GLPI funding on June 24, 2026 against 225.0 committed, adding 17.4 of annual rent. These events are described as completed, not proposed. Status of proposed items: the Council Bluffs financing by GLPI of up to 150.0 is a commitment at PENN's request and not yet drawn or leased; the New Orleans project is planned. After the quarter and before the cutoff (subsequent events): on October 15, 2026 the Company gave notice to redeem all exchangeable shares for common stock on or about December 15, 2026, and fixed rent under the AR PENN Master Lease rose by 4.4 on November 1, 2026 (10-Q Q3 pages 21 and 31). ### Credit translations

1. Landlord claims and cross-default. Evidence: the AR PENN and 2023 Master Leases are "cross-defaulted, cross-collateralized, and coterminous, and subject to a parent guarantee" (10-Q Q3 page:21), and a default under a master lease "could result in a default under our debt agreements" (10-K page:35). Risk mechanic: a lease default or termination removes operating properties and accelerates debt through cross-default, so lease performance gates the whole capital structure. Credit implication: lease coverage and rent escalation are first-order credit drivers alongside debt leverage; GLPI funding of development raises fixed rent (analyst arithmetic: 10.1, 11.7 and 17.4 of added annual rent on 130.0, 150.0 and 216.3 of funding, or 7.9% on 496.3 in total). Category: Analyst Interpretation on Documentary Facts.
2. Refinancing and maturity. Evidence: bank facilities now mature in 2031 and 2033 and 600.0 of notes was issued due 2031 (10-Q Q3 pages 17-18). Risk mechanic: the near-term maturity wall is the 400.0 of notes due January 15, 2027, treated as long-term on intent and ability to refinance, with liquidity of 2020.9 (CP-1) available. Credit implication: refinancing risk is low in the next twelve months on liquidity but depends on market access for the 2027 and 2029 notes; the term loans are floating (weighted-average rates of 6.600% to 6.941% across maturity buckets, 10-Q Q3 page:78). Category: Analyst Interpretation.
3. Interactive durability. Evidence: Interactive revenue rose 50.7 in Q3 2026 while the tax gross-up rose by 51.9 and online gaming revenue fell, with the loss narrowing on lower marketing, general and administrative, and labor costs (10-Q Q3 page:64). Risk mechanic: the segment's improved result rests on cost removal after the ESPN agreement ended and may not persist if marketing returns to win share, while revenue ex gross-up is flat. Credit implication: a recovery in consolidated earnings is partly dependent on an unproven profit path; the conflict list shows none that counters it. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
4. Sponsor and shareholder alignment. Evidence: no sponsor, no dividends, buybacks of 354.4 in FY2025 and none in nine months 2026, and 750.0 of authorization to 2028 (10-Q Q3 page:80). Risk mechanic: management discretion over cash returns is not constrained in the pages read, and the activist settlement refreshed the board. Credit implication: monitor restricted-payment capacity and repurchase resumption against the 2027 maturity; Not assessable without the debt documents. Category: Analyst Interpretation with Insufficient Information on covenants.
5. Collateral and recovery mechanics (no recovery conclusion). Evidence: interests in owned real property, with named exceptions, collateralize the credit facilities (10-K page:64) while the majority of real estate is leased (10-Q Q3 page:7). Risk mechanic: owned real property available to secure bank debt is limited by the leased structure, and landlord rights rank through lease terms. Credit implication: recovery would depend on lease treatment as well as asset value; value and priority are Not supplied. Category: Analyst Interpretation.
