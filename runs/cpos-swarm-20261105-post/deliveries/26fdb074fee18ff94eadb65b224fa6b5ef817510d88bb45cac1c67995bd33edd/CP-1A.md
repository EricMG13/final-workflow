### Business and transaction view

Post is a decentralised, acquisition-built food holding company with four reportable segments: Post Consumer Brands, Weetabix, Foodservice and Refrigerated Retail. For fiscal 2025 the 10-K reports cereal and granola at 32.4% of consolidated net sales, eggs and egg products at 29.6% and pet food at 19.2% (10-K page:11). Each segment depends on a few large customers, Walmart alone being 17.4% of consolidated net sales (10-K page:16). Operating pressure is concentrated in cereal and pet food volumes, while Foodservice earnings have been lifted by avian influenza pricing that management says is about 60 million above the segment's normalised annual run rate (Q3 release page:7). The credit-relevant history is a continuing churn of the perimeter: the full purchase of 8th Avenue on July 1, 2025, the sales of the pasta business on December 1, 2025 and of Crystal Farms on May 1, 2026, and in fiscal 2026 the refinancing of 5.50% notes with 6.50% notes and the add-on of 6.250% notes. Ownership is public with no sponsor, no controlling holder identified in the pack and no sponsor behaviour to assess; the shareholder-ownership table is not in the pack because the annual report incorporates it from the proxy statement by reference. Documented capital allocation shows heavy share repurchases relative to free cash flow, which is a leverage and liquidity mechanic rather than a control-rights finding. No transaction is pending in the sources. Subsequent to the quarter, only further buybacks and narrowed guidance are documented. The business is well documented; ownership, control rights and legal capacity are not.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

All transactions below are completed and documented in filings; no pending transaction, sponsor, merger or LBO is in the pack, and no offering document is supplied, so use of proceeds is taken from the notes to the financial statements. In fiscal 2025, Post issued 600.0 of 6.250% senior notes due October 2034 on October 9, 2024 and used part of the proceeds to redeem the remaining 5.625% notes due January 2028 (10-K page:136). It bought Potato Products of Idaho on March 3, 2025 for 120.0 with cash on hand, and on July 1, 2025 acquired the preferred stock and the 39.5% common equity it did not own in 8th Avenue for a preliminary purchase price of 798.8, which included retiring all 8th Avenue debt other than leaseback financial liabilities, and assumed 111.4 of leaseback financial liabilities (10-K page:117, page:118). In fiscal 2026, Post sold the pasta business on December 1, 2025 for a preliminary 375.0 with closing proceeds of 378.5, and the buyer assumed 78.2 of leaseback financial liabilities, leaving 33.2 of leaseback liabilities in Post's debt, as CP-1 shows (Q3 10-Q page:10). On December 15, 2025 it issued 1,300.0 of 6.50% senior notes due March 2036 at par for net proceeds of 1,284.7 and redeemed all 1,235.0 of its 5.50% notes due December 2029, paying 22.6 of premiums and recording a 17.5 loss on extinguishment (Q3 10-Q page:19, page:22). On March 13, 2026 it issued a further 600.0 of 6.250% notes due October 2034 at 100.75% of par, received 600.4 and repaid the revolver balance, using the remainder for general corporate purposes (Q3 10-Q page:20). It sold Crystal Farms on May 1, 2026 for a preliminary 50.0 with closing proceeds of 45.7, after writing the business down by 43.3 across the nine months, and sold the Sparks Facility for a gain of 21.6 (Q3 10-Q page:12). The liquidity effect, shown in CP-1, is a revolver that moved from 440.0 drawn at September 30, 2025 to undrawn, cash of 265.6 and principal debt 223.8 higher than at September 30, 2025, with the first maturity now the 575.0 convertible in August 2027. Post's shares are repurchased under an authorization of up to 600.0 effective May 9, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:48), with 490.7 remaining on August 5, 2026 (Q3 release page:6). The creditor reading is that the refinancing extended maturities and cleared the revolver, but at a higher coupon on the replaced notes, and that net proceeds from sales were more than spent on repurchases.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: cereal and granola, and pet food, together were 51.6% of fiscal 2025 net sales (32.4% plus 19.2%; 10-K page:11), the 10-K states the cereal category "has experienced weakness, and we expect this trend to continue" (page:16), and Q3 FY2026 Post Consumer Brands volumes excluding 8th Avenue fell 7.1% (Q3 release page:4). Risk mechanic: fixed-cost manufacturing platforms lose absorption when volumes fall, and private label and category competition limit pricing. Credit implication: debt service capacity depends on Foodservice and acquisitions offsetting core volume loss, so refinancing in 2027 and later rests on holding Adjusted EBITDA near the guided level. Category: Analyst Interpretation on cited facts. Counter-evidence: Post Consumer Brands segment Adjusted EBITDA rose 11.2% in Q3 with 8th Avenue included (Q3 release page:4).
2. Earnings quality in Foodservice. Evidence: Q3 declines "reflect the lapping of avian influenza pricing in the prior year period" (Q3 release page:4) and FY2026 guidance includes about 60 million of Foodservice earnings above a 500 million run rate (page:7). Risk mechanic: egg prices spike with disease and revert, so the segment's contribution is cyclical and partly a pricing windfall. Credit implication: reported leverage of 4.6x on the credit agreement basis is on a peak-influenced denominator, and management's own comparable entry level for fiscal 2027 is about 1.48 billion, below the guided fiscal 2026 range. Category: Management Language for the run-rate figures. Counter-evidence: Foodservice volumes rose 4.3% in Q3.
3. Concentration. Evidence: Walmart is 17.4% of consolidated net sales, and the top two customers are 42.0% of Foodservice and 34.9% of Refrigerated Retail (10-K page:16). Risk mechanic: the loss of distribution or a price concession at one customer hits volume and margin across segments simultaneously. Credit implication: concentration raises downside tail risk under the 4.6x leverage. Category: Documentary Fact with Analyst Interpretation of mechanism.
4. Capital allocation. Evidence: buybacks of 916.1 against free cash flow of 401.5 (CP-1). Risk mechanic: debt is not falling while interest expense, net rose 22% in nine months. Credit implication: leverage reduction depends on earnings growth, not repayment, and the Q4 implied by guidance is lower than a year earlier (CP-1 calculation 8). Category: Analyst Interpretation.
5. Portfolio churn. Evidence: two business disposals (pasta, Crystal Farms) and a facility sale within five months, two acquisitions in the prior fiscal year, and integration, transaction and restructuring add-backs recurring in every period (CP-1). Risk mechanic: add-backs labelled non-recurring recur, so Adjusted EBITDA overstates repeatable cash earnings. Credit implication: use the unadjusted sensitivity of 5.57x total debt to operating profit plus depreciation and amortization (CP-1) alongside the 4.75x adjusted figure. Category: Analyst Interpretation.

Key uncertainties: ownership concentration, executed financing terms, any rating view, and the position after June 30, 2026.

## Calculations

1. Cereal, granola and pet food share of FY2025 net sales: 32.4% + 19.2% = 51.6% (10-K page:11). These are the filing's percentages and overlap is not stated, so the sum assumes the categories are separate, which the 10-K's product table supports (cereal and granola 2,645.5 and pet food 1,570.4 on separate lines, 10-K page:157).
2. Share of net sales from product lines: cereal and granola 2,645.5 / 8,158.1 = 32.4%; eggs 2,413.1 / 8,158.1 = 29.6%; pet food 1,570.4 / 8,158.1 = 19.2%. These reproduce the printed percentages.
3. Buyback excess over free cash flow: 916.1 - 401.5 = 514.6 (CP-1).
4. Segment net sales check: 4,024.6 + 542.2 + 2,641.0 + 953.3 = 8,161.1, less corporate and eliminations 3.0 = 8,158.1 (10-K page:155).
5. Disposal proceeds: pasta closing proceeds 378.5 + Crystal Farms 45.7 = 424.2, equal to the investing line in CP-1.
6. Leaseback balance after sale: 8th Avenue leaseback liabilities assumed 111.4, less 78.2 assumed by the pasta buyer, is 33.2, equal to the balance in the June 30, 2026 debt table. This reconciles only if no payments reduced the balance, which the single 33.2 balance at both September 30, 2025 and June 30, 2026 indicates (CP-1).
