### Credit view

Post Holdings is a highly leveraged but liquid food producer whose credit depends on holding earnings steady while it chooses between buybacks and debt reduction. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 1,242.4 million, no revolver drawings, fixed-rate notes averaging 5.5%, and no principal due until the USD 575.0 million convertible in August 2027. The weakest point is leverage of 4.59x net and 4.75x total on adjusted EBITDA (5.57x total on unadjusted earnings), with interest cost rising faster than earnings and buybacks above free cash flow. The outcome is decided by whether adjusted EBITDA stays near the guided USD 1,560 to 1,570 million for fiscal 2026 and the roughly USD 1.48 billion comparable level management gives for fiscal 2027, and whether cash is redirected from repurchases to repayment before the 2027 maturity.

### Key findings

Post's financial risk is high but its near-term liquidity risk is low. At June 30, 2026 principal debt was USD 7,676.0 million and net debt USD 7,410.4 million, which is 4.75x and 4.59x twelve-month adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,615.9 million, and 5.57x total debt on operating profit plus depreciation and amortization of USD 1,378.4 million [CP-1 leverage chart and financials table; Documentary Fact for the figures, Analyst Interpretation for the reading]. Leverage was 4.84x total at September 30, 2025 and has moved little since, because the debt added in the period was offset by earnings growth rather than repayment [CP-1 leverage chart; Analyst Interpretation].

Interest is the pressure point. Interest expense, net was USD 419.1 million over the twelve months and rose 22% in the nine months to June 30, 2026 against the prior-year nine months, while third-quarter adjusted EBITDA fell 5.0%. The company-reported interest coverage under its credit agreement fell from 4.1x at March 31, 2026 to 3.8x at June 30, 2026 [CP-1 interest cover chart; Documentary Fact]. Coupons on the notes now run at about USD 421.1 million a year, so interest cover is unlikely to recover without earnings growth or repayment [CP-1 calculation 9; Analyst Interpretation].

Capital allocation is the main discretionary lever and is being used for shareholders. Treasury stock purchases of USD 916.1 million in nine months exceeded free cash flow of USD 401.5 million by USD 514.6 million, a gap met from USD 424.2 million of disposal proceeds and USD 223.8 million of net new debt [CP-1 cash flow section; CP-1A governance behaviour; Documentary Fact]. A new share repurchase authorization of USD 600.0 million was approved in May 2026 and USD 490.7 million remained on August 5, 2026 [CP-1A transaction summary; Documentary Fact]. This is rated Neutral or Mixed on governance because it is documented as a shareholder-return choice, not as a transfer away from creditors, but it means the leverage trajectory depends on management discretion rather than on a commitment to repay.

Liquidity and the debt profile are the main support. Cash of USD 265.6 million and an undrawn USD 1,000.0 million revolver less USD 23.2 million of letters of credit give USD 1,242.4 million of liquidity, 2.2x the USD 575.0 million convertible due August 2027 (calculated below). The revolver matures in February 2029, and the other notes mature between 2030 and 2036 [CP-1 liquidity and maturity charts; Documentary Fact]. The August 2027 convertible falls within twelve months of the as-of date, so its settlement, which Post may make in cash, shares or both, is the first refinancing event to monitor [CP-1 debt table; Analyst Interpretation].

Earnings quality and durability are weaker than the adjusted figures suggest. Adjusted EBITDA includes USD 232.9 million of add-backs, which is 14.4% of the total, among them USD 83.0 million of stock-based compensation, and integration, transaction and restructuring items that recur in every period [CP-1 Adjusted EBITDA review; CP-1A credit translation on portfolio churn; Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation]. Foodservice earnings include roughly USD 60 million above a normalised run rate from avian influenza pricing that is being lapped, and management's own comparable entry level for fiscal 2027 of about USD 1.48 billion sits below guided fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,560 to 1,570 million [CP-1A credit translations 2; Management Language]. Fiscal 2026 guidance implies a fourth quarter of USD 369.5 million to USD 379.5 million, below the USD 425.4 million of the prior-year quarter [CP-1 calculation 8; Analyst Interpretation].

Covenant protection is thin and cannot be measured. The only maintenance test found is a secured net leverage covenant of 4.25:1.00 that applies only when revolver usage exceeds 30% of commitments, a trigger of USD 300.0 million against usage of USD 23.2 million, so the test did not apply at June 30, 2026 [CP-1 covenants section; Documentary Fact]. Headroom is Not calculable because the secured net debt and credit agreement EBITDA definitions were not supplied. The company's reported total net leverage of 4.6x is a different measure and must not be read as covenant headroom [CP-1; CP-1 correction of the earlier wording].

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, liquidity: USD 1,242.4 million of cash and undrawn revolver, a revolver that matures in February 2029 and sits undrawn, and no principal due before August 2027 [CP-1 liquidity and maturity charts; Documentary Fact]. Second, the debt structure: principal debt of USD 7,676.0 million is almost entirely fixed-rate notes at a weighted average of 5.5%, only USD 1,000.0 million (13.0% of principal, calculated below) is secured, and maturities beyond 2027 are spread across fiscal 2030 to 2036 [CP-1 debt by instrument; Documentary Fact]. Third, cash generation: free cash flow was USD 401.5 million in nine months and USD 553.1 million over twelve months, covering about 7.2% of principal debt a year [CP-1 financials table and calculation 1; Documentary Fact, ratio calculated here]. Fourth, diversification: four segments with segment adjusted EBITDA of USD 197.3 million in Post Consumer Brands, USD 140.8 million in Foodservice, USD 37.3 million in Weetabix and USD 26.6 million in Refrigerated Retail in the third quarter, so no single segment carries the whole result [CP-1 segments table; Documentary Fact]. Fifth, a covenant structure with no regularly tested leverage test while the revolver is lightly used [CP-1 covenants section; Documentary Fact].

Weaknesses. First, leverage: 4.59x net and 4.75x total on adjusted EBITDA, 5.57x total on unadjusted earnings, with little progress since September 30, 2025 [CP-1; Documentary Fact, Analyst Interpretation]. Second, rising interest burden, with coverage reported at 3.8x and falling [CP-1 interest cover chart; Documentary Fact]. Third, the use of cash: buybacks above free cash flow, with the revolver cleared by new notes rather than by cash from operations [CP-1 cash flow section; CP-1A governance behaviour; Documentary Fact, Analyst Interpretation]. Fourth, volume pressure in the core categories: cereal, granola and pet food were 51.6% of fiscal 2025 net sales, and Post Consumer Brands volumes excluding 8th Avenue fell 7.1% in the third quarter [CP-1A operating model and credit translation 1; Documentary Fact, Analyst Interpretation]. Fifth, customer concentration: Walmart is 17.4% of consolidated net sales, and the two largest customers are 42.0% of Foodservice and 34.9% of Refrigerated Retail [CP-1A credit translation 3; Documentary Fact]. Sixth, adjusted EBITDA quality and perimeter churn, with USD 232.9 million of add-backs and two business sales in five months [CP-1; CP-1A; Documentary Fact]. Seventh, an evidence gap: no executed indentures or credit agreement, no ownership table, no ratings and no data after June 30, 2026, which limits conclusions on legal capacity, control rights and covenant headroom [CP-0 and CP-1A gaps; Insufficient Information].

### Credit thesis

Post can service and refinance its debt in the near term on liquidity and fixed-rate, long-dated notes, but with net leverage near 4.6x, rising interest costs and buybacks above free cash flow, its credit quality depends on earnings holding up and management choosing to reduce debt.

Category: Analyst Interpretation. Basis: CP-1 and CP-1A as cited above.
