### Business and transaction view

Primo Brands operates one segment: a vertically integrated North American branded water business with retail distribution to more than 200,000 outlets and a recurring direct-to-consumer arm made up of Direct Delivery, Exchange and Refill. Revenue is split about evenly between regional spring water and everything else, premium water is the fastest-growing category, and one retail customer accounted for about a fifth of FY2025 net sales, which is the main concentration disclosed. Margin is exposed to freight, materials and tariff costs, and integration costs are fading but still sit in cost of sales. There is no pending sale, merger or acquisition in the sources. The transactions that matter to a creditor are historical or financial: the November 2024 combination of BlueTriton and Primo Water, the March 2026 term loan refinancing, a series of sponsor secondary offerings with company repurchases alongside them, and a new repurchase program approved on November 3, 2026. The sponsor affiliate held about 32.0% of voting power in February 2026 and the stockholders agreement gives it approval rights over large dividends, share repurchases and added debt above a stated leverage level while it holds at least 30% of the Class A shares; its present holding after the August 2026 sale is not disclosed, so those rights may or may not apply. Documented actions show cash returns to shareholders and a pre-combination dividend to the sponsor, and no priming, uptiering, drop-down or collateral release in 2026, although the February 2025 exchange offers stripped covenants and guarantees from original notes that were not tendered. Behaviour is therefore read as mixed on the evidence available, not as extraction-oriented or creditor-adverse. Legal capacity, willingness and feasibility cannot be separated without the credit agreement, the indentures and the stockholders agreement, none of which is in the pack.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no pending acquisition, disposal or merger in the sources, and no new bond issue in 2026. The one financing transaction in the period is the Fifth Amendment of March 31, 2026, which refinanced term loans then outstanding at 3,067.6 into new term loans of 3,090.0 maturing in March 2031, repricing the SOFR margin to 2.75% from 2.25%, while the revolving credit facility of 750.0 is described as maturing in February 2030 subject to a springing maturity whose condition is not stated (Q3 10-Q `page:16`, `page:17`; 10-K `page:156`). The company recorded a loss on modification and extinguishment of debt of 17.7 in nine months 2026. The three note series, the 3.875% Senior Secured Notes due October 31, 2028, the 4.375% Senior Secured Notes due April 30, 2029 and the 6.250% Senior Notes due April 1, 2029, date from exchange offers completed with new indentures on February 12, 2025, in which the issuers co-issued new notes for the original Primo Water Holdings and Triton Water Holdings series; holders who did not tender kept original notes whose covenants were largely removed (10-K `page:158` to `page:160`). Ownership transactions are the other group. The November 8, 2024 combination of BlueTriton and Primo Water was accounted for as an acquisition of Primo Water with BlueTriton as accounting acquirer (10-K `page:146`). The sponsor affiliate sold shares in secondary offerings in March 2025 and May 2025 totalling 99,250,000 shares and again on August 7, 2026, when it sold 20,000,000 shares at 24.37 a share and the company, using cash on hand, bought 410,340 shares from it for about 10.0 while receiving no offering proceeds (10-K `page:46`; Q3 10-Q `page:19`). The status of each is closed. After quarter end, on November 3, 2026, the board declared a dividend of 0.12 a share and approved a new repurchase program of up to 300.0 that replaces a program with 40.8 left (Q3 10-Q `page:39`); this is announced and unexecuted. The company also sold production facilities held for sale for gross proceeds of 41.3 in nine months 2026 and had agreements after December 31, 2025 to sell further facilities for combined proceeds of 38.3 (Q3 10-Q `page:15`; 10-K `page:191`). The nature of the nine-month acquisitions of 48.0 shown in investing cash flow is not described in the sources and stays Insufficient Information. The effect of any of these on leverage is limited: the refinancing raised principal by 22.4, the repurchases consume cash that would otherwise reduce net debt, and the facility sale proceeds are modest against total debt. How the company would fund a larger repurchase while keeping leverage near the level in the financial base is not stated.
