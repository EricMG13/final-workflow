### Credit view

Primo Brands Corporation's fundamental credit view is moderately cautious: a scaled branded-water business with improving cash flow and ample liquidity, but leverage that rests heavily on management add-backs and a cluster of note maturities in 2028 and 2029. The strongest support is USD 1,063.8 million of liquidity at 2026-09-30, USD 625.3 million of operating cash flow in nine months 2026 and a term loan now due in March 2031. The weakest point is that trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,436.1 million is slightly below year-end, leaving leverage at 3.66x gross and 3.32x net, or 4.34x gross before add-backs, while shareholder returns continue. Three things decide the outcome: how freight and materials costs are passed through, whether shareholder returns are balanced against deleveraging, and the refinancing of the 2028 and 2029 notes.

### Key findings

Primo Brands earned slightly less per dollar of revenue in 2026 even as revenue grew. Net sales for nine months 2026 were USD 5,243.3 million, up 2.6%, but Adjusted EBITDA was USD 1,102.0 million, down 1.0%, and the margin was 21.0% against 21.8%. Cost of sales grew faster than sales on freight, materials and depreciation, and the filings flag tariffs and supply chain disruption as continuing cost risks. Q3 2026 was better: net sales rose 3.1% and Adjusted EBITDA rose 1.6% to USD 411.0 million [Financial base, Business context].

Leverage is moderate on the company's definition and materially higher without add-backs. Total debt before unamortized costs was USD 5,249.7 million at 2026-09-30 and unrestricted cash was USD 477.2 million, giving net debt of USD 4,772.5 million. On trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,436.1 million that is 3.66x gross and 3.32x net. On trailing EBITDA of USD 1,209.1 million before USD 227.0 million of add-backs it is 4.34x gross and 3.95x net. Add-backs are shrinking as integration costs wind down, from USD 365.1 million in FY2025 to USD 124.4 million in nine months 2026, but they are still management-defined. Net leverage was 3.37x at year-end 2025 and 3.32x at 2026-09-30, so the improvement is small and came mainly from a USD 100.5 million rise in cash [Financial base].

Cash flow improved, but part of the gain is working capital. Operating cash flow was USD 625.3 million for nine months 2026 against USD 477.2 million, of which USD 141.1 million came from payables and accruals, which can reverse. Free cash flow was USD 319.6 million against USD 203.4 million. Cash interest paid was USD 200.4 million, and trailing Adjusted EBITDA covered trailing cash interest of USD 298.7 million 4.81x [Financial base].

Shareholder returns compete with deleveraging. Dividends of USD 131.2 million and buybacks of USD 70.1 million took USD 201.3 million, 63% of nine-month free cash flow. Over the trailing twelve months dividends and buybacks of USD 364.1 million were about equal to free cash flow of USD 362.1 million. The board approved a new repurchase program of up to USD 300.0 million on 2026-11-03 and declared a dividend of USD 0.12 a share. The sponsor affiliate sold 20,000,000 shares in August 2026 and the company bought back 410,340 shares from it for about USD 10.0 million. The stockholders agreement gives the sponsor approval rights over large dividends, most repurchases and debt that would take total net leverage above 3.5x while it holds at least 30% of the shares; its holding after August 2026 is not disclosed, so whether those rights still apply is unknown [Financial base, Business context].

Liquidity is ample and the maturity wall sits later than the term loan. Liquidity of USD 1,063.8 million is USD 477.2 million of cash plus USD 586.6 million of revolver availability after USD 163.4 million of letters of credit, with the revolver assumed undrawn because the filing speaks of undrawn availability. The term loan was refinanced to March 2031 at a SOFR margin of 2.75%, up from 2.25%. The derived ladder puts USD 532.3 million due in 2028 and USD 1,490.2 million in 2029, mainly the 3.875%, 4.375% and 6.250% notes, against USD 2,943.2 million in 2031. The revolver matures in February 2030 with a springing maturity whose condition is not stated, and the company says it does not expect operating cash flow to repay all debt at maturity [Financial base].

Security and covenants favour the secured lenders and are only partly tested. About 82.3% of debt before unamortized costs, the term loan and the two secured note series, ranks first lien, and 13.6% is the unsecured 6.250% notes (Calculations). The revolver's first lien net leverage covenant of 5.00x and interest coverage covenant of 2.00x are printed, the company states compliance, and neither reported ratio nor definition is supplied. A management-basis proxy puts first lien net leverage at 2.68x and interest coverage on the expense basis at 4.50x, which shows direction only [Financial base, Source readiness].

Business concentration and claims are unsized. One customer was about 21% of FY2025 net sales, regional spring water was 50.0% of nine-month 2026 net sales, and the same category is the subject of the Patane labelling class action. A securities class action and a consumer delivery class action are also pending, and the company estimates no loss beyond amounts accrued. Operating leases of USD 612.1 million are outside the debt figure [Business context, Financial base].

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Scale and brands: a vertically integrated North American network of more than 50 production facilities and more than 200 depots, with regional spring water, purified water and premium water brands, and growth in premium water of 31.7% for nine months 2026 [Documentary Fact; Business context]. Liquidity: USD 1,063.8 million at 2026-09-30, no facility expiring within twelve months, and a term loan refinanced to March 2031 [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Cash generation: free cash flow of USD 319.6 million for nine months 2026, up from USD 203.4 million, and trailing cash interest cover of 4.81x on Adjusted EBITDA [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Falling add-backs: USD 124.4 million for nine months 2026 against USD 262.5 million, as integration winds down [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Creditor protections: first lien security for the term loan and secured notes, a sponsor veto on leverage-increasing debt while the holding is at least 30%, and no priming, uptiering or collateral release in 2026 [Documentary Fact; Business context]. Management states the objective of reducing net leverage [Management Language; Business context].

Weaknesses. Leverage depends on add-backs: 4.34x gross and 3.95x net before add-backs of USD 227.0 million against 3.66x and 3.32x after [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Margin pressure: gross margin of 29.9% against 31.1% for nine months 2026 on freight and materials costs [Documentary Fact; Business context]. Shareholder returns: USD 201.3 million of nine-month dividends and buybacks and a new USD 300.0 million program, alongside a small improvement in net leverage [Documentary Fact; Financial base]. Maturity cluster: derived USD 2,022.5 million due in 2028 and 2029, and a springing maturity on the revolver with no stated condition [Analyst Interpretation; Financial base]. Concentration: one customer at about 21% of FY2025 net sales, with no 2026 update [Documentary Fact; Business context]. Asset quality: goodwill and intangible assets of USD 6,497.3 million are 60.0% of total assets and exceed stockholders' equity of USD 3,048.7 million, so tangible equity is negative [Analyst Interpretation; Calculations]. Unquantified claims: three class actions with no estimate beyond amounts accrued [Documentary Fact; Business context]. Documentation gaps: no credit agreement, indentures or stockholders agreement, so legal capacity to distribute or add debt cannot be tested [Insufficient Information; Source readiness].
