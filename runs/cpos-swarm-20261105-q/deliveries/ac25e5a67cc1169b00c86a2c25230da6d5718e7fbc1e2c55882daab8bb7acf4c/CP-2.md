### Credit view

Qnity Electronics, Inc. has a moderate-leverage, well-funded credit profile with earnings quality that is weaker than the headline ratios suggest. The strongest support is financial: total debt of USD 4,019 million is 2.37x LTM Adjusted Operating EBITDA, liquidity is USD 2,362 million, the revolver is undrawn to October 2030 and no large maturity falls before 2032. The weakest point is the durability of the cash behind those ratios. Demand is volume-driven, cancellable and concentrated in a few customers, adjusted EBITDA leaves out legacy and transformation cash costs, and a 44% share of DuPont legacy liabilities sits outside debt. The outcome will be decided by whether AI-linked volume growth persists while cash after interest, legacy payments and shareholder returns keeps reducing leverage. Covenant headroom cannot be verified from the sources.

### Key findings

First, leverage fell and liquidity is ample. Qnity Electronics, Inc. reports total debt of USD 4,019 million against cash of USD 1,124 million and a fully undrawn USD 1,250 million revolver (USD 1,238 million after letters of credit), so liquidity is USD 2,362 million [CP-1]. Total debt to LTM Adjusted Operating EBITDA is 2.37x, down from 2.79x at FY2025, and net debt to EBITDA is 1.71x against 2.16x. Debt maturities are USD 24 million a year to 2030 and USD 3,980 million after that, so the notes and term loan fall due in 2032 and 2033, beyond the revolver's October 2030 expiry. The mechanism is that EBITDA grew while debt was little changed; the implication is low near-term refinancing risk and a cushion against an earnings shortfall.

Second, the adjusted measures overstate cash available for creditors. For nine months 2026, operating cash flow less capex was USD 357 million, against company adjusted free cash flow of USD 607 million; the USD 250 million difference is transformation cash costs, indirect legacy payments to DuPont and a separation foreign tax cost [CP-1]. Qnity also paid USD 125 million in dividends and buybacks in the same period. Debt reduction therefore depends on cash that is smaller than adjusted figures show and is shared with shareholders, with the restricted payment baskets not known.

Third, revenue visibility is short. Customer contracts carry no long-term purchase commitments, the top ten customers were 34% of 2025 net sales, and most sales are in Asia Pacific [CP-1A]. Growth in 2026 has been primarily volume, attributed by management to AI-driven demand. A cut in orders by one large customer would reach earnings quickly through lost absorption, and leverage is sensitive to that: LTM Adjusted Operating EBITDA would have to fall 21.0% for total debt to reach 3.0x and 32.3% to reach 3.5x (illustrative thresholds, not covenants).

Fourth, claims outside debt are material and growing. Qnity bears 44% of certain DuPont legacy liabilities, including PFAS settlements; the 10-Q states exposure could be up to about USD 100 million above the amount accrued, and a new North Carolina settlement added to the accrual in 2026 [CP-1, CP-1A]. The Tax Matters Agreement can add tax indemnities if the spin-off fails to qualify as tax-free. These are senior-ranking cash claims that sit outside the debt figure.

Fifth, interest is a new and partly floating cost. Interest expense was USD 237 million over the last twelve months and USD 64 million in Q3 2026, so LTM Adjusted Operating EBITDA covers annualised latest-quarter interest 6.62x and LTM interest expense 7.15x [CP-1]. The term loan carries a variable rate, and the annual report states that a 100 basis point change would move annual interest expense by USD 24 million (K p:82); no hedge is reported in the pinned filings. Cash interest paid is not printed for 2026, so a cash-basis cover ratio cannot be built.

Sixth, the capital structure came from the separation. The term loan and note proceeds were paid to DuPont as a cash dividend, and Qnity had no independent board while the separation agreements were negotiated [CP-1A]. There is no sponsor, and no creditor-adverse transaction is documented since; the July 2026 repricing lowered the borrowing margin without changing security or maturity. Capacity to make distributions or incur debt cannot be tested because the credit agreement and indentures are not supplied.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Qnity Electronics, Inc. has moderate and falling leverage: total debt to LTM Adjusted Operating EBITDA is 2.37x and net debt to EBITDA is 1.71x [CP-1; Documentary Fact from a CP-1 figure]. It has deep liquidity of USD 2,362 million, with no committed facility expiring within twelve months and USD 785 million of its USD 1,124 million cash held in foreign subsidiaries, which may limit how much is freely available in the United States [CP-1; Documentary Fact]. Its debt is long-dated, with scheduled amortisation of about USD 24 million a year and the notes and term loan due in 2032 and 2033 [CP-1; Documentary Fact]. Its operating base is growing on volume in both segments, with an LTM Adjusted Operating EBITDA margin of 30.9% on USD 5,490 million of LTM net sales [CP-1, CP-1A; Documentary Fact, with the margin a company measure]. Its products are consumables, over 90% of 2025 revenue came from products used in manufacturing or incorporated in devices, and about 70% of raw materials are sourced locally with no supplier above 10% of raw material spend, which reduces input and tariff risk [CP-1A; Documentary Fact]. There is no sponsor and no documented creditor-adverse act since the separation [CP-1A; Analyst Interpretation].

Weaknesses. Demand is concentrated and cancellable: the top ten customers were 34% of 2025 net sales, two named customers were about a tenth of sales each in 2026, and contracts carry no long-term purchase commitments [CP-1A; Documentary Fact]. Adjusted Operating EBITDA excludes real cash costs, so operating cash flow less capex of USD 357 million trails company adjusted free cash flow of USD 607 million for nine months 2026 [CP-1; Documentary Fact from CP-1 figures]. Claims outside debt include a 44% share of DuPont legacy liabilities, tax indemnities and operating leases of USD 514 million, and the legacy exposure is not capped [CP-1, CP-1A; Documentary Fact]. Shareholder returns of USD 125 million over nine months, with USD 425 million of repurchase authorization remaining, compete with debt reduction [CP-1; Documentary Fact]. The term loan is floating-rate and no hedge is reported, so a 100 basis point move changes interest expense by USD 24 million a year [K p:82; Documentary Fact, added here]. Qnity has about eleven months of stand-alone results at September 30, 2026, so the trend in interest, tax and cost lines is not like-for-like with the carve-out periods [CP-0, CP-1; Analyst Interpretation]. Management's descriptions of itself as the premier provider and of consecutive profitable growth are Management Language and are not adopted [CP-1A].
