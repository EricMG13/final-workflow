### Business and transaction view

Qnity Electronics, Inc. is a Delaware-incorporated supplier of consumable materials to semiconductor fabrication and to electronics interconnect, reported in two segments, Semiconductor Technologies and Interconnect Solutions. It became independent on November 1, 2025 through a pro-rata distribution by DuPont de Nemours, Inc., and its shares have traded on the New York Stock Exchange since November 3, 2025. The credit-relevant structure is a stand-alone, widely held company with no sponsor, whose debt was raised just before the distribution, at the issuer level with subsidiary guarantees, and was paid out to the former parent. The debt amount was set in connection with the separation. Operating strength is evident in the filings: volume-led sales growth in both segments, driven by AI-linked demand in advanced nodes, advanced packaging and thermal management, and a consumable revenue model. Two features constrain the creditor view. First, customer contracts carry no long-term purchase commitments and the top ten customers are concentrated, so revenue visibility rests on customer forecasts that can be cancelled. Second, Qnity has contractually taken on a share of DuPont's legacy liabilities and some tax exposure under agreements negotiated before it had an independent board. Post-separation behaviour is documented as a quarterly dividend, a share repurchase programme, a borrower-favourable term loan repricing and debt amortization. No document shows priming, uptiering, drop-downs or collateral release. Missing is any executed document or management presentation, so terms and management's own capital allocation priorities are visible only through filing summaries.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The defining transaction is the separation of Qnity from DuPont de Nemours, Inc., announced on May 22, 2024 and completed on November 1, 2025 by a pro-rata distribution of one Qnity share for every two DuPont shares held on the record date of October 22, 2025 (K p:6). Qnity common stock began trading on the New York Stock Exchange on November 3, 2025. There is no sponsor and no change-of-control transaction in the sources, and no acquisition or disposal of a business is reported in the 2026 filings read for this module. The annual report states that Qnity may pursue bolt-on and strategic acquisitions (K p:17), which is intent, not an event.

New debt was raised in two steps before the distribution. On August 15, 2025 the issuer sold senior secured notes due 2032 and senior unsecured notes due 2033, each guaranteed by every subsidiary that is a borrower or guarantor under the credit facilities, with the secured notes sharing first-priority liens pari passu with the credit facilities (K p:116 to p:118). On October 31, 2025 it entered a credit agreement for a seven-year term loan and a five-year revolving facility, and the annual report states that the full term loan, together with the notes proceeds, was paid directly to DuPont as a cash dividend in connection with the separation (K p:119). The aggregate principal incurred in connection with the spin-off is stated as 4.1 billion (K p:43), and the financial base handoff carries the instrument-level terms, the maturities and the liquidity position. The use of proceeds is therefore a documented distribution to the former parent, recorded in the cash flow statement as net transfers to the parent. This is a debt-funded distribution at separation, made when the parent still controlled the subsidiary and set its terms; the annual report states Qnity had no independent board or management team representing its interests while the separation agreements were negotiated (K p:45).

Since the distribution the documented actions are a quarterly dividend, a share repurchase authorization, a repricing of the term loan and scheduled amortization. The repricing, effective July 1, 2026, cut the margin on term SOFR borrowings from 2.00% to 1.75% with no material change to principal, maturity, amortization or covenant structure, and carries a 1.00% premium on repricing events for six months (Q3 p:20); it is a borrower-favourable amendment to a creditor-owned instrument, not a creditor-adverse one. The amendment document itself is not supplied. Liquidity effect: the financial base shows an undrawn revolver and cash, with no facility expiring within twelve months. Status of every transaction item is final and closed; the only open items are contingent legacy payments to DuPont described under governance below.
