### Credit view

QXO, Inc. is a liquid but heavily acquisitive credit whose leverage cannot yet be measured on the enlarged group, so the fundamental view is cautious and conditional on integration. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 4,704 million at June 30, 2026, no scheduled principal on that debt before 2030 and a customer base with no customer above 1% of sales. The weakest point is that borrowings of USD 6,111 million, rising to USD 9,111 million after the July 1, 2026 term loan, are measured against last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA of USD 724.8 million that excludes TopBuild, 52.3% of which comes from add-backs. What decides the outcome is whether TopBuild and Kodiak earnings arrive and whether the add-backs fade. [CP-1, CP-1A]

Thesis (Analyst Interpretation): QXO, Inc. has funded a roll-up of three businesses with a mix of preferred equity, shares and USD 9,111 million of borrowings, and its credit quality is set by whether the enlarged group converts that scale into cash earnings that comfortably exceed its interest and preferred dividend burden. [CP-1, CP-1A]

### Key findings

1. Borrowings have nearly doubled and the earnings base has not caught up (Documentary Fact for the amounts, Analyst Interpretation for the reading). QXO, Inc.'s borrowings were USD 3,100.0 million at December 31, 2025 and March 31, 2026, USD 6,111 million at June 30, 2026 after USD 3,000 million of notes were sold into escrow for TopBuild, and USD 9,111 million after the USD 3,000 million incremental term loan on July 1, 2026. Reported gross leverage at June 30, 2026 was 8.43x and net leverage 4.60x, but the earnings measure of USD 724.8 million has no TopBuild contribution and only three months of Kodiak, while the debt includes the TopBuild financing. The mechanism is a timing mismatch between debt and earnings; the implication is that these ratios are not a steady-state reading and the third-quarter 2026 report is the next decisive evidence. [CP-1, CP-1A]
2. Adjusted EBITDA relies on large add-backs (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on recurrence). Of USD 724.8 million of last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA, USD 379.1 million (52.3%) is add-backs, including USD 127.5 million of stock compensation, USD 78.7 million of transaction costs, USD 68.9 million of transformation costs, USD 51.7 million of inventory fair value and USD 48.6 million of restructuring. EBITDA before the add-backs is USD 345.7 million, which gives gross leverage of 17.68x and net leverage of 9.65x at June 30, 2026. Restructuring, transaction and transformation items appear in each period reported, so QXO, Inc. may keep incurring them while it integrates three businesses; if they recur, leverage tolerance is weaker than the adjusted figures imply. [CP-1]
3. Interest cover is flattered by the timing of coupons (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation). Last-twelve-month cash interest was USD 221.0 million, giving cover of 3.28x, and cover was 2.58x in the six months to June 30, 2026. The coupons on the June 30, 2026 borrowings at printed rates are USD 401.0 million a year, so cover on that run-rate is about 1.81x before the incremental term loan, whose margin is not supplied, and before any TopBuild earnings. If the incremental loan were priced like the existing term loan, run-rate interest would be about USD 570.2 million and cover about 1.27x on earnings excluding TopBuild. This is an illustrative floor, not an estimate: it shows how much of the credit depends on TopBuild earnings. [CP-1]
4. Cash conversion is thin in the first half and after preferred dividends (Documentary Fact). Cash from operations was negative USD 146 million in the first half of 2026 as receivables and inventory built, and LTM cash from operations less capital expenditures was USD 141.2 million against preferred dividends paid of USD 143.8 million, leaving USD -2.6 million. The first quarter is seasonally weak and the company reports a net loss of USD 511.4 million over the period. [CP-1, CP-1A]
5. Liquidity and maturities are the main support (Documentary Fact). Liquidity was USD 4,704 million at June 30, 2026 (cash USD 2,774 million plus asset-based revolver availability USD 1,930 million), excluding USD 3,000 million of escrowed proceeds that funded the TopBuild closing. No scheduled principal on the June 30, 2026 borrowings falls due before the revolver's 2030 maturity (USD 11 million drawn); the maturities are USD 1,500 million in 2031, USD 3,100 million in 2032, USD 3,000 million in 2033 (the incremental loan) and USD 1,500 million in 2034. The springing fixed charge test on the revolver is not live, as availability of USD 1,930 million compares with a trigger of at most USD 200 million. The implication is low refinancing risk through 2030, but post-closing cash is Not supplied. [CP-1]
6. Rate exposure and claims ahead of or beside the debt are rising (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation). Floating-rate borrowings are 14.1% of June 30, 2026 borrowings and 42.4% including the incremental term loan, so 100 basis points adds about USD 38.6 million of annual interest. The USD 3,000 million of 2031 and 2034 notes are unsecured behind the secured revolver, term loans and 2032 notes, and the group has senior-to-common preferred stock whose dividends could reach USD 264.1 million a year. Series C holders can require cash redemption on a fundamental change, an event that also triggers a 101% put on the notes, and the amounts are not netted in any source. [CP-1, CP-1A]
7. Governance is documented but not fully assessable (Documentary Fact; Insufficient Information). Documented actions to date are a Kodiak purchase funded with preferred stock and shares, no common dividends and preferred dividends paid in cash, with an option to pay Series C dividends in shares. Voting power is concentrated in the chairman at about 22.1% and a 40% board designation right sits with his investor group, as of the annual report. Related-party arrangements, executed covenant definitions and current ownership percentages after the TopBuild share issuance are Not supplied. [CP-1A]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths.

Liquidity cushion (Documentary Fact). QXO, Inc. held USD 4,704 million of liquidity at June 30, 2026, against no scheduled principal on that debt until 2030, which gives it time to integrate before a refinancing wall. [CP-1]

Maturity and rate mix (Documentary Fact). 85.9% of June 30, 2026 borrowings were fixed-rate and the earliest large maturity is USD 1,500 million in 2031, which limits both refinancing and rate pressure until the incremental term loan lifts the floating share to 42.4%. [CP-1]

Customer and demand profile (Documentary Fact; Management Language on demand). No customer exceeded 1% of net sales and management describes repair and re-roofing demand as largely non-discretionary, which supports revenue visibility but is management characterisation, not audited fact. [CP-1A]

Equity-funded growth to date (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on cash retention). Kodiak was paid for with preferred stock and shares, with the target's own debt repaid at closing, and common dividends are not being paid, which retained cash inside the group. [CP-1A]

Covenant headroom (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation). The only described maintenance test is not live, with availability of USD 1,930 million against a trigger of at most USD 200 million, so covenant pressure is low until availability falls sharply. [CP-1]

Weaknesses.

Leverage unmeasured after closing (Insufficient Information). Borrowings of USD 9,111 million after the July 1, 2026 term loan have no matching reported earnings; the missing datapoints are TopBuild earnings and cash after closing, and they change every ratio. [CP-1, CP-1A]

Earnings quality (Analyst Interpretation). Add-backs make up 52.3% of Adjusted EBITDA, so management's adjusted figure depends on items that recur during integration. Last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA margin of 7.3% sits below the 9.5% reported for FY2025, although the FY2025 base holds Beacon only from April 29, 2025. [CP-1]

Cash flow after claims (Documentary Fact). Last-twelve-month cash from operations less capital expenditures of USD 141.2 million does not cover preferred dividends paid of USD 143.8 million, and the preferred burden can rise to USD 264.1 million a year. [CP-1, CP-1A]

Integration load (Analyst Interpretation). QXO, Inc. is integrating Beacon, Kodiak and TopBuild at once with TopBuild accounting incomplete, and management's plan to more than double EBITDA is management language that no filing figure yet confirms. [CP-1A]

Concentrations and control (Documentary Fact; Insufficient Information). Three suppliers made up nearly 35% of purchases, the business reports one operating segment so no segment profit is available, and control rights are concentrated; whether control is used against creditors is not evidenced either way. [CP-1A]

## Calculations

All figures are from the financial base [CP-1] unless marked; US dollars in millions.

1. Add-back share = add-backs 379.1 / LTM Adjusted EBITDA 724.8 = 52.3%. EBITDA before add-backs 345.7 / 724.8 = 47.7%. Leverage on EBITDA before add-backs: 6,111 / 345.7 = 17.68x gross and 3,337 / 345.7 = 9.65x net (CP-1 calculation 10).
2. EBITDA less capital expenditures = 724.8 - 112.2 = 612.6; over LTM cash interest 221.0 = 2.77x.
3. Cash from operations less capital expenditures, less preferred dividends paid: 141.2 - 143.8 = -2.6.
4. Fixed-rate share = (1,500 + 2,250 + 1,500) / 6,111 = 5,250 / 6,111 = 85.9%; floating 14.1%. After the incremental term loan, floating = (850 + 11 + 3,000) / 9,111 = 3,861 / 9,111 = 42.4%; fixed 5,250 / 9,111 = 57.6%. A 100 basis point rise on 3,861 = 38.6. The incremental loan is assumed floating because the filing states Term SOFR or base rate pricing.
5. Run-rate coupon on June 30, 2026 borrowings = 850 x 5.64% + 2,250 x 6.75% + 1,500 x 6.500% + 1,500 x 6.875% + 11 x 4.70% = 401.0 (CP-1 calculation 6). Cover on that run-rate = 724.8 / 401.0 = 1.81x. Illustrative: incremental term loan priced at the existing term loan rate of 5.64% (an assumption; the filing does not state the margin) = 3,000 x 5.64% = 169.2; run-rate 401.0 + 169.2 = 570.2; cover 724.8 / 570.2 = 1.27x. Both exclude interest income, commitment fees and any TopBuild earnings.
6. Illustrative earnings needed, not a covenant or target: gross leverage of 6.0x on 9,111 needs EBITDA of 1,518.5, which is 793.7 above 724.8; 5.0x needs 1,822.2, which is 1,097.4 above 724.8.
7. Secured share of borrowings after closing, assuming the incremental term loan carries the term loan's security (the filing says it is incurred under the same credit agreement): revolver 11 + term loan 850 + incremental 3,000 + 2032 notes 2,250 = 6,111, of 9,111 = 67.1%; unsecured notes 3,000 / 9,111 = 32.9%.
8. Preferred dividend ceiling 264.1 and Series C stated value 3,000 are from CP-1A calculations 4 and 5.
