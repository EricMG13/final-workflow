### Business and transaction view

The credit story is an acquisition-led roll-up. QXO changed from a small software business into a roofing distributor on April 29, 2025 by buying Beacon, added Kodiak (lumber, trusses, windows and doors, construction supplies) on April 1, 2026, and agreed on April 18, 2026 to buy TopBuild (insulation distribution and installation), which closed on July 1, 2026, after the latest balance sheet date. The operating business is a branch-based distributor with a diversified customer base, no customer above 1% of sales, concentrated suppliers, strong seasonality and results driven by repair and re-roofing demand that management describes as largely non-discretionary. Each deal was paid for with a different mix: Kodiak with preferred stock and shares while its own debt was repaid, TopBuild with new debt, preferred stock and shares. Debt sits at the operating subsidiary; preferred stock, which carries senior-to-common cash dividends and a cash redemption right on a fundamental change for the Series C holders, sits at the parent. For a creditor the questions are integration execution on three businesses at once, how much of the added earnings reach the borrower's cash flow, and how control rights and preferred claims are used. The sources describe actions to date but not the executed covenant packages or any related-party arrangements, so governance conclusions are bounded.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Beacon (closed April 29, 2025). Under a merger agreement dated March 20, 2025, QXO acquired Beacon for a net purchase price that the June 2026 quarterly report gives as 10.64 billion (10-Q Q2 2026, page:53). Financing signed on April 29, 2025 comprised a 2.25 billion term loan, 2.25 billion of 6.75% senior secured notes due 2032 and an asset-based revolver of up to 2.0 billion, alongside equity raised in 2024 and 2025; the pages read do not give a full sources and uses table. A voluntary 1.40 billion term loan prepayment followed on May 29, 2025, and the quarterly report states that the equity proceeds of May 2025 were used to repay term loan debt (10-Q Q2 2026, page:21 and page:48). The term loan margin was reduced in November 2025. Beacon is treated as the predecessor for financial reporting (10-K, page:67).

Kodiak (closed April 1, 2026). QXO agreed on February 10, 2026 to buy Kodiak from Court Square Capital Partners and closed on April 1, 2026 for a net purchase price of 2.22 billion according to the quarterly report; the annual report's earlier figure was approximately 2.25 billion and the share count is stated as 13.2 million and 13.3 million in different places (logged in conflicts below, not reconciled). Consideration was cash of 478 to sellers, 1,503 to settle Kodiak's secured term loan and asset-based facility including accrued interest, and 257 of QXO shares. Series C proceeds funded a portion of the cash: 200,000 shares of Series C Preferred Stock were issued for 2.0 billion gross proceeds to funds managed by affiliates of Apollo Global Management, Inc. Kodiak added net sales of 595 and net income of 15 in the second quarter of 2026. Status: complete, with purchase accounting preliminary.

TopBuild (closed July 1, 2026, a subsequent event for the June 30, 2026 balance sheet). The merger agreement dated April 18, 2026 values the deal at approximately 15 billion in cash and stock. About 91.0% of TopBuild holders elected cash, and after proration each TopBuild share received about 249.67 in cash and 10.212 QXO shares; QXO issued about 312.0 million shares and paid about 6.4 billion in cash. Use of proceeds named in the filing: 3.0 billion of senior notes (6.500% due July 15, 2031 and 6.875% due July 15, 2034) sold on June 17, 2026 into a segregated escrow and released on closing; a 3.0 billion incremental term loan under the existing term loan agreement maturing July 1, 2033; and 1.0 billion from the remaining 100,000 shares of Series C Preferred Stock, which exhausted the 3.0 billion Series C commitment. The filing says the initial accounting is incomplete and that preliminary purchase price allocation is expected in the quarterly report for the period ending September 30, 2026. It does not say whether TopBuild's own debt was assumed or repaid, what cash TopBuild brought, or the cash left after closing; these are Insufficient Information and matter directly for liquidity and leverage.

New debt issues, summarised for the creditor. The 2031 and 2034 notes are guaranteed by the subsidiaries that guarantee the term loan and the 2032 notes and are unsecured after the escrow release, so they rank behind the secured term loan, secured notes and asset-based revolver on the collateral; each carries a 101% put on specified change of control and, for these two series, ratings events. The incremental term loan is floating rate with a margin not stated in the pages read, 1% annual amortisation and a 1.00% repricing premium for six months. Preferred stock raised alongside the debt adds senior-to-common claims at the parent. Quantities are in the financial base handoff. Liquidity effect: the 3,000 of escrow cash was excluded from liquidity of 4,704 at June 30, 2026 and was spent on closing, so the post-closing liquidity position is Not supplied.

### Credit translations

1. Acquisition-led growth. Evidence: three acquisitions in about fourteen months and 6,111 of borrowings at June 30, 2026 before a further 3,000 incremental term loan (from the financial base). Risk mechanic: earnings from Kodiak and TopBuild must replace the cash flow burdened by new interest, preferred dividends and integration and transaction costs; first-half 2026 operating cash flow was negative and trailing Adjusted EBITDA excludes TopBuild. Credit implication: leverage tolerance and debt service depend on integration delivery that is not yet visible in reported figures; monitoring posture should treat the third-quarter 2026 report as the next decisive evidence. Countering evidence: liquidity of 4,704 before closing and no scheduled principal on the June 30, 2026 debt before 2030.
2. Revenue durability. Evidence: no customer above 1% of sales, repair and re-roofing share described by management as non-discretionary, 97% U.S. sales. Mechanic: demand tied to weather and the housing stock, but first-quarter earnings are near nil, so cash timing is seasonal. Implication: revenue is durable at the customer level; working capital and liquidity must carry the first quarter. Countering: supplier concentration of nearly 35% in three suppliers and vendor rebate dependence.
3. Margin and cash flow. Evidence: product cost increases outpaced selling price increases in 2026, and the company adds back large restructuring, transaction and transformation costs (financial base). Mechanic: incomplete pass-through of product costs compresses gross margin. Implication: cash conversion and the quality of Adjusted EBITDA are the key uncertainties, not top-line growth.
4. Claims ahead of and beside debt. Evidence: Series C holders can require cash redemption at the greater of stated value plus accrued dividends and as-converted value on a fundamental change, alongside the 101% put on the notes. Mechanic: a change of control triggers both a debt put and a preferred cash call on the same event. Implication: the preferred cash call and the notes put arise from the same event and the amounts are not netted in any source; the Series C stated value of 3,000 compares with liquidity of 4,704 before closing (see Calculations). This is a mechanic, not a recovery view.
5. Control and alignment. Evidence: voting concentration and board designation rights. Mechanic: control rights can influence capital allocation and approval of extraordinary transactions. Implication: willingness cannot be inferred from ownership identity; the documented behaviour to date is equity-funded growth and no common dividends, which is supportive, but the absence of executed covenant documents leaves capacity unassessed. Countering: none logged.
