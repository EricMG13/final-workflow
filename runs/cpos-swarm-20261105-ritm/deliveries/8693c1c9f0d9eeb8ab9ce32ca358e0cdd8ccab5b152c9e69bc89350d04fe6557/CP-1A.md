### Business and transaction view

Rithm Capital Corp. is a publicly listed, internally managed REIT that describes itself as a global asset manager focused on real estate, credit and financial services (10-K page:8). It is not a sponsor-owned credit; the pack names no controlling shareholder and the Part III ownership information is incorporated by reference from a proxy statement that is not in the pack (10-K page:3). The operating model is an integrated platform of five reported segments: Origination and Servicing (Newrez and NRM), Residential Transitional Lending (Genesis), Asset Management (Sculptor, Crestline and Rithm Advisers), Investment Portfolio and Commercial Real Estate (Elecor) (10-Q Q3 page:14 to page:16). Origination and Servicing is the largest business by assets, equity and earnings contribution (10-Q Q3 page:132); in the nine months to September 30, 2026 it produced US$968,563 thousand of segment pre-tax income against group pre-tax income of US$760,219 thousand, so the other segments and the corporate category net to a loss (10-Q Q3 page:25). The credit mechanic is that revenue in the largest segment depends on the fair value of mortgage servicing rights and on origination margins, both rate-sensitive, while funding is mostly secured and short-dated (CP-1). The December 2025 acquisitions of Crestline (cash consideration US$324.7 million, bringing insurance and reinsurance strategies and interest-sensitive insurance contract liabilities) and Paramount, now Elecor (total consideration approximately US$1.8 billion, 12.2 million square feet of Class A offices in New York City and San Francisco) are the main credit-relevant change in history (10-Q Q3 page:20; page:150). They bring fixed operating costs, depreciation and property debt: depreciation and amortization rose to US$278,619 thousand in nine months 2026 from US$71,492 thousand (10-Q Q3 page:8). Asset Management and Commercial Real Estate both reported pre-tax losses in the nine months (10-Q Q3 page:25). No leveraged buyout, sponsor recapitalisation, priming, uptiering or collateral release is documented in the pack. The financing actions documented are plain senior unsecured note issues, a preferred equity issue and securitizations, set out in the transaction summary below. What cannot be assessed is legal capacity under the notes, because the indentures are not supplied.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

There is no sponsor transaction, buyout or recapitalisation in the pack; the context lists no deal terms. The transactions below are the issuer's own financings and acquisitions, with status and liquidity effect.

On May 14, 2026 Rithm Capital Corp. issued US$500.0 million of 8.500% senior unsecured notes due June 1, 2031 in a private offering, for net proceeds of approximately US$493.7 million, under an indenture with U.S. Bank Trust Company, National Association; interest is payable on June 1 and December 1 from December 1, 2026; the notes are redeemable from June 1, 2028 at 104.250 stepping to 100.000 from 2030, and before then at a make-whole price or up to 40% at 108.500 with equity proceeds; they rank equally with the Company's other senior unsecured debt and were not guaranteed by any subsidiary at issuance (10-Q Q3 page:58 to page:59). The 10-Q does not state the use of proceeds in the text read; the nine-month cash flow statement shows repayment of unsecured notes of US$18,326 thousand and no large unsecured repayment, so the issue increased unsecured debt (10-Q Q3 page:13). Status: closed and outstanding. Liquidity effect: adds to cash and extends the unsecured profile to 2031; it also adds interest cost at 8.500%. Earlier senior notes: US$500.0 million of 8.000% notes due July 15, 2030 issued June 20, 2025, and US$775.0 million of 8.000% notes due April 1, 2029 issued March 19, 2024 (10-Q Q3 page:168); the notes due October 15, 2025 were redeemed in the second quarter of 2025 (10-Q Q3 page:63). The three outstanding notes each carry a requirement that Total Unencumbered Assets be not less than 120% of outstanding unsecured debt, and the 2029 and 2030 notes carry a 101% repurchase right on a change of control or Mortgage Business Triggering Event (10-Q Q3 page:61; page:63; page:168).

On January 13, 2026 Rithm priced 10,000,000 shares of 8.750% Series F Fixed-Rate Reset Cumulative Redeemable Preferred Stock with a US$25.00 liquidation preference for net proceeds of approximately US$242.1 million (10-Q Q3 page:111). The Series D dividend rate resets on November 15, 2026 to the five-year Treasury rate plus 6.223% (10-Q Q3 page:174); this is a future-dated term, not a fact about Q3 2026.

On December 1, 2025 Rithm completed the acquisition of Crestline for cash consideration of US$324.7 million, recognising goodwill of approximately US$182.8 million; it brought insurance company investments at fair value of US$844,822 thousand and interest sensitive insurance contract liabilities of US$920,776 thousand (10-Q Q3 page:20). The 10-K cash flow statement shows "Acquisition of Paramount, net of cash acquired" of US$903,606 thousand and non-cash "Assumed financing in Paramount transaction" of US$3,706,617 thousand (10-K page:232 to page:233); the mix of cash, equity and assumed debt inside the US$1.8 billion consideration is not stated in the text read, so that composition is Insufficient Information and the two cash-flow figures are not reconciled to it here. During Q3 2026 Elecor sold a 49% interest in a CRE joint venture to an unrelated third party, deconsolidated it and recognised a gain of US$26.8 million; the Note 17 roll-forward shows US$900,000 thousand of secured notes on real estate deconsolidated in the nine months (10-Q Q3 page:38; page:56). ### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: Origination and Servicing earned the large majority of nine-month 2026 segment pre-tax income, and change in fair value of MSRs net of economic hedges was a gain in Q3 2026 and a loss in nine months 2026 (10-Q Q3 page:8; page:25). Mechanic: servicing and origination revenue depends on interest rate and prepayment moves and origination volume, so reported income can swing without a change in cash earnings. Implication: coverage of funding costs is less predictable than for a fee-based or contract-based issuer; committee should look at cash servicing fees separately from fair value changes. A logged conflict that counters this: none. A limitation that counters it: the nine months include acquisition effects.
2. Business mix shift. Evidence: Asset Management and Commercial Real Estate reported pre-tax losses in nine months 2026 while depreciation and amortization rose (10-Q Q3 page:8; page:25). Mechanic: acquired real estate and an asset manager add fixed costs, interest expense on property debt and depreciation before fee or rent growth scales. Implication: near-term earnings support for debt service from the new businesses is unproven; progress depends on leasing (occupancy 83.5% against 85.3% at December 31, 2025, recalculated for the joint venture sale, 10-Q Q3 page:150) and AUM inflows (management language, release Q3 page:1 to page:2).
3. Structural position. Evidence: the senior notes are unsecured and were not guaranteed by any subsidiary at issuance, while much of the debt is secured or sits in consolidated non-recourse entities (10-Q Q3 page:56; page:59; CP-1). Mechanic: parent noteholders have claims on parent assets and distributions from subsidiaries after subsidiary creditors. Implication: loss given default for the notes depends on residual value after secured and subsidiary creditors; no recovery conclusion is given here.
4. Sponsor alignment. Evidence: no sponsor; internally managed; insiders invest alongside clients (10-Q Q3 page:121). Mechanic: the owner base is public shareholders and management, so there is no sponsor extraction channel documented, but dividends are required for REIT status. Implication: neutral on documented actions; ownership concentration is Insufficient Information.
5. Key uncertainties: legal capacity under the notes, the composition of the Paramount consideration, and ratings, all missing from the pack.
