### Business and transaction view

SERVICE CORP INTERNATIONAL is the largest listed deathcare operator in North America by its own account, running 1,491 funeral service locations and 506 cemeteries at September 30, 2026, of which 317 combine both. It has no sponsor, no controlling holder disclosed in the sources and no pending or completed transaction in the period under review, so the credit question is operating durability and capital allocation rather than deal structure. The business model sells at the time of need and in advance. Advance sales build a deferred revenue backlog, 17.72 billion US dollars at September 30, 2026, whose revenue is recognised mainly at death or delivery, so cash and revenue are separated in time and part of the backlog (insurance-funded, 9.15 billion US dollars) is not on the balance sheet. Pricing power and cemetery growth currently offset lower funeral volumes. Management keeps a leverage target of 3.5x to 4.0x and returns cash through dividends and buybacks within covenant limits that are not supplied. For creditors the key facts are the scale and diversification of the network, the visibility of the backlog, the concentration in three states, the trust and surety structure that sits behind preneed sales, and a consistent pattern of returning cash to shareholders through dividends and buybacks. The main open items are ownership and board detail (proxy statement not supplied), the credit agreement payment restrictions, and the ratings that are not in the sources.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

No acquisition of the issuer, sponsor investment, merger, spin-off or recapitalisation is described in the supplied sources, and the context lists no deal terms, quotes or ratings. The transaction row is therefore Insufficient Information, not an adverse conclusion. The financing events in the period under review are ordinary-course. In November 2025 the company amended its 2.2 billion US dollar credit agreement due January 2028 into a new 2.5 billion US dollar bank credit agreement due November 2030, made up of a 1.75 billion US dollar revolver (with a 100.0 million sublimit for letters of credit) and a 750.0 million US dollar term loan; at closing the term loan rose by a net 121.4 million and the revolver fell by a net 115.0 million (10-K page:102). The agreement is guaranteed by a majority of domestic subsidiaries and has a maximum leverage ratio and certain dividend and share repurchase restrictions. In September 2024 it issued 800.0 million US dollars of unsecured 5.750% Senior Notes due October 2032 (10-K page:102, page:123). A corporate headquarters debt facility due February 2037 funds the new headquarters; 110,852 thousand was drawn at September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:18), and its commitment size and terms are Not supplied. In the nine months to September 30, 2026 the company issued or added 586.1 million US dollars of debt, all on the revolver (530.0 million) and the headquarters facility (56.1 million), and paid 570.7 million, including 550.0 million on the revolver (Q3 10-Q page:19); no senior notes were issued or redeemed. On June 11, 2026 the board raised the repurchase authorization by about 472.0 million US dollars, and 498.8 million remained at September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:20). Subsequent to September 30, 2026 the company repurchased 232,968 shares for 18.2 million US dollars (Q3 10-Q page:20). The 7.500% Senior Notes due April 2027 are the nearest maturity and are within the current maturities of 195,060 thousand (CP-1). No new debt issue, refinancing or amendment dated after September 30, 2026 is in the sources.

### Credit translations

- **Revenue durability.** Evidence: backlog of 17.72 billion US dollars at September 30, 2026 and recognised preneed cemetery property revenue growth (Q3 10-Q page:31, page:40). Risk mechanic: revenue is recognised from earlier sales, which lowers volatility, but insurance-funded backlog is outside the balance sheet and future revenue exceeds trust assets (Q3 10-Q page:31). Credit implication: supports debt service visibility; the counter-evidence is falling funeral volume (nine-month services performed down 2.7%) and the lowered outlook, so the stability is revenue-side, not margin-side. No logged conflict counters this.
- **Margin and cash-flow risk.** Evidence: Q3 and nine-month gross margin fell while selling compensation, advertising and health insurance costs rose (Q3 10-Q page:38, page:40; CP-1 calculation C12). Risk mechanic: a high fixed-cost base and front-loaded selling costs on preneed growth cut near-term margin. Credit implication: coverage ratios could ease if volume keeps falling; the covenant ratio reported at 3.69 is below its 5.00 maximum, but its definition is Not supplied, and the conflict on floating-rate cost (below) limits interest-cost conclusions.
- **Concentration risk.** Evidence: California, Texas and Florida are three of the largest states by revenue and hold 32.2% of locations (10-K page:25, page:11; C2). Risk mechanic: a single catastrophe or regulatory change in these states hits many locations at once. Credit implication: insurance and surety capacity matter; revenue by state is Not supplied.
- **Sponsor and capital allocation alignment.** Evidence: dividend payout target, buyback pace and leverage target (10-K page:18; Q3 10-Q page:20, page:25). Risk mechanic: returns to shareholders are discretionary and sized to cash flow; in FY2025 they exceeded operating cash flow less capex. Credit implication: creditors rely on management policy and covenant restrictions rather than a sponsor commitment; the restrictions are Not supplied, so legal capacity to increase distributions cannot be assessed.
- **Key uncertainties.** Ratings, credit agreement terms, guarantor and security detail for the notes, ownership percentages and the proxy statement are not in the sources.
