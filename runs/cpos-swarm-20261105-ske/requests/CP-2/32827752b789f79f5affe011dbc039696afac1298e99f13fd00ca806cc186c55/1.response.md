### Credit view

Skeena is a pre-revenue, single-asset construction credit that is funded for now but unproven, and the evidence supports a cautious, not an adverse, view. The strongest support is liquidity: CAD 134,808 thousand of unrestricted cash and CAD 500,853 thousand of restricted cash at June 30, 2026, a US$750,000,000 fixed-rate note due April 1, 2031 and an interest reserve covering three semi-annual payments. The weakest point is that no earnings or cost-to-complete figure exists, so leverage, cover and funding adequacy cannot be measured. The outcome turns on delivery of Eskay Creek and the September 30, 2027 completion test, on release of restricted cash, and on the notes' legal terms, which are not in the pack.

### Key findings

Skeena has no revenue and reports no EBITDA, so every earnings-based measure is Not supplied and the credit view rests on funding and project delivery [CP-1, financial base; Not calculable]. At June 30, 2026 the company held CAD 134,808 thousand of unrestricted cash and CAD 500,853 thousand of restricted cash, with CAD 316,513 thousand of undiscounted financial liabilities and commitments due within one year; the restricted balance is what makes the near-term picture covered, and its release conditions are not supplied [CP-1, liquidity and maturity table; CP-1A, transaction summary].

The April 10, 2026 notes offering is the pivot of the credit. It replaced the terminated US$350,000,000 term loan, funded the Gold Stream buy-down of US$184,000,000 and placed an interest reserve and a construction disbursement account in restricted cash. The notes carry 8.50% interest, mature on April 1, 2031 and are carried at CAD 1,055,969 thousand net of an embedded derivative asset, or CAD 1,082,552 thousand gross; their trading value implies a fair value of CAD 1,119,655 thousand, which exceeds both figures [CP-1, debt and financing instruments]. The company says proceeds should fund capital needs through commencement of commercial production in 2027 only if the conditions precedent are met; that is management language and the conditions are not supplied [CP-1A, transaction summary; Management Language].

Other claims sit beside the notes. The Gold Stream is a derivative liability of CAD 209,286 thousand and ranks pari passu with the notes; the two royalty liabilities are CAD 78,709 thousand and CAD 136,009 thousand; lease liabilities are CAD 66,483 thousand; and the closure and reclamation provision rose to CAD 104,777 thousand from CAD 46,227 thousand at December 31, 2025 [CP-1, claims outside the debt figure]. Total liabilities of CAD 1,746,295 thousand exceed shareholders' equity of CAD 170,801 thousand. A surety bond provides reclamation security, with collateral of unstated amount; this corrects the earlier financial base statement that none was found [CP-1A, correction].

The business is concentrated in one project. Management says Eskay Creek is fully permitted and expects commercial production in 2027, but those are management statements and the retained filings still list permit challenges as a risk [CP-1A, company and operating model; Management Language]. Documented issuer behaviour is equity-funded and supportive of funding, with bought deals in February 2025 and October 2025, and no creditor-adverse action is documented; no sponsor or controlling holder is identified [CP-1A, ownership and issuer behaviour].

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Skeena has pre-funded its build: restricted cash of CAD 500,853 thousand, held for construction and interest, sits beside unrestricted cash of CAD 134,808 thousand, and both together of CAD 635,661 thousand exceed the CAD 316,513 thousand of undiscounted obligations due within one year [CP-1; Analyst Interpretation, because release of restricted funds is conditional]. The debt is a single fixed-rate bullet maturing on April 1, 2031 with no maturity inside the construction window other than interest, and the interest reserve covers three semi-annual payments [CP-1, debt and financing instruments; Documentary Fact]. The buy-down cut the Gold Stream from 10.55% to 3.52% of payable gold, reducing the stream's claim on future output [CP-1A, Gold Stream; Documentary Fact]. Equity markets were open to the company in 2025 and the company has documented no debt-funded distributions [CP-1A, ownership and issuer behaviour; Documentary Fact]. The notes' trading value exceeds their carrying amount, which is consistent with, but does not prove, market acceptance of the credit [CP-1; Analyst Interpretation].

Weaknesses. Skeena has no revenue, no EBITDA and no operating cash flow: six-month operating cash outflow was CAD 30,235 thousand and additions to mineral property, plant and equipment were CAD 203,600 thousand, so the build depends on financing already raised [CP-1; Documentary Fact]. Cost to complete, project budget and schedule are not supplied, so adequacy of the CAD 500,853 thousand of restricted cash cannot be tested [CP-1A; Insufficient Information]. Claims beside the notes are large relative to the equity base: the Gold Stream, two royalty liabilities, leases and the closure provision, plus a new 1% royalty and an option that could issue shares [CP-1A; Documentary Fact]. The company holds US dollar debt and restricted cash while reporting in Canadian dollars, recorded a foreign exchange loss of CAD 17,684 thousand in the six months and does not hedge [CP-1; Documentary Fact]. Single-asset concentration means a permit, construction or metallurgical event affects the whole credit [CP-1A; Analyst Interpretation]. If the Gold Stream completion test is not met by September 30, 2027 the stream percentage steps up from 3.52% [CP-1A; Documentary Fact]. Whether the Gold Stream covenants (debt service coverage and a cash minimum) still apply after the buy-down is Not supplied [CP-1; Insufficient Information].
