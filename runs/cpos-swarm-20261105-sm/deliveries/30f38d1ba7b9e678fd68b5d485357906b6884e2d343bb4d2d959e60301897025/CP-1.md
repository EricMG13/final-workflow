### EBITDAX and definition review

The company's adjusted EBITDAX starts from net income and adds back interest, tax, depletion, depreciation and amortization, exploration, stock-based compensation, the full mark-to-market on derivatives, transaction and integration costs and the divestiture result, and then deducts net derivative settlement losses (or adds gains). Items that matter for credit comparison:

| Item | Nine months 2026 (US$m) | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net derivative loss added back | 1,109 | Non-cash mark-to-market; excluded from EBITDAX |
| Net derivative settlement loss deducted | (457) | Realized hedge losses reduce EBITDAX, so the measure is net of cash hedge results |
| Transaction and integration costs added back | 177 | Of which cash 157 per the operating cash reconciliation; EBITDAX is therefore higher than a measure that treats these costs as recurring |
| Gain on divestiture deducted | (260) | Removed from EBITDAX as a non-operating result |
| Net cash provided by operating activities | 2,865 | Operating cash flow was lower than adjusted EBITDAX by the interest, tax, one-time cost and working capital items in the reconciliation |

The covenant uses "total funded debt, as defined in the Credit Agreement" over "adjusted EBITDAX" (Q3 page:60). The company's November release defines net debt as senior notes principal plus revolver drawings less cash and also calls it "total funded debt" (R3 page:11). Whether the covenant nets cash, and whether covenant EBITDAX is pro forma for acquisitions and divestitures, is not stated in the supplied sources. The Fourth Amendment of 2026-01-30 amended the covenant definitions (Q3 page:17), so the definition in force today is not shown. The metrics in this handoff follow the company's reported adjusted EBITDAX, not the covenant definition.
