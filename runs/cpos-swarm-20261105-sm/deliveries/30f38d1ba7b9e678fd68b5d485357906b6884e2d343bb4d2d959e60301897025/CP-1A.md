### Business and transaction view

SM Energy is an independent oil and gas producer whose profile changed in one step on 2026-01-30, when it acquired Civitas Resources for stock and became a four-basin operator, then partly reversed the change by selling its southern Maverick Basin assets on 2026-04-30. For a creditor the transaction matters for four reasons. First, it was paid mainly in equity, with no sponsor and no leveraged buyout structure; the debt increase came from assumed notes, not new borrowing, and the revolver was left undrawn. Second, management used asset-sale proceeds and cash flow to retire the shortest notes and part of the highest-coupon assumed issue, so the first note maturity is mid-2028 and revolver availability is intact. Third, the operating base is oil-weighted and exposed to price, basis and takeaway constraints, partly offset by a hedge book priced below recent realized oil prices that shows as a liability. Fourth, the evidence on ownership control, legal capacity under the indentures and reserves after the merger is missing, so conclusions about willingness are supported by documented actions only, and conclusions about permissions are not drawn. The documented behaviour is deleveraging with continuing shareholder returns; no priming, uptiering, drop-down, debt-funded distribution or related-party leakage appears in the supplied text.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

On 2025-11-02 SM Energy signed a merger agreement with Civitas Resources, Inc. (10-K page:14). Stockholders approved the share issuance on 2026-01-27 and the merger closed on 2026-01-30 (Q3 page:33). Each Civitas share was converted into 1.45 SM Energy shares; 124 million shares were issued, equal to 52 percent of the enlarged share count, valued at 2.4 billion on the closing price. The purchase accounting (preliminary) shows equity consideration of 2,409, replacement awards of 29, cash of 226 and total consideration of 2,664 (Q3 page:11); the cash included 201 to repay the Civitas revolver, which released its liens, and 25 for an employee retention program (Q3 page:12). SM Energy is the accounting acquirer, the deal was a tax-free reorganization, and 18 of transaction costs were expensed in the nine months (Q3 page:10). The company assumed 4.9 billion principal of Civitas unsecured senior notes (Q3 page:18); the purchase price allocation lists senior notes assumed at a fair value of 5,090 (Q3 page:11). The Fourth Amendment to the credit agreement, signed at closing, added the Civitas subsidiaries as guarantors, extended the maturity of elected commitments to 2031-01-30, raised commitments from 2.0 billion to 2.5 billion and the borrowing base from 3.0 billion to 5.0 billion, and amended financial covenant definitions (Q3 page:17). Management reports that it actioned about 400 million of run-rate synergies against an original 200 million target (R3 page:1); this is Management Language and no synergy realization in the cash flow statement is supplied.

The divestiture and the debt actions that followed form the second leg. On 2026-02-17 SM Energy agreed to sell about 61,000 net acres in its southern Maverick Basin for 950 million of cash, with a deposit of 7.5 percent placed in escrow (10-K page:183). The sale closed on 2026-04-30 and, after a post-quarter settlement, total net cash proceeds were 894 with a gain of 260 (Q3 page:13). New debt issued in 2026: 1.0 billion of 6.625 percent Senior Notes due 2034, issued at par on 2026-03-09, with net proceeds of 984 after fees (Q3 page:20), used to fund a tender offer. The tender repurchased 894 principal of the 8.375 percent notes due 2028, at 103.175 percent of principal, for total consideration of 924, leaving 456 outstanding (Q3 page:20). Proceeds of the sale redeemed at par the 400 of 5.0 percent notes due 2026 on 2026-05-11 and the 419 of 6.75 percent notes due 2026 on 2026-06-01, and cash flow redeemed the 417 of 6.625 percent notes due 2027 on 2026-09-04 (Q3 pages:20-21). There was no new issuance after the 2034 notes in the supplied sources, and the revolver was undrawn at each quarter end. Net debt fell from 7,353 at 2026-03-31 to 6,014 at 2026-09-30 as stated in the CP-1 handoff, and management says it is on track for low one-times leverage at mid-cycle prices by year-end 2026 (R3 page:2, Management Language; the term is not defined in the sources).

Use of proceeds, liquidity effect and status are all documented for the 2034 notes, tender and redemptions. For the merger itself, no sponsor, bridge financing or new committed financing is described; the revolver was amended, not drawn. No transaction announced but not yet closed appears in the sources as of the cutoff.

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability and margin risk. Evidence: oil is the dominant revenue line (Q3 page:14) and gas realizations fell to 0.17 per Mcf in Q2 on regional differentials (Q3 page:40). Mechanic: price-taking producer with partial hedges; swap and collar floors are below Q3 realized oil prices, so settlements reduce cash now, while the hedge book still limits downside. Implication: cash flow and leverage move with oil and basis; a fall in prices would be cushioned for 2026 and 2027 by hedge volumes (Q3 page:24), but the volumes against 2027 production are not determinable because 2027 production guidance is not supplied. Counter-evidence: the hedge liability and conflicts logged below.
2. Merger and integration. Evidence: 124 million shares issued; 4.9 billion of notes assumed; synergy claims are Management Language (R3 page:1). Mechanic: scale gain and cost take-out against integration cost, unmet drilling commitment and operational downtime on acquired assets (R3 page:2). Implication: the combined entity carries more debt than the standalone company, but the sources show the debt falling and liquidity intact; integration cost and execution risk remain through 2026.
3. Refinancing and maturity. Evidence: no notes mature until mid-2028 (Q3 page:21). Mechanic: the shortest notes were retired from sale proceeds and cash flow, and high-coupon assumed debt was partly replaced with a lower-coupon issue. Implication: refinancing risk and interest burden are reduced for the next two years; the 2028 maturities and 2030-2033 high-coupon notes remain the next negotiation points.
4. Sponsor alignment and distributions. Evidence: widely held listed equity, no sponsor, equity-paid deal. Mechanic: shareholder returns are funded from free cash flow beside debt reduction. Implication: no extraction pattern is documented; the monitoring point is the pace of buybacks against leverage targets, because management states returns are evaluated "in the context of our leverage trajectory" (Q3 page:64).
5. Key uncertainties. Reserve base after the merger and sale; customer concentration after the merger; current covenant terms; ratings; legal capacity. Implication: these block analysis of asset coverage and permitted distributions, and are logged as gaps, not as adverse conclusions.
