### Business and transaction view

Starwood Property Trust, Inc. is a Maryland real estate investment trust that lends against commercial and residential real estate and infrastructure, owns property through its Property Segment, and services and invests in commercial mortgage securities. It is a holding company run through wholly-owned subsidiaries and is externally managed by SPT Management, LLC, which is controlled by its chairman and chief executive and is an affiliate of Starwood Capital Group. For a creditor the central business facts are that funding is mostly secured and asset-level, that unsecured notes are issued at the registrant level and swapped to floating rates, that the July 2025 acquisition of Fundamental moved the company toward owned, long-lease net lease property, and that the manager’s fee arrangements and related-party loans make governance a monitoring topic. The most important gap is documentary: the indentures, credit agreements and the management agreement are not supplied, so legal capacity (what the documents permit) cannot be separated from willingness (what was done) and feasibility (liquidity and market access). No filing after the second quarter of 2026 is supplied, so transaction history after August 6, 2026 is unknown, not absent.

### Credit translations

1. Manager economics and control. Evidence: the base fee is a percentage of defined stockholders’ equity, the incentive fee needs core earnings above a hurdle, and removal triggers a fee of three times the average annual fees (10-K FY2025 pages 26, 237 and 238). Risk mechanic: the base fee does not fall with asset quality or leverage, and the cost of removal reduces the board’s practical ability to change manager if performance weakens. Credit implication: manager alignment with creditors rests on board oversight rather than on contract terms the creditor can observe; monitoring posture should track fees against earnings, and the credit module should record that the management agreement itself is not supplied. Counter-evidence: no incentive fee was incurred in the second quarter of 2026, and half of any incentive fee is paid in shares.
2. Related-party lending. Evidence: ten board-approved related-party loan transactions, including loans where the registrant lacks certain co-lender consent rights because of an affiliate’s interest (10-Q Q2 2026 page:69). Risk mechanic: a lender to affiliate-connected borrowers may have weaker control in a workout and faces a conflict between the manager’s affiliates and the registrant. Credit implication: concentration to affiliate-connected borrowers is an open monitoring item; the sources give transaction-level amounts but no aggregate, so no exposure conclusion is drawn.
3. Funding access and maturity management. Evidence: regular unsecured, term loan, CLO and ABS financings in 2025 and 2026, with the July 2026 notes replacing the 2026 and January 2027 maturities (10-Q Q2 2026 page:101). Risk mechanic: refinancing earlier than maturity extends the maturity profile and reduces reliance on a single market, but the registrant’s funding is mostly secured on pledged assets, and access depends on collateral values. Credit implication: refinancing risk in the near term is reduced for unsecured debt; the credit module should test secured maturities, availability and covenant headroom. Counter-evidence: leverage in the financial base rose over the same period.
4. Shift toward owned net lease property. Evidence: Fundamental’s 100.0% occupancy and 16.8 year weighted remaining lease term (10-Q Q2 2026 page:141) and ABS debt service coverage covenants (10-Q Q2 2026 page:52). Risk mechanic: long contracted rents add revenue visibility, while ABS debt at special purpose vehicles is secured on those properties and is structurally ahead of unsecured claims on them; covenants at the vehicle can restrict cash release. Credit implication: property income is more predictable, but cash reaching the registrant depends on vehicle-level tests whose levels are not supplied.
5. Key uncertainty. Management’s statement that it expects to resolve nearly 900 million dollars of underperforming assets is Management Language (Release Q2 2026 page:1); the nonaccrual loan evidence belongs to the credit module (10-Q Q2 2026 page:33).
