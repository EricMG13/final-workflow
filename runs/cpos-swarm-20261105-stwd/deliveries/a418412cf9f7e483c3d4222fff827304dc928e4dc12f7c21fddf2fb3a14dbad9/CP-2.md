### Credit view

Starwood Property Trust, Inc. is an adequately funded real estate lender whose credit quality is weakening at the margin. Its strongest support is funding access: it issued USD 600.0 million of unsecured notes in May 2026 and USD 500.0 million in July 2026, cleared its 2026 and January 2027 maturities, and has USD 1,399.0 million of lender-approved undrawn secured capacity. Its weakest point is earnings against leverage: second quarter 2026 net income attributable was USD 6.6 million, distributable earnings per share trailed the dividend, and debt to stockholders’ equity rose to 3.65x from 3.25x, with nonaccrual loans near USD 947.3 million. Resolution of those loans and continued secured funding decide the outcome.

### Key findings

First, the registrant’s financial risk has risen over the first half of 2026. Total debt on the balance sheet increased to USD 23,694.1 million from USD 22,094.2 million while stockholders’ equity fell to USD 6,499.0 million from USD 6,795.5 million, because dividends declared of USD 357.3 million exceeded net income attributable of USD 58.4 million (Analyst Interpretation on the cause; the amounts are Documentary Fact). The mechanic is simple: a payout above earnings is paid from cash that is then replaced with debt or retained-earnings erosion, so the equity layer that sits beneath every creditor shrinks.

Second, earnings are weak on the filed basis and only adequate on the company basis. The registrant reported net income attributable of USD 6.6 million for the second quarter of 2026 against distributable earnings of USD 151.5 million, a company measure that removes the credit loss provision and unrealized items. Distributable earnings per share of USD 0.78 for the first half trailed dividends declared of USD 0.96 per share, a ratio of 0.81x. The credit implication is that the dividend is not covered by either measure, so the registrant depends on asset resolution and deployment of capital to close the gap (Documentary Fact for the figures; Analyst Interpretation for the reading).

Third, asset quality is the swing factor. The registrant had about USD 947.3 million of loans on nonaccrual at June 30, 2026 by our sum of the amounts the quarterly report states, 36.8% of commercial loans at amortized cost carry loan-to-stabilized-value above 70%, and that bucket holds USD 362.0 million of the USD 406.2 million commercial credit loss allowance. Interest stops accruing on nonaccrual loans, which lowers net interest income and ties up capital that supports the leverage ratio; foreclosures in the first half added USD 347.5 million of owned property. Management says it expects to resolve nearly USD 900 million of underperforming assets by year end or shortly after, which is Management Language and not evidence of resolution.

Fourth, funding structure is a strength with a catch. Most debt is matched-term secured funding, 68% of repurchase agreements do not permit valuation adjustments based on capital market events, and the registrant refinanced USD 1,100.0 million of unsecured notes in the quarter and the month after. The catch is rank and opacity: USD 18,932.4 million of secured and securitized financing is secured on pledged assets, corporate term loans of USD 2,533.1 million are secured on subsidiary equity, and the USD 4,930.8 million of unsecured notes rank behind both on those assets. Covenant levels are not supplied, so headroom is Not calculable even though the registrant states compliance.

Fifth, governance adds a monitoring item. The registrant is externally managed by an affiliate of its sponsor, the management agreement was not negotiated at arm’s length and carries a termination fee of three times average annual fees, and several loans were made to borrowers connected to affiliates of the manager with board approval. The mechanic is weaker creditor control rather than an observed loss; no creditor-adverse action was found in the pages read (Insufficient Information on the documents).

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. The registrant has proven access to secured and unsecured markets (Documentary Fact): 2025 saw a USD 534.4 million share issuance, USD 1,550.0 million of new senior notes and a USD 700.0 million term loan, and 2026 added a USD 600.0 million note, a USD 275.0 million term loan upsizing and several securitizations, which reduces refinancing risk. The funding is mostly matched to assets, with the next four quarters of projected principal inflows exceeding projected required financing repayments by USD 785.1 million (Documentary Fact, company projection), so asset repayments fund most secured maturities. The platform is diversified across commercial lending, infrastructure lending, owned property and servicing, with a named special servicing portfolio of USD 93.6 billion (Documentary Fact), which gives several earnings sources. The unsecured note stack is long-dated after July 2026: the first unsecured maturity is the USD 380.8 million convertible notes in July 2027 (Analyst Interpretation from the maturity table). About 79% of unsecured principal is swapped to floating rates, which matches floating-rate assets but adds hedge counterparty exposure (Analyst Interpretation).

Weaknesses. Leverage has risen at each balance sheet date in the financial base, to 3.65x total debt to stockholders’ equity (Documentary Fact for the inputs, calculated ratio). Earnings do not cover the dividend, and GAAP interest cover is 1.22x on a trailing basis (Analyst Interpretation, includes fair value and consolidated VIE items). Nonaccrual loans of about USD 947.3 million and an office share of 17.6% of the commercial portfolio concentrate asset-quality risk (Documentary Fact; sum calculated). The structure is complex: GAAP assets include USD 30,868.1 million of securitization VIE assets, and the registrant states it is not obligated to provide financial support to those VIEs, so ratios on different perimeters are not comparable (Documentary Fact). Governance is the sponsor’s: manager fees, a termination fee and related-party loans reduce creditor control (Documentary Fact; creditor effect is Analyst Interpretation). The most important evidence is missing: governing debt documents, covenant levels, guarantor terms and ratings are not supplied (Insufficient Information), and no filing covers the period after the second quarter of 2026.
