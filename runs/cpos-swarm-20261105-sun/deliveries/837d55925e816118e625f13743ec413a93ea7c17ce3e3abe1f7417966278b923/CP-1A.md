### Business and transaction view

The business is a four-segment energy infrastructure and fuel distribution group: Fuel Distribution, Pipeline Systems, Terminals and Refinery. It operates across 33 countries and territories in North America, the Greater Caribbean and Europe, with about 14,000 miles of pipeline, over 170 terminals and capacity to distribute over 17 billion gallons annually (Q3 10-Q page:7). The step change came from the Parkland acquisition on 2025-10-31, which added Canadian and Caribbean distribution and the Burnaby refinery, on top of the NuStar acquisition of 2024-05-03, which added pipelines and terminals. Ownership is concentrated: Energy Transfer owns the general partner, a minority of the common units and all incentive distribution rights, and through SunocoCorp controls the board appointment right. The creditor view is a larger, more diversified earnings base than before the acquisitions, financed largely with debt and preferred equity, under a sponsor whose economic interest (incentive distributions) is separate from that of the debt holders. Documented behaviour since 2024 shows continued acquisition activity paired with reported leverage reduction and rising distributions, which is mixed for creditors rather than adverse. The decisive open items are the fair-value work on Parkland, TanQuid, Delta and Offen, the funding of Offen, and the absence of the executed documents that set distribution, debt and change-of-control limits.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The defining transaction is the Parkland acquisition, completed on 2025-10-31 by Sunoco Retail LLC, a wholly owned corporate subsidiary. Parkland shareholders received 0.295 SunocoCorp units and C$19.80 in cash per Parkland share, or could elect cash or units subject to proration. The Partnership paid about 2.60 billion US$ to Parkland shareholders and transferred 51,517,198 SunocoCorp common units, which it had received in exchange for issuing 51,517,198 Class D Units to SunocoCorp (10-K page:7). Total consideration was 5,015 US$m (4,551 US$m net of cash acquired), against assumed long-term debt of 3,797 US$m, goodwill of 1,528 US$m and intangible assets of 1,871 US$m, all on a preliminary allocation (10-K page:238). The cash portion was funded in part by the September 2025 issuance of 1.00 billion US$ of 5.625% senior notes due 2031 and 900 million US$ of 5.875% senior notes due 2034, and by the 1.5 million Series A Preferred Units sold for net proceeds of about 1.47 billion US$ (10-K page:140). The Parkland senior note exchange closed on 2025-11-07 with about C$1.47 billion and 2.58 billion US$ of Parkland notes tendered, leaving 111 US$m of Parkland notes outstanding at 2025-12-31 (10-K page:140 and page:139); the Q3 10-Q shows those residual Parkland notes redeemed in 2026 (page:14). SunocoCorp was repurposed as a publicly traded corporation holding the Class D Units, and the Partnership must keep SunocoCorp distributions equal to its own through the equalisation period (Q3 10-Q page:19).

In 2026 the Partnership acquired TanQuid on 2026-01-16 (15 fuel terminals in Germany and one in Poland) for 239 US$m with 346 US$m of assumed debt at fair value, funded from cash and the revolving credit facility, and Delta on 2026-04-01 for about 81 US$m, with terminals and distribution assets in five Caribbean markets (Q3 10-Q page:9 and page:10). Purchase accounting for TanQuid is preliminary. After quarter end, on 2026-10-27, Sunoco Retail acquired Offen Petroleum, a US fuel distributor of about 2.5 billion gallons a year, for about 600 US$m in cash plus working capital; the funding source and the effect on 2026-10-31 liquidity are not stated, and fair value work is incomplete (Q3 10-Q page:10). On 2026-10-08 the receivables financing agreement was terminated with no borrowings outstanding (Q3 10-Q page:15).

New debt issued in the period: in March 2025, 1.00 billion US$ of 6.250% senior notes due 2033, used to repay 600 US$m of 5.750% notes due 2025 and part of the revolver (10-K page:140); in September 2025, the 5.625% 2031 and 5.875% 2034 notes above; in March 2026, 600 US$m of 5.375% senior notes due 2031 and 600 US$m of 5.625% senior notes due 2034, both maturing on 2031-07-15 and 2034-07-15, with part of the proceeds used to redeem the 500 US$m 6.000% notes due 2026 and the 600 US$m 6.000% notes due 2027 (Q3 10-Q page:14). Net of these redemptions the March 2026 issuance added 100 US$m of principal. Parkland's remaining notes were redeemed in March 2026, the 3.875% CAD notes due 2026 in June 2026 and the 6.000% CAD notes due 2028 in August 2026. The earlier history includes the NuStar acquisition of 2024-05-03 (about 51.5 million Sunoco common units issued, about 3.5 billion US$ of debt assumed, and NuStar preferred units, subordinated notes and credit facility repaid after closing), the sale of 204 West Texas convenience stores to 7-Eleven on 2024-04-16 for about 1.0 billion US$ with a 586 US$m gain, and the formation of the ET-S Permian joint venture with Energy Transfer on 2024-07-01 (10-K page:240 to page:242).

### Ownership, governance and sponsor behaviour

Energy Transfer owns 100% of the general partner membership interest, 28,463,967 common units and all incentive distribution rights, and controls SunocoCorp, which owns all Class D Units (Q3 10-Q page:7). Under a Delegation Agreement dated 2025-10-27, Energy Transfer delegated its power to elect, appoint and remove the Sunoco GP LLC board to SunocoCorp; the rights terminate on certain SunocoCorp insolvency events or at Energy Transfer's election, and the general partner agreed not to amend the partnership agreement against those rights or to withdraw while SunocoCorp is listed or not wholly owned by Energy Transfer (10-K page:197). The 10-K states that general partner officers and directors "also have a contractual duty to manage our General Partner in a manner beneficial to Energy Transfer" and that conflicts of interest may arise (10-K page:91). An Omnibus Agreement dated 2025-10-31 has the Partnership indemnify SunocoCorp, provide all general and administrative services and fund SunocoCorp's distributions equal to those on Sunoco common units through the eighth full quarter after closing (10-K page:197 and page:198).

### Credit translations

Evidence: Energy Transfer holds the general partner, the incentive distribution rights and the delegated board appointment right (10-K page:197, Q3 10-Q page:7). Risk mechanic: control and the top-tier incentive share (50% of marginal cash above the highest target, 10-K page:122) mean the controlling party benefits from distribution growth while creditors benefit from retained cash. Credit implication: monitoring posture should weight distribution policy and any change in control provisions; the debt documents that govern restricted payments are not supplied.

Evidence: Four acquisitions closed in twelve months with open fair value work (10-K page:238; Q3 10-Q page:9 and page:10). Risk mechanic: acquired earnings enter the trailing base gradually while acquired debt and purchase prices enter the balance sheet at once, so leverage ratios and goodwill can move with the accounting allocation. Credit implication: refinancing and leverage tolerance depend on integration delivering the earnings behind the CP-1 trailing ratio of 3.72x; the Offen funding and the CP-1 liquidity of 3,060 US$m should be read together.

Evidence: 7-Eleven accounted for about 13% of FY2025 revenues under a take-or-pay supply agreement (10-K page:261). Risk mechanic: a single customer with a contractual minimum gross profit stabilises Fuel Distribution earnings but concentrates counterparty risk. Credit implication: revenue durability is supported at the segment level, and a loss or renegotiation of the contract is a named downside to monitor.

Evidence: The refinery's output is sold largely to other segments (Q3 10-Q page:22). Risk mechanic: Refinery segment earnings depend on crude differentials and intersegment transfer terms that the sources do not describe. Credit implication: margin and cash-flow risk from a single-site asset is a key uncertainty; the transfer pricing basis is not supplied.
