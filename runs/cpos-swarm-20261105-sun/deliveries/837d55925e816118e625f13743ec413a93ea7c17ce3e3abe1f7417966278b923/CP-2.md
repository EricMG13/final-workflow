### Credit view

Sunoco LP is a sponsor-controlled fuel distribution and midstream credit whose leverage has fallen to about 3.5x and whose liquidity is ample. Its strongest support is scale and diversification: trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 3,535 million across four segments, net debt of 3.51x that figure, and liquidity of USD 3,060 million against USD 1,049 million of notes due in 2027. The weakest point is that the improvement rests on recently acquired earnings, management-adjusted EBITDA and a sponsor whose incentive distributions and board control sit beside creditor interests, while funding for the USD 600 million Offen purchase is not described. Whether Parkland integration and refinery margins keep earnings at this level decides whether leverage keeps falling.

### Key findings

Leverage has fallen sharply, but part of the fall is perimeter change. Sunoco LP's net debt over trailing Adjusted EBITDA fell from 6.11x at FY2025 to 3.51x at Q3 2026, and the reported covenant net leverage fell from 4.03x to 3.5x [CP-1]. The mechanism is that Parkland (acquired on 2025-10-31) contributed two months to FY2025 earnings but eleven months to the Q3 2026 trailing window while its debt was fully on the balance sheet at both dates [CP-1, CP-1A]. The implication is that headroom against the 5.50x covenant ceiling is wide on the reported basis, yet the trajectory should be judged on pro forma earnings that the sources do not supply.

Liquidity is strong against a staggered maturity profile. Sunoco LP held USD 760 million of cash and USD 2,300 million of undrawn revolver, total USD 3,060 million, at 2026-09-30, after USD 111 million drawn and USD 84 million of letters of credit [CP-1]. Maturities are USD 1,049 million in 2027, USD 900 million in 2028, USD 2,723 million in 2029 and USD 2,309 million in 2030 including the revolver [CP-1]. The 2027 notes are classified as long-term only on management's intent and ability to refinance, so the mechanism is market access rather than cash on hand. The implication is that refinancing risk is low in the next twelve months and moderate in 2027 to 2029, when USD 4,672 million falls due.

Debt service is well covered on a cash basis. Trailing Adjusted EBITDA of USD 3,535 million covers trailing cash interest of USD 726 million 4.87 times, against a covenant minimum of 2.25x that the filings do not report a figure against [CP-1]. Only USD 398 million of debt is variable-rate, so a 100 basis point rate move changes annual interest expense by about USD 4 million (Q3 10-Q page:48). The implication is limited interest-rate sensitivity; the more relevant sensitivity is operating, not financial.

Cash generation covers distributions, with timing help. Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow of USD 2,251 million exceeded capital expenditure of USD 579 million and distributions of USD 880 million [CP-1]. The mechanism includes a USD 316 million working-capital inflow, a USD 102 million last-in first-out liquidation benefit to pre-tax income and a USD 94 million non-cash foreign exchange loss [CP-1]. Distribution commitments rise with the rate and unit count and include equal payments to SunocoCorp through 2027-12-31 and USD 76 million of incentive distributions for Q3 2026 [CP-1A]. The implication is that free cash flow after distributions is positive (see Calculations) but is not a stable annuity: it depends on working capital timing and fuel prices.

Earnings quality needs care. Adjusted EBITDA is a management measure that adds back USD 200 million of unconsolidated affiliate EBITDA, USD 131 million of other non-cash adjustments and removes a USD 445 million favourable inventory valuation adjustment in nine months 2026 [CP-1]. Covenant ratios use credit-agreement definitions that are not supplied [CP-0, CP-1]. The implication is that the derived ratios in this synthesis are a guide and the covenant headroom is a reported claim, not a verified result.

Structure and control weigh on creditor protection. Energy Transfer owns the general partner, 28,463,967 common units and all incentive distribution rights, and controls the board appointment right through SunocoCorp [CP-1A]. Notes are guaranteed by subsidiaries and rank effectively behind secured obligations including the revolver, and structurally behind debt of non-guarantor subsidiaries, which hold USD 11.73 billion of long-term assets (Q3 10-Q page:47); the line for lease-related financing and other subsidiary debt is USD 597 million and its obligors are not identified [CP-1]. The implication is that the capacity to pay out cash or add debt depends on indenture and partnership agreement terms that are not supplied, so legal capacity is not assessed.

Acquisition pace is the main forward risk. Four acquisitions closed within twelve months, the latest being Offen on 2026-10-27 for about USD 600 million in cash [CP-1A]. The mechanism is that purchase prices and acquired debt enter the balance sheet at once while earnings arrive gradually, and fair value work is open. An all-debt illustration would lift net leverage to about 3.68x before any Offen earnings (see Calculations). The implication is that further leverage movement depends on funding choice and integration.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Scale and diversification (Documentary Fact): four segments with Fuel Distribution, Pipeline Systems, Terminals and Refinery all contributing in Q3 2026, and operations in 33 countries and territories [CP-1A]. This reduces dependence on one margin source and supports revenue visibility. Fee-based and contracted income (Documentary Fact): pipeline and terminal throughput, remaining performance obligations of USD 2,038 million and a 15-year take-or-pay fuel supply agreement with minimum gross profit with the largest customer [CP-1A]. This supports cash conversion in weaker fuel-margin periods. Liquidity and maturity management (Documentary Fact): USD 3,060 million of liquidity, a revolver maturing 2030-06-17 and 2026 and 2027 notes replaced by notes due 2031 and 2034 [CP-1, CP-1A]. This lowers near-term refinancing risk. Deleveraging record (Documentary Fact): covenant net leverage reported at 4.03x, 3.98x, 3.7x and 3.5x across the last four reporting dates [CP-1]. This shows a funding and earnings path that creditors can monitor. Equity cushion (Analyst Interpretation): total equity of USD 8,512 million including USD 1,477 million of preferred units sits beneath USD 13,163 million of debt, which is 60.7% of debt plus equity (see Calculations) [CP-1]. Preferred and Class D equity were used for part of the Parkland price [CP-1A].

Weaknesses. Acquisition dependence (Documentary Fact): Parkland, TanQuid, Delta and Offen in twelve months with open fair value work [CP-1A]. This makes trailing metrics unrepresentative and exposes leverage to integration. Sponsor control and cash leakage (Documentary Fact): Energy Transfer control, incentive distributions at the top tier share and a general partner that must favour Energy Transfer's interests under its contractual duty [CP-1A]. This competes with debt reduction for cash. Commodity and single-site exposure (Analyst Interpretation): the Burnaby Refinery earned USD 238 million of Segment Adjusted EBITDA in Q3 2026, largely on intersegment sales whose pricing terms are not described [CP-1A]. This adds margin volatility. Thin per-gallon margins and working capital swings (Analyst Interpretation): fuel is most of revenue and working capital moved by USD 316 million in nine months 2026 [CP-1, CP-1A]. Customer concentration (Documentary Fact): 7-Eleven was about 13% of FY2025 revenues [CP-1A]. Disclosure limits (Insufficient Information): the indentures, credit agreement, partnership agreement, ratings and pro forma earnings are not supplied, which stops conclusions on covenant headroom, restricted payments and rating-linked pricing. Market view of the debt (Documentary Fact): the aggregate fair value of debt was USD 12.88 billion against a carrying amount of USD 13.16 billion at 2026-09-30, the reverse of the position at 2025-12-31 (Q3 10-Q page:15); the sources do not explain the move.
