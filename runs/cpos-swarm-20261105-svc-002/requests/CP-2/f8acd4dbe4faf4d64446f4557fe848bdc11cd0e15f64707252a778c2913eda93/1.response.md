### Credit view

Service Properties Trust is a highly leveraged, thinly covered REIT whose credit is improving through asset sales and 2026 equity, but it still depends on one tenant and on refinancing a revolver that expires on June 29, 2027. The strongest support is deleveraging: principal debt fell USD 852.5 million to USD 4,652.3 million, indenture tests have headroom, and net lease rent is contractual with 97.6% occupancy. The weakest point is liquidity of USD 690.1 million, nearly all an undrawn secured revolver, against USD 1,586.9 million of maturities in 2027 and 2028 and cash interest cover of 1.41x. The outcome turns on the revolver extension or refinancing, the pace and price of hotel sales, and TravelCenters of America continuing to pay rent. [CP-1 Debt and capital structure, Liquidity, Covenants; CP-1A Business and transaction view]

### Thesis

Service Properties Trust is a deleveraging, hotel-to-net-lease transition credit whose debt service is supported by concentrated contractual rent but whose near-term outcome depends on refinancing the 2027 revolver and executing hotel sales on acceptable terms. (Analyst Interpretation; basis: CP-1 Financial base view and CP-1A Business and transaction view.)

### Key findings

The most important finding is that liquidity is narrow and time-limited. At September 30, 2026 the Trust held cash of USD 40.1 million and an undrawn revolver of USD 650.0 million, together USD 690.1 million, and the revolver matures on June 29, 2027 with two six-month extensions subject to a fee and conditions; the company says it is exploring an extension or refinancing, with no replacement agreed. Fixed-rate maturities are USD 584.2 million in 2027 and USD 1,002.8 million in 2028, so USD 1,586.9 million falls due before the end of 2028 against cash of USD 40.1 million. Availability of the revolver depends on collateral performance and covenant tests whose reported ratios are not in the pack. The mechanism is that a single secured facility supplies nearly all liquidity while the largest maturities arrive within the same window, so a failed or delayed refinancing would leave asset sales and new secured debt as the only sources. (Documentary Fact for the amounts and dates; Analyst Interpretation for the conclusion.) [CP-1 Liquidity, Debt maturities, Debt and capital structure]

The second finding is that leverage and cover are weak even after 2026 deleveraging. Principal debt of USD 4,652.3 million is 9.78x calculated last-twelve-month core EBITDA of USD 475.6 million (9.70x net of USD 40.1 million cash), down from 10.77x at FY2025, and core EBITDA covers last-twelve-month cash interest 1.41x, up from 1.35x. The Trust has a net loss of USD 463.7 million over the last twelve months, including USD 271.0 million of impairments, and operating cash flow less real estate improvements was negative USD 73.0 million. The EBITDA proxy includes earnings of hotels that have since been sold, so forward leverage on the retained portfolio is likely higher than shown. (Documentary Fact for reported amounts; Analyst Interpretation for the proxy and its direction, which is our inference and not a reported number.) [CP-1 Chart data, Financials and credit metrics, Calculations]

The third finding is that deleveraging depends on asset sales and equity that may not repeat. Debt fell by USD 852.5 million in nine months, funded by net equity proceeds of USD 541.8 million, new net lease mortgage notes, hotel and property sales and cash on hand. Each hotel sale lowers both debt and EBITDA, and further proceeds are subject to buyer demand; sales of eight hotels for USD 50.6 million closed after the quarter. The mechanism is that leverage improves only if proceeds retire debt at a higher multiple than the EBITDA sold. (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation for the mechanism.) [CP-1 Latest quarter and events; CP-1A Transaction summary]

The fourth finding is rent concentration. TravelCenters of America pays 68.2% of net lease annualized minimum rent under five master leases that expire in 2033, with rent coverage of 1.32x to 1.61x on those leases against 2.17x for the net lease portfolio, and BP Corporation North America guarantees the leases up to a cap of USD 2,782.9 million. Coverage is tenant-reported and unaudited, and the tenant's own financials are not in the pack. The leases expire one year after the 8.875% notes due 2032. (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation for the timing link.) [CP-1A Company and operating model, Credit translations]

The fifth finding is covenant headroom in the indentures, which is adequate on the reported tests. Total debt to adjusted total assets is 53.9% against a 60% maximum, and income available for debt service to debt service is 1.83x against a 1.50x minimum, after being 58.7% and 1.59x at December 31, 2025. Holding debt constant, adjusted assets could fall by about 10.2% before the 60% test is reached, and income available for debt service could fall by about 18.0% before the 1.50x test is reached. Revolver covenants (minimum fixed charge coverage of 1.30x, collateral debt yield of 10%) have no reported figures, so their headroom is Not calculable. (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation for the sensitivities, derived in Calculations.) [CP-1 Covenants and restrictions]

The sixth finding is subordination. Secured principal debt of USD 2,927.3 million is 62.9% of principal debt, up from 40.5% at December 31, 2025, because secured and equity capital replaced unsecured notes; USD 1,225.0 million of unsecured notes have no guarantees. No amendment, exchange or uptier of existing notes is documented, but unsecured creditors now sit behind more secured claims, which affects loss given default for them. (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation for the implication.) [CP-1 Debt and capital structure; CP-1A Governance and sponsor behaviour]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Deleveraging is real and documented: principal debt fell from USD 5,504.8 million at December 31, 2025 to USD 4,652.3 million at September 30, 2026 (Documentary Fact) [CP-1 Calculations]. Contractual rent is a stable core: net lease properties were 97.6% occupied with USD 395.5 million of annualized minimum rent and a 7.0 year weighted remaining term, which gives revenue visibility if tenants pay (Documentary Fact) [CP-1A Company and operating model]. Indenture tests have headroom on all five reported measures at September 30, 2026 (Documentary Fact) [CP-1 Covenants and restrictions]. All debt is fixed rate except the undrawn revolver, with an average annual coupon of 5.65% on current principal, so interest cost is not exposed to rates in the near term (Documentary Fact for the debt table; Analyst Interpretation for the exposure view) [CP-1 Calculations]. The manager bought shares in the April 2026 equity offering and distributions were held at a low level, which is documented supportive behaviour (Documentary Fact) [CP-1A Governance and sponsor behaviour]. Retained hotels improved in the third quarter of 2026, with RevPAR up 8.3%, although the data is manager-reported and management attributes part of the gain to a weak 2025 comparison (Management Language, qualified) [CP-1A Company and operating model].

Weaknesses. Leverage is high and cover thin at 9.78x and 1.41x on our own EBITDA proxy, because the company reports no EBITDA (Analyst Interpretation) [CP-1 Chart data]. Liquidity depends on one secured revolver that expires June 29, 2027, and maturities of USD 584.2 million in 2027 and USD 1,002.8 million in 2028 follow (Documentary Fact) [CP-1 Liquidity, Debt maturities]. Hotels are 74.7% of revenue with an operating expense ratio of 85.7% in the third quarter of 2026 and a segment loss after impairments, so earnings are operationally levered and volatile (Documentary Fact) [CP-1A Calculations, Company and operating model]. Tenant concentration is high: one tenant is 68.2% of net lease rent and carries the lowest rent coverage among the top ten brands (Documentary Fact) [CP-1A Company and operating model]. Earnings quality is poor: nine-month 2026 net loss was USD 463.0 million including USD 270.9 million of impairments (Documentary Fact) [CP-1 Financials and credit metrics]. The structure is secured-heavy and related-party dependent: the Trust has no employees, RMR manages it, and the Chair controls both the hotel operator Sonesta and, through ABP Trust, RMR's parent; terminating RMR without cause would require a fee based on 19 to 20 years of fees (Documentary Fact) [CP-1A Company profile, Governance and sponsor behaviour]. Revolver covenant ratios, tenant financials, ratings and the investor presentation are absent (Insufficient Information; the missing items are listed in Gaps and the absence is not an adverse conclusion).
