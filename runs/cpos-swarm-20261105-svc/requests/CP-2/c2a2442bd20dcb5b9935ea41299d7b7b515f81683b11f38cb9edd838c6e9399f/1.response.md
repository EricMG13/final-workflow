### Credit view

The fundamental credit view is that Service Properties Trust is a highly leveraged, loss-making REIT in the middle of a deliberate portfolio transition, with enough liquidity and covenant headroom to meet its obligations over the near term and a set of concentrated and related-party dependencies that make the outcome of the 2027 refinancing the central uncertainty. Confidence in this view is Medium, not High: the executed financing and management documents, the revolver covenant results and any ratings are not in the pack, the earnings measure is derived rather than reported, and the trailing figures include hotels that have since been sold.

Financial risk is high on every measure that can be calculated. Derived debt to trailing adjusted EBITDA is 9.39x (9.31x net of unrestricted cash), interest expense cover is 1.33x, and the nine-month net loss of 462,956 includes 270,883 of impairment, which is non-cash but shows asset values being reset as hotels are sold. Operating cash flow of 132,226 exceeded real estate improvements by only 22,164 over nine months, and book equity of 712,749 is small against 4,652,322 of debt principal. Against that, debt principal fell by 852,487 in nine months, liquidity is 690,118, and every indenture covenant test is met with headroom of about six points on the two asset-based debt tests and 0.33x on debt service cover. Business risk is mixed: a long-dated, triple-net rent base of 395,492 a year at 97.6% occupancy provides visibility, but it is concentrated in one tenant, while the hotel business, which still produces most revenue, is shrinking, loss-making at the segment level and run by a related-party operator. Governance is entrenched in an external manager whose removal is costly and is treated in the credit agreement as a change of control. The revolver, the only committed liquidity line, matures within eight months of the as-of date and its first extension needs a fixed charge coverage level whose result is not disclosed.

### Key findings

First, earnings cover is thin for the debt carried. The financial base handoff derives trailing adjusted EBITDA of 495,599 and shows debt principal of 4,652,322, giving 9.39x leverage and interest cover of 1.33x on interest expense and 1.47x on cash interest paid. Cash interest is paid semi-annually on most notes, so quarterly cash cover is not representative, and the trailing earnings include hotels sold before September 30, 2026, so the run-rate base may differ from the trailing figure; the effect is not calculable from the sources. Q3 2026 derived adjusted EBITDA of 127,761 was below the 138,960 of Q3 2025. The credit implication is limited tolerance for earnings pressure and dependence on asset sales and refinancing rather than internal cash generation to reduce debt.

Second, the 2027 refinancing is the central test. The revolver of 650,000 is undrawn and matures on June 29, 2027, and 580,155 of zero coupon secured notes mature in 2027, together with other small amounts, a total of 584,150 or 12.56% of principal. The company says it is exploring extension or refinancing of the revolver with no assurance of terms. The first extension option requires a 1.50x fixed charge coverage ratio, and the revolver covenant results are not disclosed, so headroom for the extension cannot be tested. Cash on hand is only 40,118, so liquidity of 690,118 depends almost entirely on the revolver. Demonstrated market access in 2026 (745,000 of mortgage notes and 541,798 of net equity) reduces but does not remove this risk.

Third, the capital structure has moved toward secured debt. Secured principal is 2,927,322 or 62.9% of the total at September 30, 2026, against 40.5% at December 31, 2025 (derived), because unsecured notes of 1,550,000 were redeemed using mortgage notes, equity and cash. Unsecured notes of 1,725,000 now sit behind secured debt, behind the pledged and non-guarantor subsidiaries, and some are unguaranteed. Asset coverage of the unsecured tranche under the indentures is 273.2% against a 150% minimum, which is positive but is measured on covenant adjusted assets at original cost, which add back accumulated depreciation and impairment and so exceed GAAP book values.

Fourth, concentration and related-party dependence limit diversification and creditor control. TravelCenters of America accounts for 34.2% of historical real estate investment and 68.2% of annual minimum rent, its leases expire in 2033, and a guarantee by a BP subsidiary is capped at 2,782,917. Sonesta manages 62 of 87 hotels, is 34% owned by the company and shares a controlling shareholder with the manager, and the manager's removal is both costly and a change of control under the credit agreement. These arrangements support operating continuity but weaken creditors' ability to influence outcomes and mean fee and operator terms cannot be assumed to be arm's length.

Fifth, covenant compliance is documented for the notes but not for the revolver. All five indenture tests are met at September 30, 2026, with debt service cover of 1.83x against a 1.50x minimum, up from 1.59x at the adjusted year-end figure. The revolver has a minimum fixed charge coverage test and a collateral debt yield test whose results are Not supplied, so one of the two main sources of acceleration risk in the structure cannot be verified.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. A long-dated net lease rent base provides revenue visibility: annual minimum rents of 395,492, a weighted average remaining term of 7.0 years, 97.6% occupancy, rent coverage of 2.17x reported by management, and a partial guarantee by a BP subsidiary on the largest tenant's leases. The company has materially reduced debt (principal down by 852,487 in nine months) and shown access to secured and equity markets, with the manager and officers buying shares in the April 2026 offering. Liquidity of 690,118 and indenture test headroom give a cushion for the next twelve months. Hotel operating indicators improved, with comparable RevPAR of 122.81 against 113.93, and asset sales continue to generate proceeds, including 50,895 after quarter end.

Weaknesses. Leverage of 9.39x and interest cover of 1.33x (both derived) leave little room for earnings pressure, and the group is loss-making with a nine-month net loss of 462,956 and thin free cash flow. The earnings base is shrinking as hotels are sold, from 160 to 87 in a year, with segment losses in hotels. Rent is concentrated in one tenant and one expiry year, and the hotel business depends on a related-party operator and a manager that is entrenched. Secured debt is now the larger share of the structure, and the 2027 refinancing of the revolver and zero coupon notes is unresolved. The disclosure is incomplete: revolver covenant results, executed legal documents and ratings are not in the pack.
