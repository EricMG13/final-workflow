### Credit view

Synchrony Financial has a sound fundamental credit profile with moderate, identifiable risks. The strongest support is deposit-led funding (USD 82,951 million of deposits against USD 17,025 million of borrowings at 2026-09-30), liquid assets of USD 19.5 billion and a common equity Tier 1 ratio of 12.9% that the company states exceeds regulatory minimums, with a net charge-off rate of 4.79% in Q3 2026. The weakest point is concentration: five partner programs produced 54% of interest and fees, and distributions of USD 3,262 million in the nine months of 2026 exceeded net earnings of USD 2,582 million. What decides the outcome is whether partners renew and credit losses stay near current levels while buybacks continue to lower the capital ratio.

### Key findings

Synchrony Financial funds its balance sheet mainly with deposits, which were USD 82,951 million at 2026-09-30 and 83% of funding sources, while borrowings were USD 17,025 million. Because debt due within one year of that date was USD 4,500 million against liquid assets of USD 19.5 billion, near-term maturities are covered several times by liquidity even before counting the USD 2,100 million of undrawn committed securitization capacity. This lowers refinancing and liquidity risk for creditors, and the main condition is continued access to deposits and the Federal Reserve discount window, whose availability was USD 13.0 billion.

The business depends on a short list of partners. The five largest programs produced 54% of interest and fees and 52% of loan receivables at year-end 2025, and three of them each exceeded a tenth of interest and fees; contract expiry dates run from 2030 to 2035, and some agreements let the partner buy the accounts on termination. A loss or repricing of one program would reduce revenue and the receivables book that backs the securitized notes and supports the bank, so concentration, not leverage, is the first risk a committee should monitor.

Earnings remain strong but are lower than a year earlier. Net earnings were USD 892 million in Q3 2026 against USD 1,077 million in the prior-year quarter, and USD 2,582 million for the nine months, while the net charge-off rate fell to 4.79% in Q3 2026 from 5.43% in Q2 2026. Retailer share arrangements of USD 3,193 million in the nine months move with program performance, so part of the benefit of better credit goes to partners and part of the pressure is absorbed by them. For debt service the only available proxy is 2.12x (pre-tax earnings plus interest expense, over interest expense, for the last twelve months), but that proxy includes deposit interest, is not an EBITDA measure, and the filings give no EBITDA.

Capital allocation is the main trend to monitor. Distributions in the nine months of 2026 were USD 3,262 million against net earnings of USD 2,582 million, which is 1.26x, while the common equity Tier 1 ratio on the recast basis moved from 13.5% at year-end 2025 to 12.9%, and borrowings to equity rose from 0.91x to 1.03x. New unsecured notes of USD 1,850 million and Series C preferred stock were issued in the same period. For creditors this means the equity buffer is being run down by choice and the company stays above stated regulatory minimums, so the pace of buybacks against capital targets is the variable to watch.

Structure matters for who holds which claim. Deposits are bank liabilities; securitized borrowings of USD 8,915 million are secured on restricted loans of USD 21,898 million, a ratio of 2.46x; the holding company owes about USD 7,511 million of senior and subordinated notes and relies on bank dividends and regulatory permission. Unsecured notes are 47.6% of borrowings, up from 44.6% at year-end 2025. The documented covenants restrict liens on and loss of control of the bank, and no leverage or restricted-payments test is visible, so legal capacity to distribute is not fully known.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Deposit funding is the largest strength: it is diversified between direct deposits of USD 76.2 billion and brokered deposits of USD 6.8 billion, and it lowers rollover risk relative to market funding. Liquidity is large and not dependent on committed bank lines: liquid assets were USD 19.5 billion and discount window capacity USD 13.0 billion. Credit quality was supportive in Q3 2026, with the net charge-off rate lower than in Q2 2026, delinquencies of 4.34% at 30 days and 2.06% at 90 days, and an allowance of 10.01% of receivables. Contract tenure is long, with expiry dates for the largest partners from 2030, and regulatory capital is stated as above minimums with the Bank well capitalized. Nothing documented shows sponsor extraction or creditor-adverse behaviour.

Weaknesses. Concentration in five partner programs is the largest business weakness, and the partner purchase rights and termination terms are not in the sources. Earnings were below the prior-year quarter and a reserve build raised the provision. Distributions above earnings, a lower recast capital ratio and a rising unsecured share of borrowings reduce the margin beneath creditors. The structure places holding-company creditors behind deposits and trust investors for access to the bank's assets, and no parent-only statements for 2026 interim periods are available, so holding-company liquidity is stale at year-end 2025. Standard EBITDA leverage and cash interest cover cannot be computed.
