### Business and transaction view

Business and transaction view: Synchrony is a consumer financing company that issues private label, dual, co-brand and general purpose credit cards, installment loans and deposit products through partner programs in five sales platforms. Its economics are tied to a short list of partner programs, the sharing of program performance through retailer share arrangements, a deposit-led funding model and heavy regulation as a savings and loan holding company with a bank subsidiary. There is no sponsor-led or leveraged transaction in the sources: the transaction section therefore records one completed asset purchase (a commercial card portfolio), the 2026 senior note, preferred stock and securitized issuance, and a larger share buyback programme. For a creditor the central questions are partner concentration and renewal, whether shareholder distributions are outrunning earnings, and how much of the funding stack sits at the holding company rather than the bank. The facts below separate what the filings state from management language and from analyst reading.

### Credit translations

- Revenue durability: Evidence: the five largest programs were 54 percent of interest and fees, with expiry dates from 2030 to 2035 (F1, 10-K pages:13, 14). Risk mechanic: a single non-renewal or a partner purchase of accounts would remove a large block of receivables and fee income, while long terms give visibility to the contract dates. Credit implication: monitoring posture should track renewals and partner health; revenue visibility is high until 2030 and concentrated afterwards. The counter-evidence is the extension of the Amazon agreement and the new OnePay programme in 2025.
- Margin and cash-flow risk: Evidence: retailer share payments rose while net charge-offs fell (F14), and other expense rose with technology and operating losses. Risk mechanic: the sharing of program performance caps the benefit of better credit, so margin gains from lower losses are shared, while cost growth is not shared. Credit implication: debt service capacity is supported by earnings that remain well above interest cost, but margin resilience depends on credit staying at current levels. The earnings fall against the prior-year quarter is a named counter to a stable-margin view.
- Concentration risk: Evidence: three partners each over a tenth of interest and fees (F1). Risk mechanic: partner financial distress or a pricing dispute would affect volume, receivables quality and share payments together. Credit implication: concentration should be a monitoring item; no recovery conclusion is drawn here.
- Capital allocation and sponsor alignment: Evidence: distributions exceeded net earnings in the nine months alongside new securities issuance (Ownership, governance and capital allocation). Risk mechanic: returning capital faster than it is earned lowers the equity cushion, and new senior notes at the holding company add claims that rank behind the Bank's deposits and the trusts' secured notes in terms of asset access. Credit implication: the key uncertainty is the pace of buybacks against the capital ratio and rating-agency targets; no sponsor exists to provide external support.
- Key uncertainties: executed indentures, contract terms, ownership percentages and the financial supplement are Not supplied; a logged conflict of presentation (the capital recast) counters any simple year-over-year capital trend.
