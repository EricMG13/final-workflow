### Financial base view

TransAlta enters the cutoff with ample liquidity but a weaker earnings base than a year ago. On management's own measure, Adjusted Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDA was 4.1 times at Sept. 30, 2026 against 4.0 times at Dec. 31, 2025, so the ratio now sits above the stated long-term target range of 3.0 to 4.0 times. The rise came from the denominator: last-four-quarter Adjusted EBITDA fell from 1,104 to 1,006 while Adjusted Net Debt fell from 4,396 to 4,121. On a plain balance-sheet basis that treats the 750 of exchangeable securities as debt, the calculated ratios are 4.19 times gross and 3.85 times net of unrestricted cash at Sept. 30, 2026. Liquidity is strong in the sources' own terms: 1,867 of available liquidity, no cash drawn on the 2,222 of committed facilities, and the syndicated facility extended to June 2030 and the bilateral facilities to June 2028 on July 17, 2026. Scheduled debt maturities are small relative to liquidity, with 52 due in 2026 and 332 in 2027 on the maturity table, but a large share of the debt is project-level and subject to distribution tests, so group liquidity is not all freely movable. The earnings picture is mixed: the third quarter improved (Adjusted EBITDA 264 against 238) on stronger Gas and Energy Marketing results, but nine-month Adjusted EBITDA is 759 against 857, because Centralia Unit 2 ceased coal-fired operations at the end of 2025 and Alberta spot prices fell. Cash flow from operations also fell, and the nine-month cash increase relied on 338 of common share proceeds. Events after quarter end (the US$1 billion Colorado acquisition with US$750 million of assumed project debt, the US$305 million sale of a 49 per cent interest in the Oklahoma wind portfolio, and the mothballing of Battle River Units 4 and 5 and Sundance 6) change the capital structure in ways the Sept. 30 statements do not show. No figure here comes from audited FY2025 statements: FY2025 comparatives are limited to the Dec. 31, 2025 balance sheet, management-reported full-year measures and the quarterly table.

### Earnings drivers and quality

The source states that nine-month Adjusted EBITDA fell by 98, or 11 per cent, and that the third quarter rose by 26 (Q3 release page:22). The segment table shows where: Energy Transition fell by 90 over nine months (84 to (6)) and Hydro by 48 (246 to 198), while Gas rose by 32 and Corporate improved by 14 (Calculations C8). The source attributes the Energy Transition fall to no production at Centralia Unit 2 after it ceased coal-fired operations at the end of 2025 (page:18, page:32), so this is a lost earnings stream, not a timing effect. Hydro fell on lower Alberta spot and ancillary prices and lower environmental credit sales (page:26). The Alberta average spot price was 32 per MWh over nine months against 44 (page:34), while the realized merchant price was 104 against 107, which shows the hedging programme absorbed most of the price fall (page:34). The source gives a sensitivity of about 1 million of Adjusted EBITDA per 1 per MWh change in Alberta spot for the rest of 2026, and hedged production of 6,732 GWh at 65 per MWh for full-year 2027 (page:24). Credit implication (analyst interpretation): on the hedge volumes reported, earnings are more resilient to Alberta price weakness than spot prices suggest through 2027, but the earnings stream lost at Centralia is not replaced, and the sources state 77 per cent of Alberta capacity is exposed to the merchant market (page:12).

Adjusted EBITDA excludes costs that are real cash costs in the period. The reconciliation excludes Centralia Order compliance costs and community fund expenses of 27 in Q3 and 38 over nine months, termination and restructuring costs of 18, legal costs of 9, ERP integration costs of 6 and acquisition-related costs of 4 over nine months (page:54, page:56). The Centralia costs relate to the U.S. Department of Energy order that keeps the unit available; FERC rejected the company's cost recovery petitions for the first and second 90-day orders on Oct. 1 and 19, 2026, while affirming the ability to recover costs and allowing new petitions (page:65). No recovery is recognised in the figures supplied. A reader who treats the Order costs as recurring while the Order is renewed (four consecutive 90-day orders so far) should deduct them: Calculations C10 shows the effect on leverage.

### Cash flow quality and funding

Operating cash flow before working capital fell to 386 from 509 over nine months, and cash flow from operating activities to 317 from 415, helped by a working capital movement that the source attributes to lower collateral provided (page:43). FCF (management-defined, after sustaining capital, preferred dividends and non-controlling interest distributions, before common dividends) was 338 against 421; common dividends paid were 59, so FCF less common dividends is 279 (Calculations C9). The cash balance rose from 205 to 345, but the cash flow statement shows 338 of net proceeds from the common share issue; without it cash would have fallen by 198 (Calculations C9). The source says the offering proceeds funded the cash portion of the Colorado acquisition and were partly used to repay the drawn syndicated facility in the interim (page:5). Credit implication (analyst interpretation): internal cash generation covered dividends and capital spending but not the acquisitions, debt repayment and Order costs without the equity raise; the Colorado cash consideration was pre-funded rather than leaving a free cushion.
