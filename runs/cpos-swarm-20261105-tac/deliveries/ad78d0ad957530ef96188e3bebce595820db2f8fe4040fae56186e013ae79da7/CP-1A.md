### Business and transaction view

TransAlta is a listed power generator with no controlling owner disclosed in the sources, whose credit profile is set by three things: a part-contracted, part-merchant fleet, a deliberate shift away from coal toward gas and data centre demand, and a programme of acquisitions and partial asset sales that is changing where its debt sits. The fleet is diversified by fuel and region (hydro, wind and solar, gas, with Alberta, other Canadian provinces, the United States and Western Australia), and 53 per cent of capacity is contracted with a weighted average contract life of 9 years; but 61 per cent of capacity is in Alberta and 77 per cent of that is exposed to the merchant market, which the company hedges through commercial customer contracts and financial hedges (hedged production of 9,364 GWh for 2026 and 6,732 GWh for 2027). The significant business changes in the last twelve months are the end of coal-fired operation at Centralia Unit 2 (kept available under a U.S. Department of Energy order whose costs the company has not yet recovered), the retirement of the Ada cogeneration facility, the mothballing of Sheerness Unit 1, Battle River Units 4 and 5 and Sundance 6, the Far North acquisition in Ontario, and the Colorado gas acquisition and Oklahoma wind minority sale that closed on Oct. 22, 2026, after the Sept. 30 balance sheet date. Each of these moves has a different credit mechanism: contracted gas assets add visible cash flow but bring assumed project debt; a minority sale at asset level raises cash while giving a co-owner a share of the Oklahoma cash flows; mothballing preserves optionality but gives up near-term earnings and relies on data centre demand that is, in the company's words, still progressing. Management and board changes in 2026 (a new chief executive from April 30, a new chief financial officer from May 1) coincide with a large capital programme. The company's own characterisations of its position are management language and are labelled so; the documented actions suggest funding discipline (equity issued ahead of the acquisition, credit facility repaid, facility maturities extended) but the pack gives no covenants, debt documents or ownership detail from which creditor protections could be assessed.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

Colorado acquisition (subsequent event, closed Oct. 22, 2026). The company acquired Mountain Peak Power and Canyon Peak Power, two fully contracted natural gas-fired peaking facilities totalling 318 MW near Denver, from an indirect subsidiary of Blackstone, Inc. The purchase price was US$1 billion, including the assumption of US$750 million of project debt; the US$250 million cash portion was funded by the common share offering completed on June 9, 2026 (18,230,000 shares at 19.20 per share, gross proceeds of about 350). The agreement was announced on June 3, 2026 and was subject to Canyon Peak Power reaching commercial in-service and regulatory approvals; closing came on Oct. 22, 2026 (Q2 release page:4 and page:5; Q3 release page:5 and page:98). The accounting is a business combination whose fair values and purchase price allocation are not yet determined (Q3 release page:98). Credit mechanism: the group takes on US$750 million of project-level debt that is not on the Sept. 30 balance sheet, backed by contracted peaking assets, while the cash cost was pre-funded with equity. The sources give no EBITDA, debt terms, amortization or covenants for the assumed project debt, so its effect on consolidated leverage is not calculable and its ranking against group creditors is Insufficient Information.

Oklahoma wind minority sale (subsequent event, completed Oct. 22, 2026). The company sold a 49 per cent interest in its 500 MW Oklahoma wind portfolio (White Rock West, White Rock East, Horizon Hill) to Ares Infrastructure funds for cash proceeds of US$305 million before closing adjustments, and keeps operational oversight under a long-term asset management agreement (Q3 release page:5, page:98). Credit mechanism: cash comes in and the company keeps control and an operating role, but a co-owner now takes 49 per cent of the portfolio's cash flows, giving an outside investor 49 per cent of the portfolio; the accounting treatment, the EBITDA given up and the use of proceeds are not supplied. Management describes the transaction as "strengthening our balance sheet" (Management Language, Q3 release page:2); that effect is not quantified in the pack.

Far North (closed Feb. 2, 2026). Four natural gas-fired facilities totalling 310 MW in Ontario for a purchase price of 95 subject to adjustments (aggregate 107 including working capital adjustments of 12), funded from cash and credit facility borrowings (Q1 release page:4; Q3 release page:77). The acquired business contributed revenue of 27 and earnings before taxes of 4 from Feb. 2 to Sept. 30, 2026 (Q3 release page:77).

Share offering (June 9, 2026). 18,230,000 common shares at 19.20 for gross proceeds of about 350; proceeds were pre-funding for the Colorado cash price and were partly used meanwhile to repay drawings on the syndicated credit facility (Q2 release page:5). Net proceeds of 338 are in the nine-month cash flow statement (Q3 release page:70).

Credit facility extension (July 17, 2026). The syndicated facility moved from June 30, 2029 to June 30, 2030 and the bilateral facilities from June 30, 2027 to June 30, 2028 (Q3 release page:40). This is a maturity extension of existing committed facilities, not new money.

New debt issues. No new note, bond or term loan issue is reported for 2026 in the sources; the only debt issuance in the cash flow statements is 450 in the nine months ended Sept. 30, 2025, with none in 2026 (Q3 release page:70), and the company says 2025 refinancings lowered interest cost on certain senior notes (Q3 release page:19). The terms of the 2025 notes are not supplied. The US$750 million of project debt becomes consolidated through the Colorado acquisition but is assumed, not issued by the company.

Mothballing and wind-down of coal. Centralia Unit 2 ceased coal-fired operations at the end of 2025 and remains available for operation under successive 90-day U.S. Department of Energy orders, the latest running to Dec. 12, 2026; costs to comply are recorded in operating expense and excluded from Adjusted EBITDA; FERC rejected two cost recovery petitions on Oct. 1 and 19, 2026 with new petitions allowed (Q3 release page:32, page:65). The coal-to-gas conversion is supported by a tolling agreement with Puget Sound Energy through 2044 (Q3 release page:44).
