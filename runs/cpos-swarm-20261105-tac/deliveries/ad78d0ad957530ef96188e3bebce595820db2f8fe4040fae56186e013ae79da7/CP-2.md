### Credit view

TransAlta's fundamental credit position is that of a diversified, partly contracted power generator with strong stated liquidity and a visible hedge programme, whose leverage has drifted above management's own target range as the earnings base narrowed, and whose portfolio is being reshaped by transactions that closed after the balance sheet date. On the financial side, management's Adjusted Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDA is 4.1 times at Sept. 30, 2026 against 4.0 times at Dec. 31, 2025, with the calculated balance-sheet ratios at 4.19 times total debt and 3.85 times net debt (financial base handoff, Chart data: leverage); the rise comes from last-four-quarter Adjusted EBITDA of 1,006 against 1,104, because Total Consolidated Net Debt fell to 3,450 from 3,725. Cover is adequate but lower: Adjusted EBITDA to cash interest paid is 3.80 times for nine months 2026 against 4.18 times for nine months 2025. Liquidity is the principal support: 1,867 of Available liquidity, no cash drawn on 2,222 of committed facilities, a syndicated facility maturing in Q2 2030 and bilateral facilities in Q2 2028, and long-term debt maturities of only 52 in 2026 and 332 in 2027. On the business side, 53 per cent of capacity is contracted with a nine-year weighted average life, hedges cover 9,364 GWh for 2026 and 6,732 GWh for 2027 at about 65 per MWh, and the fleet spans hydro, wind and solar and gas across four regions; but 61 per cent of capacity is in Alberta with 77 per cent exposed to the merchant market, the coal earnings at Centralia have ended, and thermal units (Sheerness Unit 1, Battle River Units 4 and 5 and Sundance 6) are mothballed or being mothballed with an earnings effect that is not quantified. The main creditor-relevant uncertainty is structural: 1,585 of non-recourse and OCP bond debt sits behind distribution tests, one project entity failed its Q3 test, US$750 million of project debt was assumed on Oct. 22, 2026 with no terms in the sources, and the instrument-level ranking, guarantees and covenants of the group debt are not supplied. This is a Medium-confidence view: liquidity and near-term maturity facts are well supported by filed statements, while leverage direction, forward earnings and structural ranking rest on management-defined measures and incomplete documents. Issuer-reported ratings of BB+ (S&P, outlook negative) and Ba1 (Moody's, stable) are consistent with a sub-investment-grade profile and are context only.

### Key findings

First, leverage is above management's target and has moved the wrong way for a reason that is earnings, not debt: the financial base handoff shows Total Consolidated Net Debt down from 3,725 to 3,450 and credit facility drawings repaid from 95 to nil, yet last-four-quarter Adjusted EBITDA fell from 1,104 to 1,006, lifting the reported ratio from 4.0 times to 4.1 times. The fall in nine-month Adjusted EBITDA (759 against 857) is more than explained by Energy Transition (down 90, the end of coal at Centralia) and Hydro (down 48, lower Alberta prices and credit sales), while Gas improved by 32 and the third quarter was up 26. The Centralia loss is permanent in the sources' terms; the Hydro fall depends on Alberta prices. Creditors should read the 4.1 times as a ratio whose denominator has not yet stabilised.

Second, liquidity and near-term maturities are comfortable on reported figures: 1,867 of Available liquidity against long-term debt maturing of 550 across 2026 to 2028 (calculated below, about 3.39 times), no cash drawings, and facility extensions to 2030 and 2028. Two qualifications apply: 211 of non-committed letters of credit reduce committed availability, and some cash is held in project entities (the sources give 100 and 84 for restricted cash and do not reconcile them), so the freely movable share of the 345 of cash is not calculable.

Third, the cash generation base is weaker than the earnings headline suggests. Operating cash flow was 317 against 415, free cash flow 338 against 421, and the nine-month cash increase of 140 would have been a decrease of 198 without 338 of share proceeds, which were raised mainly to pre-fund the Colorado cash price. Free cash flow still covered common dividends of 59 with 279 to spare, so distributions are not the pressure point; acquisitions, debt repayment and the Centralia Order costs are.

Fourth, the October 2026 transactions shift the debt profile in ways the Sept. 30 statements do not show. The Colorado acquisition adds US$750 million of assumed project debt and contracted peaking capacity of 318 MW; the Oklahoma sale brings in US$305 million of cash and gives an outside investor 49 per cent of that wind portfolio. The sources supply neither the acquired earnings nor the debt terms, so the direction of net leverage cannot be determined from the pack.

Fifth, the Centralia Order imposes costs that are real cash costs and excluded from Adjusted EBITDA (38 over nine months, 27 in Q3). The unit's coal-fired operations ended but a federal order keeps it available in 90-day steps, and FERC rejected the first two cost recovery petitions while allowing new ones. If the Order continues without recovery, deducting the nine-month cost lifts the illustrative leverage figures to 4.36 times total debt and 4.00 times net debt (financial base handoff, Calculations C10, which excludes any pre-2026 Order cost).

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Liquidity: 1,867 of Available liquidity, no cash drawn on 2,222 of committed facilities, extended maturities, and small debt maturities in 2026 (52) and 2027 (332). Revenue visibility: 53 per cent of capacity is contracted with a weighted average life of 9 years, and hedged production is 9,364 GWh for 2026 and 6,732 GWh for 2027, which the sources show absorbed most of the 2026 fall in Alberta spot prices (realized merchant price 104 against 107 while spot fell to 32 from 44). Diversification: 89 facilities in four regions across hydro, wind and solar and gas. Balance sheet repair in absolute terms: drawn credit facilities repaid, Total Consolidated Net Debt down by 275, interest expense down to 241 from 266 over nine months. Contracted growth: Far North and Colorado add contracted gas capacity, and the Centralia conversion has a tolling agreement with capacity payments through 2044. Prudent pre-funding: the Colorado cash price was equity funded before closing.

Weaknesses. Leverage above target at 4.1 times with the calculated total and net ratios at 4.19 and 3.85 times, and rising since December. Narrowed earnings base: nine-month Adjusted EBITDA down 98, with a lost coal earnings stream and unquantified effects from the mothballed thermal units (Sheerness Unit 1, Battle River Units 4 and 5, Sundance 6). Alberta concentration: 61 per cent of capacity and 77 per cent merchant exposure, with hedge cover that the sources document only to 2027. Structural opacity: 1,585 of non-recourse and OCP debt behind distribution tests (one entity failed its test), exchangeable securities of 750 classified as current, US$750 million of assumed project debt, and no guarantee or ranking detail for 1,673 of senior unsecured debt. Cash flow softness: operating cash flow 317 against 415 and free cash flow 338 against 421, and a nine-month cash build dependent on share proceeds. Unrecovered Order costs and a negative outlook at one rating agency, with up to 59 of credit-contingent collateral. Reliance on management-defined measures for EBITDA, net debt and free cash flow, none of which can be tied to covenant definitions from the pack.

### Credit thesis

TransAlta's credit rests on strong stated liquidity and hedged, part-contracted cash flows that offset a narrower earnings base, leverage above its own target and an October 2026 portfolio reshaping whose debt effects the sources do not let us measure.

- **C3 Nine-month cash flow cover.** Free cash flow 338 less common dividends paid 59 = 279 (financial base handoff C9). Cash change 140 less share proceeds 338 = -198.
- **C4 Net debt change.** Total Consolidated Net Debt 3,725 less 3,450 = 275 reduction (both management-defined, financial base handoff).
