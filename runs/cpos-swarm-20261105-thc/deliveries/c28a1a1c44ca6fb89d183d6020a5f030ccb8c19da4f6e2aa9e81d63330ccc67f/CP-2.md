### Credit view

Tenet's credit profile is sound and moderately levered, but it relies on earnings that are partly non-recurring and partly owned by partners, so the view stays at Medium confidence. The strongest support is USD 4,574 million of liquidity with the USD 1,900 million revolver undrawn, net debt of 2.11x last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA to cash interest of 6.03x, and no maturity above USD 1,931 million before 2030. The weakest point is earnings quality: Adjusted EBITDA includes a USD 147 million transition benefit in Q3 2026 and earnings that belong to noncontrolling partners, so total debt is 3.18x on a partner-adjusted basis. What decides the outcome is whether commercial pricing and volume offset exchange coverage losses and the 2027 Medicaid changes, and whether shareholder payouts stay below free cash flow. [CP-1; CP-1A]

### Assessment

- Fundamental credit view: sound, with moderate leverage and strong liquidity, restrained by earnings-quality and policy risk. Category: Analyst Interpretation. Confidence: Medium. Basis: leverage, cover and liquidity figures [CP-1, Chart data and Financials tables]; payer mix, policy and Conifer rows [CP-1A, Company and operating model; Transaction summary].
- Thesis: Tenet can service and refinance its debt comfortably on reported earnings, but reported earnings overstate sustainable cash available to creditors, and the improvement in leverage depends on continued margin gains rather than on debt reduction.
- Confidence is below High because CP-1A judges CP-2 readiness as ready with limitations, and its gap ledger carries two High items: the CommonSpirit agreements and the indentures and credit agreement are not supplied. These limit covenant headroom, the size of the lost Conifer revenue and distribution capacity; they are stated once here and in Gaps.

### Key findings

First, the balance sheet and liquidity carry the credit. Tenet reports USD 13,243 million of total debt and USD 10,569 million of net debt against last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA of USD 5,004 million, which is 2.65x total and 2.11x net. Liquidity is USD 4,574 million, made up of USD 2,674 million of cash and an undrawn USD 1,900 million revolver, which is 1.28x the USD 3,579 million of debt maturing through 2029. Cash interest cover is 6.03x. The September 2026 refinancing moved USD 2,000 million from 2027 secured and 2028 unsecured notes into 2034 unsecured notes, so the maturity wall through 2029 is USD 3,579 million and the large maturities begin in 2030 at USD 3,493 million. [CP-1, leverage, cover, liquidity and maturities tables; CP-1A, Transaction summary]

Second, the improvement in leverage is earnings-driven. Net debt rose from USD 10,288 million at December 31, 2025 to USD 10,569 million at September 30, 2026, an increase of USD 281 million, while last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA rose from USD 4,566 million to USD 5,004 million. Net leverage fell from 2.25x to 2.11x only because the denominator grew. Cash fell from USD 2,883 million to USD 2,674 million over the same period, after buybacks of USD 1,615 million and partner distributions of USD 614 million in nine months 2026. [CP-1, Financials and credit metrics]

Third, reported earnings overstate recurring cash capacity. Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA includes a USD 147 million favorable transition and timing effect; without it, leverage is 2.73x total and 2.18x net. Operating cash flow for nine months 2026 includes USD 540 million from the CommonSpirit settlement, and the matching redemption payment sits in financing. Adjusted EBITDA is consolidated before noncontrolling interests, and distributions to partners were USD 838 million over the last twelve months; Adjusted EBITDA less those distributions gives 3.18x total and 2.54x net. These are management-attributed or analyst sensitivities, not reported covenant measures. [CP-1, Interpretation; CP-1A, Company and operating model]

Fourth, free cash flow is largely distributed. Operating cash flow less capital expenditure was USD 3,328 million for the last twelve months, or USD 2,788 million without the CommonSpirit receipt. After partner distributions of USD 838 million and buybacks of USD 1,813 million, USD 677 million remains, or USD 137 million without the receipt, before acquisitions and purchases of noncontrolling interests. Tenet therefore builds little cash buffer from operations once payouts are made. The board added USD 2,000 million of buyback authorization in June 2026 and USD 1,875 million remained at September 30, 2026. [CP-1, Calculations; CP-1A, Behaviour rows]

Fifth, the policy and payer-mix risk is real and unsized. Managed care is 68.5% of hospital net patient service revenue and the ten largest payers account for 66% of that revenue. Enhanced marketplace subsidies lapsed at the end of 2025 and exchange admissions have fallen; Medicaid supplemental payments were USD 1,107 million in nine months 2026 and face changes from 2027. Tenet itself says it cannot estimate the effect. The footprint is concentrated in eight states, with most outpatient centers in Arizona and Texas. [CP-1A, Company and operating model]

Sixth, the CommonSpirit exit is cash-supportive but removes recurring revenue that cannot be sized. Tenet is to receive USD 1,900 million over three years, of which USD 540 million was settled by offset, and holds a USD 1,360 million note receivable paid in about USD 453 million installments to January 2029. The revenue cycle contract ends in 2026, and the revenue and margin it contributed are not disclosed. [CP-1A, Transaction summary]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths.

Liquidity and maturity profile (Documentary Fact). USD 4,574 million of liquidity, an undrawn USD 1,900 million asset-based revolver and no maturity above USD 1,931 million before 2030. Mechanism: cash and borrowing-base availability cover near-term maturities with room to spare. Implication: refinancing and liquidity risk are low through 2029. Basis: CP-1 liquidity and maturities.

Leverage and coverage (Documentary Fact). 2.65x total debt and 2.11x net debt to last-twelve-month Adjusted EBITDA, with 6.03x cash interest cover, on a base of fixed-rate notes. Mechanism: fixed coupons insulate interest cost from rate moves. Implication: debt service is comfortable on reported earnings. Basis: CP-1 leverage and cover.

Scale and diversification across two segments (Documentary Fact). Fifty-one hospitals plus a surgery network of 535 centers and 25 surgical hospitals, with both segments contributing Adjusted EBITDA. Mechanism: two earning streams with different growth rates. Implication: partial offset if one segment weakens. Basis: CP-1A company rows.

Liability management without creditor-adverse action (Analyst Interpretation). Two refinancings in ten months extended maturities and, in 2026, cut secured debt; no priming, uptier or drop-down appears in the supplied text. Mechanism: orderly access to capital markets. Implication: supportive behaviour, but legal capacity is unproven. Basis: CP-1A behaviour and timeline rows.

Margin trend (Documentary Fact). Adjusted EBITDA margin was 22.6% for the last twelve months against 21.4% for FY2025. Mechanism: pricing and cost control. Implication: improving earnings supports leverage, subject to the quality caveats. Basis: CP-1 financials.

Weaknesses.

Earnings quality (Analyst Interpretation). Adjusted EBITDA includes a USD 147 million transition benefit and a one-time CommonSpirit settlement that is also in operating cash flow. Mechanism: non-recurring items inflate the run rate. Implication: underlying leverage is 2.73x total and 2.18x net on the stated sensitivity. Basis: CP-1 interpretation; CP-1A transaction rows.

Partner leakage (Analyst Interpretation). Noncontrolling holders received USD 838 million over the last twelve months and redeemable noncontrolling interests are USD 2,179 million outside debt. Mechanism: consolidated EBITDA is partly owned by others. Implication: partner-adjusted leverage is 3.18x total; put-right terms are not supplied. Basis: CP-1 and CP-1A partner rows.

Payout intensity (Analyst Interpretation). Buybacks and partner distributions absorb most free cash flow, and net debt rose in nine months 2026. Mechanism: cash goes to equity and partners before debt reduction. Implication: deleveraging depends on earnings growth; capacity for further payouts is unproven because the supplied documents describe restrictions inconsistently. Basis: CP-1 financials; CP-1A conflict.

Payer and policy exposure (Documentary Fact for the facts; Management Language for the policy effect). Payer concentration, falling exchange admissions and 2027 Medicaid changes against a concentrated footprint. Mechanism: price and mix drive margin. Implication: margin compression can reduce leverage headroom before liquidity is affected. Basis: CP-1A company rows.

Claims outside debt (Documentary Fact). Operating lease liabilities of USD 1,257 million at December 31, 2025, professional and general liability reserves of USD 317 million current and USD 979 million long-term, and defined benefit obligations of USD 238 million sit outside the USD 13,243 million debt figure. Mechanism: fixed and contingent claims on cash. Implication: lease-adjusted and reserve-adjusted leverage is higher than reported. Basis: CP-1 claims outside debt.
