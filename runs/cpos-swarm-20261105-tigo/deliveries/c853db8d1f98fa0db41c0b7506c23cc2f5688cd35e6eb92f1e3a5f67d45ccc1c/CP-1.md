### Financial base view

Millicom's financial base is strong on earnings and weaker on balance-sheet flexibility. Group Adjusted EBITDA reached 1,092 in Q3 2026 and 2,958 over nine months, and cash interest including leases was covered 4.56x over nine months, so debt service is not the pressure point. The pressure is the size and funding of the step-up: gross debt rose from 6,886 at year-end 2025 to 8,648 at September 30, 2026, lease liabilities were 3,190 (2,563 restated at year-end 2025, with Coltel bringing 515), and net debt was 7,983. On the issuer's own definition leverage is 2.53x, down from 2.76x at March 31, 2026 but above the 2.17x at year-end 2025. On a simple trailing basis, with no pro forma uplift and no lease deduction, total debt to Adjusted EBITDA is 2.32x and net debt to Adjusted EBITDA 2.14x. The weak points are liquidity and visibility: cash of 848 compares with 872 due within one year, the only quantified revolving facility (a 600 headline commitment, 565 extended to October 15, 2027) is described only as of December 31, 2025, and neither covenant levels nor debt by instrument are available after year-end 2025. Equity free cash flow was 867 over nine months against dividends paid of 587, and management says it is on track for around 1.1 billion in 2026, but the 1.50 per share interim dividend payable in two installments in January and April 2027 and a dividend payable of 579 (424 at year-end 2025) compete with deleveraging. What decides the view is whether Coltel, which contributed a net loss of 168 to date, reaches profit and whether local-currency debt, 61% of the total in local currency or swapped, stays refinanceable.
