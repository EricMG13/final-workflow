### Credit view

Millicom's credit is sound on earnings and cash generation but thinner on liquidity and creditor visibility than those earnings suggest. The strongest support is USD 2,958 million of nine-month Adjusted EBITDA, which covered cash interest including leases 4.56x, and equity free cash flow of USD 867 million against USD 587 million of dividends paid. The weakest point is the debt-funded Colombia, Ecuador and Uruguay expansion: gross debt is USD 8,648 million, management leverage is 2.53x against a target below 2.5x, cash of USD 848 million is below the USD 872 million due within a year, and revolving facility status, covenants and debt documents are not available for 2026. What decides the outcome is whether Coltel's integration and the 2026 cash flow target of about USD 1.1 billion reduce leverage while dividends keep rising and local-currency refinancing stays open.

### Key findings

1. Millicom's leverage stepped up with the acquisitions and is only starting to come down. Gross debt rose from USD 6,886 million at year-end 2025 to USD 8,648 million at September 30, 2026 and net debt from USD 5,357 million to USD 7,983 million, so management leverage went from 2.17x to 2.53x after peaking at 2.76x at March 31, 2026; on a plain trailing basis total debt is 2.32x and net debt 2.14x of Adjusted EBITDA. Acquired debt (USD 1,424 million at Coltel) and local-currency borrowing drove the increase, so leverage tolerance depends on cost savings turning into EBITDA. [Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 Chart data: leverage, Balance sheet and net debt at period ends, CP-1A Transaction register]

2. Debt service capacity is strong at the current scale. Nine-month 2026 Adjusted EBITDA of USD 2,958 million covered cash interest including lease interest 4.56x and debt interest alone 6.85x, and Q3 2026 cover was 4.30x including leases against 4.44x in Q1 2026. Because Adjusted EBITDA is stated before lease costs, the all-in figure is the relevant one for debt service. [Documentary Fact for the figures, Analyst Interpretation for the choice of basis; basis CP-1 Chart data: interest cover, Calculations]

3. Liquidity is the least visible part of the credit. Millicom holds USD 848 million of cash against USD 872 million of debt due within one year and USD 986 million in the second year, and the only quantified committed line, a revolving facility with USD 565 million extended to October 15, 2027, was undrawn at year-end 2025 but is not reported on for 2026. Total liquidity at September 30, 2026 is therefore not calculable, and the split of cash between the holding company and subsidiaries is not supplied. [Documentary Fact for the figures, Insufficient Information for 2026 facility availability; basis CP-1 Liquidity, Chart data: liquidity, Chart data: debt maturities]

4. Claims outside the debt figure add to fixed charges. Lease liabilities are USD 3,190 million (net financing obligations of USD 11,173 million with net debt), capital-expenditure payables USD 2,101 million, dividends payable USD 579 million, and a Guatemala settlement leaves USD 68 million payable over seven years; including leases, trailing total debt is 3.17x Adjusted EBITDA. Lease payments of USD 483 million in nine months rank with debt service as fixed cash calls. [Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 Claims outside the debt figure, Calculations]

5. Shareholder distributions compete with deleveraging. Millicom pays a growing dividend: USD 3.00 per share in four installments plus a USD 1.50 per share interim dividend payable in January and April 2027, after USD 754 million of dividends and USD 119 million of buybacks in FY2025 against equity free cash flow of USD 916 million. The Niel family group's 44.1% holding gives a large owner, but no support commitment, standstill or holding-company payment limit is documented, so willingness to protect creditors is untested. [Documentary Fact for the payouts and holding, Analyst Interpretation for the competition with deleveraging; basis CP-1A Governance and sponsor behaviour, Ownership]

6. Colombia is now the largest earnings centre and the main execution risk. The two Colombian segments together produced USD 959 million of nine-month Adjusted EBITDA against USD 737 million for Guatemala, Coltel contributed a net loss of USD 168 million with severance costs of about USD 71 million, and purchase accounting for Coltel is provisional. Foreign exchange and other effects added 18.6 points to Q3 2026 service revenue growth of 61.0%, against 5.4% organic, so part of reported growth depends on currency levels. [Documentary Fact for the figures, Analyst Interpretation for the implication; basis CP-1A Credit translations, Operating model, CP-1 Perimeter, restatements and subsequent events]

7. Group structure shapes who is exposed to which cash flows. About 70% of gross debt is held at operating entities and 30% at the corporate level, and the annual report describes operating-level debt as generally non-recourse to the holding company, with USD 629 million of guarantees at year-end 2025. For holding-company creditors the mechanism is dependence on upstreamed cash, some of which is restricted by subsidiary covenants and local reserves; guarantor, security and ranking terms are not supplied. [Analyst Interpretation; basis CP-1 Debt register, Covenants, CP-1A Ownership and control]

### Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

1. A diversified, scaled footprint: Millicom operates in twelve countries with a postpaid base of 17,194 thousand customers at Q3 2026, and the largest segment, Guatemala, produced USD 737 million of the USD 3,485 million of nine-month segment Adjusted EBITDA. This supports revenue visibility and spreads regulatory and currency risk. [Documentary Fact; basis CP-1A Company and operating model, Operating model]

2. Cash generation that covers interest and distributions: nine-month equity free cash flow of USD 867 million exceeded dividends paid of USD 587 million, and FY2025 equity free cash flow of USD 916 million exceeded USD 873 million of dividends and buybacks. [Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 Financials and credit metrics, CP-1A Calculations]

3. Visible deleveraging path: management lowered its year-end leverage target to below 2.5x and raised 2026 equity free cash flow guidance to around USD 1.1 billion, and reported leverage fell from 2.76x at March 31, 2026 to 2.53x at September 30, 2026. [Management Language for the targets, Documentary Fact for the ratios; basis CP-1 Balance sheet and net debt at period ends, CP-1A Timeline]

4. Funding diversity and tenor: 61% of gross debt is in local currency or swapped, average maturity is 3.5 years and the largest maturity bucket, USD 2,094 million, falls after five years. [Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 Debt register, Chart data: debt maturities]

5. Rating position at year-end 2025: Moody's Ba2 with a stable outlook (affirmed October 21, 2025) and Fitch BB+, one notch below investment grade; no 2026 rating action is in the sources. [Documentary Fact, dated; basis CP-1A Timeline, CP-1 Gaps]

**Weaknesses**

1. Higher leverage after debt-funded acquisitions: net debt up 49% since year-end 2025 and management leverage of 2.53x above the 2.5x target, with trailing leverage of 3.17x once leases are included. [Documentary Fact for the figures, Analyst Interpretation for the characterisation; basis CP-1 Calculations]

2. Thin and opaque liquidity: cash is USD 24 million below debt due within one year, and revolving facility availability, letters of credit drawn and holding-company cash are not supplied for 2026. [Documentary Fact and Insufficient Information; basis CP-1 Liquidity]

3. Obligations outside debt: leases, capital-expenditure payables, dividends payable, litigation (claims of USD 217 million, provisions of USD 91 million) and tax exposures (USD 378 million, provisions of USD 60 million) rank beside debt service as cash calls. [Documentary Fact; basis CP-1 Claims outside the debt figure]

4. Integration and concentration risk in Colombia, where Coltel is loss-making, one-off items above Adjusted EBITDA reached USD 149 million in nine months 2026, and the Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 48.0% from 49.0% in Q3 2026. [Documentary Fact; basis CP-1A Operating model, Credit translations]

5. Rising distributions and an untested support stance from a 44.1% holder. [Analyst Interpretation; basis CP-1A Governance and sponsor behaviour]

6. Currency and macro exposure: stronger Colombian, Paraguayan and Bolivian currencies lifted reported results, and Bolivia has moved to a flexible exchange rate regime. [Documentary Fact; basis CP-1A Operating model, CP-1 Perimeter, restatements and subsequent events]

7. Missing creditor-protection evidence: indentures, covenant levels, guarantor structure and transaction agreements are not supplied, so legal capacity to prime, distribute or release collateral cannot be tested. [Insufficient Information; basis CP-1A Governance and sponsor behaviour, Gaps]
