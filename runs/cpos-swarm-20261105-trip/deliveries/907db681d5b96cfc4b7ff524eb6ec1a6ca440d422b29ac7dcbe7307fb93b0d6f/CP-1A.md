### Business and transaction view

Tripadvisor, Inc. is in the middle of a deliberate change of business mix, and the creditor question is whether the new mix earns enough to carry the debt before the old mix erodes further. The old core, Hotels and Other, earns click-based and media advertising from travel partners and depends on traffic from search engines; management itself says search placement changes and AI overviews are reducing that traffic and that the segment will keep being affected. The new core, Experiences, is a commission-based marketplace for tours and activities led by Viator, which grew through 2025 but turned negative in the third quarter of 2026 after macro disruptions, higher cancellations and weaker Tripadvisor point-of-sale traffic. Against that, the company repaid its convertible notes in cash in April 2026, sold TheFork to American Express with closing on November 1, 2026 for about 741.0 of net cash, and started a further restructuring plan on November 4, 2026. Ownership changed in April 2025 when the merger with LTRIP removed the controlling stockholder, so there is no sponsor and no documented extraction by an owner, but also no anchor holder, and a cooperation agreement of March 22, 2026 and shareholder activism costs show outside pressure on strategy. What cannot be assessed from the pack is legal capacity: the credit agreement, the sale agreements and the beneficial ownership table are not supplied, so restrictions on asset sales, prepayment of sale proceeds and investor control are known only from filing summaries. The financial scale of these points is in the financial handoff: LTM Adjusted EBITDA of 207.3, debt principal of 833.9 and cash of 706.8 before the sale proceeds.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The one transaction in the period is the sale of TheFork, the company's European online restaurant reservation and management platform, to American Express Travel Related Services Company, Inc. (a strategic buyer; no sponsor is involved). On June 14, 2026 the company entered into a put option agreement to sell the legal entities comprising TheFork "for $700.0 million in cash, subject to certain adjustments", and the Q3 10-Q states that American Express "provided an irrevocable commitment to acquire TheFork" (Q3 10-Q page:11). After the French Works Council consultation completed on July 30, 2026, the company exercised the put on August 1, 2026 and signed the Equity Purchase Agreement on August 2, 2026. The sale closed on November 1, 2026; the Q3 10-Q states "The total net cash consideration received at closing was approximately $741.0 million", reflecting adjustments for closing working capital, cash and indebtedness and net of transaction expenses; management expects a fourth-quarter 2026 gain of approximately $487.0 million and transaction costs of $16.0 million to $18.0 million netted against the gain, and warns that both amounts "may differ materially" (Q3 10-Q page:11 and page:65). Status: closed, with final adjustments still open. Accounting: TheFork was classified as held for sale and as a discontinued operation from the second quarter of 2026, with assets held for sale of 290.7 and liabilities held for sale of 62.3 at September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:12 and page:16), and an accumulated other comprehensive loss of 21.4 to be netted against the gain (page:17). A Transition Services Agreement of up to 12 months, for finance, information technology, human resources and other administrative services, started at closing (page:12-13), and the company expects no significant effect on future operating cash flows (page:65). Liquidity effect: the proceeds arrived after the September 30, 2026 balance sheet and are in no reported cash figure; management states that the proceeds provide "additional flexibility in its capital structure", that it "will continue to maintain optionality with respect to its use of the net proceeds", and that it "currently expects that the Company will prioritize reduction of its long-term debt" (Q3 10-Q page:65). That is a Management Language statement of intent, not an executed commitment, and the pack does not show whether the Term Loan B can be prepaid with sale proceeds or whether the credit agreement requires any mandatory prepayment from asset sales, because the credit agreement is not supplied. Credit effect, analyst reading: the sale removes a segment that contributed 221 of FY2025 consolidated revenue (10-K page:189) and, with it, diversification away from advertising, but it leaves the capital structure unchanged until proceeds are applied, and it adds to cash against a term loan of 833.9.

New debt issues: none in 2026. The company raised no new debt in the first nine months of 2026; the only debt movement was the April 1, 2026 repayment of the 0.25% Convertible 2026 Senior Notes at maturity for $345.4 million including interest, funded by cash on hand, after which the capped calls expired unexercised (Q3 10-Q page:25-26). Earlier issues that define the present structure: the $500 million Term Loan B Facility issued July 8, 2024 at 99.75% of par, used to redeem the $500 million 2025 Senior Notes on July 15, 2024; the $350 million tack-on of March 20, 2025 at 98.56% of par, also due July 8, 2031 at SOFR plus 2.75%; and the Third Amendment of June 5, 2025, which redefined the revolver's springing maturity by reference to the 2026 notes and a cash test (10-K page:91 and page:160-163). Analyst reading: because the condition refers to notes outstanding of $200 million or more, it appears moot now that the notes are repaid, but the amended text itself is not in the pack. The revolver of $500.0 million matures June 29, 2028 and is undrawn.

Other transactions in the history that bear on the creditor: the merger with LTRIP, which closed on April 29, 2025 at an aggregate transaction price of $437 million, accounted for as a repurchase of the company's own shares held by LTRIP; before it, on March 20, 2025, the company lent LTRIP $326.7 million (about $327 million) so LTRIP could settle its exchangeable debentures, and that loan balance was folded into the transaction price (Q3 10-Q page:10; 10-K page:72). The aggregate cash paid including expenses was $410.7 million, plus 3,037,959 shares valued at about $38.9 million, less $13.1 million of LTRIP tax losses retained (Q3 10-Q page:10). The sources state that the tack-on proceeds were used to repay the 2026 notes; they do not state that the tack-on funded the merger. As an analyst reading only, gross debt rose by about 341 during 2025 while about 450.7 left the company in the merger repurchase and share buyback, and the notes were then repaid from cash in 2026; the filings do not link the two flows. A further restructuring plan was initiated on November 4, 2026 for the Hotels and Other segment with expected pre-tax costs of approximately $15 million to $20 million (Q3 10-Q page:37), and the Q3 release adds that management expects annualized gross savings of approximately $40 million (Q3 PR page:3), a release-only management claim that does not appear in the 10-Q.
