### Credit view

TPG RE Finance Trust, Inc. is a moderately leveraged, externally managed commercial real estate lender whose asset quality looks stable but whose cushions are narrowing. The strongest support is a senior, floating-rate loan book with a weighted average risk rating of 3.0, no non-accrual loans and commitments of USD 5,168.7 million, funded 78.8% without market-value margin calls. The weakest point is leverage: debt of USD 4,198.1 million against equity of USD 1,024.1 million gives 4.03x, above the 3.75:1 the company expected in its 10-K, with USD 52.6 million of cash tied up for covenants. What decides the outcome is whether loan repayments and the October 2026 CLO bring leverage and liquidity back toward the company's own range while earnings cover the dividend.

### Thesis

The lender's senior, floating-rate, mostly multifamily and industrial loan book and long-dated, largely non-marked funding support its credit quality, but a rise in leverage to 4.03x, thin usable liquidity and dividend cover below one after realized losses leave a narrowing cushion that depends on repayments and refinancing execution through 2028.

### Key findings

TPG RE Finance Trust, Inc. grew its balance sheet with debt in 2026, and that is the finding that matters most. Debt principal rose from USD 3,309.5 million at 2025-12-31 to USD 4,198.1 million at 2026-09-30, while stockholders' equity fell from USD 1,068.0 million to USD 1,024.1 million, so the company's own total leverage ratio rose from 3.02x to 4.03x and the debt-to-equity ratio excluding the Term Loan B from 3.02x to 3.64x. The 10-K had said the company expected total debt to equity to be generally below 3.75:1. On the ratio that includes the Term Loan B, TRTX is above that range; reducing net debt by about USD 286.8 million, or adding about USD 76.5 million of equity, would bring it back to 3.75x. The basis of the 3.75:1 expectation after the Term Loan B is not stated, so this is a pointer, not a breach [Documentary Fact; CP-1: Financials and credit metrics; CP-1A: logged difference on leverage expectation].

The company's funding became more corporate and more recourse. A USD 400.0 million Term Loan B at Term SOFR plus 2.75% maturing in May 2033 and an undrawn USD 100.0 million Revolver closed in May 2026; both are fully recourse, as is the USD 265.0 million secured revolving facility. Together with partial recourse to Holdco on the secured credit agreements, nominal recourse exposure is USD 1,051.7 million, about 1.03 times equity, and the cash the company holds for its liquidity covenant, USD 52.6 million, is consistent with five percent of that exposure. Against this, 45.7% of debt is non-recourse CRE CLO debt and 78.8% is not subject to market-value margin calls [Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation; CP-1: Funding structure and recourse].

Liquidity improved in headline terms but is thin in usable terms. Near-term liquidity of USD 291.6 million at 2026-09-30 compares with USD 143.0 million at 2025-12-31, but USD 52.6 million of the USD 70.8 million of cash is held for covenants, and only USD 87.3 million of the USD 777.8 million of secured credit agreement availability counts as available without pledging more collateral. After covenant cash, liquidity of about USD 239.0 million is about equal to USD 236.6 million of unfunded loan commitments [Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation; CP-1: Liquidity].

Earnings softened in the latest quarter and cover of the dividend is below one after realized losses. Net income was USD 9.4 million in Q3 2026 against USD 22.0 million a year earlier, and net interest income fell 5.7%. Distributable Earnings before realized losses covered the common dividend 0.98 times in Q3 2026, but after the USD 13.0 million realized loss on a Plano foreclosure Distributable Earnings were USD 5.1 million against common dividends declared of USD 18.5 million, a cover of 0.28 times. Over nine months of 2026 they covered declared common dividends 0.98 times before and 0.75 times after realized losses, while the company also bought back shares for USD 24.6 million [Documentary Fact; CP-1: Earnings measures; CP-1A: Governance].

Asset quality indicators remain stable but the book is transitional and growing. Weighted average risk rating is 3.0, no loan was on non-accrual at 2026-09-30, and the allowance is 147 bp of commitments, or USD 76.1 million. Multifamily and industrial are 75.2% of commitments and loans are 99.7% floating rate. Offsetting this, 64.0% of commitments are bridge loans, seven real estate owned properties are held after a September 2026 foreclosure, and office exposure rose from 4.3% to 7.9% after a USD 212.0 million San Francisco loan, after management said in April 2026 that office exposure was below 5% [Documentary Fact and Management Language; CP-1: Credit quality; CP-1A: logged difference on office exposure].

Refinancing risk is concentrated in 2028 and depends on extensions. Debt maturing in 2028 is USD 1,130.8 million, 26.9% of principal, including USD 792.6 million of secured credit agreements and USD 265.0 million of the secured revolver; CLO maturities follow loan repayments. A USD 1.0 billion managed CRE CLO closed on 2026-10-02, after the balance sheet date, with about USD 820.0 million of investment-grade notes placed, but its use of proceeds is not disclosed, so its effect on leverage and recourse is unknown [Documentary Fact; CP-1: Chart data: debt maturities; CP-1A: Transaction summary].

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strength: collateral quality indicators. The loan book is entirely senior first mortgage loans, with a weighted average risk rating of 3.0 unchanged since 2025-12-31, no non-accrual loans, a weighted average loan-to-value of 65.9% at origination and an allowance of 147 bp of commitments. The mechanism is that senior, floating-rate lending with interest rate floors protects income and gives asset-level lenders collateral cover; the implication is limited realized losses so far (Documentary Fact; CP-1: Credit quality). The loan-to-value is an underwriting measure, not a current valuation.

Strength: funding structure. 78.8% of debt is not subject to market-value margin calls, 45.7% is non-recourse CLO debt isolated from parent assets, the Term Loan B runs to May 2033 and the Revolver to May 2031, and secured credit agreement lenders are four banks. The mechanism is that forced deleveraging through margin calls is limited and term funding is long; the implication is lower exposure to market-value margin calls, although the secured credit agreements remain repurchase-style borrowings with credit marks (Documentary Fact; CP-1: Funding structure and recourse).

Strength: disclosure and interest rate positioning. The company publishes a loan-by-loan schedule, risk ratings, recourse by facility and a liquidity table, and a 25 bp rise in rates adds about USD 2.9 million to annual net interest income because net floating-rate exposure is USD 1,186.2 million. The implication is good monitorability and a positive rate sensitivity (Documentary Fact; CP-1A: Company and operating model).

Weakness: leverage and thin equity cushion. Total leverage of 4.03x is above the 10-K's 3.75:1 expectation, equity fell 4.1% in nine months, and the indicative ratio of debt principal to total assets is 80.1% against a covenant maximum of 83.333%; assets could fall by only about USD 205.2 million, or 3.9%, before that indicative ratio reaches the maximum. The mechanism is that realized and provisioned losses reduce equity directly while debt is fixed; the implication is limited tolerance for credit losses before covenant pressure, subject to the unsupplied covenant definition (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1: Covenants, Calculations).

Weakness: usable liquidity and lender discretion. Cash tied to covenants rises with each draw on recourse debt, secured credit agreement availability depends on lender-approved collateral, and unfunded commitments are USD 236.6 million. The mechanism is that liquidity can shrink when repayments slow or when the recourse base grows; the implication is dependence on repayments and the October 2026 CLO for headroom (Analyst Interpretation; CP-1: Liquidity).

Weakness: earnings cover and distributions. Dividends and buybacks exceeded earnings after realized losses in Q3 2026, and nine-month Distributable Earnings of USD 42.3 million were below declared common dividends of USD 56.7 million. The mechanism is that distributions drawn from equity reduce the cushion beneath creditors; the implication is that continued distributions at this level depend on earnings recovery (Documentary Fact; CP-1: Earnings measures).

Weakness: external management and affiliate arrangements. The manager earns 1.50% of Equity as defined regardless of performance, the fee base implied by the Q3 2026 fee exceeds stockholders' equity, three of eight directors are TPG employees and affiliate payments are disclosed but small. The mechanism is that the manager's income does not fall with credit losses; the implication is indirect alignment with creditors and no documented sponsor support (Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation; CP-1A: Ownership and control, Governance).

Weakness: missing measures. EBITDA, covenant ratio values and executed agreements are not available, so leverage and coverage on lender definitions cannot be tested (Insufficient Information; limitation stated in the gaps).
