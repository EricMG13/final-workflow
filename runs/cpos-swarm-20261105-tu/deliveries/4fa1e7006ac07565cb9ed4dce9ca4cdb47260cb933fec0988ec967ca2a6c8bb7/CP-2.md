### Credit view

TELUS Corporation is a large Canadian telecommunications borrower whose credit is sound on liquidity and covenants today but weakening in direction. The strongest support is scale and recurring mobile and fibre earnings (LTM Adjusted EBITDA C$ 7,315 million), a reported leverage ratio of 3.5x against a 4.25x covenant, cash of C$ 1,387 million, C$ 657 million of revolver availability to 2030 and investment-grade rating wording. The weakest point is leverage: reported 3.5x relies on half equity credit for C$ 7,322 million of junior subordinated notes, gross debt to EBITDA is 4.30x, cash interest cover fell to 4.62x and 2026 guidance was cut. What decides the outcome is whether the dividend reset, capital discipline and unquantified asset sales bring leverage toward the year-end 2028 target while commercial paper and bond markets remain open.

### Key findings

First, leverage is the central issue and depends on definition. Net debt of C$ 25,963 million over LTM EBITDA excluding restructuring and other costs of C$ 7,315 million is 3.55x unrounded (3.5x as reported), above the issuer's new 2.5x to 3.0x objective range, and below the 4.25x covenant limit by 0.70x on the issuer's own measure. The issuer gives one-half equity credit to its junior subordinated notes; gross debt of C$ 31,456 million is 4.30x LTM EBITDA, and before the equity credit net debt was 4.02x at March 31, 2026. The covenant definitions are not supplied, so headroom is indicative [CP-1: Chart data leverage; Definitions review].

Second, cash interest cover is declining. LTM EBITDA over cash interest paid was 4.62x at June 30, 2026, 5.09x at March 31, 2026 and 5.31x at FY2025. Interest paid rose on 2025 junior subordinated issuance, a promissory note and a C$ 51 million prepayment premium, so part of the fall is one-off; the issuer's own coverage measure is 4.9x against a 2.00x covenant floor [CP-1: Chart data interest cover].

Third, liquidity is adequate; refinancing relies on market access. Cash is C$ 1,387 million, revolver availability C$ 657 million after a C$ 2,093 million commercial paper backstop, securitization availability C$ 680 million and a non-revolving facility C$ 344 million. Current maturities of long-term debt are C$ 3,802 million and include commercial paper that the revolver supports. The maturity schedule, dated March 31, 2026, shows C$ 3,148 million falling due in the rest of 2026, C$ 1,928 million in 2027 and C$ 2,319 million in 2028 [CP-1: Chart data liquidity and debt maturities].

Fourth, earnings and guidance weakened in the quarter. Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA was C$ 1,777 million against C$ 1,812 million, with a net loss of C$ 1,830 million after a C$ 2,135 million non-cash TELUS Digital impairment, which does not touch covenants. Management lowered 2026 guidance: service revenue flat to a 2 per cent decline, Adjusted EBITDA down 2 to 4 per cent, free cash flow about C$ 1.8 billion from about C$ 2.45 billion, and capital expenditures about C$ 2.6 billion from about C$ 2.3 billion (Management Language) [CP-1: Subsequent events and guidance].

Fifth, the deleveraging plan rests on management actions rather than organic cash flow. The quarterly dividend was reset to C$ 0.1875 per share from C$ 0.4184, with management estimating about C$ 2.7 billion of cash savings through 2028; the 3.0x target date moved from year-end 2027 to year-end 2028 and the objective range moved from 2.2x to 2.7x to 2.5x to 3.0x. Asset sales of TELUS Health non-core assets and real estate are under way with no assets, prices or timing supplied [CP-1A: Capital allocation; Conflicts].

Sixth, the perimeter changed in 2025 and structural position is incomplete. TELUS bought out the TELUS Digital minority on October 31, 2025 and sold minority equity in the Terrion tower partnership for net cash proceeds of C$ 1,246 million while keeping 50.1 per cent. The senior notes are effectively subordinated to subsidiary obligations; guarantor terms and Terrion governance are not supplied [CP-1A: Transaction summary; Ownership and control].

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths.

Scale and recurring earnings (Documentary Fact). TELUS Corporation earned LTM Adjusted EBITDA of C$ 7,315 million, and mobile network revenue of C$ 1,743 million in Q2 2026 compared with C$ 1,723 million a year earlier. Subscriber growth in mobile, connected devices and internet supports revenue visibility even with declining ARPU. This lowers the probability that operating earnings alone force a default; the weakness is pricing, not volume [CP-1: Financials and credit metrics; CP-1A: Company and operating model].

Covenant headroom (Analyst Interpretation on documentary figures). Reported leverage of 3.5x sits 0.70x below the 4.25x limit, an EBITDA cushion of C$ 1,206 million (16.5 per cent) at constant net debt, and coverage of 4.9x sits 2.9x above the 2.00x floor. The covenants are not affected by goodwill revaluation, so the C$ 2,135 million impairment does not reduce headroom. This limits near-term acceleration risk, subject to unseen covenant definitions [CP-1: Covenant and liquidity position].

Liquidity and access (Documentary Fact and Management Language). Cash of C$ 1,387 million, revolver availability of C$ 657 million expiring August 2030, securitization availability of C$ 680 million and a non-revolving facility availability of C$ 344 million cover the issuer's stated objective of at least C$ 1 billion of available liquidity. The investment-grade wording from management supports commercial paper and bond access, though agency ratings are not supplied [CP-1: Covenant and liquidity position].

Management response (Management Language). The dividend reset, removal of the reinvestment plan discount and the end-2028 leverage target direct cash toward debt reduction, and junior subordinated notes and Terrion minority equity have reduced reported leverage. This improves debt service capacity if executed, but the savings figure is a management estimate [CP-1A: Capital allocation and disclosure].

Weaknesses.

Leverage above objective and definition-dependent (Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation). Reported 3.5x exceeds the 2.5x to 3.0x range, gross leverage is 4.30x, and the equity credit on junior subordinated notes depends on agency treatment that is not supplied. If that treatment changed, reported leverage would move toward the gross figure and headroom would shrink [CP-1: Definitions review].

Falling cash interest cover (Documentary Fact). Cover declined from 5.31x to 4.62x over two quarters as interest paid rose, and junior subordinated notes carry coupons of 5.375 to 7.000 per cent. Refinancing of lower-coupon senior notes at current rates would add to interest, an inference the sources do not quantify [CP-1: Chart data interest cover; Debt register].

Weaker earnings momentum and guidance (Management Language and Documentary Fact). Adjusted EBITDA fell, TELUS Digital Adjusted EBITDA was C$ 72 million against C$ 89 million, and management attributes the Digital weakness to demand ramp-downs and churn at certain hyperscale customers. Lower free cash flow guidance reduces internally funded deleveraging and increases reliance on asset sales [CP-1: Segment and KPI view; CP-1A: Company and operating model].

Distributions and capital intensity (Documentary Fact). Dividends declared were 109 per cent of cash from operations less capital expenditures over the twelve months to June 30, 2026, and capital expenditure guidance rose to about C$ 2.6 billion with a spectrum auction in October 2027. The dividend reset addresses the first point after quarter-end; the spectrum payment is a future call on cash [CP-1A: Governance and behaviour; Capital allocation].

Refinancing dependence (Analyst Interpretation). Current maturities of C$ 3,802 million include commercial paper of about C$ 2.1 billion (US$ 1.5 billion) that consumes revolver capacity one for one. Commercial paper itself depends on rollover, while other near-term maturities can draw on cash, securitization and bank facility availability or new issuance. Market closure would tighten liquidity quickly [CP-1: Chart data liquidity].

Structure and information (Insufficient Information). Guarantor terms, Terrion governance and its own debt, the audited annual statements and the credit agreements are not supplied. Missing evidence is not an adverse finding, but it prevents a view of loss given default or covenant robustness and keeps confidence at Medium [CP-0; CP-1A: Gaps].

### Credit thesis

TELUS Corporation is a scale telecommunications borrower with adequate liquidity and covenant headroom whose credit quality is under pressure from above-objective, definition-dependent leverage, falling cash interest cover and lowered guidance, so the outcome depends on delivering dividend-funded deleveraging and asset sales while market access stays open (Analyst Interpretation).
