### Business and transaction view

Victory Capital is a diversified, asset-based investment manager whose credit profile rests on three things the sources document. First, a fee-on-assets revenue model: revenue moves with average assets under management, the fee rate and net flows, and fund advisory agreements are cancellable on short notice, so revenue is recurring in form but not contractually locked. Second, a multi-franchise, centralized-platform structure that management says keeps about two-thirds of operating expense variable and capital spending minimal, which supports cash conversion but depends on the investment teams being retained. Third, a strategic relationship with Amundi that is at once shareholder, board-nominating party, exclusive offshore distributor and exclusive supplier of Amundi's active products into the United States, which concentrates ownership, distribution and governance in one counterparty. The consolidated company's only debt is one term loan with an undrawn revolver (CP-1); ownership has no sponsor named, but no controlling-holder register is in the pack beyond Amundi and employee ownership. The transaction that matters most to a creditor is the pending acquisition of First Eagle for cash, common shares and a new series of non-voting convertible preferred stock, financed in part by a debt commitment letter whose amount and terms are not in the pack. All views here are on the standalone, pre-First Eagle company, and every First Eagle consequence is a gap, not a conclusion.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The Amundi US transaction closed on April 1, 2025. In exchange for all the shares of Amundi's U.S. business, Victory issued 3,293,471 shares of common stock and 19,698,274 shares of preferred stock (net of 44,026 preferred shares forfeited in a post-closing adjustment on August 1, 2025, per 10-K page:109), together 26.1% of fully diluted shares; the purchase price was approximately 1,326 US$ million, settled entirely in Company shares (Q3-10Q page:11). The 10-K exhibit index lists a certificate of designations for Series A Non-Voting Convertible Preferred Stock, and Amundi may require Victory to nominate two Amundi designees to the board (10-K page:135). Alongside the equity, Victory and Amundi entered exclusive distribution agreements, a 15-year exclusive offshore distribution arrangement that auto-renews in 2040 (10-K page:14). The purchase price allocation was finalized in Q1 2026 (Q3-10Q page:12). Pioneer Investments contributed revenue of about 155 US$ million in Q3 2026 and 517 US$ million in nine months 2026, and the filing states that net income attributable to Pioneer is "impractical to determine" because discrete franchise-level financial information is not prepared (Q3-10Q page:12). The Amundi deal is a completed transaction, so for creditors it explains the 2025 jump in assets and in goodwill and intangibles, not a new financing event.

Debt events in the period are refinancings, not new money. On September 23, 2025 the Sixth Amendment extended the revolver to September 23, 2030 and repriced the term loan at 985.0 US$ million; on May 18, 2026 the Seventh Amendment repriced the term loan to SOFR plus 1.75% (Q2-10Q page:18; Q3-10Q page:20). No bond, note or other new debt issue appears in the pack. Costs of the Seventh Amendment were 2.4 US$ million, of which 2.0 US$ million was a loss on debt extinguishment (Q3-10Q page:21).

The pending First Eagle transaction, all of it a subsequent event relative to the Q2 2026 reports and still pending at the cutoff, is described in the Q3 2026 report: on August 25, 2026 Victory agreed to acquire GC Ferry Holdings, Inc. from GC Ferry Parent, L.P. through a two-step merger; consideration is a combination of cash, newly issued common stock and a new series of non-voting convertible preferred stock; Victory entered a debt commitment letter with certain financial institutions to fund the cash portion and related fees; closing is expected by the end of the first quarter of 2027, subject to approval by Victory stockholders, regulatory approvals and client consents (Q3-10Q pages:12 and 13). Management calls it "transformational" and says it adds a scaled CLO and alternative credit platform (R3 page:2), which is Management Language. For a creditor the mechanics are: cash consideration funded by new debt raises leverage from the current low standalone level by an amount that is not disclosed; new preferred stock adds a senior-to-common claim, as the Amundi preferred already is; share issuance dilutes and may shift control, and the closing depends on stockholder and client consents, so completion risk and the post-closing capital structure are both open. Transaction costs already expensed were 15.5 US$ million in Q3 2026 and 22.5 US$ million in nine months 2026 (Q3-10Q page:13). Litigation followed the proxy: two stockholder complaints and ten demand letters seek, among other things, to enjoin closing until more disclosure is made (Q3-10Q page:48); Victory calls them without merit and gives no loss estimate. No amount of consideration, cash portion, commitment size, lenders, pricing, ticking fees or conditions of the financing is in the pack, and no First Eagle financial statements are supplied, so the new debt issue, pro forma leverage, and pro forma liquidity are Not supplied and Not calculable. Dated after the Q3 reporting period but inside the cutoff: the definitive proxy statement (October 16, 2026) and the dividend of 0.50 dollars per share approved on November 4, 2026.

### Credit translations

1. Evidence: fund advisory agreements terminable on not more than 60 days' notice (10-K page:26), and revenues that "are based on the level of client assets we manage" (10-K page:16). Mechanic: assets can be withdrawn or fall with markets with little notice, so fee revenue and EBITDA fall quickly. Implication: debt service capacity is high today but is exposed to a market or performance shock; monitoring posture should track monthly flows and AUM. Counter: management says about two-thirds of operating expense is variable, which dampens margin impact.
2. Evidence: Amundi as 26.1% fully diluted holder, two board nominees, exclusive distribution (Q3-10Q page:11; 10-K page:135, page:14). Mechanic: one counterparty influences ownership, governance and the offshore revenue channel; a dispute or change could affect distribution and strategic direction. Implication: key uncertainty on counterparty dependence; creditor control is not affected as there is no sponsor, but equity ownership is partly in a preferred class with its own terms Not supplied.
3. Evidence: pending First Eagle acquisition with a debt commitment letter and cash consideration (Q3-10Q pages:12 and 13); CP-1 shows low standalone leverage on a single term loan. Mechanic: cash funded by new debt will raise debt service and leverage by an undisclosed amount, while preferred issuance adds a claim ranking ahead of common; the benefit depends on integration and net flows of an acquired platform. Implication: leverage tolerance after closing is the central open question; committee readiness is limited until the financing documents are in the pack. Counter: the conflict log has no direct conflict on the First Eagle terms, but absence of amounts is itself the limitation.
4. Evidence: net flows swung from outflow to inflow (R1 page:1; R3 page:2) and Q3 2026 revenue fell against Q2 2026 on annual fee timing (R3 page:3). Mechanic: quarterly revenue is volatile around performance and annual fees. Implication: use LTM rather than single-quarter EBITDA for any ratio, as CP-1 does.
