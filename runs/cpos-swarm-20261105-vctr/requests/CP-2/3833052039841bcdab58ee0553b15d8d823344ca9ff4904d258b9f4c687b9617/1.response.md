### Credit view

Victory Capital's standalone credit profile is sound on every measure the sources allow, but the credit view after the First Eagle acquisition is open. The strongest support is balance sheet and cash generation: USD 975.15 million of term loan against USD 856.348 million of last-twelve-months adjusted EBITDA (1.14x gross, 0.90x net), cash interest cover of 14.83x, USD 307.724 million of liquidity and a first maturity of any size in September 2032. The weakest point is that earnings are fee income on assets that clients can withdraw on short notice, and a large cash-and-shares acquisition is pending whose debt, terms and target financials are not in the sources. What decides the outcome is the size and pricing of the First Eagle financing, whether closing happens as planned, and whether net inflows persist after the record third quarter of 2026.

### Key findings

1. The standalone balance sheet carries one debt instrument, the term loan under the 2019 Credit Agreement of USD 975.15 million at September 30, 2026, alongside an undrawn USD 100.0 million revolver, described as first lien, maturing September 23, 2030 [CP-1 capital structure]. The interest rate is 5.82%, floating at SOFR plus 1.75% since the May 18, 2026 repricing. Leverage on management's adjusted EBITDA fell from 1.44x at FY2025 to 1.14x at the last twelve months to Q3 2026 [CP-1 leverage chart]; the fall comes from higher EBITDA (USD 682.851 million to USD 856.348 million) and from the FY2025 figure including only three quarters of the Amundi US business, which means the comparison flatters the improvement. On the stricter proxy of operating income plus depreciation and amortization (USD 727.102 million), leverage is 1.34x gross and 1.06x net [CP-1 definition risks]. Either way leverage is far under the single covenant level of 4.00x, but that covenant is defined on first lien debt and a covenant EBITDA that are not in the sources, so headroom is Not calculable.

2. Cash interest cover rose from 8.57x at FY2025 to 14.83x on the last twelve months [CP-1 interest cover chart], and part of that rise is the last-twelve-months window dropping an unexplained third quarter of 2025 cash interest payment of USD 30.025 million. Third quarter of 2026 cash interest was USD 13.696 million, so the last-twelve-months cash interest of USD 57.742 million is not out of line with the recent quarterly pace.

3. Liquidity at September 30, 2026 was USD 307.724 million against USD 170.126 million at June 30, 2026. The change is cash: USD 207.724 million against USD 70.126 million, with the revolver unchanged [CP-1 liquidity]. Calls on it include USD 53.8 million of contingent consideration for the WestEnd acquisition estimated payable within a year, quarterly term loan amortization of about USD 2.46 million, the dividend of USD 0.50 a share payable December 23, 2026, and the unknown cash portion of First Eagle. Part of consolidated cash sits in a regulated broker-dealer whose cash is generally not available for corporate purposes [CP-1 claims outside debt]; the split is Not supplied.

4. Cash generation is strong but is allocated to equity holders. Operating cash flow less capital expenditure was USD 621.193 million over the last twelve months [CP-1 calculations], 0.64x of gross debt (calculated below). In nine months 2026 dividends, repurchases, taxes on net share settlement and contingent payments used USD 443.212 million of USD 479.74 million operating cash flow, 92.4% (calculated below), and net term loan repayment was USD 7.388 million [CP-1 cash flow]. This is Analyst Interpretation: nothing in the sources shows these distributions funded by debt, and no creditor-adverse action is documented [CP-1A governance], but the credit agreement's restricted payment terms are not supplied, so legal capacity to distribute is unknown.

5. The business model concentrates risk in assets and people rather than in assets on the balance sheet. Revenue is fees on assets; fund advisory contracts are terminable on not more than 60 days' notice; the largest franchise, Pioneer Investments, was acquired from Amundi and sits at about the 42% ceiling the company states; Amundi is also a 26.1% fully diluted holder with two board nomination rights and the exclusive offshore distributor [CP-1A operating model; ownership]. Management says about two-thirds of operating expense is variable, a Management Language claim, but personnel compensation rose in the third quarter of 2026, so the cushion is not proved [CP-1A operating model].

6. First Eagle changes the credit profile. Consideration is cash, new common stock and a new series of non-voting convertible preferred stock, funded in part under a debt commitment letter; closing is expected by the end of the first quarter of 2027, subject to stockholder, regulatory and client approvals; stockholder litigation seeks to delay it [CP-1A transaction summary]. No amounts are given. Any post-closing leverage, liquidity, ranking or covenant statement is Not calculable and is withheld.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. (1) Low standalone leverage and strong cover on the sources' own measures, Documentary Fact from the CP-1 tables, with the caveat that adjusted EBITDA is a management measure that adds back share-based compensation and acquisition costs; the stricter proxy still gives a leverage level well within the covenant level. (2) A long, simple debt structure: one term loan to September 23, 2032, amortizing 1% a year, with calculated annual maturities of USD 9.85 million before the bullet of about USD 918.5 million, and a revolver to September 23, 2030 that is undrawn (Documentary Fact for the dates, Analyst Interpretation for the calculated schedule). (3) Liquidity of USD 307.724 million, with minimal capital spending of USD 3.024 million in nine months 2026 (Documentary Fact). (4) Scale and diversity across eight investment franchises, a Solutions Platform and four client channels, with the largest franchise at about 42% of assets under management (Documentary Fact). (5) Net inflows in the second and third quarters of 2026 after outflows in the first quarter of 2026 and in the nine months of 2025: USD 11.702 billion of long-term net inflows in the third quarter of 2026 (Management Language for "record", Documentary Fact for the amount).

Weaknesses. (1) Revenue visibility is short because fund advisory contracts can be ended on 60 days' notice and revenue moves with markets, flows and fee realization, which fell to 44.8 bp in the third quarter of 2026 from 47.9 bp in the second quarter (Documentary Fact). (2) Reliance on Amundi for ownership, board seats and offshore distribution concentrates a single counterparty (Documentary Fact); the Amundi preferred stock terms are Not supplied. (3) The pending First Eagle acquisition with undisclosed debt and a cash component creates leverage, execution and litigation risk (Documentary Fact for existence; the credit effect is Insufficient Information). (4) Earnings quality: adjusted EBITDA exceeds the proxy by USD 129.246 million (15.1% of adjusted EBITDA, calculated below), and the share-based compensation addback jumped in the third quarter of 2026 without the one-time part being separated (Documentary Fact). (5) Distributions to equity absorb most operating cash flow, leaving debt reduction at the scheduled amount (Analyst Interpretation). (6) Absent monitoring data: covenant ratio, guarantors and collateral, restricted payment capacity and ratings (Insufficient Information).
