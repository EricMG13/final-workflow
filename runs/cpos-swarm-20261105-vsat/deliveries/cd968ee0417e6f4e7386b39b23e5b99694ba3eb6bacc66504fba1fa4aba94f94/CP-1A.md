### Business and transaction view

Viasat, Inc. is a Delaware-incorporated global satellite communications and defense technology company with two segments and no financial sponsor or parent named in the pack. Communication services (FY2026 revenue USD 3,299.7 million) sells aviation, government satcom, maritime and fixed broadband connectivity from an owned fleet of 23 in-service or operational satellites; defense and advanced technologies (USD 1,340.6 million) sells encryption, tactical networking and space systems to governments. For a creditor the business is recurring in services, which are 71% of FY2026 revenue, but its cash needs are driven by roughly USD 1.0 billion a year of capital spending and by satellite programs that have already cost it: the ViaSat-3 F1 reflector issue left less than 10% of throughput recovered and led to a net loss of about USD 905.5 million in FY2024. The defining transaction is the May 2023 Inmarsat acquisition for about USD 2.7 billion of consideration, which created a second, separately financed debt group; in the last two fiscal years the documented actions are repayment (the 2025 Notes redeemed in May 2025, the Original Inmarsat Term Loan repaid early in November 2025, total debt down from USD 7,204.3 million to USD 6,570.5 million) and asset sales (the Navarino UK interest for USD 203.0 million in March 2026), with no dividend ever declared. Strongest support: diversified commercial customers, a firm backlog of USD 4,217.7 million at June 30, 2026, and the in-flight connectivity base of about 4,630 aircraft. Weakest points: concentration in the U.S. Government at 16% of FY2026 revenue and five contracts at 19%, the incomplete ownership picture, and the 2027 refinancing. What decides the outcome is whether ViaSat-3 F2 and F3 reach commercial service as expected and lift revenue while spending falls, and how the 2027 Notes and the Inmarsat revolver expiring in March 2027 are handled. The pack ends at June 30, 2026, so whether the satellites entered service on the expected dates is not evidenced. Ownership, control rights and sponsor behaviour cannot be judged beyond the facts below because the proxy statement is not in the pack.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The principal transaction in the evidence is the Inmarsat acquisition, completed on May 30, 2023 and described by the company as the purchase of Connect Topco Limited, a private Guernsey company, "in exchange for approximately $550.7 million in cash and 46.36 million shares of our common stock" (10-K page:6). The 10-K states total consideration of about USD 2.7 billion, comprising USD 2.1 billion of fair value of the shares and USD 550.7 million in cash, with goodwill of USD 1.5 billion in communication services (10-K page:169-170) and USD 31.3 million of transaction costs in FY2024. The financing of that deal and of Inmarsat since then is visible in the debt record: the 2023 Term Loan Facility of USD 616.7 million was entered into at the closing (10-Q June 2026 page:33), the 7.500% Senior Notes due 2031 of USD 733.4 million were issued in September 2023 to replace a USD 733.4 million unsecured bridge facility (page:42), Inmarsat amended its facilities in March 2024 to establish a USD 1.3 billion Inmarsat Term Loan Facility and a USD 550.0 million revolver (page:34), and certain Inmarsat Holdings subsidiaries issued USD 1.975 billion of 9.000% Senior Secured Notes due 2029 in September 2024 (page:41), the proceeds of which, with cash, redeemed all of the remaining USD 1.97 billion of Inmarsat 2026 Notes (10-K page:181). Status of the transaction: completed, with the share issuance of about 46.36 million shares and a role rationalization of about 800 positions that management announced as complete in November 2023 and describes as intended to achieve operational and cost efficiencies (10-K page:110; Management Language).

New debt and debt events in the periods since FY2025, all completed: in May 2025 the remaining USD 442.6 million of 5.625% Senior Notes due 2025 were redeemed at par (10-Q June 2026 page:38); in November 2025 the USD 300.0 million Original Inmarsat Term Loan was repaid early (page:34); on January 21, 2026 Viasat and ViaSat Technologies Limited entered into a USD 188.7 million direct loan facility with the Export-Import Bank of the United States, fully drawn at USD 188.7 million at March 31, 2026, repayable in 16 semi-annual installments from May 2026 to November 25, 2033 (10-Q Dec 2025 page:56; 10-K page:178); the former Ex-Im facility was repaid at maturity on October 15, 2025 (10-K page:178). No new debt was issued in Q1 FY2027 and no financing is described after June 30, 2026 in the sources. The use of proceeds for the Ex-Im facility is financing of ViaSat-3 F1 launch and insurance costs and exposure fees.

Disposals and receipts that affect liquidity: the Link-16 tactical data link sale for about USD 1.96 billion in cash completed in January 2023 (discontinued operations, 10-K page:133); the energy services system integration business divested in December 2024 (10-K page:111); the Navarino UK sale agreed in December 2025 and completed in March 2026 for USD 203.0 million of proceeds and a gain of USD 168.1 million, to a subsidiary of ICG (10-K page:110, page:133); and the Ligado settlement, under which a USD 420 million lump sum was received in October 2025, USD 100 million remains outstanding "pending resolution of certain matters", and quarterly payments of about USD 16 million with a 3% annual escalator began on September 30, 2025 (10-Q June 2026 page:46). Of the lump sum, USD 267.5 million was recorded as deferred revenue and USD 152.5 million as interest income (10-Q Dec 2025 page:52). A subsequent event after fiscal year end: a Cooperation Agreement dated May 6, 2026 between Viasat and Carronade Capital Management, LP and the persons on its Schedule A is listed as an exhibit filed with a report dated May 7, 2026 (10-K page:124); its terms, purpose and any board or governance changes are Not supplied. ### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: services were 71% of FY2026 revenue, connectivity contracts require "advance or recurring monthly payments" (10-Q June 2026 page:6), firm backlog stood at USD 4,217.7 million, and aviation services revenue rose from USD 1,048,225 thousand to USD 1,191,426 thousand in FY2026 (10-K page:205). Risk mechanic: recurring service billing and a growing in-flight connectivity base (about 4,630 aircraft) support revenue visibility and cash conversion, but fixed services revenue fell from USD 741,601 thousand to USD 607,693 thousand as bandwidth was reallocated to aviation (10-K page:106), so growth depends on a shrinking consumer line being replaced. Credit implication: supports debt service capacity and refinancing access, tempered by capacity constraints before new satellites enter service; the backlog excludes future recurring IFC revenue (10-K page:108), so it understates aviation visibility. The Q1 FY2027 revenue decline (product revenues fell) is a counter-signal, small in size.
2. Margin and cash-flow risk. Evidence: capital expenditure of USD 992,774 thousand in FY2026, a USD 169.4 million impairment and other liabilities for the EMEA exit in FY2025, and in FY2024 a reduction of about USD 1.67 billion in the carrying value of satellites under construction, partly offset by about USD 770.0 million of insurance claim receivables (10-K page:137). Risk mechanic: owned-satellite capital intensity and launch or anomaly risk absorb cash flow ahead of revenue, and management itself expects higher operating costs before new satellites reach commercial service (10-Q June 2026 page:74). Credit implication: free cash flow depends on capital spending falling as satellites complete; FY2026 cash flow also benefited from the Ligado lump sum and the Navarino proceeds (CP-1), so run-rate debt service coverage is weaker than the headline. The ViaSat-3 F1 reflector issue is the documented precedent for execution risk.
3. Concentration risk. Evidence: U.S. Government 16% of FY2026 revenue and five largest contracts 19%; "A majority of our contracts can be terminated at the convenience of the customer" (10-Q June 2026 page:69); DCAA is auditing fiscal 2025 incurred costs (page:9). Risk mechanic: government funding delays, audits and termination rights can interrupt cash receipts and revise revenue; a USD 15.1 million reserve exists for refunds. Credit implication: moderate concentration that matters most in a downturn or shutdown; no single commercial customer reaches 10%.
4. Sponsor alignment. Evidence: no sponsor, no dividends, repayment actions, no distributions beyond TrellisWare's minority. Risk mechanic: absent a controlling owner with a distribution incentive, leakage risk to creditors is low on documented actions, but ownership concentration and board rights are Not disclosed. Credit implication: alignment appears neutral to supportive on documented actions only; willingness is not inferred from identity. Counter-evidence: the Cooperation Agreement of May 2026 is unexplained and could alter board composition; its terms are Not supplied.
5. Structure and key uncertainties. Evidence: two debt groups with separate collateral and guarantors (CP-1), the Inmarsat revolver maturing March 28, 2027, the 2027 Notes in current debt, and a Viasat revolver with a springing maturity. Risk mechanic: creditor claims are structurally separated, so group-level cash and leverage drive recoveries and refinancing choices, and the springing date can remove revolver availability early. Credit implication: refinancing timing and group-level disclosure are the key uncertainties; recovery prospects cannot be assessed beyond noting the liens described, and no recovery conclusion is made.
