### Credit view

Viasat's credit is adequate but exposed: debt has fallen and liquidity is ample, yet a USD 600.0 million secured-note maturity falls due by mid-2027 and cash generation depends on satellite spending easing. The strongest support is liquidity of USD 2,886.1 million and a recurring services base, with gross leverage down to 4.58x on derived EBITDA for the twelve months to June 2026 from 5.70x in FY2025. The weakest point is thin earnings after spending: derived EBITDA less capital expenditure was only 1.39x cash interest, and operating income covers just 0.32x of interest expense. What decides the outcome is whether the ViaSat-3 satellites lift revenue while capital spending of about USD 1.0 billion a year falls, and how the 2027 Notes and the Inmarsat revolver are refinanced.

### Key findings

1. Viasat's total debt was USD 6,570.5 million at June 30, 2026, down from USD 7,204.3 million at March 31, 2025, and gross leverage is 4.58x with net leverage 3.37x on derived, unadjusted EBITDA of USD 1,435.6 million for the twelve months, against 5.70x gross and 4.43x net in FY2025. Evidence is the CP-1 metrics; the mechanism is lower debt plus a recovery from the impairment-depressed FY2025 base, and the implication is a better leverage position that is still above 4x gross and has stalled since March 2026 (net debt USD 4,838.3 million then and USD 4,834.7 million now).
2. Liquidity is large but not all permanent. Cash of USD 1,735.8 million exceeds current debt of USD 661.9 million, and undrawn revolvers add USD 1,150.3 million, but the Inmarsat revolver of USD 550.0 million matures on March 28, 2027 and the Viasat revolver of USD 647.5 million carries a springing maturity tied to other material debt, with conditions the filings do not describe. The implication is that dependable liquidity after early 2027 is closer to cash plus whatever is extended.
3. The 5.625% Senior Secured Notes of USD 600.0 million are in current debt at June 30, 2026, and the fiscal-year schedule places them between April and June 2027 by inference. The larger wall follows: USD 3,246.7 million falls in FY2030 and USD 1,341.3 million in FY2031. Refinancing access and cost matter because the Inmarsat term loan and notes carry effective or stated rates of about 9%.
4. Earnings are thin after capital intensity. Derived EBITDA covers cash interest 4.73x and interest expense 4.20x, but after capital expenditure of USD 1,013.7 million the remaining USD 421.9 million is only 1.39x cash interest of USD 303.7 million, and operating income of USD 108.8 million covers 0.32x of interest expense of USD 342.2 million. Depreciation of USD 1,014.5 million and amortization of USD 312.3 million carry the gap. The implication is that debt service relies on spending discipline and on new satellites adding revenue.
5. The reported cash flow flatters the run-rate. FY2026 operating cash flow of USD 1,589.9 million includes the Ligado lump sum of USD 420.0 million, leaving about USD 177.1 million of free cash flow for FY2026 and about USD 158.3 million for the twelve months, against reported USD 597.1 million and USD 578.3 million. FY2026 other income also includes the USD 168.1 million Navarino gain, which is outside derived EBITDA. A further USD 100 million Ligado payment remains outstanding with no collection date.
6. Business risk is moderate. Services are 71% of FY2026 revenue and firm backlog is USD 4,217.7 million, aviation services revenue rose in FY2026 and Q1 FY2027, and no commercial customer reaches 10% of revenue; against that, the U.S. Government is 16% of FY2026 revenue, the five largest contracts are 19%, product revenue fell in Q1 FY2027, and derived EBITDA for Q1 FY2027 was USD 361.3 million against USD 388.3 million a year earlier. Satellite execution risk is documented by the FY2024 ViaSat-3 F1 reflector issue, which led to a net loss of about USD 905.5 million after insurance receivables.
7. Structure and behaviour. Of the USD 6,570.5 million, USD 5,294.4 million is secured term debt, notes and the Ex-Im loan, USD 1,133.4 million is senior unsecured notes and USD 142.7 million is finance leases, split between a Viasat group and an Inmarsat group with separate collateral and guarantors. The documented behaviour is supportive for creditors: notes redeemed, an Inmarsat term loan repaid early, no dividends ever declared, no sponsor or controlling holder identified. Ownership shares, ratings and the May 2026 Cooperation Agreement terms are not supplied, so willingness is judged on actions only.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. (1) Deleveraging: total debt is USD 633.8 million lower than at March 2025 and derived coverage of cash interest rose from 3.24x to 4.73x (Documentary Fact, CP-1 figures; the ratios are derived, so read as Analyst Interpretation of filed lines). (2) Liquidity: USD 2,886.1 million, with cash covering all USD 661.9 million of current debt and leaving USD 1,073.9 million after it (Documentary Fact). (3) Recurring services base and backlog: services are 71% of revenue, connectivity is billed in advance or monthly, and firm backlog is USD 4,217.7 million (Documentary Fact; the backlog excludes future recurring in-flight connectivity revenue, so visibility is understated). (4) Customer breadth: no commercial customer at 10% of revenue and the in-flight connectivity base of about 4,630 aircraft is growing (Documentary Fact). (5) Segment balance: defense and advanced technologies earned segment operating profit of USD 216.3 million in FY2026, steady with the prior year, beside a communication services swing to profit that mainly reflects the absence of the FY2025 EMEA impairment (Documentary Fact). (6) Creditor-supportive actions: debt repaid and no dividends (Documentary Fact for the actions; the inference that they signal willingness is Analyst Interpretation).

Weaknesses. (1) Near-term maturities and expiries: USD 600.0 million of secured notes by mid-2027, the Inmarsat revolver in March 2027 and the Viasat revolver springing date (Documentary Fact, with the notes' window an inference). (2) Capital intensity: capital expenditure of USD 992.8 million in FY2026 and free cash flow of negative USD 122.0 million in FY2025 (Documentary Fact); the dependence of cash generation on spending falling is Analyst Interpretation. (3) Thin earnings after depreciation: operating income of USD 108.8 million against interest expense of USD 342.2 million (Documentary Fact, derived ratio). (4) One-off support in FY2026: the Ligado lump sum and the Navarino gain (Documentary Fact). (5) Structure: two debt groups, secured debt ahead of USD 1,133.4 million of unsecured notes, floating-rate term loans of USD 2,542.5 million at effective rates between 8.86% and 9.38% (Documentary Fact). (6) Concentration: the U.S. Government at 16% and the five largest contracts at 19% (Documentary Fact), with termination-for-convenience rights described by management (Management Language, qualified). (7) Visibility gaps: no company-defined EBITDA, covenant levels or group-level financials (Insufficient Information; missing evidence is not read as adverse).

Derived EBITDA less capital expenditure (purchases of property, equipment and satellites, and other assets), then divided by cash interest: LTM 1,435.6 - 1,013.7 = 421.9 (1,435,586 - 1,013,689 = 421,897 thousand) and 421,897 / 303,748 = 1.39x; FY2026 1,462,590 - 992,774 = 469,816 and 469,816 / 326,548 = 1.44x; FY2025 1,263,327 - 1,030,182 = 233,145 and 233,145 / 390,043 = 0.60x.

4. Liquidity: 1,735.8 + 600.3 + 550.0 = 2,886.1 (CP-1); cash less current debt: 1,735.8 - 661.9 = 1,073.9; undrawn revolvers 600.3 + 550.0 = 1,150.3.
