### Credit view

VSE Corporation is a profitable aviation aftermarket credit that has become much larger and materially more leveraged, and its credit quality now depends on integrating the Precision Aviation Group business and turning earnings into cash. The strongest support is earnings growth, with a record Adjusted EBITDA margin of 19.8% in Q3 2026, liquidity of USD 545.0 million and annual scheduled principal of no more than USD 34.1 million through 2031. The weakest point is cash conversion: free cash flow was negative USD 3.4 million for the nine months to September 2026 while net debt stands at 3.04x trailing adjusted EBITDA and the covenant levels are unknown. Delivery of the fourth-quarter cash flow management expects, with margins holding, decides the outcome.

### Key findings

1. VSE has levered up through an equity-heavy acquisition, and its leverage path is mostly a matter of earnings timing. Net debt is 3.04x trailing adjusted EBITDA against 1.24x at December 2025 [CP-1 leverage chart; S7 page:7], because the trailing earnings include only five months of the acquired business while all of its debt is on the balance sheet. Management's full-year 2026 guidance of revenue growth of 65% to 66.5% and an Adjusted EBITDA margin of 18.9% to 19.1% [S7 page:3] implies full-year Adjusted EBITDA of about USD 347 million to USD 354 million (calculated below), which would put net debt at about 2.6x on a flat net debt. This is a mechanical, not a proven, deleveraging, and it relies on guidance that the filings do not yet evidence (Analyst Interpretation on a Management Language basis). The credit implication is that leverage tolerance is acceptable if the guided run-rate is delivered and thin if the acquired earnings disappoint.

2. VSE's liquidity and maturity profile make near-term default or refinancing risk low. Cash of USD 65.8 million and unused revolver commitments of USD 479.2 million total USD 545.0 million against USD 32.3 million of principal due within twelve months, and annual scheduled principal is no more than USD 34.1 million through 2031, with the balance of the term loan due from 2032, mostly at its 2033 maturity [CP-1 maturity wall; S4 page:19]. The risk mechanic that remains is acquisition spending: the revolver helps fund bolt-on deals (Atech, acquired on 2026-08-31, was funded with cash and revolver borrowings, and USD 20.0 million was drawn at 2026-09-30 [S4 page:9 and page:17]) so liquidity can fall if acquisitions continue before free cash flow turns up.

3. Cash conversion is the weak spot. Operating cash flow was USD 21.3 million for nine months and free cash flow negative USD 3.4 million, after a USD 148.4 million inventory build [CP-1; S4 page:6], while Q3 2026 free cash flow was USD 46.6 million. Growth programs, including the new OEM auxiliary power unit program the Company names [S4 page:33], absorb inventory ahead of revenue. The credit implication is that debt reduction comes from year-end working capital release and earnings, not from structural cash generation shown so far; the write-down of slow-moving inventories was a critical audit matter in FY2025 [S1 page:40].

4. VSE's cost of debt is floating and only partly hedged. Secured floating debt is USD 917.75 million with USD 250.0 million of swaps, leaving about USD 667.8 million exposed; each 100 bp rise adds roughly USD 6.7 million of annual interest, about 2.2% of trailing adjusted EBITDA (calculated below). Trailing adjusted EBITDA covers contractual annual interest of USD 57.7 million 5.20x. Cash interest cover cannot be shown for 2026 because cash interest is not reported (FY2025 was 6.81x) [CP-1].

5. The business risks are concentration and dependence on OEMs. One affiliated customer group was about 20% of FY2025 revenue and its post-acquisition share is not disclosed [CP-1A; S1 page:11]; repair, now 58.8% of Q3 2026 revenue, depends on OEM approvals and licenses such as the Pratt & Whitney PT6 fuel pump license [CP-1A; S4 page:14]. The mechanism is that the loss of the group or an approval removes high-margin work while inventory and capacity remain. The implication is that earnings quality is exposed to two single points of failure whose terms are not in the pack.

6. Creditor control and structure cannot be tested. The filings say a maximum Total Net Leverage Ratio and a minimum Interest Coverage Ratio exist and were met [S4 page:19], but give no levels; the credit agreement, its first amendment and the seller's exchange and registration agreements are not supplied. Management's own leverage measures use different definitions. The implication is that headroom, permitted distributions and the effect of a possible share overhang on equity market access (used four times since 2024 to fund acquisitions) are open.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths.

Earnings scale and margin (Documentary Fact; Management Language for the characterisations). VSE's Adjusted EBITDA reached USD 107.2 million in Q3 2026 at a margin of 19.8% against 16.7% a year earlier, and in Q3 2026 adjustments net to a small deduction (EBITDA of USD 107.6 million against Adjusted EBITDA of USD 107.2 million [S7 page:7]), so that margin is close to unadjusted; over nine months add-backs are about a tenth of adjusted EBITDA. Basis: CP-1 financials and credit metrics; CP-1A cost-of-sales margin rows. The mechanism is operating leverage from a repair-weighted mix, which supports debt service if sustained.

Equity-funded acquisition financing (Documentary Fact). About USD 1.3 billion of net equity and tangible equity unit proceeds funded a portion of the PAG purchase, and unit holders' prepaid stock purchase contracts settle in shares in 2029, so the equity layer under the debt grew and the units add only USD 61.0 million of debt. Basis: CP-1A transaction summary; CP-1 debt table. This lowers debt-to-capitalisation (book debt about 24.7% of book debt plus equity, calculated below).

Liquidity and maturity profile (Documentary Fact). USD 545.0 million of liquidity, a revolver to 2030, and a term loan to 2033 with USD 9.0 million a year of amortization leave no large refinancing event until 2030, and then only the USD 20.0 million revolver draw. Basis: CP-1 liquidity and maturity charts.

Diversified end markets with a repair moat (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation). Revenue is 98.9% commercial across airlines, cargo, MRO providers and owners, and FAA-certified repair capacity and OEM licenses are barriers to entry. Basis: CP-1A company and operating model rows. The mechanism is aftermarket demand tied to fleet usage rather than new deliveries.

Weaknesses.

Cash conversion (Documentary Fact). Nine-month free cash flow was negative USD 3.4 million after a USD 148.4 million inventory build, with inventories at about 19.9% of total assets. Basis: CP-1 financials; CP-1A working capital row. A stalled OEM program would raise net debt while earnings look stable.

Leverage and floating-rate exposure (Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation). Net debt is 3.04x trailing adjusted EBITDA, with about USD 667.8 million of unhedged floating debt. Basis: CP-1 leverage and liquidity figures. Rising rates or an earnings miss would compress cover; covenant levels are unknown.

Customer and OEM concentration (Documentary Fact). A single customer group was about 20% of FY2025 revenue and PAG's customer mix is not disclosed. Basis: CP-1A concentration row.

Acquired-asset and integration risk (Documentary Fact). Goodwill and intangibles are 62.6% of total assets, tangible equity is only USD 178.7 million, purchase accounting is preliminary for several deals, and internal controls are being integrated. Basis: CP-1 financials; CP-1A integration row. This limits balance-sheet support in a stress and increases impairment risk if acquired earnings disappoint; it is a mechanic, not a recovery view.

Opaque creditor protections (Insufficient Information). Covenant levels, guarantor and security details, and seller-agreement terms are Not supplied, which matters for headroom, structural position and distributions.
