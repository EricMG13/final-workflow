### Business and transaction view

Vestis is a standalone, publicly listed provider of uniform rental and workplace supplies in the United States and Canada, created by the spin-off from Aramark on September 30, 2023. The sources disclose no sponsor, and no parent or borrower distinct from the listed registrant is named in them; they show no leveraged buyout, acquisition or dividend recapitalisation in the period examined. The one transaction that defines the capital structure is the separation itself: the company borrowed under a new senior secured credit agreement and transferred roughly 1,457 million (US$) of proceeds to Aramark (10-K page:112). Since then the only financing changes are a refinancing of one term loan in February 2024, a covenant amendment in May 2025 that traded relief for restrictions on dividends, buybacks and receivables facilities, and a receivables securitization put in place in August 2024.

The operating model gives good revenue visibility: recurring weekly rental service under multi-year contracts is 95% of revenue, more than 300,000 accounts and no meaningful concentration (the ten largest customers are under 10% of revenue). The countervailing evidence is that revenue is nonetheless contracting: the nine-month decline in revenue and the quarter's lower volume are explained in part by a deliberate exit of unprofitable business under the transformation plan, and fiscal 2025 revenue included the loss of a national account customer. The mechanism matters for creditors: a recurring base protects against sudden revenue loss, but contract exit costs are modest relative to a service-quality failure, and the business has a high fixed cost base of plants and routes, so volume decline compresses operating leverage unless cost is removed at the same pace. This is why the earlier financial handoff shows earnings recovery driven by cost reduction and pricing rather than volume.

The credit-relevant change is the transformation plan launched in the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Management targets annual cost savings of at least $75 million by the end of fiscal 2026 and has roughly $30 million realised through the third quarter (Q3 release page:2). These are management claims, not filed facts; the filed fact is that third-party consulting and severance costs have been incurred and that the estimated plan cost has been raised since the 10-K. Governance signals are mixed rather than adverse: no extraction-oriented behaviour is documented, dividends are suspended by the credit agreement amendment, but the principal financial officer role is filled on an interim basis and securities class actions allege misleading statements about pricing practices and outlook (allegations, not findings). Evidence quality is High for filed facts and Low for management claims on savings and market position.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transaction history in the sources is the separation from Aramark and the financings around it; there is no sponsor transaction, acquisition or disposal of size, and no new debt issue in fiscal 2026 to the cutoff. On September 29, 2023 the company and certain subsidiaries entered into a senior secured credit agreement, and the separation was completed on September 30, 2023 by a distribution of Vestis shares to Aramark holders at one Vestis share for every two Aramark shares; the company used about 1,457 million of term loan proceeds to transfer cash to Aramark (10-K page:112, page:145). The agreement was amended on February 22, 2024 to refinance the original 800 million term loan A-1 with an 800 million term loan B-1 due February 2031, and on May 1, 2025 to raise the maximum net leverage ratio in stages, grant a one-time 15 million bad debt adjustment to covenant EBITDA, cap receivables facilities at 250 million and restrict dividends and share repurchases (10-K page:113, page:115). A three-year 250 million accounts receivable securitization was put in place on August 2, 2024 (10-K page:146). Use of proceeds on the securitization was repayment of part of the term loans (Q3 10-Q page:50). In fiscal 2026 the sources show only repayments of principal, with no new instrument. Liquidity effect and covenant terms are in the earlier financial handoff and are company-reported; the executed amendments are not supplied. The company also sold its equity stake in Aramark Uniform Services Japan Corporation in the first quarter of fiscal 2025 (Q3 10-Q page:15). Status of every item above: completed; none is draft or pending.
