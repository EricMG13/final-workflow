### Credit view

Vestis is a leveraged but improving credit. Earnings and cash flow have recovered through fiscal 2026, and company-reported net leverage fell from 4.72x at October 3, 2025 to 4.10x at July 3, 2026, with liquidity of USD 351.8 million and no bank maturity before fiscal 2028. The strongest support is a recurring rental revenue base and USD 71.0 million of principal debt repaid since fiscal year end. The weakest point is that revenue and net volumes are still falling, so the recovery rests on a cost programme whose savings management, not the filings, has quantified. Two things decide the outcome: whether covenant EBITDA stays ahead of a maximum net leverage ratio that steps down from 5.00x to 4.75x and then 4.50x, and whether the receivables facility due August 2, 2027 is renewed or replaced.

### Key findings

Vestis has reduced reported leverage on every measure while its top line has contracted. Company-reported net leverage moved from 4.72x at October 3, 2025 to 4.83x at January 2, 2026, 4.47x at April 3, 2026 and 4.10x at July 3, 2026, and the leverage on the financial handoff's basis (company Adjusted EBITDA, principal debt plus finance leases) moved from 5.19x to 4.33x for total debt and from 5.07x to 4.13x for net debt over the same dates [CP-1, Chart data: leverage]. The mechanism is both a smaller numerator (principal debt of USD 1,097.5 million at July 3, 2026 after repayments) and a larger denominator, because trailing Adjusted EBITDA rose from USD 257,425 thousand to USD 290,437 thousand. For creditors this is genuine progress, categorised as Documentary Fact for the reported ratios and Analyst Interpretation for the reading that it reflects operations rather than only repayment.

The covenant cushion is adequate today and narrows on a known schedule. At July 3, 2026 the reported ratio of 4.10x sat 0.90x below the 5.00x maximum, equal to an EBITDA cushion of USD 52,961 thousand or 18.0% of covenant EBITDA; holding July 3, 2026 net debt constant, the same cushion falls to USD 40,272 thousand (13.7%) at 4.75x and USD 26,174 thousand (8.9%) at 4.50x [CP-1, Calculation steps]. Any shortfall in plan savings or a rise in net debt therefore bites first at the 4.50x test from the first quarter of fiscal 2027. The calculation is static and company-reported; it is not a forecast. The minimum interest cover covenant of 2.00x looks distant on an indicative basis (3.35x on covenant EBITDA over interest expense, net), but the executed definition is not supplied and the company does not print the ratio.

Business risk is moderate: the revenue base is recurring but shrinking. About 95% of fiscal 2025 revenue was recurring rental under multi-year contracts, spread over more than 300,000 accounts with the ten largest customers under 10% of revenue [CP-1A, Company and operating model]. Against that, nine month revenue fell 1.9%, net volumes fell 2.0%, partly through a deliberate exit of unprofitable business, and fiscal 2025 included the loss of a national account [CP-1, Financial base view; CP-1A, Credit translations]. The mechanism is operating leverage: with a fixed plant and route network, falling volume compresses margin unless cost is removed at least as fast, so the recovery in earnings depends on the transformation plan and on pricing, both of which management reports as working through the third quarter.

The transformation plan carries the earnings case and is mostly a management claim. Management targets annual savings of at least USD 75 million by the end of fiscal 2026 and reports roughly USD 30 million realised through the third quarter, while the estimated plan cost has risen from a range of USD 25 million to USD 30 million in the 10-K to a range of USD 35 million to USD 40 million in the latest 10-Q [CP-1A, Conflicts and inherited items]. Category: Management Language for the savings, Documentary Fact for the cost estimate change. For creditors the implication is that covenant headroom is partly a function of adjustments and savings that have not been independently verified, and execution risk includes service disruption and customer attrition.

Liquidity is adequate and bank maturities are distant. Liquidity of USD 351.8 million at July 3, 2026 comprised USD 294.2 million of revolver availability and USD 57.7 million of cash, with no revolver borrowings; the term loans mature in fiscal 2028 (USD 436.0 million) and fiscal 2031 (USD 661.5 million) [CP-1, Chart data: liquidity and debt maturities]. The revolver commitment of USD 300 million also matures in September 2028, so the first bank refinancing is just under two years away, not immediate.

The receivables securitization is the nearest funding event and a source of cash flow flattery. The USD 250 million facility is scheduled to terminate on August 2, 2027, and USD 212.3 million of receivables had been sold and derecognized at July 3, 2026, up USD 9.8 million since October 3, 2025 [CP-1, Calculation steps]. The mechanism is that the sold balance is not debt on the balance sheet; if the facility ended without replacement, receivables would return to the balance sheet and be funded from cash and the revolver, the credit agreement caps such facilities at USD 250 million, and the company's own net debt table does not include the sold balance. How the covenant counts the sold balance is unresolved: adding it to company net debt at July 3, 2026 gives 4.82x against the 5.00x maximum, and the same exercise at October 3, 2025 gives 5.45x, above the maximum then in force although the company reported compliance, which suggests the covenant does not count the whole balance on that basis. Category: Analyst Interpretation resting on an illustrative calculation, with the executed definition Not supplied.

Cash flow quality improved but should be read with care. Nine month operating cash flow was USD 160,873 thousand against USD 33,302 thousand a year earlier, and trailing twelve month free cash flow of USD 136,411 thousand was 47.0% of trailing Adjusted EBITDA [CP-1, Calculation steps]. Of the USD 127,571 thousand increase, the accepted financial handoff attributes USD 79.0 million to changes in operating assets and liabilities, while receivables sold rose by USD 9.8 million and USD 34,256 thousand of plan cash costs were absorbed in operating cash flow. Implication: free cash flow is real but includes working capital release that is unlikely to repeat at the same scale, and dividends and buybacks are restricted until conditions are met, so cash is going to debt service rather than shareholders.

Governance looks creditor-neutral with monitoring items. No sponsor, dividend recapitalisation or creditor-adverse action is documented, dividends are restricted, and the company reports its covenant ratio and liquidity each quarter; offsetting this, the chief financial officer role is interim, securities class actions alleging misleading disclosure are in discovery, and tax-free spin-off restrictions and indemnification obligations to Aramark remain unquantified [CP-1A, Governance and sponsor behaviour]. Category: Documentary Fact for the events, Insufficient Information for the exposures.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. First, recurring revenue and breadth: recurring weekly rental is about 95% of revenue, with no meaningful customer concentration, which supports revenue visibility and limits event risk [Documentary Fact, from the business handoff]. Second, deleveraging with real cash generation: principal debt is down USD 71.0 million since October 3, 2025 and trailing free cash flow of USD 136,411 thousand funded it, with the revolver undrawn [Documentary Fact for the debt and cash flow, from the financial handoff]. Third, liquidity and maturity profile: liquidity of USD 351.8 million and no bank maturity until fiscal 2028 give time to execute the plan [Documentary Fact]. Fourth, a single secured bank facility with no junior debt in the sources: all funded borrowings sit in one credit agreement (finance leases and the receivables facility sit alongside it) guaranteed by the company and substantially all wholly-owned domestic material subsidiaries and secured on substantially all tangible assets, which keeps the structure simple and the covenant package, once read in full, central to monitoring [Documentary Fact; legal capacity Not supplied]. Fifth, improving margins: trailing Adjusted EBITDA margin was 10.8% against 9.4% for fiscal 2025 on the company's non-GAAP definition [Management Language, company-defined].

Weaknesses. First, leverage remains high and the covenant tightens: reported net leverage of 4.10x is below the 5.00x maximum, but the maximum falls to 4.50x from the first quarter of fiscal 2027, which leaves a smaller cushion at constant net debt [Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on the cushion]. Second, a shrinking top line: revenue down 1.9% and volume down 2.0% over nine months mean earnings recovery is cost-led [Documentary Fact]. Third, dependence on a management-quantified plan whose cost estimate has risen and whose savings are unaudited [Management Language]. Fourth, off-balance-sheet receivables funding with a defined end date and unresolved covenant treatment [Documentary Fact; Analyst Interpretation on the covenant]. Fifth, definition risk: two company figures for fiscal 2025 Adjusted EBITDA differ, and covenant EBITDA includes adjustments the company defines [Documentary Fact]. Sixth, leadership and legal overhang: an interim chief financial officer and pending securities class actions [Documentary Fact; exposure Insufficient Information]. Seventh, supply chain concentration: about 60% of uniforms and linens are made in two plants in Mexico, with tariff sensitivity not quantified [Documentary Fact; sensitivity Insufficient Information].
