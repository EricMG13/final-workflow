### Credit view

Weatherford International plc has a sound fundamental credit profile at the 2026-09-30 balance sheet date, with low reported leverage and ample liquidity, but its earnings are cyclical and currently depressed by Middle East disruption. The strongest support is total debt of USD 1,480 million, or 1.48x trailing company-defined adjusted EBITDA, against cash of USD 1,161 million and undrawn bank commitments of USD 766 million. The weakest point is the earnings and cash flow trend: nine-month 2026 revenue fell to USD 3,400 million and operating cash flow before working capital fell to USD 438 million. What decides the outcome is how fast regional activity recovers, and whether the pending redomestication or shareholder payouts change obligor and covenant protections that the sources do not reveal. Confidence is Medium. [CP-1 financial base view; CP-1A business and transaction view]

### Key findings

The first finding a committee must read is that Weatherford carries little leverage on every measure the sources allow. Total debt of USD 1,480 million is 1.48x trailing adjusted EBITDA of USD 1,001 million and 1.71x EBITDA before add-backs of USD 867 million; net debt of USD 282 million is 0.28x. The ratio on the company's definition has crept up from 1.39x at 2025-12-31 because trailing EBITDA fell while debt was flat, and net leverage fell only because cash rose. [CP-1 leverage chart data and calculations 5 and 6]. The mechanism is that low leverage gives room to absorb a further earnings decline, but the cushion is in cash that is partly trapped, not in falling debt.

The second finding is that liquidity is strong and the near-term maturity is already funded. Cash of USD 1,161 million is 4.40x the current portion of long-term debt of USD 264 million, which is mostly the 2030 notes that Weatherford called on 2026-10-20 for redemption from cash on 2026-10-30. Undrawn commitments are USD 766 million after letters of credit, of which only about USD 599 million can be borrowed for cash on the facility split disclosed in the annual report. Liquidity is 1.30x total debt, or 1.19x on the cash-drawable basis. Redemption completion is not evidenced, and the pro forma net debt after it would be about the same, since the notes are repaid with cash. [CP-1 liquidity, calculations 8 and 11; CP-1A transaction summary]

The third finding is that earnings are weakening in 2026 and cash flow quality is lower than it looks. Nine-month 2026 revenue of USD 3,400 million is 6.3% below USD 3,629 million a year earlier and operating income of USD 383 million is 31.2% below USD 557 million, because the Iran Conflict cut Middle East, North Africa and Asia revenue even though oil prices and rig counts were higher. Operating cash flow rose to USD 503 million from USD 408 million only through a working capital release of USD 65 million against an outflow of USD 116 million, so cash flow before working capital fell by USD 86 million. The mechanism is operating leverage on a fixed cost base plus receivable timing, and it matters because free cash flow supports both debt service and shareholder payouts. [CP-1 cash flow tables and calculation 10; CP-1A operating model rows on the Iran Conflict and cost structure]

The fourth finding is that concentrated receivables, trapped cash and contingent instruments can reduce usable liquidity. The largest Mexican customer was 14% of net receivables at 2026-09-30 and has a history of late payment; about USD 164 million of cash cannot be immediately repatriated, with USD 128 million held in Russian operations; and letters of credit and surety bonds totalling USD 1,065 million sit outside debt and can be called on breach of contract or performance. That total is 0.72x total debt and is not additive to the liquidity measure. [CP-1 claims outside the debt total; CP-1A concentration rows]

The fifth finding is that the capital structure and legal perimeter are in transition and the sources do not describe the lender effects. Debt was refinanced into 6.75% notes due 2033 in 2025, NCS Multistage was acquired mostly for shares, and entities were added as co-issuers of the notes, most recently on 2026-09-25. The parent has shareholder approval to move from Ireland to a Delaware corporation, subject to court sanction, with a planned intellectual property transition under evaluation. The mechanism is that obligor, guarantor and collateral arrangements could change while the documents that govern them are not available. [CP-1A transaction summary, governance table and conflicts table]

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. Low reported leverage (Documentary Fact): total debt is 1.48x and net debt 0.28x of trailing company-defined adjusted EBITDA, and the interest burden is light, with adjusted EBITDA 9.02x trailing cash interest paid [CP-1]. Large liquidity (Documentary Fact): USD 1,161 million of cash and USD 766 million of undrawn commitments, with zero borrowings on the facility [CP-1]. A refinanced, longer debt profile (Documentary Fact): most bond debt now matures in 2033 and the remaining 2030 notes are called, which the company expects to reduce annualized cash interest by about USD 20 million (Management Language) [CP-1; CP-1A timeline]. Diversified operations (Documentary Fact): three well-lifecycle segments across about 75 countries, with most revenue earned internationally [CP-1A operating model]. Free cash flow covers payouts (Analyst Interpretation): nine-month 2026 adjusted free cash flow of USD 379 million exceeded the shareholder return of USD 113 million, which is 29.8% of it, and no debt-funded distribution is evidenced [CP-1; CP-1A ownership and capital allocation]. No sponsor or extraction behaviour is documented (Documentary Fact) [CP-1A governance table].

Weaknesses. Earnings are cyclical and falling in 2026 (Documentary Fact): revenue and operating income are lower and adjusted EBITDA for nine months fell 8.5% to USD 710 million from USD 776 million [CP-1]. Cash flow before working capital fell (Analyst Interpretation): the rise in reported operating cash flow depends on receivable collections that may not repeat [CP-1]. Regional and customer concentration (Documentary Fact): the largest region is the disrupted one, a Mexican customer has a late payment history, and Russia carries sanctions and cash access risk [CP-1A]. Fixed cost leverage (Documentary Fact): cost of products and services rose to 69% of revenue from 65% in FY2025 as costs fell more slowly than revenue [CP-1A]. Definition and covenant opacity (Insufficient Information): adjusted EBITDA is company-defined and covenant ratios and definitions are not reported, so headroom cannot be measured [CP-1]. Structural transition (Insufficient Information): the effect of the redomestication and the intellectual property transition on guarantors and collateral is undescribed, and ownership beyond a widely held listing is undisclosed [CP-1A].

### Credit thesis

Weatherford is a low-leverage, well-funded oilfield services issuer whose creditworthiness is driven by the speed of recovery from a regional demand shock and by the stability of its obligor and covenant structure through the pending corporate move. (Analyst Interpretation; basis: financial base and business context handoffs.)
