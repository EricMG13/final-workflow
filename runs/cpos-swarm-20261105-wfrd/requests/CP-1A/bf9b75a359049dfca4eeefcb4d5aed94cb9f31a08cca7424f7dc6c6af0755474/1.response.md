### Business and transaction view

Weatherford is a listed, widely held global oilfield services group that sells equipment and services across the well lifecycle through three segments: Drilling and Evaluation, Well Construction and Completions, and Production and Intervention. The consolidated reporting entity is Weatherford International plc, an Irish public limited company with principal executive offices in Houston, Texas. The sources disclose no sponsor, no controlling shareholder and no dividend recapitalisation history, so creditor control rests on the debt documents rather than on an owner. The creditor-relevant story in the sources is operating and structural. First, demand follows customer capital spending and rig activity, and in 2026 the Iran Conflict has cut regional activity even as oil prices were higher; revenue has fallen while margins were held by cost action. Second, receivable and cash concentration (a Mexican customer with late payments, Russian operations holding cash, cash that cannot be repatriated freely) can slow conversion of earnings to usable liquidity. Third, the capital structure was reshaped in the last twelve months: a 2025 refinancing into 6.75% notes due 2033, an equity-funded acquisition of NCS Multistage Holdings, Inc. that closed on 2026-09-01, an announced redemption of the remaining 8.625% notes due 2030 from cash, and a pending redomestication of the parent from Ireland to Delaware that shareholders approved on 2026-09-03 and that awaits court sanction. The terms of the redomestication that bear on lenders (guarantors, collateral, the intellectual property transition) are not described in the sources. Governance evidence is limited to documented actions: refinancing and redemption that reduce interest cost, continued dividends and buybacks funded from operating cash flow, and co-issuer additions on the notes whose purpose is not explained. This supports a Supportive to Neutral reading of issuer-level behaviour, with Insufficient Information on legal capacity. The view carries Medium confidence because filings control the facts, but the executed credit agreement, the indentures and the proxy statement that would show ownership are not in the pack.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

New debt issues: the sources show one new bond issue in the last two years and none in 2026. On 2025-10-06 Weatherford International Ltd. issued 6.75% senior notes due 2033-10-15 (principal 1.2 billion as printed); the tender offer and redemption of most of the 8.625% senior notes due 2030 used the proceeds of that issue and cash on hand, and the company recorded a 39 loss on extinguishment and redemption premium (10-K page:84 and page:44; Documentary Fact). The mechanism for a creditor is lower coupon and a maturity pushed from 2030 to 2033 on most of the bond debt, with the stub of the 2030 notes (principal about 236) left outstanding until the October 2026 call. On 2025-09-18 the credit agreement was amended to raise the commitment from 720 to 1 billion with an accordion to 1.15 billion and to extend its maturity (10-K page:55; Documentary Fact); the amendment text is not supplied. No new borrowing is reported in the nine months to 2026-09-30 (CP-1 cash flow table; Q3 10-Q page:3 and page:4).

Acquisition: on 2026-05-31 Weatherford signed a definitive merger agreement to acquire NCS Multistage Holdings, Inc. for shares or a mix of shares and cash, subject to proration (Q2 10-Q page:14), and it closed on 2026-09-01 for total consideration of 128, made up of 13 cash net of cash acquired, 1.0 million ordinary shares valued at 101 and 7 for assumed equity awards (Q3 10-Q page:15; Documentary Fact). The acquired business brought tangible assets of 150, intangible assets and goodwill of 46 and liabilities of 34, plus 34 of noncontrolling interest (page:15). Management states that it expects "at least $15 million of cost synergies upon closing and integration" (Q2 release page:2; Management Language, not adopted). Mechanism: because most consideration was shares and assumed awards, the deal added little cash outflow or debt, so leverage effects are limited, but the 10-Q does not say whether NCS carried debt, and integration risk and cost remain. Appetite for further acquisitions is not inferred from one deal. The Q3 10-Q records that Advent-NCS Acquisition Limited Partnership, NCS's largest shareholder, received 357,159 shares, resold under a registered prospectus supplement (page:38); this is not a control position.

Redomestication: the first proposal (Ireland to Texas, announced 2026-04-02) received over 60% support but not the 75% needed and failed in June 2026 (Q1 10-Q page:12; Q2 release page:5). An updated proposal to redomesticate to Delaware was approved by shareholders on 2026-09-03 and is subject to sanction by the High Court of Ireland and other steps, with completion expected in the fourth quarter of 2026 (Q3 10-Q page:16). Management expects annual cash savings of 20 to 30 from 2027 (Q3 release page:6; Management Language). Related restructuring activities, including the planned intellectual property transition, are "being evaluated for their potential accounting and tax implications" (Q3 10-Q page:16). Creditor mechanism: a change of parent and a transfer of intellectual property can alter which entities hold assets, who guarantees the debt and how the collateral is arranged, and whether credit agreement or indenture conditions apply. The sources do not describe any of those effects, so the lender impact is Insufficient Information and the pending status must be carried into every consumer. The 2026-09-25 supplemental indenture adding Weatherford International Holding LLC as co-issuer of the 2033 notes and releasing its guarantee (Q3 10-Q page:10 and page:39) may be a step in this reorganisation, but the sources do not say so and this is not inferred.

This is a subsequent event after the 2026-09-30 balance sheet, announced and expected before the cutoff but not confirmed as completed in any source. Because the facility's early maturity trigger refers to more than 200 of the 2030 notes outstanding (Q3 10-Q page:11), completing the redemption would remove that trigger (Analyst Interpretation).

### Credit translations

1. Revenue durability. Evidence: revenue for nine months 2026 fell from 3,629 to 3,400 while rig counts and oil prices were higher (Q3 10-Q pages:2, 18, 19). Risk mechanic: the Iran Conflict removed regional activity in Middle East, North Africa and Asia, the group's largest region (10-K page:81; Q3 10-Q page:20), so revenue is driven by a regional shock and not by commodity prices alone; management expects recovery to be gradual and uncertain (Q3 10-Q page:29, Management Language). Credit implication: coverage and leverage metrics in CP-1 are exposed to a further or longer disruption; the timing of recovery is the key uncertainty. Counter-evidence: three segments and about 75 countries diversify the base, and sequential Q3 2026 revenue rose in North America and Latin America (Q3 release page:7, Documentary Fact).
2. Margin and cash flow risk. Evidence: cost of products and services was 69% of revenue in FY2025 against 65% as fixed costs fell more slowly than revenue (10-K page:42); nine-month operating income fell from 557 to 383 (Q3 10-Q page:2). Risk mechanic: fixed cost leverage cuts margins when volume falls; cost action (restructuring of 28) offsets only partly. Credit implication: free cash flow conversion shown in CP-1 depends on working capital release and cost savings that may not repeat.
3. Concentration risk.
