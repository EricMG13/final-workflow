### Business and transaction view

Whirlpool is a listed, widely held major and small appliance manufacturer with no sponsor named in the sources, and its recent transactions have been defensive: sell down non-core stakes, raise equity, cut distributions, and refinance bank and euro debt into secured paper. The business is concentrated in North America, which supplied most nine-month 2026 net sales of 10,467 million through MDA North America at 7,141 million, and in one retail channel, with Lowe's about 15% of 2025 net sales. Demand in North America is soft, tariffs and cost inflation are compressing margins, and management has cut its 2026 earnings outlook twice since May. The June 2026 refinancing gave the company a 2,000 million asset-based facility, undrawn at quarter end, and replaced the 2026 and 2027 euro notes with second lien notes, so near-term maturities are cleared at the cost of subordinating unsecured creditors in the collateral pool. Governance behaviour in the sources is on balance supportive of liquidity (equity issuance, dividend cuts, asset sales and sale-leasebacks) with a mixed effect on creditor ranking. The decisive unknowns are legal: what the indentures permit, what the ABL borrowing base allows, and who the large holders are, none of which is supplied. Source evidence is primary and dated, except management characterisations, which are labelled.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

The transactions that matter to creditors are these, all dated on or before the cutoff of 2026-11-05. On February 27, 2026 Whirlpool issued 11,500,000 depositary shares of its 8.50% Series A Mandatory Convertible Preferred Stock (net cash proceeds 557 million) and 7,898,550 common shares at 69 dollars per share (net cash proceeds 524 million), and used about 900 million of the proceeds to repay part of its prior revolving credit facility (10-Q Q3 pages:52-53, 57). In March 2026 it paid 157 million to buy the legal title to five assets previously held under synthetic leases (10-Q Q3 page:11). In May 2026 it amended the Long-Term Credit Agreement to cut the commitment from 3.50 billion to 2.25 billion, excluded the March 31, 2026 quarter from the interest coverage test and required a full refinancing before July 1, 2026 (10-Q Q1 page:17). In June 2026 it agreed with Arcelik to sell its remaining 25% of Beko Europe, terminate deferred rights from the 2022 Russian business divestiture and release accrued indemnities, for net cash of 84 million plus a 2.9% Arcelik stake, booking a gain of 139 million (10-Q Q3 pages:13, 46). On June 16, 2026 it issued 1,000 million of 7.500% Senior Secured Second Lien Notes due 2031 and 1,000 million of 7.875% Senior Secured Second Lien Notes due 2034 under a Rule 144A private placement, entered a new asset-based credit facility of up to 2,000 million with J.P. Morgan as agent, and terminated the Long-Term Credit Agreement; the notes and the facility share one collateral pool, first-priority for the facility and second-priority for the notes, guaranteed by domestic and Canadian subsidiaries that are ABL obligors. The proceeds and ABL borrowings repaid the 1.250% Senior Notes due 2026, the 1.100% Senior Notes due 2027 and the unsecured revolver by July 1, 2026, with 212 million of cash restricted at June 30 for that payment (10-Q Q3 pages:17-21; 10-Q Q2 page:7). The indenture limits additional debt, restricted payments, liens, investments, asset sales and mergers, requires a 101% change of control offer and may require asset-sale offers at 100% (10-Q Q3 page:18). The ABL carries a springing fixed charge coverage test of 1.00 to 1.00 when Availability falls below the greater of 10% of the Line Cap and 135 (10-Q Q3 pages:20-21). Other liquidity actions were sale-leasebacks of two assets for about 111 million in June and one for about 59 million in September, with initial annual rent of about 9 million and 5 million, and a receivables purchase agreement amended in Q3 to capacity of up to 250 million with a first-priority security interest for the purchaser in unsold program receivables (10-Q Q3 pages:11-12, 23). On July 1, 2026 it announced the Supsa plant closure. In Q3 2026 it recorded a 124 million impairment of its Whirlpool India stake and a 54 million charge on the Casas Bahia bankruptcy reorganization (10-Q Q3 pages:14, 55). The December 2025 background is the sale of about 11% of Whirlpool India for about 166 million, reducing ownership from 51% to about 40%, with deconsolidation and a gain of 251 million (10-Q Q3 page:45). Debt raised in 2026 and its effect on the debt stack and liquidity are quantified in CP-1: total debt of 6,852 million, cash of 1,103 million and undrawn committed facilities of 2,193 million at September 30, 2026. No transaction was reported after September 30, 2026 other than management statements in the October 26, 2026 release (Q4 price increases and the net debt expectation); the 10-Q Q3 reports "ITEM 5. OTHER INFORMATION None." (10-Q Q3 page:68). The executed indenture and ABL agreement are not supplied, so lien, guarantee, baskets and permitted payment terms are known only as the issuer summarises them.
