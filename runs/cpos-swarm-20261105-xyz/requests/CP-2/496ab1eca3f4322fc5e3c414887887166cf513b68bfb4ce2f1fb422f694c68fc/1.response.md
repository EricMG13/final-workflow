### Credit view

Block, Inc. has a moderate credit profile: strong stated liquidity is set against leverage that looks high on filed earnings and low on management's adjusted earnings. The strongest support is liquidity: USD 7,859.3 million of cash and cash equivalents and an undrawn USD 900.0 million revolver, against USD 5,775.0 million of note principal and no note due before the 2027 convertible. The weakest point is earnings quality: total debt to EBITDA is 4.67x on the filed basis but 1.69x on management's Adjusted EBITDA, because share-based compensation and large legal charges are added back, while USD 764 million for the Department of Justice matter is accrued but unpaid. The outcome turns on the final size and timing of the legal payments, the unpublished covenant level and whether credit losses in the growing lending book stay contained.

### Key findings

First, Block's liquidity is its main credit support: cash and cash equivalents of USD 7,859.3 million and an undrawn USD 900.0 million revolver exceed total debt of USD 7,318.5 million, and no note matures before the USD 575.0 million convertible in November 2027, so near-term refinancing exposure is mostly in the secured warehouse lines, whose final maturities are USD 515.0 million in 2027 (USD 447.4 million of it classified current), rather than the bond stack. Second, leverage is highly sensitive to the earnings definition: filed-basis total debt to EBITDA rose from 4.19x at FY2025 to 4.67x at LTM Q3 2026 while interest cover fell from 8.44x to 3.99x, whereas management's basis shows 1.69x and 11.03x; the add-backs include contingencies, restructuring and other charges and share-based compensation, so the covenant outcome cannot be inferred. Third, legal claims sit outside the debt figures: USD 764 million is accrued for the Department of Justice matter, accrued legal contingencies rose from USD 173.0 million to USD 980.6 million, and the filing says losses above the accrual are reasonably possible; a USD 150 million insurer payment in the derivative settlement is a possible partial offset but is not final. Fourth, reported operating cash flow less capital expenditure of USD 3,453.4 million over the LTM period is flattered by this unpaid accrual and excludes lending outflows. Fifth, lending growth increases funding reliance on secured warehouse borrowings of USD 1,543.5 million and brokered certificates of deposit of USD 381.4 million, which rank ahead of unsecured note holders on those assets and depend on the bank subsidiary remaining well capitalized.

### Strengths and weaknesses

Strengths. (1) Liquidity and maturity profile, Documentary Fact: stated liquidity of USD 10,064.0 million, the undrawn revolver maturing in January 2031, note maturities spread from 2027 to 2033 and the 2026 maturities of USD 1,575.0 million repaid in cash, which supports debt service without market access (an Analyst Interpretation). (2) Gross profit mix, Documentary Fact: payments and lending drive gross profit while bitcoin revenue, 27% of Q3 2026 revenue, contributes 2% of gross profit, so bitcoin price swings change revenue more than earnings. (3) Fixed-rate unsecured note stack, Analyst Interpretation: coupons from 0.25% to 6.50% give a run-rate annual coupon of USD 293.9 million on USD 5,775.0 million of principal, about 18.8% of the add-back-free LTM EBITDA of USD 1,567.0 million (a calculated share), so the fixed charge looks manageable if earnings hold.

Weaknesses. (1) Add-back dependence, Documentary Fact for the figures and Analyst Interpretation for the reading: Adjusted EBITDA of USD 4,336.4 million is more than double filed EBITDA of USD 1,567.0 million, and contingencies charges recur across 2026 quarters, so the add-backs are not one-off in the period. (2) Unpaid legal claims, Documentary Fact: the accrual and the possibility of excess losses are claims outside debt that will draw on cash, and the final agreement and court approval are pending. (3) Credit losses in a growing lending book, Documentary Fact: transaction, loan and consumer receivable losses were USD 601.7 million in Q3 2026 against USD 363.5 million a year earlier, which reduces operating income directly. (4) Customer cash, Analyst Interpretation: customers payable exceed customer funds by USD 2,343.2 million before USD 1,454.2 million of settlements receivable, a residual of USD 889.0 million that corporate cash may fund, so net debt of negative USD 540.8 million may overstate surplus cash. (5) Control and capital allocation, Documentary Fact and Analyst Interpretation: Class B holders control a majority of votes, and share repurchases of USD 700.9 million in 2026 compete with deleveraging for the same cash.

## Calculations

All figures are accepted values from the financial and business handoffs; no new source figure is introduced. Arithmetic done here from those values:

1. Cash plus revolver minus total debt: 7,859.3 + 900.0 = 8,759.3; total debt 7,318.5; cash alone exceeds total debt by 7,859.3 - 7,318.5 = 540.8 (equal to the negative net debt of 540.8).
2. Adjusted EBITDA to filed EBITDA: 4,336.4 / 1,567.0 = 2.77 times, which supports "more than double". Check: 1,567.0 x 2 = 3,134.0, less than 4,336.4.
3. Run-rate coupon share: 293.9 / 1,567.0 = 18.8% of filed LTM EBITDA (a calculated figure, not a filed one; the coupon is the handoff's calculated run-rate).
4. 2027 maturities as stated: 575.0 + 515.0 = 1,090.0 (convertible plus warehouse final maturities; handoff table value 1,090.0).
5. Customer cash residual: 7,700.1 - 5,356.9 = 2,343.2; 2,343.2 - 1,454.2 = 889.0, as in the financial handoff.
6. Repurchase and repayment: 2026 repayments 575.0 + 1,000.0 = 1,575.0 (business handoff).
7. Leverage change: 4.67x - 4.19x = 0.48x increase; cover change 8.44x to 3.99x is a decrease of 4.45x. The periods differ (FY2025 against LTM Q3 2026), so the comparison is across different twelve-month windows.
