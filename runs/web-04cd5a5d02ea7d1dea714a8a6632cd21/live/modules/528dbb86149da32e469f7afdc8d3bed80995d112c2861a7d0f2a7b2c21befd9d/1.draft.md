### Business and transaction view

TransDigm's installed component base supports repeat replacement and maintenance demand, with certification requirements providing a plausible barrier to switching. That is an operating-risk benefit, not proof of contracted backlog or immunity from aircraft utilization, OEM production disruptions, defense ordering or price scrutiny. The company's reported aftermarket economics are management characterizations and remain mix-sensitive.

For creditors, capital allocation is the principal counterweight: an established acquisition program and documented distributions mean improved operating cash generation need not become debt reduction. The September debt-funded dividend declaration is direct evidence of shareholder extraction; its October settlement must not be folded into September cash. Refinancing and repricing demonstrate historical market access and extend parts of the debt profile, but do not certify future access, legal headroom or unrestricted availability of consolidated cash. There is no proposed deal to underwrite beyond these historical transactions.

### Transaction summary and new debt issues

**Proposed transaction — Insufficient Information.** Type, parties, sponsor, proceeds, debt raised, terms and liquidity effect: Not supplied in context page:1. The following are completed historical transactions disclosed by the issuer, not a reconstructed proposed deal. **Acquisitions — Documentary Fact; completed.** FPT was acquired on March 1, 2024, for approximately USD 57 million in cash; it supplies specialist fuel tanks and flotation systems and is reported in Airframe (Annual page:137: “The acquisition was financed through existing cash on hand”). SEI was acquired on May 21, 2024, for approximately USD 171 million in cash, including its working-capital settlement; aerial firefighting and liquid-transportation products were added to Airframe (page:135: “The acquisition was financed through existing cash on hand”). CPI's Electron Device Business was acquired on June 6, 2024, for approximately USD 1,385 million in cash and added to Power & Control (page:133). Raptor Scientific was acquired on July 31, 2024, for approximately USD 647 million in cash and added to Airframe (page:131: “The acquisition was financed through existing cash on hand”). The filing describes purchases of all outstanding stock; individual sellers and any target financial sponsors are not established here. No sponsor involvement is inferred.

CPI's funding is explicitly differentiated: Annual page:133 says cash included “a portion of the cash proceeds from the new long-term debt issued during the first quarter of fiscal 2024”. Cash funding for the other targets is not automatically relabeled debt funding. Each deal consumes cash and adds integration demands; no realized synergy, target annualized EBITDA or acquisition-driven deleveraging is asserted. Annual pages 131–137 leave measurement periods open and state that acquisition pro forma sales and results are not provided on materiality grounds. These are historical cash purchases with incomplete earnings underwriting, not evidence of failed integration.

**Acquisition financing — Documentary Fact; issued and drawn.** On November 28, 2023, the issuer raised USD 1,000 million of Tranche J loans and USD 1,000 million of 7.125% senior secured notes due December 1, 2031. The original Tranche J spread was Term SOFR plus 3.25%, due February 28, 2031 (Annual page:146). Page:147 says proceeds, with cash, were used “primarily to fund the acquisition of CPI's Electron Device Business”. Issuance provided liquidity ahead of acquisition closing; it also added funded debt before a full year of target earnings. These original loan terms are historical, not the year-end repriced spread.

**Refinancing and amendment chronology — Documentary Fact; completed.** On February 27, 2024, the revolver was extended from May 2026 to February 2029, commitment increased from USD 810 million to USD 910 million and margin reduced to Term SOFR plus 2.25% (Annual page:147: “extend the maturity date”). The same date brought USD 2,200 million of 6.375% secured notes due March 1, 2029 and USD 2,200 million of 6.625% secured notes due March 1, 2032. Page:147 says proceeds were used to repurchase the secured notes due 2026; page:149 dates redemption of all USD 4,400 million to March 28, 2024, at par plus accrued interest. This addresses a nearer maturity but is refinancing, not principal deleveraging.

On March 22, 2024, Tranche I was repriced to Term SOFR plus 2.75%, and approximately USD 1,708 million of Tranche H due February 2027 was replaced with Tranche K due March 22, 2030 at Term SOFR plus 2.75% (Annual page:148: “repaid in full” and “replaced such loans”). A USD 550 million additional 6.375% secured-note issuance due 2029 funded redemption of USD 550 million of 7.50% subordinated notes due 2027, completed April 22, 2024 (pages 149–150). Extending funding while replacing subordinated with secured borrowing changes the creditor mix; filing summaries do not establish the complete collateral-ranking consequences.

On June 4, 2024, Amendment No. 16 repriced USD 997 million of Tranche J to Term SOFR plus 2.50% and converted USD 2,644 million of Tranche I due August 2028 into Tranche J due February 28, 2031 (Annual page:150: “amended and extended”). Lower margins and longer maturity improve financing flexibility without reducing principal. No coercive exchange, priming or non-pro-rata treatment is established by these disclosed actions.

On July 12, 2024, the receivables facility capacity increased to USD 650 million and maturity extended to July 11, 2025, at Term SOFR plus 1.45% (Annual page:150). Page:151 says it is “collateralized by substantially all” domestic trade receivables. Its borrowing base depends on receivables, so this is separate short-term collateral-dependent liquidity, not an unconditional extension of the long-term funding runway.

**Dividend recapitalisation — Documentary Fact; debt issued and dividend declared before year-end, cash settlement subsequent.** On September 19, 2024, USD 1,500 million of 6.00% secured notes due January 15, 2033 and USD 1,500 million of Tranche L at Term SOFR plus 2.50%, due January 19, 2032, were issued; Tranche L was fully drawn (Annual pages 54 and 151). Page:151 explicitly links both issues and cash on hand to the special dividend declaration. Annual page:54 states that approximately USD 4,348 million of cash payments were funded by USD 3,000 million of new secured debt plus existing cash, and “These payments were made in October 2024.” The declaration and debt belong to year-end facts; payment does not. New secured claims financed shareholder cash extraction rather than a new operating asset.

**Earlier distribution — Documentary Fact; paid during FY2024.** On November 27, 2023, the issuer paid a USD 35.00 per-share special dividend and eligible option equivalents; approximately USD 2,020 million was funded by existing cash (Annual page:54). This is cash extraction but is not separately labeled debt funded. CP-1's FY2024 total distribution cash-flow figure includes other equivalents and is not identical to this event amount.

**Subsequent events — Documentary Fact; excluded from year-end balances.** In addition to the October dividend settlement, the remaining USD 162.5 million of receivables availability was drawn in October (Annual page:152: “Subsequent Event”). Annual page:56 presents issuer pro forma liquidity after the dividend, rather than an actual October balance. CP-1's illustrative cash after that payment alone is USD 1,913 million; it excludes other subsequent flows and is not actual post-payment cash. These events qualify, but do not replace, the reported year-end liquidity.
